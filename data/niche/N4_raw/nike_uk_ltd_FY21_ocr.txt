Principal risks and uncertainties
The management of the business and the execution of the company's strategy are subject to the following risks
and uncertainties:
Impact of Coronavirus (COVID-19): The global pandemic continues to be dynamic'and the UK & Ireland
faced two further national lockdowns for prolonged periods during the financial year, with tight restrictions in
movements of people and physical store closures. The company will continue to closely monitor and navigate
through this period of uncertainty and dynamically adjust to any changing landscape.
During this unprecedented time, our first priority remains the health and safety of our employees, partners and
communities, following the guidance of science-based recommendations, Government and Local Health
Authority directives. Furthermore, we will continue to flexibly manage and control our variable operational
spend to align with Nike group company directives and any potential future downturmn in commission-based
revenues that we may encounter from the sale of footwear, apparel or accessories on behalf on Nike group
companies.
Potential change in market conditions and administrative requirements following the UK decision to leave
the EU: The company continues to closely monitor, consider and fully prepare for any direct or indirect areas
of potential impact to the business as a result of the UK exit from the EU. This has been through the
meticulous planning prior to the 31 December 2020 exit date and in the subsequent monitoring and adjusting to
new requirements on the business during the initial months following the UK's official separation from the
EU.
.Loss of market share to competitors: The company operates in a competitive marketplace and therefore, in
order to mitigate potential reduced commission-based revenue risk, it continues to undertake appropriate local
marketing activity throughout the year on behalf of Nike group companies, to facilitate ongoing brand.
awareness and resultant consumer demand. The company further benefits from additional brand exposure
gained through significant Global marketing activities from other Nike group companies.
Pension risk: The company has a closed Defined Benefit Pension Scheme which faces inherent risk as its
funding position can be adversely impacted by investment performance and liability exposure to marketplace
: factors. The company actively engages with the Pension Trustee Board who utilise external professional
investment fund managers and'advisors on all investment management and technical support.
Capital management: The company relies on the Group Treasury Department for the management of its'
capital. This department identifies, evaluates and hedges financial risks at a group level, providing cash
funding to the company as and when required. The group's objective when managing capital (which includes
share capital, borowings, working capital and cash and cash equivalents) is to maintain a flexible capital
structure that reduces the cost of capital to an acceptable level of risk and to safeguard each company's ability
to continue to operate while taking advantage of strategic opportunities in order to max imise stakeholder
returns sustainably. Further information on capital can be.found in the primary financial statements and notes

Methodology
: The Scope 1 and Scope 2 energy use and greenhouse gas emissions data for 2021 has been independently
produced from information provided by the company to an external consultancy with expertise in this area.
To calculate the footprint, data was collated.from across the company and from its suppliers to identify the
amount of energy used in our operations. The company uses the most robust and accurate data source available
for each component of its energy use and carbon erission calculations. Assumptions and estimations are only
-used when strictly necessary by means of the most appropriate data and assumptions available.
Full data from suppliers for both energy and gas suppliers were available for company leased property, with no
estimates required to be applied. For leases for which the company do not procure energy, data was not
ayailable. The average Co2e and kWh's per square meter for the leased'offices are applied to the subleased
square meterage to provide an estimated total energy and GHG footprint.
For business travel, the company analyses mileage information to establish the level of scope 1 emissions.
undertaken in staff's own cars have been calculated based on mileage expense claim, applying the average UK
split between petrol and diesel vehicles to estimate relative fuel emissions.
In line with SECR guidance as a non-listed entity, the company have not inciuded air and train business travel :
within its footprint. Refrigerant losses on company air conditioning units are not considered to be material and
as such these are not reported in the emissions data.

Property, plant and equipment
Property, plant and equipment are stated at historic purchase cost less accumulated depreciation.
Cost includes the original purchase price of the asset and the costs attributable to bringing the asset to its
working condition for its intended use.
Depreciation is provided oni all property, plant and equipment at rates calculated to write off the cost, less
estimated residual value, of each asset (other than assets under construction) evenly over its expected useful
life, as follows:
Short leaseholdbuildings improvements  over the lease term
Plant, machinery and office equipment  over 2 to 10 years
Assets'in the course of construction represent the cost of purchasing, constructing and installing property, plant
and equipment ahead of their productive use. No depreciation is charged until the asset enters productive use
and is re-classified into one'of the categories above.
In addition, impairment reviews are caried out annually, which may result in the writing down of assets to
reflect their net book value.
Taxation including deferred tax
The tax expense represents the sum of the current tax and deferred tax:
The current tax is based on taxable profits for the year. Taxable profit differs from net profit as reported in the
income statement because it excludes items of income or expense that are taxable or deductible in other years
and it further excludes items that are never taxable or deductible. The company's liability for current tax'is
calculated using tax rates that have been in force during the year.
Deferred tax is the tax expected to be payable or recoverable' on differences between the carrying amounts of
assets and liabilities in the financial statements and the coresponding tax bases used in the computation of
. taxable profit, and is accounted for using the balance'sheet liability method. Deferred tax liabilities are..
generally recognised for all taxable temporary differences and deferred tax assets are recognised to the extent
that it is probable that taxable profits will be available against which deductible temporary differences can be
utilised. Such assets and liabilities are not recognised if the temporary difference arises from goodwill or from
the initial recognition (other than in a business combination) of other assets and liabilities in a transaction that
affects neither the tax profit nor the accounting profit.
Deferred tax liabilities are recognised for taxable temporary differences arising on investments in subsidiaries,
except where the company is able to control the reversal of the temporary difference and it is probable that the
temporary difference will not reverse in the foreseeable future.
The carrying amount of deferred tax assets is reviewed at each balance sheet date and reduced to the extent that
it is no longer probable that sufficient taxable profits will be available against which the temporary differences
can be utilised: Deferred tax is calculated at the average tax rates that are expected to apply in the period when
the liability is settled or the asset is realised.-
