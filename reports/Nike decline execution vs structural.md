# Execution Errors Broke Nike; Structure Caps Repair

Nike's decline is **primarily a self-inflicted execution failure**, and Elliott Hill's returning insider team can fix it. The combined evidence also shows that it ran into **structural shifts that cap both how far and how fast Nike can recover**. The decisive test is a control group. Over the same quarters and under the same tariffs, Adidas under Bjørn Gulden grew about 14% currency-neutral, including +17% in North America and +15% in Greater China (Q2 2026), while On, Hoka, New Balance and Asics grew roughly 13 to 22%. Nike's revenue problem is therefore Nike-specific. Every cause that partners, former executives, analysts and Chinese media name is a Nike decision: the DTC pivot and wholesale exit, oversupply of Dunk and Jordan retro, dismantled category teams, and discounting. Where Nike has pulled the reverse levers (running specialty, Vomero and Pegasus, reopened accounts), the numbers have turned: Nike's run-specialty sales rose 35% while On and Hoka fell. The structural side is just as real, though. The sport-lifestyle category that holds Nike's largest franchises is shrinking. The market has fragmented for good, with challengers taking about 3 points of share from Nike and Adidas over 2019 to 2024. China is in a deflationary price war, and guochao preference puts a ceiling on foreign share. The case team should argue **"fixable, but smaller and slower"**: Hill can restore growth, probably no earlier than FY28, but not the pre-2021 dominance, and China is the slowest repair of all.

*Source conventions used throughout.* Each citation gives the outlet and date. **(opened)** means a researcher fetched and read the page. **(snippet only)** means the claim comes from a search-result summary and was not verified on the page; treat these as leads to confirm before they appear on a slide. **(abstract only)** marks academic work where only the abstract or index record was read. Chinese-language sources keep their original titles, followed by an English translation.

## Execution side: peers grew under identical conditions, so Nike's revenue loss is its own

### The Adidas control test is the single strongest piece of evidence

Adidas under Gulden (CEO since January 2023) went from its **first loss in about three decades in 2023 (net −€14m, operating margin 1.3%) to record sales of €23.68bn in 2024**, with the operating margin back at 5.6% ([Börsen-Zeitung, "Adidas sprints ahead," 11 Feb 2025, opened](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/english/adidas-ahead)). Full-year 2025 revenue reached about €24.8bn (+13% currency-neutral) with €2.056bn of operating profit, an 8.3% margin ([Retail Sector UK, Jan 2026, snippet only](https://www.retailsector.co.uk/688420-adidas-posts-record-e24-8bn-revenue-eyes-e2-3bn-profit-in-2026/)). In Q2 2026 Adidas posted record quarterly revenue of €6.743bn, **+14% currency-neutral, with North America +17% and Greater China +15%**, and raised full-year growth guidance to 9 to 10% ([adidas EQS ad-hoc release via Deutsche Börse, 30 Jul 2026, opened](https://live.deutsche-boerse.com/news/EQS-Adhoc-adidas-grows-top-line-14-and-achieves-record-sales-in-Q2-increases-full-year-top-line-guidance-32ebe0ea-82c7-4bfa-8076-f52718607d4b)). Adidas is taking share in Nike's home market and in Nike's worst region at the same time, so neither US demand nor Chinese conditions can fully explain Nike's decline.

The mechanism matters as much as the outcome. Intersport, Europe's largest sporting-goods retail group, says Gulden rebuilt retailer trust through weekly exchanges and now ranks **Adidas as its No. 1 supplier, ahead of Nike**, because Nike "left retailers short of goods" while it prioritized DTC ([Börsen-Zeitung, 11 Feb 2025, opened](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/english/adidas-ahead)). Adidas also showed a guidance playbook Nike can copy: in February 2024 it guided to about €500m operating profit against a €1.29bn consensus, then beat repeatedly ([BusinessDay, 1 Feb 2024, snippet only](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/2024-02-01-adidas-shares-fall-after-disappointing-2024-forecast/)). Gulden is also, like Hill, a returning insider: he worked at Adidas in the 1990s and later ran Puma. German press headlines frame the contrast directly, including Börsen-Zeitung's "Der Fehler von Nike" ("Nike's mistake") and "Die Aufholjagd von Adidas" ("Adidas's catch-up race") ([Börsen-Zeitung, snippet only](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/meinung-analyse/nike-adidas-handel); [Börsen-Zeitung, snippet only](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/kapitalmaerkte/die-aufholjagd-von-adidas)).

### Tariffs explain Nike's margin squeeze, not its revenue decline

Tariffs hit the whole industry. Nike raised its gross annualized tariff-cost estimate to **$1.5bn** in September 2025 ([Modern Retail, 30 Sep 2025, snippet only](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/nike-grappling-with-further-fallout-from-tariffs-now-expects-1-5b-in-annualized-costs/)), and its nine-month FY26 gross margin fell 250bp to 41.0% ([Nike 10-Q, FY26 Q3, SEC, snippet only](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000037/nke-20260228.htm)). Under Armour lost 240bp of gross margin, "mainly due to higher tariffs" ([Under Armour FY2026 Q4 8-K, 12 May 2026, opened](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001336917/000133691726000059/exhibit991-fiscal2026q4.htm)). Yet On's gross margin *rose* to **65.4%** despite higher US tariffs ([On Holding Q2 2026 6-K, 11 Aug 2026, opened](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001858985/000185898526000018/a26q2-ex993xpressrelease.htm)), and Adidas, New Balance and Asics all grew double digits under the same regime. The lesson is that tariffs hurt most where pricing power is weak. Nike's weak pricing power is itself a brand-heat problem that execution created.

### Nike's own history and its critics both point to reversible decisions

Former Nike marketing executive Massimo Giunco traces the decline to three 2020 decisions under John Donahoe: **dismantling category teams, the DTC shift that ceded shelf space to On, Hoka, New Balance and Asics, and replacing brand-building with data-driven "sales activation"** that led to discounting ([FashionUnited, ~2 Aug 2024, opened](https://fashionunited.com/executive/report/just-cant-do-it-nikes-ongoing-strategy-struggles/2024080261226)). Matt Powell (BCE Consulting) names the same errors and adds over-distribution of limited editions and R&D spending diverted from product to digital ([BBC via Yahoo Finance, 2 Oct 2026, opened](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/whats-gone-wrong-nike-worlds-205114433.html)). Nike has recovered from earlier slumps the same way: after Reebok overtook it in the mid-1980s it answered with Air Jordan (1985) and visible Air (1987), and after the FY1998 profit collapse of about 40% it restructured and recovered ([Nike timeline, Wikipedia, snippet only](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_timeline); [TextilWirtschaft, "Nike: Gewinneinbruch im Fiskaljahr 1998" ("Nike: profit collapse in fiscal year 1998"), snippet only](https://www.textilwirtschaft.de/fashion-management/nike-gewinneinbruch-im-fiskaljahr-1998-27828)). These historical sources are snippet-level only and should support the narrative, not carry numbers.

## Execution side: channel and supply choices explain the lost shelf and the lost premium

### Academic evidence shows retailers punish brands that go direct

The best causal evidence comes from outside Nike. Van Crombrugge, Breugelmans, Gryseels and Cleeren (*Journal of Marketing*, 2025, 89(2): 141 to 162) studied nearly 2,000 retailers after a supplier launched its own webshop. On average, **retailers cut orders by 19% and wholesale prices rose 21%**, and smaller independents cut back the most ([Erasmus University news summary, opened](https://www.eur.nl/en/news/when-brands-sell-direct-how-do-retail-partners-respond); [AMA press release, opened](https://www.ama.org/press-releases/press-release-from-the-journal-of-marketing-how-retailers-change-ordering-strategy-when-a-supplier-starts-its-own-direct-channel/); [author open-access PDF](https://pure.eur.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/167363444/van-crombrugge-et-al-2024-express-how-retailers-change-ordering-strategies-when-suppliers-go-direct.pdf)). The figures come from the summaries; the full text was not read. The study is about toys, not sneakers, but it shows the mechanism behind Nike's wholesale losses: a channel choice triggers a predictable retailer "exit" response. Because a supplier decision caused it, a supplier decision can reverse it. The important caveat is that **no study yet measures how fast retailers return after a brand pulls back from DTC**, so the speed of Nike's wholesale recovery is an open empirical question. Singapore Management University's case "Nike's Consumer Direct Offense Strategy: A Hit or a Miss?" (Osinga, Chandukala and Mittal, 2025) documents the same arc of early success in FY21 to FY22, then slowing demand and an inventory build-up ([SMU InK, opened index](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cases_coll_all/520)).

### Retailers confirm the DTC pivot caused the damage

Dick's Executive Chairman Ed Stack said in November 2025 that Foot Locker "did not react quickly enough as its largest brand pivoted toward a direct-to-consumer model," and that "every brand has a renewed interest in being supportive of Foot Locker" ([SGB Media, 25 Nov 2025, opened](https://sgbonline.com/exec-dicks-sg-eyes-inventory-reset-store-closings-in-foot-locker-transformation/)). The quote does not name Nike, but the context makes the reference clear. Partners where Nike reopened accounts are seeing results. Academy Sports launched Jordan in about 145 stores in April 2025, called Nike "a growth engine for us," and planned about 200 Jordan shops ([SGB Media, 2025, snippet only](https://sgbonline.com/exec-academys-chief-merchant-talks-jordan-launch-nike-success-tariff-disruption/)).

### The resale premium collapsed when Nike flooded supply

Resale prices give the cleanest read on brand heat, and their timing points to oversupply. StockX data show the **average premium on new Air Jordan 1 Retro High releases fell from 61% in 2020 to 4% in 2023**, with some 2023 colorways selling below retail ([Reuters via Cyprus Mail, 28 Sep 2023, opened](https://cyprus-mail.com/2023/09/28/nike-air-jordans-are-losing-resale-value-is-sneaker-culture-moving-on)). A SneakerPing study of 3,538 releases from August 2024 to August 2026 found that **70.7% of 605 Jordan releases last sold below retail, at a median 20% under**, and tied this to Nike releasing more than 700 Jordan styles in each of 2022 and 2023 (this figure is not in SneakerPing's own report and could not be traced to a primary source, so it is unverified; Sole Retriever archive counts show about 100 core adult retro releases a year in 2022 to 2023, see `research_notes/jordan_extracts/rabbit_hole/release_counts.md`; [BallerAlert, 13 Sep 2026, opened; SneakerPing methodology not verified](https://balleralert.com/sneaker-resale-culture-market-decline/)). Consumer-behavior research explains why oversupply damages more than price. Berger and Heath (*Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2008) show that people abandon a taste once groups they do not identify with adopt it ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18729697/); [author PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Divergence.pdf)). Amaldoss and Jain (*Management Science*, 2005) show that exclusivity value depends on followers existing *without* owning the same item ([INFORMS, abstract only](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0399)). Together with the Caoui et al. NBER working paper already in the team's files, this supports a two-step execution story: oversupply first erased the scarcity premium, then mass adoption pushed core adopters away. Supply discipline is the matching fix, and Hill has said Nike was "oversupplying" Jordan retro and will cut volume ([BBC via Yahoo Finance, 2 Oct 2026, opened](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/whats-gone-wrong-nike-worlds-205114433.html)). Early signs are positive: StockX's January 2026 index showed **Nike average sneaker prices up 5% and Jordan up 6%**, described as "early signs of recovery" ([SGB Media, 12 Jan 2026, opened](https://sgbonline.com/stockx-2025-big-facts/)).

## Execution side: running proves the playbook works, at a scale too small to carry Nike

Running is where Hill's levers have been pulled longest, and the data are clear. In the 12 months to September 2025, **Nike's run-specialty dollar sales rose 35.4% while On fell 19.7% and Hoka fell 7.4%** (Karnan Associates data presented with Circana; [SGB Media, 3 Dec 2025, opened](https://sgbonline.com/the-tre-sessions-run-specialty-growth-trailing-broader-market/)). Circana data show US running shoe dollars and units each up about 13% in H1 2026, a category tailwind for Nike's Performance segment ([WWD/FN, 2026, snippet only](https://wwd.com/footwear-news/shoe-industry-news/consumer-spending-trends-running-shoes-1239125374/)). Partners agree on the product. JD Sports CFO Dominic Platt said Nike is "doing absolutely the right things" and that Vomero and Pegasus are "resonating well with customers" ([SGB Media, 23 Sep 2026, opened](https://sgbonline.com/exec-jd-sports-not-waiting-for-nike-recovery/)). Goldman Sachs, after Q1 FY27, cited "healthy Performance growth" ([Stocktwits/The Fly, 2 Oct 2026, opened](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/nke-stock-cracks-premarket-analysts-slash-targets-on-weak-outlook-citi-calls-nike-a-cost-cutting-story/cZDjOoORBKe)). Meanwhile the challengers are slowing in Nike's core channel: On cut its FY2026 guide to low-20s constant-currency growth after a Q2 miss, with Americas growth of 13.0% constant currency and only 4.5% reported ([On Holding Q2 2026 6-K, 11 Aug 2026, opened](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001858985/000185898526000018/a26q2-ex993xpressrelease.htm)), and Hoka slowed from about 24% to 16% growth in FY2026 with low-double-digit growth guided for FY2027 ([FashionUnited, 22 May 2026, snippet only](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/hoka-and-ugg-drive-deckers-brands-to-5-47-billion-dollars-in-fy26-net-sales/2026052288200)).

The consumer side shows a matching stabilization. Piper Sandler's Fall 2025 "Taking Stock With Teens" survey found Nike's footwear mindshare erosion "stabilizing sequentially" at **46%, still more than three times No. 2 Adidas at 14%**, with male footwear share growing for the first time since Fall 2022 ([SGB Media, 9 Oct 2025, opened](https://sgbonline.com/piper-sandler-teen-survey-finds-on-overtaking-hoka-in-popularity-nikes-erosion-stabilizing/); [Piper Sandler teen survey page, opened](https://www.pipersandler.com/teens)). YouGov's BrandIndex still ranks Nike No. 1 in US fashion purchase consideration ([YouGov, 20 Mar 2026, opened](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/54374-nike-leads-us-fashion-brand-consideration-as-skechers-gains-ground)).

The limit is scale. Fleet Feet's CEO Joey Pointer said in late December 2025 that Nike's product is "just good" and that **Fleet Feet still sold more sandals than total Nike shoes**; he expected Nike in about half of Fleet Feet doors by end-2026 ([Footwear News via Yahoo, 5 Jan 2026, opened](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/fleet-feet-ceo-sounds-off-150000978.html)). Running is a much smaller share of Nike revenue than lifestyle, so running wins alone cannot offset the lifestyle clean-out within FY27. This is the hinge of the whole debate: the execution fix is visibly working, but in the part of the business that is too small to move the total.

## Structural side: lifestyle deflation, fragmentation and a lower ceiling

### The category holding Nike's biggest franchises is shrinking

Circana reports that **sport-lifestyle footwear declined in H1 2026** even as running grew, and expects the US footwear industry to finish 2026 "generally flat" ([WWD/FN, 2026, snippet only](https://wwd.com/footwear-news/shoe-industry-news/consumer-spending-trends-running-shoes-1239125374/)). Retailers tied to lifestyle product confirm it. Dick's Q2 FY26 release (25 Aug 2026) reported a **Foot Locker comp of −3.6% against +4.9% for the Dick's banner**, said Foot Locker "has greater exposure to legacy footwear silhouettes," and cut Foot Locker's FY26 comp guide to −2% to 0% ([DKS 8-K Ex. 99.1, SEC, 25 Aug 2026, opened](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001089063/000108906326000033/dks-2026801xex991earningsr.htm)). On the call, Stack described a "hangover" of excess legacy lifestyle sneakers and said "several suppliers… have got some inventory that have been promoted on their sites" ([MediaPost, 26 Aug 2026, opened](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/417489/dicks-gets-clobbered-with-nike-fallout.html?edition=143678)). Dick's shares fell about 30% that day ([CNBC, 25 Aug 2026, snippet only](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/25/dicks-sporting-goods-dks-earnings-q2-2026.html)). Stack's tone shifted from November 2025 ("we don't see that promotional activity impacting our business a whole lot") to August 2026 ("increasingly promotional"), which shows the lifestyle clean-out running longer than partners expected. JD's Hibbett and City Specialty like-for-like sales fell 8.4% in Q2 ([SGB Media, 23 Sep 2026, opened](https://sgbonline.com/exec-jd-sports-not-waiting-for-nike-recovery/)). Adidas is no exception: its Q2 2026 footwear grew only **+1% and lifestyle only +2%** ([adidas EQS release, 30 Jul 2026, opened](https://live.deutsche-boerse.com/news/EQS-Adhoc-adidas-grows-top-line-14-and-achieves-record-sales-in-Q2-increases-full-year-top-line-guidance-32ebe0ea-82c7-4bfa-8076-f52718607d4b)), which signals that the terrace-shoe cycle that lifted Adidas is fading too. Lifestyle demand is cyclical, and Nike's portfolio sits on the wrong side of the cycle.

### Taste and share have fragmented, and some of the loss is permanent

Teen data show losses concentrated among the trend-setting cohorts. Between Fall 2023 and Fall 2024, Nike's footwear mindshare fell from 61% to 57%, **female teens from 56% to 48%, and upper-income female teens from 56% to 45%**, while New Balance and Adidas gained ([SGB Media, 9 Oct 2024, opened](https://sgbonline.com/exec-piper-sandlers-fall-survey-finds-continued-shifts-in-brands-winning-with-teens/)). By Fall 2025 the figure was 46%, roughly 15 points lower in two years. A circulating Spring 2025 figure of 49% conflicts with this series and is snippet-only, so it should not be used. GlobalData estimates **Nike's global sportswear share at 12.7% in 2025, down 1.5 points** ([FashionUnited, ~9 Dec 2025, opened](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/sportswear-emerging-markets-are-driving-the-next-growth-phase/2025120969546)). McKinsey and WFSGI estimate challengers took about **3 percentage points of share from Nike and Adidas combined over 2019 to 2024**, helped by lower barriers to entry, and expect market growth to slow to about 6% a year through 2029 ([McKinsey, *Sporting Goods 2025*, snippet only](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/retail/our-insights/sporting-goods-industry-trends)). The winners keep winning: New Balance reached about **$9.2bn in 2025 sales (+19%)**, its fifth straight year of double-digit growth ([FashionUnited, 19 Feb 2026, snippet only](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/new-balance-achieves-9-2-billion-dollars-in-annual-sales/2026021986388)), and Asics posted FY2025 sales of ¥810.9bn (+19.5%) at a record 17.6% operating margin ([Alchempro, Feb 2026, snippet only](https://www.alchempro.com/news/apparel-news/japan-s-asics-posts-record-profit-as-fy25-operating-margin-hits-17-6--308433-newsdetails.htm)). On StockX, the **Asics Gel-1130 was 2025's best-selling sneaker** and Asics sales rose 45% ([StockX "Big Facts," 12 Nov 2025, opened](https://stockx.com/about/stockx-rolls-out-latest-big-facts-report-revealing-top-resale-trends-in-2025/)).

Retailers have made that fragmentation part of their operating model. JD Sports CEO Régis Schultz said "We are not waiting for anything," that JD's share with "some of the new brands" in key regions now exceeds its share with Nike, and called athletic footwear a "maturing market" ([SGB Media, 23 Sep 2026, opened](https://sgbonline.com/exec-jd-sports-not-waiting-for-nike-recovery/)). Non-English press reports the same shift abroad: Korean press reports that Nike's preference among US teens fell by double digits as Hoka, On and ASICS closed the gap ([Daum, Korean press, snippet only](https://v.daum.net/v/ZkMC3f5TrT?f=p)), and President Online reports On, HOKA and ASICS gaining in Japanese street counts ([President Online, Japanese, snippet only](https://president.jp/articles/-/113157)). The academic record here is thin: **no peer-reviewed study of switching to Hoka or On was found**. Structural claims about permanent comfort-driven switching therefore rest on industry data, not academic evidence.

### Brand stature and the sell-side mood have moved against Nike

Several signals suggest Nike's scale is now partly a disadvantage. Kantar BrandZ 2026 reported that **Zara overtook Nike as the world's most valuable apparel brand** ([Kantar, 13 Jul 2026, opened](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/brands/most-valuable-global-brands-2026)). Kylian Mbappé left Nike for On in September 2026; academic Tim Derdenger argues athletes now want to join "change and growth," and the BBC notes Nike's removal from the S&P 100 ([BBC via Yahoo Finance, 2 Oct 2026, opened](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/whats-gone-wrong-nike-worlds-205114433.html)). YouGov shows **Skechers at 35.5 in purchase consideration, almost level with Nike's 36.3** ([YouGov, 20 Mar 2026, opened](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/54374-nike-leads-us-fashion-brand-consideration-as-skechers-gains-ground)), and consumers reportedly do not see Nike as good value for money ([Retail Dive, 11 Mar 2026, snippet only](https://retaildive.com/press-release/20260311-nike-leads-us-fashion-brand-rankings-as-athletic-and-heritage-brands-domi-1/)).

The sell-side has moved from doubting the plan to doubting the revenue base. After Q1 FY27 (1 Oct 2026; revenue about $11.2bn, −4%), **at least 14 firms cut price targets, mostly into the $19 to $36 range, with almost no downgrades**. Citi said the quarter was "turning into a cost-cutting story"; Goldman cited "weakness in Sportswear, Jordan, and China outweighing healthy Performance growth" and questioned whether FY27 is the trough; BofA cut FY27 and FY28 EPS by 21% and 22%; BNP Paribas cut to the Street-low $19 ([Stocktwits/The Fly, 2 Oct 2026, opened](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/nke-stock-cracks-premarket-analysts-slash-targets-on-weak-outlook-citi-calls-nike-a-cost-cutting-story/cZDjOoORBKe)). Nike guided FY27 revenue to a high-single-digit decline ([investinglive, Oct 2026, snippet only](https://investinglive.com/stocks/nike-beats-q1-profit-estimates-but-sees-fiscal-2027-revenue-falling-high-single-digits/)). Williams Trading's Sam Poser, a Buy since 2024, downgraded to Hold on 2 October 2026 ([CNBC, 2 Oct 2026, snippet only; rationale not retrieved](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/10/02/heres-why-williams-trading-analyst-sam-poser-downgraded-nike-to-hold.html)). GlobalData's Neil Saunders said "Nike's problems are more deep-seated than previously acknowledged," and BNP's Laurent Vasilescu called Nike's dropped gender disclosure "a red flag" ([Fortune, 4 Jul 2026, opened](https://fortune.com/2026/07/04/nike-earnings-elliott-hill-nascent-comeback/)). Hill himself reportedly told the FT that he "underestimated" the work and time needed ([Benzinga, Jun 2026, snippet only](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/26/06/60007561/nike-ceo-elliott-hill-admits-turnaround-is-taking-longer-than-expected-we-still-have-work-to-do)). Bulls remain: Jefferies (Buy, $75) sees North America and wholesale stabilizing and argues that new CFO David Denton "owns none of the prior guidance" and can reset targets at the 16 to 17 November Investor Day ([Parameter, 28 Sep 2026, opened](https://parameter.io/nike-nke-stock-could-double-jefferies-maintains-bullish-stance-before-thursdays-earnings/)), and Bernstein calls the stock "de-risked" ([Invezz, 2 Oct 2026, snippet only](https://invezz.com/en-ae/news/2026/10/02/bernstein-says-nike-stock-now-de-risked-buy-on-post-earnings-dip/)). The post-earnings target range of $19 to $75 shows unusually wide disagreement about where the trough is.

## China: Nike's clearest execution failure, inside a deflating market

### The decline is deep, accelerating and Nike expects it to last through FY27

Greater China revenue fell for a **ninth straight quarter in Q1 FY27 (June to August 2026): US$1.18bn, −22% reported and −26% currency-neutral**, the worst of the run, against FY2021 revenue of $8.29B (+24%) when Donahoe said "Nike属于中国，为中国而生" ("Nike belongs to China, and was born for China"). The source is **《耐克失意中国市场：大中华区业绩"九连跌"，年内股价跌近50%，新品价格频破发》** ("Nike stumbles in China: Greater China down nine quarters in a row, stock down nearly 50% this year, new releases often sell below retail"), 21世纪经济报道 *21st Century Business Herald*, republished from 蓝鲸财经 Lanjing Finance ([21jingji, 5 Oct 2026, opened](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20261005/herald/e02e3ac16950b0c447cca67ae3f96e78.html)). Nike's FY2026 10-K cites "a trend of declining store traffic, elevated promotional activity and higher levels of inventory across the marketplace" and says **"We expect negative impacts from Greater China and Converse to continue throughout fiscal 2027"** ([Nike FY26 10-K, SEC, opened; segment table not reached](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm)). Traffic is a demand problem; promotions and inventory are Nike's own levers. The decline accelerated *after* Nike cut Greater China inventory units by more than 20% (snippet-level via a 21jingji summary, **《耐克在华降低折扣：CFO称盈利能力将更先触底》** ("Nike cuts discounting in China: CFO says profitability will bottom out first"), [21jingji, 2 Jul 2026, snippet only](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260702/herald/389311293936f26ca7c4ec4a795db190_zaker.html)). That is the strongest single fact against a "temporary inventory reset" story.

### Chinese media and management name self-inflicted causes

Management admitted that frequent discounting "严重削弱品牌在市场中的影响力" ("severely weakens the brand's influence in the market"), as reported in **《销售额连降七个季度，中国消费者抛弃耐克的速度，比想象中更快》** ("Sales down seven quarters in a row: Chinese consumers are abandoning Nike faster than expected"), 新浪财经 Sina Finance, originally 橙柿互动·都市快报 ([Sina Finance, 5 Jun 2026, opened](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-06-05/doc-iniaikax1918825.shtml)). The same piece documents a shoe listed at ¥899 selling for ¥429, the end of Nike's "保值" (value-retention) promise, and stores using one global template with slogans like "热爱跑步" ("Love running") that leave shoppers unable to match products to needs. It also records the departure of long-time Greater China head 董炜 (Dong Wei) in January 2026 and the arrival of Cathy Sparks. Dewu (得物) resale data show brand heat collapsing on new product, not just old stock: the **Air Jordan 11 Retro "University Blue" (retail ¥1,399) averaged ¥764.73, and the Air Jordan 4 Retro "Comic" (retail ¥1,599) averaged ¥848.49**, about 53 to 55% of retail ([21jingji, 5 Oct 2026, opened](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20261005/herald/e02e3ac16950b0c447cca67ae3f96e78.html)). **《【BT财报瞬析】连续九个季度暴跌！耐克大中华区的"失去"》** ("[BT Earnings Flash] Nine straight quarters of decline! What Nike lost in Greater China"), BT财经 via 证券之星 Stockstar, blames Nike's closure of lower-tier distributor stores for ceding ground to Anta and Li-Ning ([Stockstar, 5 Oct 2026, opened](https://wap.stockstar.com/detail/IG2026100500000064)). Its claim that Dunk revenue fell "nearly 50%" has no stated source and should not be used.

### The control group again says the market is winnable

Adidas Greater China grew **+17% in Q1 2026, its 12th consecutive growth quarter** ([Jing Daily, 30 Apr 2026, opened](https://jingdaily.com/intels/2026-04/30/adidas-posts-14-revenue-growth-in-q1-2026)), and +15% in Q2 ([adidas EQS release, 30 Jul 2026, opened](https://live.deutsche-boerse.com/news/EQS-Adhoc-adidas-grows-top-line-14-and-achieves-record-sales-in-Q2-increases-full-year-top-line-guidance-32ebe0ea-82c7-4bfa-8076-f52718607d4b)). lululemon's mainland China business grew 29% in FY2025, and Anta's premium sub-brands Descente and Kolon grew close to 60%, according to **《从同质内卷到差异突围，中国运动品牌进入分化时代》** ("From homogeneous involution to differentiated breakthroughs: China's sportswear brands enter an era of divergence"), 第一财经 Yicai, 刘晓颖 Liu Xiaoying ([Yicai, 27 Mar 2026, opened](https://www.yicai.com/news/103107768.html)). Adidas was the brand hit hardest by the 2021 Xinjiang cotton boycott, when its Tmall sales reportedly fell 78% against Nike's 59% ([SCMP, 2021, snippet only](https://m.scmp.com/business/companies/article/3127107/anta-li-ning-shares-surge-chinese-consumers-back-domestic-brands)). Its recovery, and Nike's own record FY21 in the boycott year, both argue that nationalism alone does not cause −20% quarters. A Radboud University thesis (2022) likewise found the boycott effect among sneaker collectors was temporary, though the sample was small ([Radboud theses, opened index](https://theses.ubn.ru.nl/items/c43e3528-80e9-4e70-a292-68fa1aac9e08)).

### But the floor has dropped for everyone, and guochao sets a ceiling

The domestic mass brands are stalling too, which shows a real macro headwind. In 2025 the **Anta main brand grew only +3.7%, Li-Ning +3.2% and Xtep (特步) +4.2%**, with industry discounts of about 30% off list offline (7折) and about 50% off online (5折); consultant 程伟雄 (Cheng Weixiong) said "2025年整个行业的竞争白热化，终端折扣居高不下" ("Competition across the industry turned white-hot in 2025, and retail discounts stayed high") ([Yicai, 27 Mar 2026, opened](https://www.yicai.com/news/103107768.html)). In Q2 2026 Li-Ning and Xtep retail sales turned negative, and national statistics showed sports and entertainment goods retail **−8% in May 2026** ([All Weather Finance, 17 Jul 2026, opened](https://allweatherfinance.com/domestic-sports-brands-slowed-down-collectively-in-the-second-quarter-with-361-degrees-temporarily-in-the-lead/)). Chinese brands have caught up on performance technology: on Dewu in 2024 they held 7 of the top 10 performance running spots, and Li-Ning's 䨻 (Gamma Ray Burst) shoe ranked No. 1 for practical basketball shoes, although Nike, Jordan and Adidas still led overall popularity, per **《国产品牌争夺运动鞋王》** ("Domestic brands compete for the sneaker crown"), 界面新闻 Jiemian, 覃思悦 Qin Siyue ([Jiemian, 22 Jan 2025, opened](https://www.jiemian.com/article/12280239.html)). Anta Group's revenue reportedly passed Nike China's in 2022 (RMB 53.65bn vs RMB 51.42bn, citing McKinsey) ([winshang, snippet only](https://m.winshang.com/news702268.html)), and Euromonitor data put Anta's China share at about 23% in 2024 ([Primicias, snippet only](https://www.primicias.ec/economia/sacudon-mercado-mundial-ropa-deportiva-titan-chino-anta-controlara-puma-acecha-nike-adidas-116554/)). A brand-level share series for Nike circulates in Chinese media but its original article could not be located, so it is excluded here.

Chinese academic work explains both the ceiling and the opening. **《国货消费意向与行为》** ("Domestic-brand consumption intention and behavior"), 《上海经济》 *Shanghai Economy* (2024), finds that identity, value and belonging shape purchase *intention*, while perceived usefulness and functional value for price drive *actual purchase* ([NAPSTIC record, abstract only](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/010shjj202404006)). **《传统文化广告与国潮品牌购买意愿》** ("Traditional-culture advertising and purchase intention for guochao brands"), 《毛纺科技》 *Wool Textile Journal* (2024, 52(7): 69 to 76, n = 241), finds emotional and cognitive value mediate the effect of traditional-culture ads ([NAPSTIC, abstract only](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/010mfkj202407010)), and **国潮服装品牌属性研究** ("Guochao apparel brand attributes and purchase intention"), 《丝绸》 *Journal of Silk* (2026), finds sociocultural and distinctiveness attributes drive intention through dual identification ([NAPSTIC, abstract only](https://opaj.napstic.cn/periodicalArticle/0120260700364984)). The intention-versus-behavior split is the most useful non-English finding. Guochao raises consideration of Anta and Li-Ning, but purchase still turns on product and value, which Nike can contest. Surveys point the same way: 89% of Chinese consumers said they prefer domestic fashion brands ([RetailBoss reporting a Statista survey, Mar 2024, snippet only](https://retailboss.co/89-percent-of-chinese-shoppers-preferred-homegrown-brands-over-foreign-brands-fashion-study)), yet Nike was still the most-purchased sportswear brand in China in August 2025, with 64% of respondents reporting a purchase ([Rakuten Insight via Statista, snippet only](https://www.statista.com/statistics/372774/chinese-penetration-rate-of-sportswear-brands/)). Sina's reporting adds that Chinese consumers have moved from "品牌崇拜" (brand worship) to "自我表达" (self-expression), across running, trail, hiking and gym niches. That shift is structural, but answering it is an assortment decision Nike controls.

### Nike's China fix is the right lever with a long fuse

Every China lever Nike is pulling is an execution lever: inventory cleanup, fewer promotions, a new GM, local design, and the end of online sales through distributors. Nike told Topsports (滔搏), its largest China distributor and a partner of more than 27 years, that its online arrangement in mainland China ends on **1 January 2027**. Online Nike sales were about 22% of Topsports revenue, and its shares fell as much as 26.2% to a record low ([Investing.com/Reuters, 21 to 22 Jul 2026, opened](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/chinas-topsports-hits-record-low-after-nike-ends-online-sales-partnership-4804632)). Pou Sheng (宝胜) received the same notice ([21jingji, 5 Oct 2026, opened](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20261005/herald/e02e3ac16950b0c447cca67ae3f96e78.html)), and Nike is merging Asia Pacific and Greater China into one region. The payoff is dated late: China-designed, China-developed and China-made products launch for holiday 2027, which one Zhihu commentary summarized as **《耐克说要跟上中国速度，可解药要等到2027年底》** ("Nike says it will keep up with China speed, but the cure won't come until end-2027") ([Zhihu, snippet only, personal opinion](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2079128860873045050)). The channel cut will mechanically lower FY27 and FY28 revenue before any benefit shows. China is fixable in principle, but its timeline runs well past the case window, and a successful fix most likely restores a mid-teens share, not the roughly 20% of 2020. A US and China geopolitical flare-up remains a tail risk no management team controls.

## Leadership research favors a fitted insider, but not a fast fix

The academic record does not support assuming a returning leader will succeed, but it supports Hill more than a naive reading suggests. Hendricks, Bingham, Kolev and Howell analyzed 6,429 CEO tenures in S&P 1500 firms from 1992 to 2017 and found that **firms led by the 167 "boomerang" CEOs underperformed first-time CEOs by 10.1% a year in stock returns**. The effect was concentrated in returning founders, who made up 44% of boomerangs, and in dynamic industries ([MIT Sloan Management Review, 2020, opened](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/?p=79033); [UNC Kenan Institute abstract](https://kenaninstitute.unc.edu/?p=8803)). Hill, who was President of Consumer and Marketplace rather than CEO or founder, falls outside the study's definition. Chen and Hambrick (*Organization Science*, 2012) find that **"CEO replacement per se has no general effect"**; troubled firms improve only when they replace a poorly suited incumbent with a well-suited successor ([RePEc, abstract only](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ororsc/v23y2012i1p225-243.html)). Zhang and Rajagopalan (*Strategic Management Journal*, 2010) find that outsiders suit fast cost-cutting while insiders suit long-term competitive change, exactly the product and wholesale repair Nike needs ([RePEc, abstract only](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v31y2010i3p334-346.html); interpretation from [TWST interview](https://www.twst.com/?p=567966)). Zhu, Hu and Shen (*SMJ*, 2020) find outside experience raises an insider's willingness to change course ([RePEc, abstract only](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v41y2020i10p1933-1951.html)); Hill's four years away from Nike plausibly fits, though that application is an inference. Amore, Bennedsen, Le Breton-Miller and Miller (*SMJ*, 2021) found 18% higher profitability after Italian family firms returned to a family CEO, an imperfect analogy for a return to identity-carrying leadership ([SUFE academic newsletter, abstract only](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/376696)). Schmitt and Raisch (*Journal of Management Studies*, 2013, 107 turnarounds) show that successful turnarounds combine retrenchment and recovery and shift the balance toward recovery over time ([RePEc, abstract only](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jomstd/v50y2013i7p1216-1244.html)). Nike's mix of cost cuts with a product and wholesale rebuild matches that model.

The peer precedents set the timeline. Adidas needed about two years from trough to record revenue, and margin is still short of its 10% target more than three and a half years in (H1 2026 operating margin 9.6%) ([adidas EQS release, 30 Jul 2026, opened](https://live.deutsche-boerse.com/news/EQS-Adhoc-adidas-grows-top-line-14-and-achieves-record-sales-in-Q2-increases-full-year-top-line-guidance-32ebe0ea-82c7-4bfa-8076-f52718607d4b)). Under Armour is the cautionary case: two years after founder Kevin Plank's return, FY2026 revenue fell 4% to $5.0bn, North America fell 8%, and FY2027 is guided to "decline slightly" ([Under Armour 8-K, 12 May 2026, opened](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001336917/000133691726000059/exhibit991-fiscal2026q4.htm)). Puma, in its own deliberate clean-up, fell 9.4% organically in Q2 2026 and targets a return to growth only in 2027 ([TheIndustry.fashion, 31 Jul 2026, opened](https://www.theindustry.fashion/puma-q2-sales-decline-narrowing-losses-2026/)). The difference between Adidas and Under Armour is that Adidas had a product cycle and strong wholesale relationships to lean on. Hill took over in October 2024; on the Adidas path, revenue stabilization arrives around late FY27 to FY28, with margin recovery later.

## Conclusion

The evidence changes the question more than it answers the binary. "Execution versus structural" is the wrong frame for a judge, because the two operate on different variables. Execution explains **why Nike lost share**: every competitor facing the same tariffs, the same consumers and the same Chinese slump grew while Nike shrank, and every named cause traces back to a Nike decision. Structure explains **how much share Nike can win back and how quickly**: the lifestyle category is deflating, retailers have permanently diversified, challengers are entrenched, and China's price war and guochao ceiling lower the prize. The most defensible insight for a case team is that Nike's running recovery already proves the fix works, while Foot Locker's legacy-lifestyle collapse proves the fix cannot carry the whole revenue base on the current timeline. The sell-side's "cost-cutting story" label is therefore premature but not wrong for FY27.

Three near-term events will test the thesis, and the team should frame them as falsifiable checkpoints: the Piper Sandler Fall 2026 annual teen survey (not yet published as of 13 October 2026), the 16 to 17 November 2026 Investor Day where CFO David Denton can reset targets, and China sell-through after the 1 January 2027 channel change. If teen share keeps stabilizing and the Investor Day sets Adidas-style conservative targets, the execution case strengthens. If Greater China keeps accelerating downward after the channel reset, the structural ceiling is lower than this report assumes.

## Recommended thesis statement

"Nike's decline is a self-inflicted execution failure, not a verdict on the brand. The DTC overreach, wholesale exit, franchise oversupply and loss of product focus explain why Nike shrank while Adidas, On, Hoka, New Balance and Asics grew under identical conditions, and Elliott Hill's insider team is pulling the right reverse levers, as the running recovery already shows. But the decline collided with structural shifts that leadership cannot undo: a shrinking sport-lifestyle category, a permanently fragmented market and a deflationary, guochao-tilted China. Nike can return to growth, most likely from FY28, as a smaller-share leader rather than the dominant brand of 2021; the turnaround is real, but it is slower and its ceiling is lower than the market once assumed."

## Counterarguments the team must be ready to answer

**"If this is execution, why did China accelerate downward after Nike cleaned up inventory?"** This is the hardest question. The answer is that the inventory cleanup was step one; Nike's other China levers (channel control from January 2027, local product for holiday 2027, a new GM) have not yet taken effect, and the channel cut mechanically lowers revenue first. Concede that China is the part of the thesis with the longest timeline and the lowest recovery ceiling, and point to Adidas Greater China growing +15 to +17% in the same quarters as proof the market is winnable for a Western brand.

**"Returning leaders underperform by 10% a year, and Under Armour shows it."** The boomerang study's effect is concentrated in returning founders and former CEOs; Hill is neither. Chen and Hambrick show that fit, not replacement itself, drives turnaround success, and Zhang and Rajagopalan favor insiders for long-cycle product and brand repair. Under Armour lacked what Nike has: a working product franchise (Vomero, Pegasus) and the industry's largest wholesale relationships. Gulden, also a returning insider, is the closer analogue.

**"Adidas rode a fashion cycle it did not create, so its recovery proves luck, not execution."** Partly true: Adidas footwear grew only 1% in Q2 2026 as the terrace trend faded. But Adidas kept growing 14% overall through apparel, performance and every region, and Intersport credits the recovery to retailer relationships, not just Samba. The fading Adidas lifestyle cycle actually supports the thesis: lifestyle share swings are cyclical, so Nike's lifestyle losses are not necessarily permanent, but they are not something management can force either.

**"Two years in, the sell-side calls it a cost-cutting story and FY27 revenue is guided down high single digits."** The Adidas benchmark is two years to revenue recovery and more than three years to margin recovery, which puts Nike's inflection in FY28, not FY27. The thesis already prices in a slower recovery. The key concession is that FY27 is a reset year, and the team should not argue for a near-term revenue turn.

**"Running is too small to matter; the lifestyle franchises are structurally broken."** Concede the scale point explicitly; it is why the thesis says "smaller." The answer is not that running replaces Dunk and Jordan retro, but that supply discipline (fewer Jordan styles, early +5% and +6% resale price recovery) is the matching fix for lifestyle, and that the 2026 promotional "hangover" retailers describe is a one-time clearance of oversupplied stock.

**"Challengers are entrenched and still growing 16 to 22%; fragmentation is permanent."** Fragmentation is permanent, and the thesis accepts a lower ceiling. But the challengers are decelerating precisely in US wholesale, Nike's core channel: On cut its FY2026 guide and its Americas growth slowed to 4.5% reported, Hoka is guided to low double digits, and Nike's run-specialty sales grew while On and Hoka fell.

**"Nike's scale is now the problem: athletes like Mbappé and Yamal choose challengers, and Zara passed Nike in brand value."** These are real signs of lost cultural momentum, and the team should treat them as evidence for the "lower ceiling" half of the thesis rather than dismiss them. Nike remains No. 1 in teen footwear with more than three times the share of No. 2 and No. 1 in US purchase consideration, so the brand asset is diminished, not lost.

**"Your evidence base has gaps."** Be ready to say so. Several figures are snippet-only (Bernstein's call, the Poser downgrade, New Balance and Asics results, McKinsey's 3-point share shift, Anta's 2022 revenue crossover), most academic sources were read at abstract level, no peer-reviewed study of switching to Hoka or On exists, and no study measures retailer return after a DTC pullback. Before the presentation, the team should confirm snippet-only figures in primary filings (Adidas H1 report, Anta and Li-Ning HKEX interims, Nike's 10-K segment table) and check whether the Fall 2026 Piper Sandler survey has been released.
