# Nike Runs Gulden's Playbook on Plank's Clock

Nike's decline was caused mainly by **one strategic bet of its own**: the Consumer Direct strategy that insider CEO Mark Parker launched in June 2017 and John Donahoe escalated in June 2020. Its unwind explains about **40% to 50% of the $4,964M FY2024 to FY2026 revenue decline**. Greater China execution adds another **18% to 26%**. External forces matter but rank lower: a fashion rotation away from court and canvas silhouettes (10% to 20%), a lower post-2021 ceiling for foreign brands in China (6% to 16%) and macro factors (2% to 8%). That makes the decline **mostly fixable**, and the proof is that the fix already worked where it was applied first. North America has grown for five straight quarters, and running is taking share back from On and Hoka. Two years into Elliott Hill's tenure, though, the company sits **between the two precedents**. At its eighth quarter, Bjorn Gulden's adidas had already put together four straight quarters of currency-neutral growth (+8% to +19%) and its share price was 75% to 88% above his start. Kevin Plank's Under Armour has not posted a single growth quarter in nine. Nike looks like adidas in North America and Performance, and like Under Armour in Sportswear, Greater China, guidance slippage and the share price (down 57% since Hill's first day). The combined evidence points to **sustained company-level growth no earlier than the second half of FY2028**, double-digit operating margins no earlier than FY2029 to FY2030, and a return to FY2024 revenue no earlier than about FY2031 to FY2032. Recovery means getting back to the incumbent growth line, not to the 2021 dominance. The November 16 to 17, 2026 Investor Day and the Q2 FY27 print in late December are the first two tests that can confirm or refute this.

## One strategic bet explains roughly half the decline; the rest is mostly its echo

**Slide takeaway: Nike's decline is a self-inflicted strategy error, conceived by insiders in 2017 and scaled in 2020. External forces explain about a quarter to a third of the gross headwinds, and roughly none of the net decline once currency and a growing market are counted.**

On a reported basis revenue fell from **$51,362M in FY2024 to $46,398M in FY2026**, a $4,964M decline ([FY24 10-K p. 57](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018724000044/nke-20240531.htm); [FY26 10-K p. 55](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm)). Currency actually flattered the reported number: FX translation was a detriment of about $419M in FY25 and a benefit of about $1,023M in FY26 ([FY25 10-K p. 45](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018725000047/nke-20250531.htm); FY26 10-K p. 44). The currency-neutral decline is therefore closer to **$5.5B, about 10.8%** (repo root-cause synthesis, my arithmetic). Because of that, the shares below are expressed against the reported $4,964M and their central values add up to slightly more than 100%.

The root dates to **2017, not 2020**, and that changes the leadership story. Nike's own FY21 10-K calls Consumer Direct Acceleration "a new digitally empowered phase of the Consumer Direct Offense" (FY21 10-K p. 28, OPENED). CFO Andy Campion set out the own-channel economics in 2017: "nearly twice the revenue" per transaction ([Q4 FY17 call, 8-K Ex. 99.1, 5 Jul 2017, OPENED](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018717000059/q4fy17erccexhibit991.htm)). Nike's US share fell further from 2016 to 2019 (40.5% to 34.1%) than from 2019 to 2025 (to 28.9%) ([HGI citing Euromonitor, Jan 2026, OPENED](https://hgi.io/insights/have-athletic-footwear-industry-titans-lost-their-step/)). The 2020 escalation then built the dollars that later fell. Classics fed NIKE Digital through "exclusivity at scale", Jordan ran 700+ releases a year in 2022 and 2023, digital revenue made up a third of the company-wide bonus from FY21 to FY23 (2021 proxy p. 35, OPENED), and the sport category teams were dissolved. Management later sized the reversal itself. The classics franchises were "on track to decline from peak levels by more than $4 billion by fiscal year-end" (Matt Friend, Q2 FY26 call, Dec 2025, official transcript, OPENED per repo `franchise_oversupply.md`). That $4B is a gross, management-defined figure and should not be read as 80% of the net decline. In Hill's words on the Q1 FY27 call: "we've been oversupplying our iconic retro product" ([Nike Q1 FY27 official transcript, 1 Oct 2026, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)).

The decline sits **in the channel where the strategy was concentrated**. From FY24 to FY26, NIKE Direct fell by $3,799M while NIKE Brand wholesale fell by only $305M. North America wholesale reached a record $11,958M in FY26 (repo channel data from the FY24 and FY26 10-Ks; [FY26 10-K p. 32](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm)). The discounting, the shift in marketing spend, organizational churn, the digital bonus metric, the technology stack and the lag in women's and apparel are all real. Each one, though, is a **symptom whose dollars already sit inside cause 1**, and none passes its own discriminating test. Hoka priced higher than Nike and discounted least. Demand creation reached a record 11.2% of revenue in Q1 FY27 while revenue fell 4.3% ([Q1 FY27 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000193/nke-20260831.htm), my arithmetic). Greater China had the most stable leadership and the worst decline.

Several popular explanations fail on timing or on the peer test:

| Ruled out as a driver of the FY24 to FY26 revenue decline | Decisive evidence |
|---|---|
| Tariffs | The decline began in Q1 FY25 (-10.4%), about eight months before the 2025 tariffs. North America, the only tariff-exposed segment, grew 4.8% in FY26. The **$986M IEEPA recovery** ($965M North America, $21M Converse) offset most of the FY26 tariff cost ([FY26 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm), OPENED) |
| Macro and COVID payback | US footwear spending rose every year from 2021 to 2025. Over Nike's FY24 to FY26 the peer pool grew **+15.4%** and the incumbent cohort +6.2% at constant FX, while Nike fell about 10.8% currency-neutral. Nine of 12 peers grew (repo `peer_differential.md`, SEC XBRL and company reports, OPENED, my arithmetic) |
| The 2021 Xinjiang boycott | Greater China grew +4% (FY23) and +8% (FY24) currency-neutral, then broke in Q1 FY25, the same quarter as the global classics cut (repo filing data) |
| Outsider CEO Donahoe as the root | The strategy began as Parker's 2017 plan. Parker stayed Executive Chairman throughout, and revenue rose 37% from FY20 to FY24 under Donahoe (repo filing data) |
| Hill's reset as the trigger | The June 27, 2024 guidance cut and the Q1 FY25 quarter (-10.4%) both came before Hill started on Oct 14, 2024. He chose how deep the supply cuts went, which is a cost of the cure |
| World Cup 2026 shifting spend to adidas | Nike football grew "strong double-digit" in all four geographies, and Nike doubled World Cup team kit sales versus 2022 ([Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)) |

The stock is a separate story. About **45% of the log decline** from the $177.51 record close (Nov 5, 2021) to $35.35 (Oct 12, 2026) came from multiple compression shared with the sector: adidas's trailing P/E fell by a similar 52% on flat EPS. The other 55% is the fall in EPS (repo `valuation_vs_business.md`, market data). No operating fix will reverse the sector part.

## The fix has already worked where Nike applied it first

**Slide takeaway: North America returned to growth four quarters after the classics reset, the same shape as FY2018, and faster than adidas's North America under Gulden. What remains broken is concentrated in Greater China, Converse, aged Sportswear and Jordan retro.**

North America fell for four quarters in FY25 (-11.0%, -7.9%, -4.1%, -10.9%). It then grew in each of the next five (**+4.0%, +8.8%, +3.3%, +2.7%, +2.1%** from Q1 FY26 to Q1 FY27), and NA footwear units rose 6% in FY26 (repo filing data; [FY26 10-K p. 37](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm)). Nike has gone through this shape before. After it tightened Jordan distribution in 2017, North America fell -2.7%, -4.5% and -5.6% in Q1 to Q3 FY18 and returned to +3.3% in Q4 FY18 on new innovation ([Q3 FY18 call, 8-K Ex. 99.1, 27 Mar 2018, OPENED](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018718000037/q3fy18erccexhibit991.htm); repo `timeline_forensics.md`, my arithmetic). Gulden, by contrast, needed **eight quarters** to show reported growth in adidas North America (+15% in Q4 2024), because he deliberately shrank sell-in for seven straight quarters ([adidas FY2024 release, OPENED](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/image/upload/v1741155965/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2024/Q4/adidasAG_FY_2024_Results_EN_vf_ptevlo.pdf)). On the market that matters most to Nike, Hill is ahead of the Gulden clock.

Performance shows that the trend reverses once Nike executes inside it. Nike says Performance was about **$16B in FY26** and grew "by another high single digits" in Q1 FY27. CFO Dave Denton added that it would have been "up low double digits" excluding Greater China. Running grew double digits "with consistent share gains", and Nike says it "nearly tripled our share of the Max cushioning category over the past year" ([Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)). In the 12 months to September 2025, **Nike's run-specialty dollars rose 35.4% while On fell 19.7% and Hoka fell 7.4%** (Karnan Associates data with Circana, [SGB Media, 3 Dec 2025, OPENED](https://sgbonline.com/the-tre-sessions-run-specialty-growth-trailing-broader-market/)). Fleet Feet formalized a nationwide Nike partnership across more than 285 stores from April 9, 2026 ([Footwear Magazine, 8 Apr 2026, OPENED](https://footwearmagazine.com/fleet-feet-and-nike-formalize-a-full-nationwide-partnership-built-around-the-pegasus-42/)). A force that worked "regardless of execution" would not reverse within four to six quarters.

What is still broken is narrow, but it is large. In Q1 FY27 revenue fell $507M to $11,213M. By segment, Greater China contributed -$332M, EMEA -$155M, Converse -$103M and APLA -$27M, while North America added +$107M (repo `timeline_forensics.md`, my arithmetic on the [Q1 FY27 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000193/nke-20260831.htm)). **Greater China accounts for about 65% of the Q1 FY27 decline and Converse for about 20%**, and NIKE Brand outside China fell only about 0.8% (repo synthesis, my arithmetic). Greater China fell 26% currency-neutral. Within it, wholesale fell 31% and NIKE Direct 18%, with digital down 28% and stores down 14% (Q1 FY27 10-Q, currency-neutral, OPENED via the reverification note). That was its ninth straight quarterly decline. Hill has been blunt about the cause: "we've become a lifestyle brand competing on price in China" (Q2 FY26 call, Dec 2025, official transcript, OPENED per repo `china.md`). He has also said "the majority of our partners' physical doors in Greater China have not been refreshed in the past 7 years" ([Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)).

The China comparison works like a same-store experiment. Selling through the same Topsports doors, **adidas Greater China grew between +9% and +17% currency-neutral in every quarter from Q3 2024 to Q2 2026**, including +15% in Q2 2026 ([adidas Q2 2026 release, 30 Jul 2026, OPENED](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-grows-top-line-14percent-and-achieves-record-sales-in-q2)). Domestic mass brands are stalling at the same time. In H1 2026 Anta Group grew 12.9%, but the Anta brand segment grew only 4.8%, Li-Ning 2.8%, and Xtep's group revenue *fell* 0.6% ([Anta interim, HKEX, 26 Aug 2026, OPENED](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0826/2026082600213.pdf); [Li-Ning interim, HKEX, OPENED](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0820/2026082001445.pdf); [Xtep interim, HKEX, OPENED](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0825/2026082500235.pdf)). Nationalism alone cannot explain a 26% fall in a foreign brand while another foreign brand grows double digits. Even so, China is **the slowest repair in the plan**. Nike ends all distributor online sales in mainland China from January 1, 2027. That channel was about 22% of Topsports' revenue ([Topsports HKEX inside information, 22 Jul 2026, OPENED](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0722/2026072200069.pdf)), and the cut will lower revenue before any benefit appears. Denton's guidance "assumes that China actually gets worse from a revenue perspective for the balance of this year" ([Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)).

The open fixability question is lifestyle. Sportswear is "just under half" of revenue and fell low double digits in Q1 FY27. Nike cut Dunk revenue by "nearly 50%", a headwind of roughly $200M. Jordan is 13% of revenue and fell by mid-teens. Hill acknowledged "a lack of energy in the lifestyle space right now, which is impacting traffic" ([Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)). Circana also shows US sport-lifestyle footwear declining in H1 2026 while running units and dollars rose about 13% ([FN, 14 Aug 2026, OPENED](https://wwd.com/footwear-news/shoe-industry-news/consumer-spending-trends-running-shoes-1239125374/)). If this is a cycle, Nike's newness pipeline can ride the next wave. If lifestyle sneakers are shrinking for good, part of what this report calls execution is really structural.

The table separates three paths. **Adidas was flat for four quarters, then grew double digits.** Its gross margin was back above 50% by quarter 5, and its first quarter at or above 9% operating margin came in quarter 7. Annual operating margin went from 1.3% (2023) to 5.6% (2024) to 8.3% (2025), and the target of more than 10% is set for 2028 ([adidas FY2025 release, OPENED](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/image/upload/v1772605768/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2025/Q4/adidasAG_FY_2025_Results_EN_Final_tb9z6m.pdf)). **Under Armour has shrunk in all nine quarters.** Its gross margin rose for four quarters as promotions were pulled, then tariffs erased the gain, and North America fell in every quarter. FY2026 revenue was $5.0B (-4%) against $5.7B in the last year before Plank returned. Adjusted operating income fell from $310M (FY2024) to $107M (FY2026), and in August 2026 the FY2027 guide was cut to a mid-single-digit decline, three months after it was issued ([UA Q1 FY27 8-K, 7 Aug 2026, OPENED](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000108/exhibit991-fiscal2027q1.htm)). **Nike fell steeply for three quarters, was roughly flat for four, and relapsed in quarter 8.** Its gross margin, excluding the refund, is still 200 to 450 basis points below pre-Hill levels, and FY27 is guided to a high-single-digit revenue decline.

Two caveats keep the comparison honest. Adidas's quarter 1 included about €400M of lost Yeezy revenue, so its underlying (ex-Yeezy) growth was already +9% ([Q1 2023 release, OPENED](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/raw/upload/v1705999372/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2023/adidasag_q1_2023results_en_final_ciz6cw.pdf)). Nike's 2026 quarters also carry a deliberate classics shrink: in Q2 FY26 revenue was +6% currency-neutral excluding the classics headwind (Friend, Q2 FY26 call, OPENED per repo). On both companies' underlying measures the gap narrows, but adidas still leads.

The share price tells the same story. Adidas closed at €127.70 on Gulden's first day, and at his eighth quarter (about October 2024) it stood at about €224 to €240, **up 75% to 88%**. It peaked at €263.10 in February 2025 and is now €153.25 (+20% since he started) even though operating profit roughly tripled from €669M (2022) to €2,056M (2025), a sector de-rating that Boersen-Zeitung headlined "Stark im Geschaeft, schwach an der Boerse" ("Strong in business, weak on the stock market") ([Yahoo Finance ADS.DE data, OPENED](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/ADS.DE?period1=1661990400&period2=1792000000&interval=1d); [Boersen-Zeitung, 1 Oct 2026, OPENED](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/kapitalmaerkte/stark-im-geschaeft-schwach-an-der-boerse)). UA Class C is about 33% below where it was when Plank returned ($7.14 to $4.78) ([Yahoo Finance UA data, OPENED](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UA/history/)). NKE went from $81.60 on Hill's first day to $35.35 on October 12, 2026, **down 56.7%** (repo `data/nke_daily_prices.csv`, market data). Investors gave Gulden a story-stock phase that paid for his patience. Hill has not had one.

## Hill fits the Gulden profile, but two Plank warning signs are flashing

**Slide takeaway: a returning insider who did not design the failed strategy and who can be removed by the board is the right fit on the academic evidence. Repeated slippage in the recovery date and successive restructurings are the warning signs to watch.**

On the leadership evidence, Hill sits closer to Gulden than to Plank. Gulden is a "returnee-outsider": he spent seven years at adidas in the 1990s, then ran Puma, Pandora and Deichmann ([adidas press release, 8 Nov 2022, OPENED](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/bjorn-gulden-to-become-ceo-of-adidas-ag)). Plank founded Under Armour, was its CEO until 2019, stayed on as Executive Chair while the decline continued, and holds **64.6% of the vote** through Class B shares, which "is able to direct the election of all of the members of our Board" ([UA FY2026 10-K, OPENED](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000073/ua-20260331.htm); [UA DEF 14A, Jul 2026, OPENED](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000102/ua-20260715.htm)). Hill spent 32 years at Nike, retired in 2020, never served as CEO and holds no control stake. The Bingham, Hendricks, Howell and Kolev study of 6,429 S&P 1500 CEOs found that firms led by the 167 "boomerang" CEOs underperformed by about 10% a year. The penalty was concentrated in returning founders (44% of boomerangs against 4% of all CEOs), in dynamic industries and in firms that had failed at succession planning ([Fortune, 16 Mar 2020, OPENED](https://fortune.com/2020/03/16/return-ceos-effect-on-stock); [Reuters via IBTimes, 22 Nov 2022, OPENED](https://www.ibtimes.com/boomerang-ceos-dont-always-work-out-disney-hopes-this-one-bucks-trend-3638902)). Plank scores negative on every predictor. Hill is not a boomerang under the study's definition. Nike's control structure also lets its board remove a CEO who is not delivering, as it did with Donahoe, a check that Under Armour's structure does not provide.

The team composition is similar to adidas's. Every adidas Executive Board replacement under Gulden was an internal or returning adidas person ([adidas board releases 2023 to 2026, OPENED](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/arthur-hoeld-executive-board-member-for-global-sales-to-step-down-from-the-executive-board-of-adidas-ag-mathieu-sidokpohou-appointed-as-successor)). Hill has likewise promoted insiders and returnees, and his only senior outside hires are in support and commercial functions: communications, CFO Dave Denton (from August 17, 2026) and Chief Commercial Officer Jane Ewing (ex-Walmart, from September 7, 2026) ([Nike release, 27 Aug 2026](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/releases/nike-inc-august-27-2026-senior-leadership-announcement)). The weak point is the finance function, which turned over in a single quarter just before a $2.5B restructuring and Investor Day. The chief accounting officer also resigned effective September 4, 2026 (8-K filed Aug 10, 2026, per repo REPORT.md). The say-on-pay vote fell to about 67.8% from about 94% (8-K filed Sept 10, 2026, per repo REPORT.md).

The playbooks are nearly identical, so the difference in outcome has to come from somewhere else. All three CEOs cut promotions, cleaned the channel, reduced SKUs, rebuilt wholesale and put performance innovation first. Under Armour's own diagnosis is that once discounting stopped, there was **no brand heat to sell at full price**: eCommerce fell 20% to 27% a quarter "due to planned decreases in promotional activities", and footwear has fallen in almost every quarter ([UA FY2025 8-Ks, OPENED](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691725000058/exhibit991-fiscal2025q4.htm)). Adidas had the opposite problem. Terrace demand was "extraordinary" in Gulden's first quarter, he scaled Samba while keeping supply below demand ("We have scaled up supply but are far from covering the total current demand", [Q3 2023 release, OPENED](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/raw/upload/v1706002076/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2023/adidasag_q3_2023results_final_en_vmxejg.pdf)), and the Yeezy stock he chose to sell rather than write off produced about **€1.4B of revenue and €500M of operating profit** over 2023 and 2024. In 2023 the Yeezy profit (about €300M) was larger than adidas's entire reported operating profit (€268M) ([FY2023 release, OPENED](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/raw/upload/v1710499226/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2023/adidas_ag_fy2023_results_en_final_bmt7gz.pdf)). Nike has neither a windfall to sell nor a trend that arrived on its own. Its brand heat sits in between: still No. 1 among teens at **46% footwear mindshare against 14% for adidas** (Fall 2025), after a slide through 61%, 59%, 57% and 49% (Spring 2025, confirmed) ([SGB Media, 9 Oct 2025, OPENED](https://sgbonline.com/piper-sandler-teen-survey-finds-on-overtaking-hoka-in-popularity-nikes-erosion-stabilizing/); [SGI News, Spring 2025, OPENED](https://sginews.com/Content/teen-survey-sees-upstarts-gaining-mindshare.aspx)).

Under Armour's notes identify specific tells of a stalled turnaround. Nike shows two of them clearly, one partly, and has avoided two:

| Plank-pattern tell | Under Armour evidence | Nike status at quarter 8 |
|---|---|---|
| "Trough is behind us" calls followed by guidance cuts | Turn promised for Fall-Winter 2025, then "most challenging phase" in the December 2025 quarter, then FY27 guide cut after three months (UA 8-Ks, OPENED) | **Flashing.** BNP's Laurent Vasilescu says Win Now "would be done by the end of 2025, then it was pushed out to the end of 2026", and Denton now says the actions "bleed in a little bit into fiscal '28" ([Retail Dive, Oct 2026, OPENED](https://www.retaildive.com/news/nike-turnaround-visibility-very-low-layoffs-sales-declines-q1-earnings/832015/); [Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)) |
| Restructuring estimates that keep growing | $70M to $90M (May 2024) grew to about $305M (Aug 2026) | **Flashing.** Vasilescu calls Pace "the third restructuring program since fiscal 2024". Pace targets about $2.5B of savings through FY2031 for about $1.0B of charges, "the majority" realized in FY29 and FY30 ([Nike 8-K, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000320187/000032018726000184/nke-20261001.htm)) |
| Churn among the CEO's own hires | Liedtke in three roles in 22 months; CPO out after about 18 months; CFO change mid-turnaround | **Partial.** CFO and CAO replaced in one quarter; Hill changed 11 of 15 direct reports by June 2025 ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/nike-ceo-elliott-hill-leadership-team-turnaround-changes/750495/)) |
| Margin gains without revenue stabilization | FY2025 gross margin +180 bps while revenue fell 9% | **Not present.** Nike's margin also fell, which signals a real reset rather than cosmetic profit protection |
| Home-market decline every quarter | UA North America down in all nine quarters (-5% to -14%) | **Not present.** Nike North America has grown five straight quarters |

The other returning-leader cases point the same way. Successful returns (Schultz in 2008, Jobs in 1997) brought a concrete product or experience thesis, while failures came in fast-changing markets where the returnee's old model no longer fit ([Fortune, 16 Mar 2020, OPENED](https://fortune.com/2020/03/16/return-ceos-effect-on-stock)). Hill's sport-first thesis is concrete in Performance and still missing in Sportswear, where he admits that breaking the business into consumer segments was something "I probably did underappreciate" ([Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)). Hill has also said "Die Wende dauert laenger als mir lieb ist" ("The turnaround is taking longer than I would like") ([Boersen-Zeitung, 9 Apr 2026, OPENED](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/meinung-analyse/die-aktienkurse-von-nike-adidas-und-puma-sind-weit-von-ihren-vor-fuenf-jahren)). The circulating FT quote that he "underestimated" the task is still unverified, so use the stock3 paraphrase instead: the road is longer than he hoped ([stock3, 22 Jun 2026, OPENED](https://stock3.com/news/nike-und-der-lange-lauf-zum-turnaround-17127091)).

## The recovery clock: growth late FY2028, margins FY2029 to FY2030, FY2024 revenue about FY2031 to FY2032

**Slide takeaway: running Nike on the adidas sequence, but starting from Nike's quarter 8 position, gives a recovery about two years slower than Gulden's at group level. The market already expects that.**

Gulden's sequence is the template: the share price re-rated in 0 to 6 months, China and inventory turned in 2 to 3 quarters, group growth came in 3 to 6 quarters, gross margin in 4 to 5 quarters, operating margin in 7 or more quarters, and North America in 8 or more quarters (repo `adidas_gulden.md`, built from the opened releases above). Against that template, Nike's North America and gross-margin stability are on schedule or early. Its group growth, Greater China and share price are well behind. Nike's own guidance sets the near-term floor. Denton guided FY27 revenue to "decline in the high single-digit range", with EBIT down more than revenue, adjusted EPS of **$1.15 to $1.35**, and a roughly 400 basis point Q2 revenue headwind from lapping EMEA Cyber Week promotions and a higher North America sell-in comparison ([Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf); [Q1 FY27 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000184/q1fy27exhibit991er.htm)). A 7% to 9% decline implies **FY27 revenue of about $42.2B to $43.2B** (my arithmetic).

That gives the following timeline (my judgment, built from the evidence above):

| Milestone | Adidas (quarter reached) | Nike expected | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home-market growth | Quarter 8 | Done in quarter 4 (Q1 FY26) | Repo filing data |
| First sustained group currency-neutral growth | Quarter 5 | **H2 FY28 (Dec 2027 to May 2028), about quarters 14 to 15** | FY27 guided down; "bleed" into FY28; China channel exit from Jan 2027; lapping the Dunk and Jordan cuts |
| Greater China stabilization | Quarter 2 | FY28 at the earliest; Nike says "beyond fiscal 2027" | Q1 FY27 10-Q; distributor online exit; made-for-China product from holiday 2027 |
| Gross margin back to about 44% to 45% | Quarters 4 to 5 (back to pre-crisis) | FY28 to FY29 | Ex-refund margin 40% to 43% now; Q1 FY27 gain came from logistics and FX while ASP was a drag (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 23) |
| Double-digit operating margin | Annual 8.3% after 3 years; above 10% targeted for 2028 | FY29 to FY30 | FY27 operating margin near 6% per Bernstein ([Invezz, 2 Oct 2026, OPENED](https://invezz.com/en-ae/news/2026/10/02/bernstein-says-nike-stock-now-de-risked-buy-on-post-earnings-dip/)); Pace savings mostly in FY29 to FY30 |
| Revenue back to FY24's $51.4B | Record revenue in 2024 (year 2) | About FY31 to FY32 | From about $42.7B, flat FY28, then 4% to 6% a year (my arithmetic) |

Three forces could move the clock in either direction. A lifestyle hit, Nike's version of Samba, would pull growth forward by several quarters. Nike says more than a dozen new Sportswear styles are coming, and the P-6000 and V5 Runner already grew "strong double digits" ([Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)). A deeper China slide would push it out: S&P expects Greater China down about 30% for FY27 and "negative cash flow over the next two to three years" ([Tradingpedia on S&P's cut from A+ to A with a negative outlook, 5 Oct 2026, OPENED, secondary](https://www.tradingpedia.com/2026/10/05/nike-rating-cut-as-china-slump-and-restructuring-hit-cash/)). Cash is the third constraint. FY26 free cash flow of $2,184M did not cover $2,407M of dividends, and the $1.64 annual dividend is about 131% of guided EPS at the midpoint ([FY26 10-K p. 58](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm); repo arithmetic). Gulden funded his transition year with Yeezy profits. Hill has to fund his while defending the dividend.

The sell-side has already shifted to a long clock. After Q1 FY27, Bernstein cut its target to **$45** (Outperform, "de-risked"), Williams Trading's Sam Poser downgraded to Hold with a target cut from $57 to $30, BNP Paribas set a Street-low $19, and the highest post-print targets on opened pages are $50 (Guggenheim, BTIG) ([Invezz, OPENED](https://invezz.com/en-ae/news/2026/10/02/bernstein-says-nike-stock-now-de-risked-buy-on-post-earnings-dip/); [stockanalysis.com, Poser, OPENED](https://stockanalysis.com/analysts/sam-poser/); [Stocktwits/The Fly, 2 Oct 2026, OPENED](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/nke-stock-cracks-premarket-analysts-slash-targets-on-weak-outlook-citi-calls-nike-a-cost-cutting-story/cZDjOoORBKe)). Jefferies' $75 was set on September 28, before the print. Citi called it "a cost-cutting story". Needham's Tom Nikic said turnaround visibility is "very low" ([Retail Dive, Oct 2026, OPENED](https://www.retaildive.com/news/nike-turnaround-visibility-very-low-layoffs-sales-declines-q1-earnings/832015/)). At $35.35 the stock trades at about 28 times guided FY27 EPS (my arithmetic), so the market is still paying for a recovery, just not a fast one.

## Recommended thesis statement

"Nike did not lose a fight with the market. It lost a fight with its own 2017 to 2023 Consumer Direct strategy, which explains about half of the FY2024 to FY2026 revenue decline directly and most of the rest through Greater China. Elliott Hill's returning insider team is running the same playbook that turned adidas around under Bjorn Gulden, and it has already restored growth in North America and share gains in running. But Nike lacks Gulden's windfalls (a trend arriving on its own and a Yeezy stock to sell), and it still carries Plank-style risks in Sportswear, China and slipping guidance. Nike is fixable on a longer clock than adidas: sustained growth from the second half of FY2028, double-digit margins by FY2029 to FY2030, and a return to the incumbent growth line rather than the 2021 peak."

## Five strongest proof points

**1. Peers grew while Nike fell, in the same markets and quarters.** Over Nike's FY24 to FY26 the peer pool grew 15.4% and the incumbent cohort 6.2% at constant FX, while Nike fell about 10.8% currency-neutral, and nine of 12 peers grew (repo `peer_differential.md`, SEC XBRL and company reports, OPENED; my arithmetic). In Q2 2026 adidas grew 14% currency-neutral, including +17% in North America and +15% in Greater China ([adidas release, OPENED](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-grows-top-line-14percent-and-achieves-record-sales-in-q2)).

**2. The fix worked in North America on Nike's own historical timetable, and faster than Gulden.** North America returned to growth in quarter 4 and has grown five quarters in a row (+4.0% to +8.8%), matching the FY2018 Jordan-tightening pattern. Adidas North America needed eight quarters (repo filing data; [adidas FY2024 release, OPENED](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/image/upload/v1741155965/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2024/Q4/adidasAG_FY_2024_Results_EN_vf_ptevlo.pdf)).

**3. The decline sits in the channel the strategy built.** From FY24 to FY26, NIKE Direct fell $3,799M while NIKE Brand wholesale fell $305M, and NA wholesale reached a record $11,958M (repo channel data; [FY26 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000088/nke-20260531.htm)). Management sized the classics cut at "more than $4 billion" from peak (Q2 FY26 call, OPENED per repo).

**4. Nike wins inside the trend when it executes.** Nike's run-specialty dollars rose 35.4% while On fell 19.7% and Hoka 7.4% ([SGB Media, 3 Dec 2025, OPENED](https://sgbonline.com/the-tre-sessions-run-specialty-growth-trailing-broader-market/)). Performance reached about $16B and grew high single digits in Q1 FY27 ([Q1 FY27 transcript, OPENED](https://s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2027/q1/NIKE-Inc-Q1FY27-OFFICIAL-Transcript_-FINAL.pdf)).

**5. China is a same-store natural experiment that Nike is losing on execution.** Through the same Topsports doors, adidas Greater China grew between +9% and +17% currency-neutral every quarter from Q3 2024 while Nike fell between 3% and 26%. Meanwhile Anta brand (+4.8%), Li-Ning (+2.8%) and Xtep (-0.6%) stalled in H1 2026, so nationalism does not explain Nike's fall ([adidas releases, OPENED](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/image/upload/v1785388904/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2026/Q2/EN/adidasAG_Q2_2026_Results_EN_Final_z7f9t2.pdf); [HKEX interims, OPENED](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0826/2026082600213.pdf)). Hill's own words: "we've become a lifestyle brand competing on price in China".

## Hardest objections, with answers

**"Fixable is not showing up in the total. Two years in, Nike still fell 4% in Q1 FY27 and guides FY27 down high single digits."** Concede the timing and defend the direction. The live decline is concentrated: Greater China is about 65% of the Q1 FY27 decline, Converse about 20%, and NIKE Brand outside China fell only about 0.8% (repo synthesis, my arithmetic on the [Q1 FY27 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000193/nke-20260831.htm)). Adidas's North America took eight quarters to grow. The thesis already dates group growth to H2 FY28 rather than FY27.

**"Adidas recovered on luck: Samba arrived on its own and Yeezy paid for 2023. Nike has no such luck, so the precedent does not transfer."** Mostly true, which is why the thesis gives Nike a slower clock than Gulden's. The transferable parts are the sequence (clean the channel, then rebuild wholesale with scarce product, then put performance innovation at the center) and the expectation reset. Adidas also shows that lifestyle cycles turn in both directions. Its footwear slowed to +1% and lifestyle to +2% in Q2 2026, and Gulden blamed "heavy discounting at many retailers" ([adidas Q2 2026 release, OPENED](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/image/upload/v1785388904/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2026/Q2/EN/adidasAG_Q2_2026_Results_EN_Final_z7f9t2.pdf)). The terrace cycle that lifted adidas is fading, and that opens room for Nike's next wave. Do not quote Gulden's reported "gift from the sky" line, which is unverified (36Kr, secondary).

**"This is Under Armour again: an insider comes back, the trough keeps moving and restructurings pile up."** Concede the two tells that are flashing (date slippage and Pace as a third program), then show the differences that matter. Nike's home market has grown for five quarters while UA's fell for nine. Nike is still No. 1 in teen footwear at 46% against adidas at 14%. Performance is a growing $16B business. Hill is not a founder, and the board can remove him. Under Armour, as Needham's Tom Nikic put it, "still haven't found their product" ([Retail Dive, 19 Aug 2024, OPENED](https://www.retaildive.com/news/kevin-plank-under-armour-turnaround/723957/)). Nike has found it in running and has not yet found it in lifestyle.

**"The market has fragmented for good. Nike's US share fell from 40.5% to 28.9% and challengers keep growing."** Agree, and fold it into the thesis: recovery means getting back to the incumbent line, not to $51B by FY28. Large incumbents' share fell from 27% (2019) to 24% (2024) ([McKinsey via Wayback, OPENED](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/retail/our-insights/sporting-goods-industry-trends)), which is a ceiling, not a cause of the FY25 cliff. The challengers are slowing in Nike's core channel: On lost run-specialty dollars, and Hoka is guided to low double-digit growth for FY2027 ([FashionUnited, 22 May 2026, OPENED](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/hoka-and-ugg-drive-deckers-brands-to-5-47-billion-dollars-in-fy26-net-sales/2026052288200)).

**"China is structural, and no Western brand can win there now."** Adidas grew +13% in Greater China in 2025 to about US$4.2B and +15% in Q2 2026 ([SCMP, 12 Oct 2026, OPENED via Wayback](https://www.scmp.com/business/china-business/article/3370520/adidas-ceo-taps-outdoor-sport-street-dance-new-drivers-china-competition-heightens)), so the market is winnable. The external China slice is real, at 6% to 16% of the decline, and the thesis concedes that China is the slowest repair, with a payoff dated holiday 2027 or later.

**"Even if the business recovers, the stock may not."** True, and it should be stated plainly. About 45% of the log share-price decline is a sector multiple compression, and adidas's shares are up only 20% under Gulden even though operating profit roughly tripled from 2022 to 2025 (repo `valuation_vs_business.md`; [Yahoo ADS.DE, OPENED](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/ADS.DE?period1=1661990400&period2=1792000000&interval=1d)). The thesis is about the business. The valuation question is separate, and at about 28 times guided FY27 EPS the market already prices in some recovery.

## Conclusion

The comparison changes what the case team should argue. "Insider versus outsider" and "execution versus structure" both matter less than whether a turnaround has **latent full-price demand to release once the discounting stops**. Adidas had that demand in terrace shoes and sold it scarce. Under Armour did not have it, so cutting promotions only exposed weak demand. Nike has it in running, football and basketball, and has not yet rebuilt it in the lifestyle franchises that its own Consumer Direct strategy overused. That is why North America behaves like adidas while Sportswear and China behave like Under Armour. The deciding variable for the next six quarters is Sportswear newness, not leadership pedigree or tariffs.

That also gives the team a falsifiable position, which judges reward. If Investor Day sets conservative targets, North America stays positive through the Jordan retro cut in Q2 FY27, and Greater China's decline narrows after January 2027, Nike is on a slower version of the Gulden path, with group growth from H2 FY28. If the FY27 guide is cut again within a quarter, North America turns negative, and restructuring costs keep rising, Nike is repeating the Plank pattern. In that case the realistic target becomes margin repair on a smaller revenue base, not growth.
