# N1: Brand health panel, Nike versus peers (Oct 15, 2026)

Scope: free survey and ranking publications on Nike brand health, tested for direction in 2025 to 2026 and for lead or lag against Nike revenue. Data file: `N1_brand_health_panel.csv` (204 rows, every row carries a label and URL). Extends, does not repeat, `research_notes/Nike decline root cause analysis/marketing_shift.md` (which already used Piper Spring 2022 to Spring 2025 and snippets of Interbrand, Harris and BrandZ).

Labels: OPENED = I read the primary or full page in this session. SNIPPET = number seen only in a search summary or a secondary article. DERIVED = my arithmetic, shown. REPO = taken from an existing repo file, not re-opened.

## 1. Verdict in six lines

1. **No smoking gun.** Nothing in free brand-health data decides long versus short by itself. The best-measured survey series (Piper) shows a sharp 2024 to Spring 2025 fall and then a deceleration to roughly flat in Fall 2025. The valuation-based series (Interbrand, BrandZ, Brand Finance) are still falling but are partly restatements of Nike's earnings (section 5).
2. **Piper has a definition break the repo did not know about.** The Fall 2025 infographic labels its whole Clothing and Footwear panel "*Upper income teens" (OPENED). Every earlier point in the repo series (60, 61, 59, 57, 49) is all teens. The repo's "49% to 46%" step therefore mixes cohorts. Like for like, upper-income Nike footwear share was 56 (Fall 2023), 51 (Spring 2024), 45 (Fall 2024), 46 (Fall 2025): down 10 points in a year, then +1 (section 3).
3. **Piper no longer publishes twice a year.** SGB (Apr 22, 2026) reports the survey moved to once a year. There is no Spring 2026 edition. As of Oct 15, 2026 pipersandler.com/teens still shows Fall 2025, so Fall 2026 is not out (Piper released in the first half of October in every prior year, so it is late or not yet posted). Next-edition watch item.
4. **Direction in 2025 to 2026: decelerating, not reversing.** Survey and satisfaction measures are flat to slightly down; value-model measures are down but at a slower rate in the most recent print (BrandZ -31% then -17%; Brand Finance -7%; Interbrand was the exception, accelerating to -25.9% in 2025).
5. **Lead or lag: no series leads Nike revenue.** Brand-value series are coincident or lagging (correlation highest at lag 0 or -1 years). The only series with a positive lead correlation is the change in Piper share, with n = 6 and no statistical significance (section 4).
6. **Financial conversion.** If teen share were fully representative, the 61 to 49 fall in all-teen share would map to a $2.2B NA footwear revenue fall from FY2023 to FY2025 (an elasticity of 0.76); that is an upper-bound illustration from two data points, not a finding. At the 42.9% gross margin it is about $0.95B of gross profit, under half of the $2.0B EBIT gap between the FY2024 and FY2026 margins (section 6).

## 4. Does brand health lead or lag Nike revenue?

Method: Pearson r between a brand measure in calendar year t and Nike fiscal revenue growth for the fiscal year ending May of t+k (k = -1, 0, 1, 2), using 10-K revenue from `data/nike_filing_facts.csv` (growth: FY20 -4.4%, FY21 +19.1%, FY22 +4.9%, FY23 +9.6%, FY24 +0.3%, FY25 -9.8%, FY26 +0.2%). Script: `N1_lead_lag_corr.py` in this folder (DERIVED; run with python3 -I). n in brackets. Spring Piper readings are used so the reading precedes fiscal year end.

| Series (level) | k=-1 (series lags revenue) | k=0 | k=+1 (series leads) | k=+2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Piper all-teens Nike footwear share, Spring | 0.26 (7) | 0.31 (7) | -0.10 (7) | -0.82 (6) |
| Interbrand value | 0.41 (6) | 0.60 (6) | -0.32 (6) | -0.75 (5) |
| Interbrand rank (negated) | 0.30 (9) | 0.62 (10) | 0.04 (11) | -0.38 (11) |
| BrandZ value | 0.77 (6) | 0.55 (6) | 0.62 (5) | 0.22 (4) |
| BrandZ rank (negated) | 0.69 (10) | 0.53 (11) | 0.26 (11) | 0.06 (11) |
| Brand Finance Global 500 rank (negated) | 0.07 (9) | 0.36 (10) | 0.49 (11) | 0.43 (11) |
| ACSI Nike score | 0.24 (7) | -0.45 (7) | -0.74 (7) | 0.04 (6) |

| Series (Y/Y change) | k=-1 | k=0 | k=+1 | k=+2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Piper share change | 0.06 (6) | 0.71 (6) | 0.55 (6) | 0.72 (5) |
| Interbrand value change | 0.15 (5) | 0.86 (5) | 0.73 (5) | 0.24 (4) |
| Interbrand rank change | 0.03 (9) | 0.79 (10) | 0.43 (11) | 0.22 (11) |
| BrandZ value change | 0.58 (5) | 0.08 (5) | 0.43 (4) | not computable |
| ACSI change | 0.43 (5) | 0.52 (5) | -0.53 (5) | 0.38 (4) |

Reading: with n from 4 to 11 none of these is statistically distinguishable from zero except possibly the Interbrand change at lag 0 (r 0.86, n 5, still weak). The pattern that matters is timing in the raw data, not the coefficients:
- Nike revenue growth peaked at FY21 (+19.1%) and slowed to +0.3% in FY24 (ended May 2024). Piper all-teen share was still 61 in Fall 2023 and 59 in Spring 2024. Revenue stalled before teen share fell; teen share fell 10 points over the next year, in the same fiscal year as the -9.8% revenue fall. So Piper is coincident to lagging for the headline revenue decline, not leading.
- Interbrand, BrandZ and Brand Finance are valuation models that use financial forecasts and, for BrandZ, market value. Their high correlation at k = -1 and 0 and the Interbrand cut of 25.9% in 2025 against EBIT -42.2% (FY24 $6,539M to FY25 $3,778M, DERIVED from `nike_filing_facts.csv`) mean they restate earnings. They cannot show brand caused earnings.
- ACSI correlates negatively at leads: satisfaction among customers rose to 85 in 2024 while revenue fell. It is not a leading indicator of revenue.
- Caveat: annual brand series have 4 to 11 points. A lead of one to two years cannot be established at this n; I only report that none shows it.

## 6. Financial conversion (DERIVED, labelled assumptions)

Teen share to US footwear dollars. Nike North America footwear revenue (10-K): FY2023 $14,897M, FY2025 $12,684M (-14.9%, -$2,213M), FY2026 $13,317M (+5.0%, +$633M).
- Piper all-teens Nike footwear share Spring 2023 to Spring 2025: 61 to 49 = -19.7% relative.
- Implied elasticity of NA footwear revenue to teen share over that window: -14.9 / -19.7 = 0.76 (two points only, correlation not causation; teens are a subset of Nike's customers, so this is an upper-bound illustration).
- Revenue per point of teen mindshare at the Spring 2025 base: $13,317M x (1 / 49) x 0.76 = about $205M of NA footwear revenue per point, or about $88M of gross profit at the FY26 gross margin of 42.9%. If Fall 2025 is flat (upper-income reading), the model implies no further teen-driven revenue loss in FY27, consistent with FY26 NA footwear +5.0%.
- Piper's published spend base: $2,388 per teen in Spring 2025, SGI computes $66.0B of total teen spending (SNIPPET). Piper's footwear wallet share is only in the paid report. Illustrative only: at an assumed 10% footwear share (my assumption, not sourced) the pool is $6.6B, and one point of Nike mindshare is about $66M if spend share equals mindshare. This is a third of the $205M elasticity-based figure, so the teen pool alone cannot account for the NA footwear drop; adult and wholesale effects must carry the rest. Data that would settle it: Piper footwear wallet share by income cohort, and Circana footwear sell-through by brand (N2).
- Scale check against the pitch: at the FY2024 EBIT margin of 12.7% instead of FY2026's 8.3%, FY26 revenue of $46,398M would carry about $2,041M more EBIT (4.4 points x $46,398M, DERIVED). The whole NA footwear revenue decline FY23 to FY25 ($2,213M) at a 42.9% gross margin is about $950M of gross profit, under half of that margin gap. Brand erosion in teens is a minority of the margin story.

## 7. What this means for the pitch

- Do not write "teen mindshare fell from 61% to 46%". That compares all teens with upper-income teens. Write "all-teen Nike footwear share fell from 61% (Fall 2023) to 49% (Spring 2025); upper-income share fell from 56% to 45% (Fall 2023 to Fall 2024) and was 46% in Fall 2025".
- The brand-health data supports "decline decelerated to flat by Fall 2025", which is neutral to a long case on stabilization and does not support a short on accelerating brand loss. It does not reduce the margin requirement in the thesis.
- Valuation-model brand declines (Interbrand, BrandZ, Brand Finance) should not be presented as independent evidence of brand weakness; they restate earnings.
- Watch: Piper Fall 2026 (due after Oct 15, 2026, the next print), whether it reports one cohort and whether Nike upper-income footwear holds 46%.
