- The Consumer Direct Offense launch (June 2017) is not described in these filings except retrospectively: "Since fiscal 2018, through the Consumer Direct Offense and our Triple Double strategy, we have focused on ... growing our direct connections with consumers" [D20, printed p.27]. No FY2017 or FY2018 store counts, comp sales, digital growth or top-3 customer shares are in this set.

### FY2019 (Consumer Direct Offense, year 1 full year)
- Strategy: "Through the Consumer Direct Offense, we are focusing on our Triple Double strategy" with long-term goal "Gross margin expansion of as much as 50 basis points" per year through FY2023 [D19, printed p.24].
- Mix: NIKE Direct about 32% of NIKE Brand revenue; digital commerce $3.8bn, +35% currency-neutral; comps +6%; NIKE Direct +16% c-n [D19, printed p.28]. "Within our NIKE Direct business, digital outpaced all other channels, growing at 35%" [D19, printed p.24].
- Store-channel tension: North America comps fell 3% because "growth in our full-price channel has impacted the availability and composition of off-price inventory for sale within NFS" [D19, printed p.31].
- Marketplace language: consumers want "the shift toward digital and mono-brand experiences in NIKE Direct"; "The evolution of the North America marketplace is resulting in third-party retail store closures"; but "we are currently seeing stabilization and momentum building with our strategic wholesale customers" [D19, printed p.31].
- Gross margin: +90 bps, due to higher full-price ASP on a wholesale equivalent basis, FX, "and growth in NIKE Direct" [D19, printed p.24]; "gross margin was favorably impacted by growth in our higher-margin NIKE Direct business" [D19, printed p.29]. The bps bridge was a chart (not captured in text). Segment GM: Greater China +210 bps incl. "higher NIKE Direct margins" [p.33]; APLA +170 bps incl. NIKE Direct growth and margin expansion [p.34]; Converse +180 bps "driven by higher margin in our direct to consumer business" [D19, printed p.35].
- Risk: "Consolidation of retailers or concentration of retail market share among a few retailers may increase and concentrate our credit risk" [D19, printed p.11]; NIKE Direct "also compete with multi-brand retailers selling our products" [D19, printed p.8].
- Converse: growth "primarily attributable to revenue growth in Asia across all distribution channels", offset by U.S. and Europe [D19, printed p.35].

### FY2020 (Consumer Direct Acceleration announced June 2020; COVID)
- Strategy: "In June 2020, we announced a new digitally empowered phase of the Consumer Direct Offense strategy: Consumer Direct Acceleration." First pillar: "creating the marketplace of the future". "This strategy will lead with NIKE Digital and our own stores, as well as through select strategic partners who share our marketplace vision"; goal to "accelerate NIKE to a digital-first company" [D20, printed p.27].
- Mix: NIKE Direct about 35% of NIKE Brand; digital $5.5bn, +49% c-n; comps -12%; NIKE Direct +8% c-n while "wholesale revenues declined 7%" [D20, printed p.28 and p.32]. Q4 digital +79% c-n [D20, printed p.28].
- COVID channel actions: about 90% of NIKE Brand stores in NA/EMEA/APLA closed about 8 weeks; "certain of our wholesale partners closed stores or reduced operating hours"; Nike was "shifting available inventory into our digital channel and increasing digital fulfillment capacity" [D20, printed p.27] and "investing in targeted promotions and markdowns to accelerate liquidation of excess inventory" [D20, printed p.28].
- Greater China: Q3 closures of "approximately 75% of NIKE-owned and partner stores within the region" [D20, printed p.27]. This is the only reference to partner stores; no partner-operated or franchise store counts are disclosed in any 10-K.
- Gross margin: -130 bps; channel-related item is "the negative rate impacts of supply chain costs on a lower volume of wholesale shipments in the fourth quarter" [D20, printed p.28].
- Wholesale stance: "we remain focused on building long-term momentum with our strategic wholesale customers" [D20, printed p.36].
- Distributor model: APLA (Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Uruguay) to shift "from a wholesale and direct to consumer operating model to a distributor operating model" [D20, printed p.29].
- Converse: wholesale -5%, DTC +11%; GM +60 bps with "higher ASP in our direct to consumer channel, primarily through digital" [D20, printed p.40].

### FY2021 (CDA year 1: explicit wholesale account cuts)
- Mix: NIKE Direct about 39%; digital $9.1bn, +60%; comps +4%; NIKE Direct +30% c-n; wholesale +10% [D21, printed p.29 and p.33]. "Our NIKE Direct business fueled our growth throughout the year as we navigated the pandemic" [D21, printed p.28].
- Wholesale partner reduction (key statement): "over the last three years we have significantly reduced the number of undifferentiated wholesale accounts. During fiscal 2021, we took further steps towards account and channel consolidation by reprioritizing product allocation to benefit NIKE Direct and our differentiated strategic wholesale customers. We expect that over the next two fiscal years, we will more aggressively accelerate these changes as we work to reprofile the shape of the marketplace" [D21, printed p.37].
- New goals through FY2025 include "Gross margin rate in the high 40s by fiscal 2025" [D21, printed p.28].
- Gross margin: +140 bps; "the favorable impact of growth in our higher margin NIKE Direct business, led by NIKE owned Digital, was more than offset by higher promotions in our factory stores during the first half of fiscal 2021" [D21, printed p.34]. EMEA GM -110 bps "primarily due to lower NIKE Direct margins" [p.38]; Greater China "lower NIKE Direct ASP due to higher levels of promotion to liquidate excess inventory through our factory stores" [p.39]; APLA +130 bps partly offset by "lower margin in our NIKE Direct business" [D21, printed p.40].
- Converse: DTC +24% (digital), wholesale +13% [D21, printed p.41].

### FY2022 (peak of CDA; "NIKE Brand Digital" label introduced)
- Definition change: NIKE Direct "comprised of both NIKE-owned retail stores and sales through our digital platforms (also referred to as 'NIKE Brand Digital')" [D22, printed p.1].
- Strategy: "creating the marketplace of the future ... leading with digital and our owned stores, as well as select wholesale partners that share our marketplace vision"; "we have grown our NIKE Direct business to be approximately 42% of total NIKE Brand revenues for fiscal 2022, and we have reduced the number of wholesale accounts globally" [D22, printed p.28].
- Mix: NIKE Brand Digital $10.7bn, +18%; comps +10%; NIKE Direct +15% c-n; wholesale -1% [D22, printed p.29 and p.33]. Greater China NIKE Direct -12% (digital -15%, comps -14%) amid "marketplace dynamics" [D22, printed p.39].
- Gross margin: +120 bps "primarily due to higher margin in our NIKE Direct business, a higher mix of full-price sales on a wholesale equivalent basis" [D22, printed p.34]. EMEA +570 bps "primarily due to higher NIKE Direct margins" [p.38]; Greater China -390 bps incl. "lower NIKE Direct margins" [p.39]; APLA +400 bps incl. NIKE Direct margin and mix [D22, printed p.40]. Outlook: Q1 FY2023 GM "could be negatively impacted by increased promotional activity to sell seasonal product arriving late" [D22, printed p.28].
- Converse: DTC +22% "led by strong digital demand"; wholesale -4% (China marketplace dynamics); GM +360 bps on "higher margins in direct to consumer" [D22, printed p.41].

### FY2023 (CDA continues; inventory liquidation through NIKE Direct)
- Strategy unchanged in wording: "leading with digital and our owned stores, as well as select wholesale partners" [D23, printed p.28]. The MD&A overview now says "wholesale accounts" instead of "retail accounts" [D23, printed p.28]; Item 1 keeps "retail accounts" until the FY2024 10-K.
- Mix: NIKE Direct $21.3bn, about 44% of NIKE Brand [D23, printed p.28]; NIKE Brand Digital $12.6bn (later recast to $12.4bn), +24%; comps +14%; NIKE Direct +20% c-n [D23, printed p.33]. Greater China digital -4% [D23, printed p.39].
- Gross margin: -250 bps; channel driver: "Lower margin in our NIKE Direct business, driven by higher promotional activity to liquidate inventory" [D23, printed p.34]; Nike "took action to reduce excess inventory by decreasing future inventory purchases and increasing promotional activity" [D23, printed p.28]. North America GM -310 bps incl. "lower margins in NIKE Direct due to higher promotional activity" [D23, printed p.37]. No bps split for the channel item (bridge was a chart).
- CASA distributor transition completed ("Now that we have completed the shift from a wholesale and direct to consumer operating model to a distributor model") [D23, printed p.29].
- Converse: DTC +8%, wholesale +7%; GM +50 bps despite "lower margins in direct to consumer" [D23, printed p.41].

### FY2024 (pivot language: re-investing in wholesale begins under CEO Donahoe)
- Strategy: "We are focused on growing the entire marketplace by continuing to invest in our NIKE Direct operations while also increasing investment to elevate and differentiate our brand experience within our wholesale partners" [D24, printed p.30]. Consumer Direct Acceleration is no longer named in MD&A.
- Mix: NIKE Direct $21.5bn, about 44% [D24, printed p.30]; NIKE Brand Digital $12.1bn vs recast $12.4bn, -3% "reflecting reduced digital traffic"; comps +3%; wholesale +2% c-n [D24, printed p.30 and p.35]. Product lifecycle supply cuts hurt "specifically NIKE Brand Digital revenues in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2024" [D24, printed p.31].
- Gross margin bridge now in text with bps: +110 bps total; full-price ASP +200 bps; "Lower margin in our NIKE Direct business (decreasing gross margin approximately 40 basis points)"; lower off-price margin -20 bps; FX -40 bps [D24, printed p.36].
- Wholesale-equivalent sunset announced: "the Company will replace wholesale equivalent revenues and gross margin drivers with a comparable U.S. GAAP metric" from FY2025 [D24, printed p.32].
- Regional: North America wholesale -2% "primarily reflecting liquidation of excess inventory in the prior year" [p.39]; Greater China wholesale +15% [p.41]; APLA GM -220 bps incl. "lower margin in NIKE Direct" [D24, printed p.42].
- Converse: wholesale -16%, DTC -14%; ASP -3% "primarily driven by increased promotional activity in direct to consumer"; GM -130 bps [D24, printed p.43].

### FY2025 (new CEO Elliott Hill from October 2024: wholesale reinvestment, digital reset)
- Leadership: Hill "has served as President and Chief Executive Officer of NIKE, Inc. since October 2024" [D25, printed p.8].
- Strategy: "Marketplace Management: Repositioning NIKE Brand Digital as a full-price platform and reinvesting in wholesale distribution. This includes liquidating inventory through increased markdowns across NIKE Direct, and higher sales returns and discounts with our wholesale partners to reduce inventory and create capacity for new product" [D25, printed p.30]. Two channels are now named explicitly: "We sell our products through two distribution channels" [D25, printed p.29].
- Mix: NIKE Direct $18.8bn, -13% reported, about 42% of NIKE Brand [D25, printed p.29]; NIKE Brand Digital $9.6bn, -20%, "while NIKE store sales were flat"; comps -1% [D25, printed p.34]; wholesale -6% c-n [D25, printed p.29]. Digital fell in every geography (NA -19%, EMEA -30%, Greater China -22%, APLA -9%) [D25, printed p.38 to p.41].
- Gross margin: -190 bps "primarily due to higher discounts, changes in channel mix and higher inventory obsolescence reserves" [D25, printed p.29]; bridge: NIKE Brand ASP -180 bps "primarily due to higher discounts and changes in channel mix"; other costs -90 bps; Converse -20 bps; product cost +80 bps; warehousing and logistics +20 bps [D25, printed p.35]. Note the reversal of sign: channel mix toward wholesale is now a margin headwind (lower ASP), where NIKE Direct mix was a tailwind in FY2019 to FY2022.
- Converse: wholesale -20%, DTC -17%; "Lower ASP per unit primarily reflects higher discounts in direct to consumer"; GM -380 bps [D25, printed p.43].

### FY2026 (wholesale-led recovery in North America; NIKE Direct shrinking)
- Strategy repeated ("Repositioning NIKE Brand Digital as a full-price platform and reinvesting in wholesale distribution"), plus "We are also making investments to elevate the presentation of our brands in physical retail"; actions to be completed "by the end of December 2026"; "North America has made the most progress against these actions, while Greater China and Converse will take more time" [D26, printed p.30]. Cost realignment "to serve an integrated marketplace" [D26, printed p.30].
- Mix: NIKE Brand wholesale $27.5bn, +6% reported, +4% c-n; NIKE Direct $17.7bn (-8% c-n); NIKE Brand Digital $8.6bn (-12%); NIKE store sales $9.1bn (-4%); comps -4% [D26, printed p.29 and p.33]. NIKE Direct share of NIKE Brand is not stated (derived 39%, section C).
- North America wholesale +14% "including expanded distribution, higher shipments to existing partners and fewer marketplace management actions" [D26, printed p.37]. Greater China wholesale -14% "reflecting our actions to prioritize marketplace health" and digital -29% [D26, printed p.39]; Greater China faces "a trend of declining store traffic, elevated promotional activity and higher levels of inventory across the marketplace" [D26, printed p.30].
- Gross margin: +20 bps; "Lower warehousing and logistics costs (increasing gross margin approximately 20 basis points), primarily due to channel mix" [D26, printed p.34]; footwear "Lower ASP per pair was primarily due to channel mix" [D26, printed p.33]. North America GM +210 bps incl. "lower warehousing and logistics costs driven by channel mix" [D26, printed p.37]. So the wholesale shift now helps fulfillment cost but hurts ASP.
- Converse: "in the midst of a strategic reset of the brand and marketplace" [D26, printed p.30]; wholesale -33%, DTC -33%; GM -490 bps; "Lower ASP primarily reflects higher discounts and channel mix" [D26, printed p.42].
- Wholesale concentration is rising: top three U.S. customers 29% of U.S. sales (vs 21% in FY2024) [D26, printed p.2]; risk factor reworded to "has increased and may continue to increase", citing "attrition as struggling retailers exit the market" [D26, printed p.16].

## C. Definition changes, caveats and derived figures

Definition and presentation changes
1. Digital label: FY2019 to FY2021 10-Ks say "digital commerce sales" (FY2020 overview also "NIKE Brand digital"; FY2021 overview "NIKE Brand digital revenues grew 60%" [D21, printed p.28]). From the FY2022 10-K the term is "NIKE Brand Digital" [D22, printed p.1]; comps definition changed from "exclude digital commerce sales" [D19, printed p.28] to "exclude NIKE Brand Digital sales" [D22, printed p.33].
2. FY2024 reclassification: "there were certain reclassifications made between NIKE-owned retail stores and NIKE Brand Digital in the prior period" [D24, printed p.35]. Effect: FY2023 NIKE Brand Digital was $12.6bn as first reported [D23, printed p.33] and $12.4bn as recast [D24, printed p.35]. The 10-K does not say the reclass happened in Q4 FY2024 or give a store-side figure; treat FY2023 digital and store numbers as two vintages.
3. Store sales disclosure: FY2025 10-K first gives NIKE store sales growth ("NIKE store sales were flat") [D25, printed p.34]; FY2026 10-K first gives store dollars ($9.1bn FY2026, $9.2bn FY2025) [D26, printed p.33].
4. Wholesale equivalent: defined as "(1) sales to external wholesale customers and (2) internal sales from our wholesale operations to our NIKE Direct operations, which are charged at prices comparable to those charged to external wholesale customers", intended "to provide context as to the total size of our NIKE Brand market footprint if we had no NIKE Direct operations" [D19, printed p.25; same wording D20 p.29, D21 p.29, D23 p.30, D24 p.32]. Internal sales to NIKE Direct: $5,616m FY2017, $6,332m FY2018, $7,127m FY2019, $7,452m FY2020, $9,872m FY2021, $10,543m FY2022, $12,730m FY2023, $13,009m FY2024 (CSV rows). Announced for removal in D24 p.32; removed in FY2025 ("we have removed the non-GAAP financial measure of wholesale equivalent revenues. There is no change to our reported revenues or gross margin") [D25, printed p.33]. No internal-sales figure exists for FY2025 or FY2026, and FY2025 recast the Men's/Women's/Kids' tables to reported (not wholesale equivalent) revenue.
5. Gross margin bridges for FY2019 to FY2023 were charts ("*Wholesale equivalent" footnote only survives in the text); only narrative drivers are available, no bps per driver. From FY2024 the drivers carry bps in text. Segment GM bps are text in all years.
6. "Retail accounts" became "wholesale accounts" (D23 MD&A, D24 Item 1), and "thousands" was dropped in D25 and D26. CDA/CDO names disappear from MD&A after FY2023.
7. Store table caveats: in-line counts include employee-only stores; Converse counts include Converse factory stores; Hurley stores (29 U.S.) appear only in FY2019 (Hurley sold December 2019). The tables are not dated in the text; treat as fiscal year-end. Item 2 leased store count is a separate measure (FY2026 "approximately 980").
8. The revenue note's "Sales through Direct to Consumer" row includes Converse DTC (e.g., FY2026 NIKE Brand 17,720 plus Converse 477 = 18,197) [D26, printed p.83]; MD&A "Sales through NIKE Direct" is NIKE Brand only.
9. Comparable store sales in FY2020 to FY2022 include stores temporarily closed for COVID-19 (stated in D20 p.32, D21 p.33, D22 p.33, D23 p.30); the clause is dropped from D24 onward.
10. Distributor transitions (Brazil FY2021; Chile, Argentina, Uruguay FY2023) moved revenue out of both NIKE Direct and wholesale into distributor sales (reported as wholesale), lowering APLA growth (e.g., about 5 points in FY2023 [D23, printed p.40]).
11. Not found in any of the eight 10-Ks: member counts, app metrics, NIKE Digital active users, door counts, number of wholesale accounts exited, Greater China partner-operated or franchise store counts (only the FY2020 phrase "NIKE-owned and partner stores"), FY2017 Consumer Direct Offense announcement text, and any explicit bps for "NIKE Direct mix" before FY2024.
