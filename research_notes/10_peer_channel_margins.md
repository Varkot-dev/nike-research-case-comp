# Peer evidence: DTC vs wholesale margins, and retailer gross margins

Prepared Oct 13, 2026 for the Nike pitch. Hypothesis tested: "DTC only earns a higher margin than wholesale when sold at full price; heavy DTC discounting erases the advantage."

**Sourcing notes**
- www.sec.gov returned HTTP 403 to requests without a contact User-Agent. No User-Agent was invented and no email address was used. Filings were read from annual report PDFs on the annualreports.com mirror (these PDFs contain the full Form 10-K), company IR sites, FCA National Storage Mechanism copies (JD Sports) and, where marked "via WebFetch", the sec.gov page as returned by the WebFetch tool. WebFetch quotes were extracted by the tool and were not machine-checked against the page; treat them as one step less verified than the PDF quotes.
- "p." is the printed page in the filing footer unless marked "PDF p." (page counted in the PDF file).
- Anything computed here is labelled **derived**. Management statements are labelled opinion where they are forward-looking or interpretive.
- Gross margin (GM) for brands excludes most DTC operating costs (store rent and staff, e-commerce fulfilment, digital marketing). Skechers puts store operating costs and distribution in G&A; Deckers leaves warehouse and DC costs unallocated. Keep this in mind for every GM comparison below.

---

## (a) Summary: do peers support or contradict the hypothesis?

1. **At the gross margin line, peer data contradict the strong form of the hypothesis.** Skechers, the only peer that reports GM by channel, earned a DTC GM 16.7 to 29.6 points above wholesale in every year FY2019 to H1 2025. That includes FY2019, when its DTC GM was only 59.6% against 42.9% for wholesale (derived) and the filing cites promotional activity in international markets. On FY2024 figures (DTC 66.2%, wholesale 43.3%), DTC prices would have to fall about 40% at the same unit cost before DTC GM dropped to the wholesale rate (**derived**: 1 minus 33.8/56.7). On FY2019 figures the break-even discount is about 29%.
2. **Discounting moves DTC GM by single-digit points, not enough to close the gap.** Skechers DTC GM rose 590 bps in FY2021, "primarily driven by reduced promotional activity and the increase in average selling price per unit". Under Armour attributed about 130 bps of FY2024 gross margin headwind to inventory actions "including increased promotional activities within our direct-to-consumer channel", and an 80 bp FY2025 tailwind to lower DTC discounting (via WebFetch). Foot Locker lost 290 bps in 2020 and 420 bps in 2023 to promotions. Each of these swings is 1 to 6 points, well below Skechers' 17 to 30 point GM gap.
3. **At operating margin, the evidence supports a weaker version of the hypothesis.** Deckers reported operating income by segment, with intersegment transfers to DTC at cost, so the DTC segment keeps the full brand-plus-retail margin. On that basis DTC earned a **lower** operating margin than wholesale in FY2018 to FY2020 (21.9% to 25.9% vs 25.7% to 29.8%, gap minus 3.8 to minus 5.0 points, **derived**). In FY2021 to FY2024 DTC earned a **higher** one (32.8% to 40.3% vs 27.1% to 30.6%, gap plus 2.2 to plus 10.1 points, **derived**). Deckers credited "higher-full priced selling driven by less discounting and promotional activity" in FY2019 and "higher full-price sell through" in FY2024. The DTC segment excludes unallocated warehouse and DC costs, so its true gap is somewhat smaller than shown.
4. **Management says the GM advantage is partly offset by opex (opinion).** PUMA's CFO, Q1 2025 consensus aide-memoire: channel mix "is basically a swap between the OpEx and the gross profit margin side on DTC versus Wholesale." Deckers (FY2025 10-K): "We expect increased sales in the DTC channel will continue to positively impact our gross margins." On (FY2024 annual report): GM increase "mainly driven by higher DTC sales as a percentage of net sales". No peer reports DTC opex by channel, except Deckers' segment operating income.
5. **Retail partners keep a GM of roughly 28% to 38% of their own net sales (US), or 47% to 49% under UK cost classification.** Foot Locker: 31.8% (2019), 28.9% (2020), 34.4% (2021), 31.9% (2022), 27.7% (2023), 28.9% (2024), 27.8% in H1 2025 (**derived**). Dick's: 29.19% (FY2019), 31.83%, 38.33%, 34.64%, 34.92%, 35.90% (FY2024), and 32.92% in FY2025 including Foot Locker. The Foot Locker Business earned 24.43% under Dick's. JD Sports: 47.0% to 49.1%, but JD books store costs below gross profit. US retailer GMs include occupancy and fulfilment costs, so they understate the share of the shelf price the retailer keeps. Skechers' FY2024 DTC-minus-wholesale GM gap (22.9 points, **derived**) is close to Foot Locker's 28.9% FY2024 GM. That is consistent with DTC recapturing most of what the retailer keeps, before DTC operating costs.
6. **Nike dependence at the two US partners.** Nike's share of Foot Locker purchases fell from 75% (2020) to 59% (2024). Nike's share of Dick's purchases rose from 17% (FY2021) to 25% (FY2024), and was 31% of the combined Dick's plus Foot Locker purchases in FY2025. **Net:** the data do not support "heavy DTC discounting erases the GM advantage" for any peer disclosed. They do support "DTC only beats wholesale on **operating** margin when full-price sell-through is high and the store and fulfilment base is absorbed" (Deckers FY2018 to FY2020 vs FY2021 to FY2024). Filings cannot separate how much of Deckers' flip came from less discounting and how much came from e-commerce mix and store closures. Deckers cites all three.

### 2. Deckers (DECK: UGG, HOKA), segment **operating** margin, DTC vs wholesale

It moved to brand segments in Q4 FY2025 ("the CODM shifted resource allocation decisions and performance assessment to a brand focus, rather than a distribution channel focus", FY2025 10-K p.3). Basis (FY2024 10-K p.F-38): "Inter-segment sales from the Company's wholesale reportable operating segments to the DTC reportable operating segment are at the Company's cost, and there is no inter-segment profit". Unallocated overhead includes "unallocable overhead costs associated with the Company's warehouses and DCs". Wholesale = sum of the five brand-wholesale segments (**derived**). ## (d) Verbatim quotes: DTC promotions or discounting hurting margin

1. Skechers FY2021 10-K p.19: "Direct-to-Consumer gross margin increased 590 basis points to 66.5%, primarily driven by reduced promotional activity and the increase in average selling price per unit." (The mirror image: promotional activity had depressed DTC GM in FY2020.)
2. Skechers FY2024 10-K p.22: "Direct-to-Consumer gross margin increased 20 basis points to 66.2% due to favorable channel mix and lower cost per unit, partially offset by a decrease in average selling prices."
3. Under Armour FY2024 10-K pp.31-32 (via WebFetch): "unfavorable impacts of approximately 130 basis points from proactive inventory management actions, including increased promotional activities within our direct-to-consumer channel".
4. Under Armour FY2025 10-K p.31 (via WebFetch): "approximately 80 basis points of pricing benefits largely due to lower levels of discounting and promotions within our direct-to-consumer channel."
5. adidas Annual Report 2020: "a less favorable pricing mix due to increased promotional activity and negative currency fluctuations weighed on the development in 2020." In the same sentence: "a more favorable channel mix due to the exceptional e-commerce growth ... had a positive effect on gross margin". In 2020 the channel-mix benefit and the promotion drag both showed up.
6. Deckers FY2019 10-K p.39: gross margin rose "due to higher-full priced selling driven by less discounting and promotional activity".
7. Deckers FY2023 10-K p.39: "The increase in income from operations of the DTC channel was due to higher global net sales ... at lower gross margins, as well as lower DTC SG&A expenses as a percentage of net sales."
8. JD Sports FY26 results: "the Group made controlled price investments, particularly in the online offer ... The underlying impact of these investments on our gross margin % (approximately -30bps, net) was offset by higher marketing contributions". (Retailer's own online channel.)
9. Foot Locker 2020 10-K p.24 (retailer's own direct channel): "the higher portion of direct-to-customer sales, which bear higher freight costs."
10. PUMA Q2 2025 release p.2: "increased promotional activity and unfavourable currency effects. This was partially offset by ... a positive impact from distribution channel mix."
