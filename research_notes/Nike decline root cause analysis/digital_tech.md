# Root cause test: were digital and technology failures a driver of Nike's decline?

Research date: 2026-10-13. Hypothesis under test: *Digital and technology failures (Nike app and SNKRS engagement, the costly DTC tech stack, data-driven merchandising algorithms, NIKE Direct digital declines) were a driver of the decline.*

Status labels: **OPENED** = I read the page, filing or transcript myself in this session. **OPENED (repo)** = opened by an earlier researcher and recorded in this repo; reused, not re-fetched. **SNIPPET** = seen only in a search-result summary. Filing numbers come from `data/nike_filing_facts.csv`, `research_notes/04_financial_data_pack.md`, or 10-K / 10-Q text I grepped from local EDGAR copies in this session (printed page from the "20xx FORM 10-K nn" footer that follows the quote). Earnings call quotes are from Nike's official transcript PDFs (investors.nike.com) saved locally by earlier sessions, read by me in this session. All arithmetic is mine and labelled.

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: symptom of another cause. A pure technology failure is not supported as an independent revenue driver; the tech stack is a modest contributing cause on the cost side.**

The split matters, because the hypothesis bundles two different things:

1. **"NIKE Direct digital declines" are real and very large.** NIKE Brand Digital fell from $12.1B (FY2024) to $9.6B (FY2025) to $8.6B (FY2026), about -$3.5B, roughly 70% of the $4,964M fall in NIKE, Inc. revenue (FY24 10-K p. 35; FY25 10-K p. 34; FY26 10-K p. 33; my arithmetic). Over the same two years Nike-owned stores fell only from about $9.4B (derived) to $9.1B.
2. **But the evidence says the digital decline was caused by what was sold on the platform and how it was priced and promoted, not by the platform's technology.** Every discriminating test points the same way:
   - **Timing:** the systems were built FY2020 to FY2023, and on those same systems digital grew 60%, 18% and 24% (FY21 to FY23). Member engagement was still "up double-digits" in Sept 2023. Digital turned negative only in Q3 FY2024, when classic franchises (AF1, Dunk, AJ1), which had "their highest share of business" on Nike Digital, faded.
   - **The natural experiment that kills the tech story:** North America and EMEA run on the same global Nike app and nike.com. In each region the digital-versus-stores gap opens and closes with promotion and classics actions, not with any tech event. In North America the gap was 28 points in Q4 FY2025 (digital -25%, stores +3%) when promotional days went "from over 30 ... to zero". It closed to nothing by Q4 FY2026 (digital -5%, stores -7%) and Q1 FY2027 (both -6%). In EMEA, when Nike turned promotions back on in Q3 FY2026, digital (-6%) beat stores (-20%). When EMEA lapped those promotions in Q1 FY2027, digital fell 26% while stores fell 1%.
   - **The platform still sets records when the product is hot:** "NIKE.com posted its best Black Friday ever" (Q2 FY26), "demand on the Nike App grow in Q3 in North America" (Q3 FY26), and the Mercurial "became the fastest-selling 24-hour launch for cleated footwear in the history of NIKE Direct" (Q4 FY26). A broken stack cannot do this.
   - **Peer control:** adidas's e-commerce grew 25% and 27% currency-neutral in Q1 and Q2 2026, in line with its own retail (+19%, +23%). Its channels move together because demand drives both. Nike's channels diverge exactly where Nike chose to withdraw discounts.
   - **Management never cites a system failure.** No filing or call from FY2024 to Q1 FY2027 blames an app outage, conversion problem, ERP cutover or algorithm. They blame traffic, classics, promotions, paid media and launch volume, and say the FY2026 double-digit digital traffic decline was planned ("we expect digital traffic to be down double digits in Fiscal 26", Mar 2025).
3. **Where tech genuinely hurt: cost.** Nike's 10-Ks cite "strategic technology investments" as a main driver of operating overhead growth in FY2021, FY2022 and FY2023, when overhead rose from $9,534M to $12,317M. In Mar 2026 CFO Friend said those pandemic-era supply chain and technology investments "resulted in a higher fixed cost base that weighed significantly on our EBIT margins as revenue came down." This led to the Q3 FY2026 $230M severance charge, "primarily in supply chain and technology", the Apr 2026 cuts of about 1,400 jobs (mostly tech), and the merger of the SNKRS and Nike App engineering teams. This is a margin story, and a modest one (section 6).
4. **"Data-driven merchandising algorithms"** caused the classics glut only in a loose sense. The best evidence is SNKRS. Nike's own Oct 2021 data showed it met only 7% of SNKRS demand and had "fairness" scores in the "mid-20s". Management answered with "exclusivity at scale" (500,000 pairs of one Off-White Dunk release), which fed the classics oversupply. That was a merchandising and supply decision taken in response to digital data, not an algorithmic failure. No source documents an algorithm that told Nike to over-build classics. Claims that it did are commentary (Giunco, Modern Retail sources, Substack posts).

**Upstream cause:** the Consumer Direct Acceleration model (lead with NIKE Digital, feed it with high-volume classics and paid traffic, then clear the excess with promotions), plus the product cycle turning against those classics. The digital collapse is how that model unwound. It is not an independent tech failure.

## 3. Evidence for the hypothesis (strongest first)

1. **Digital is where the revenue went.** NIKE Brand Digital $12.4B FY23 (restated; $12.6B as first reported) to $12.1B FY24 to $9.6B FY25 to $8.6B FY26 (FY24 10-K p. 35; FY25 10-K p. 34; FY26 10-K p. 33). Q1 FY27 digital $1.8B vs $2.1B (-13% currency-neutral) while stores were $2.3B vs $2.4B (-5%) (Q1 FY27 10-Q). Digital is about 70% of the FY24 to FY26 revenue decline (my arithmetic).
2. **The tech build-out was large and is being written down in headcount.** Overhead growth was explicitly attributed to strategic technology investments for three straight years (FY21 to FY23 10-Ks). Friend (Q3 FY26): "During the pandemic, we accelerated investments across supply chain and technology to support a larger digital and direct business. Those investments also resulted in a higher fixed cost base that weighed significantly on our EBIT margins as revenue came down." $230M of severance in Q3 FY26, "primarily in supply chain and technology." FY26 10-K p. 30: "realigning costs across our supply chain and technology to serve an integrated marketplace." Pace (Oct 2026): about $2.5B cumulative savings through FY31, including "a new campus in India" (data pack).
3. **The tech organization was unstable.** A leaked 2022 survey had tech staff rating Nike below the rest of the company on 48 of 51 items (Business Insider, May to Jun 2022, SNIPPET via businessinsider.in / .nl). The first Chief Digital Information Officer, Ratnakar Lavu (hired 2019), left abruptly in Feb 2023 (Retail Dive, Feb 22, 2023, OPENED). Tech layoffs came in Nov 2023 (digital product division, OPENED (repo) org_talent.md) and May 2025 (a "limited number" on Global Tech as work moved "to managed services", Retail Dive May 22, 2025, OPENED). Then the CTO role (Muge Dogan) was eliminated Dec 2, 2025 (8-K, repo), about 1,400 mostly tech roles were cut in Apr 2026 (repo), and SNKRS engineering was "combined" with the Nike App team in May 2026 (Complex and House of Heat, May 9, 2026, OPENED).
4. **SNKRS showed the scarcity model breaking early.** Oct 2021 internal data: SNKRS demand $1.69B, Nike met 7% of it, fairness in the "mid-20s" against a target of 80; "We are at risk of losing our most sneaker-obsessed consumer", with defectors moving to New Balance and independents (Complex, Oct 11, 2021, OPENED). Nike's response, releasing 500,000 pairs via Exclusive Access ("exclusivity at scale"), is one of the documented roots of the Dunk and classics oversupply that followed (see franchise_oversupply.md).
5. **The digital platform became a discount channel.** "Entering the year, our digital platforms were delivering roughly a 50-50 split of full price to promotional sales" (Friend, Q2 FY25 call, OPENED). Hill: "Prioritizing NIKE Digital revenue has impacted the health of our marketplaces" (Q2 FY25). Hill (Q3 FY26): "For years, we were running a NIKE Direct first offense."
6. **China's digital channel has been broken for years.** GC digital -15% FY22 (FY22 10-K p. 39), -8% FY24, -22% FY25, -29% FY26 (FY26 10-K p. 39), -28% Q1 FY27 (Q1 FY27 10-Q). Hill (Q1 FY27): "we are activating a plan to clean up the digital side of this market ... around fewer, higher-quality experiences through the official NIKE flagship storefronts on Tmall, JD and Douyin alongside NIKE.com and the NIKE app ... We expect our digital cleanup to take multiple seasons."

## 4. Evidence against (strongest first)

1. **North America convergence (natural experiment).** Same app, same site, same stack. The digital gap versus Nike stores went from -28 points (Q4 FY25) to +2 (Q4 FY26: digital -5%, stores -7%) to 0 (Q1 FY27: both -6%, Q1 FY27 10-Q). No tech rebuild happened in between. What changed was promotion days, markdowns, classics supply and paid media. Friend, Q3 FY26: "on the digital side, in North America, we saw continued improvement in the gap between wholesale and direct ... We saw demand on the Nike App grow in Q3 in North America."
2. **EMEA flips sign with promotions.** Q2 FY26 digital -2% vs stores -5%, with Cyber Week promotions. Q3 FY26 digital -6% vs stores -20%, when "We were also more aggressive with promotions on NIKE Digital at the end of the season, which resulted in higher markdowns and a higher off-price mix." Q1 FY27 digital -26% vs stores -1% (Q1 FY27 10-Q), lapping those promotions. Friend flags a "400 basis points" Q2 FY27 headwind partly from lapping "Cyber Week in Europe." Digital volume is a function of price.
3. **The reset was planned, quantified in advance, and self-imposed.** Friend, Mar 20, 2025 (Q3 FY25): "we are reducing promotional days, reducing markdown rates, and shifting closeout liquidation to our Nike Factory stores. Due to these actions, and as we continue to reduce investment in paid media, we expect digital traffic to be down double digits in Fiscal 26." "Nike Digital in North America went from over 30 promotional days to zero" (Jan to Feb 2025 vs 2024). Q1 FY26: "reducing the number of days of site-wide promotion by more than 50"; "We've reduced the launch share of business and we pulled back on paid media as it was largely driving bottom of funnel traffic to our platforms." FY25 10-K p. 30 lists "Repositioning NIKE Brand Digital as a full-price platform" as a deliberate management action.
4. **Before the reset, the decline was a product decline that promotions could not stop.** Q4 FY24 (Friend, Jun 27, 2024): "Although our digital business has grown at an approximately 26% CAGR since Fiscal 19, we missed our Q4 plan on softer traffic, higher promotions, and lower sales of certain classic footwear franchises ... we are aggressively adjusting our forward-looking plans for these franchises on Nike Digital, where they have their highest share of business." Q1 FY25: classics "down nearly 50% versus the prior year on NIKE Digital while we saw much better sales trends in wholesale." Higher promotions alongside lower traffic is a demand signal, not a platform signal.
5. **The platform still sets records when given product and moments.** "NIKE.com posted its best Black Friday ever this year, partially driven by strong sell-through of the Jordan Black Cat launch" (Q2 FY26). The Mercurial "became the fastest-selling 24-hour launch for cleated footwear in the history of NIKE Direct" (Q4 FY26). A'ja Wilson's "first launch sold out in 3 minutes on NIKE Digital in North America" (Q4 FY25). During the Paris Olympics opening week (Jul 26 to Aug 1, 2024), Nike's DTC site visits rose while adidas, Hoka and On fell; nike.com visits peaked at 2 million on Jul 31 (Similarweb via Reuters / Fast Company, Aug 7, 2024, OPENED via web.archive.org). Traffic follows product and sport moments.
6. **Category pattern is product, not platform.** Running grew double digits on NIKE Digital (Q2 FY26) while classics collapsed on the same platform. nike.com's running-category traffic decline from Mar 2022 to Mar 2024 (Similarweb via Footwear News, May 17, 2024, OPENED) mirrors Nike's running share loss in physical run specialty, so it is a product signal visible online, not an online failure.
7. **Timing.** The stack was built and scaled during Nike's best digital years (FY20 to FY23). The China ERP went live in 2022 (Supply Chain Dive, Jul 11, 2022, OPENED); GC digital then grew +6% in Q1 FY24 before falling. The NA retail ERP went live in Q1 FY24 and "everything has gone well" (Donahoe, Q1 FY24). No disruption from the NA wholesale go-live (summer 2024) appears in any call, filing or search result I found. Contrast Nike's 2001 i2 demand-planning failure, which Nike blamed publicly for about $100M of lost sales (CIO, SNIPPET). Nike does disclose tech failures when they happen.
8. **Peer contrast in the right direction.** adidas's e-com and own retail grow together (Q1 2026 +25% / +19%; Q2 2026 +27% / +23%, currency-neutral, adidas results releases, OPENED earlier in this session from local copies). Where demand is healthy, channels move together; Nike's divergence is created by Nike's own channel policy.
9. **Overhead did not cause the FY24 to FY26 EBIT fall in absolute terms.** Overhead fell from $12,291M to $11,360M while EBIT fell $2,689M (6,539 to 3,850; data pack). The EBIT fall came from gross profit (-$2,976M). The tech cost story is about failing to fall *fast enough*, not about rising.

## 5. Independent cause or symptom?

**Symptom.** The causal chain the evidence supports:

1. CDO / Consumer Direct Acceleration (2017 to 2023) made NIKE Digital the lead channel, cut wholesale accounts and centralised marketing into performance media (see dtc_wholesale.md, marketing_shift.md).
2. To feed the platform, Nike leaned on high-volume classics (AF1, Dunk, AJ1) and responded to SNKRS scarcity complaints with "exclusivity at scale", so supply rose (franchise_oversupply.md).
3. The tech, fulfilment and supply chain stack was scaled for a much larger digital business, inflating fixed costs (P8).
4. When classics faded (from late FY2024), Nike Digital, the channel most exposed to them, lost traffic first. Promotions and paid media propped it up, until roughly half of digital sales were promotional.
5. Hill's reset (from Q2 FY25) deliberately removed promo days, paid media, classics volume and launch share from the platform. Digital revenue fell by design, part of the demand moved to wholesale (NA wholesale +14% in FY26, FY26 10-K p. 37) and part was lost.
6. With a smaller digital business, the stack became over-sized, leading to the tech layoffs, the merger of the SNKRS and Nike App teams, the end of the CTO role and Pace.

The tech stack is downstream of the strategy at both ends: it was built *because* of the DTC strategy and dismantled *because* the strategy was reversed. There is no point in the chain where a technology malfunction changes the outcome.

## 6. Contribution estimate

**Revenue (FY2024 to FY2026, -$4,964M; Q1 FY27 -4%):**
- NIKE Brand Digital fell about $3.5B. Had digital tracked Nike's own stores (-3%), it would have fallen about $0.36B. So about $3.1B is digital-channel-specific (my arithmetic). That $3.1B is explained by management actions and product: removal of promotions and markdowns (digital was about 50% promotional), the paid media cut, cuts to classics allocation and launch share, China digital clean-up, and reallocation to wholesale (where a pair earns roughly half the revenue). These belong to the DTC-unwind, classics-oversupply, marketing-shift and China hypotheses, not to a tech failure.
- **Residual attributable to technology itself (app quality, platform failure, algorithms): indistinguishable from zero.** I put it at 0 to 5% of the revenue decline (0 to about $0.25B). The upper bound covers the possibility that a weaker app experience after the SNKRS cuts, and slow adaptation to AI and agentic shopping (newly flagged as a risk in the FY26 10-K), cost some traffic. No data measures either.
- **"Digital" as a channel label (the over-built, promotion-dependent NIKE Digital business): about 60 to 70% of the revenue decline runs through it.** That is the *location* of the decline, not its *cause*.

**Profit (EBIT -$2,689M FY24 to FY26):**
- Overhead rose from 22.9% of revenue (FY19) to 24.5% (FY26), about $0.75B of excess at FY26 revenue (all causes, my arithmetic). Had overhead fallen in line with revenue from FY24, FY26 overhead would have been about $11.1B vs the actual $11.36B, a gap of about $0.26B (my arithmetic). The technology share of that is undisclosed. Friend says the tech and supply chain fixed cost "weighed significantly". Allowing tech a third to a half of the stickiness, the tech cost drag is about $0.1B to $0.4B a year: **roughly 5 to 15% of the EBIT decline**, plus restructuring charges of about $0.3B in FY26 that are partly tech.
- Cannot quantify more precisely: Nike does not disclose technology spend.

## 7. Can management fix it?

**Partly, and they are already doing it.**
- **Cost side: yes.** Tech and supply chain cost resets (Q3 FY26 charges, the Apr 2026 cuts, managed services, the India tech campus, Pace's $2.5B savings target through FY31) directly address the over-built stack. Benefits are expected "to begin in fiscal '27 and continue to build through fiscal '28" (Friend, Q3 FY26).
- **Revenue side: the digital channel cannot be fixed by technology,** because technology was not what broke. North America shows the channel stabilises once the promo reset is lapped (digital equal to stores by Q1 FY27). Growth then depends on product newness and brand. EMEA and China have not finished the reset (EMEA digital -26%, GC digital -28% in Q1 FY27), and China's clean-up of third-party platforms will take "multiple seasons."
- **Risk:** cutting SNKRS and digital engineering deeply while the 10-K warns about AI-enabled and agentic shopping (FY26 10-K risk factors) could create a genuine tech gap in the future. That is a forward risk, not a cause of the past decline.

## 8. What I could not verify

- App-level engagement (MAU, downloads, ratings) for the Nike app and SNKRS for 2023 to 2026. Sensor Tower and Apptopia series are paywalled; Nike stopped giving member numbers (its last figures were about 160M "active members" around FY2022, SNIPPET). An AltIndex estimate of about 15K downloads a day, down 30.8% over three months (SNIPPET, undated), is not usable.
- The "90% of SNKRS team" layoff figure is unconfirmed (one House of Heat source; Nike confirmed only the team merger).
- No Bloomberg, WSJ or BI investigation documenting a specific merchandising algorithm decision was found or opened. The "data-driven" critique of Donahoe rests on ex-employee and commentator quotes (Giunco via sfin.ro, OPENED (repo); Modern Retail, Fortune, SNIPPET).
- adidas FY2025 full-year e-commerce growth (+16%) is SNIPPET only.
- Nike's technology spend is not disclosed anywhere in the filings.
