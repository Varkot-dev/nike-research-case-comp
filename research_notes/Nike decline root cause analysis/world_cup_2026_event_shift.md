# Hypothesis: the FIFA World Cup 2026 shifted football and apparel spending to adidas in Nike's Q1 FY2027 window

Date of notes: 2026-10-13. Status: **NOT SUPPORTED.** The hypothesis was settled quickly because Nike's own Q1 FY2027 10-Q contradicts its core predictions, so I did not dig further than the decisive tests needed.

## Bottom line

Nike's Q1 FY2027 (June to August 2026) relapse is a **footwear, Greater China, Converse and NIKE Direct** problem. The categories and regions the World Cup would have hit (apparel, football, North America, Latin America) **grew** at Nike in the same quarter. Nike says its Global Football business grew "strong double-digit" in all four geographies and that it doubled World Cup team kit sales versus 2022. adidas did win the tournament commercially (Q2 2026 apparel +35%), but the pie grew for both. The World Cup was not taken from Nike.

## Evidence for (what is true)

- adidas Q2 2026 (April to June): currency-neutral revenue +14%, apparel +35%, footwear +1%; football growth tied to "highly successful FIFA World Cup 2026 collections"; North America +17%, Latin America +28% (adidas ad hoc release, July 30, 2026, Deutsche Boerse, OPENED earlier by the orchestrator; search result confirms headline, SNIPPET).
- adidas sold nearly 18M World Cup linked jerseys, Mexico leading, then Argentina (Gulden on Q2 call; MarketBeat / Nasdaq call highlights, Jul 30, 2026, SNIPPET). adidas outfits Mexico and Argentina; Nike's Mexico shelf would see some substitution.
- Nike Q1 FY27 APLA: "lower revenues in Japan, Korea and Mexico" (Q1 FY27 10-Q, APLA MD&A, OPENED).
- adidas spent about EUR 212M of extra marketing, mostly World Cup related, and operating profit rose only 5% (search summary of Q2 coverage, SNIPPET). So adidas bought this share; it is costly to sustain.

## Evidence against (decisive)

1. Nike apparel grew in every World Cup relevant region in Q1 FY27 (10-Q, OPENED).
2. Nike Global Football grew double digits in all four geographies, World Cup kits doubled vs 2022 (call transcript, OPENED).
3. North America, the host market, was Nike's only growing region (10-Q, OPENED).
4. The decline is concentrated in footwear (EMEA footwear -11% cn on ASP and units), Greater China (-26% cn), Converse (-28%) and digital (EMEA digital -26%), all with company-stated causes unrelated to football (10-Q and transcript, OPENED).
5. The deterioration predates the tournament (Q4 FY26 -4% cn).

## Is it independent or a symptom?

Neither, in Nike's numbers. The event is real for adidas but did not register as a Nike loss. The one residual (Mexico weakness) is too small to measure and may equally reflect Nike's own sportswear footwear problems in that market. The real upstream causes of the Q1 FY27 relapse are the franchise oversupply and lifestyle footwear fade (see franchise_oversupply.md), Greater China channel cleanup (china.md), and Converse (emea_converse_jordan.md).

## Size estimate

Upper bound: if the entire APLA reported decline ($27M) were World Cup substitution in Mexico, that is about 5% of the $507M Q1 FY27 decline and about 0.2% of quarterly revenue. Realistic: near zero net, because Nike's own football and apparel grew. Contribution to the multi-year FY24 to FY26 decline: none (the tournament came after it).

## Fixable by management?

Not applicable as a cause. Federation contracts (Mexico, Argentina are adidas) are long-dated; Nike's own World Cup execution was a positive.
