# Root cause test: are US mall-athletic partner destocking and door closures (Foot Locker under Dick's, Hibbett and DTLR under JD) a separate channel drag on Nike in H2 FY2026 and FY2027?

Research date: 2026-10-13. Status labels: **OPENED** = I read the filing or page myself in this session. **OPENED (repo)** = opened by an earlier researcher and recorded in this repo's notes; reused, not re-fetched. **SNIPPET** = seen only in a search-result summary. Nike channel numbers come from `data/nike_channel_split.csv` (10-Q and 10-K segment tables). All arithmetic is mine and labelled "my arithmetic"; all exposure estimates are labelled "estimate".

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: symptom of another cause, and minor in size.** The partner facts are real (OPENED in Dick's SEC filings below), but the testable predictions mostly fail:

1. **Nike North America wholesale did not slow when Foot Locker cleared and closed stores.** It grew +10.8%, +9.8% and +9.0% (reported, USD) in Q3 FY26, Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27, on top of +23.9% in Q2 FY26 (my arithmetic on the channel CSV). The "+4, +9, +3, +3, +2" series in the hypothesis is **total** North America revenue. The deceleration there comes from **NIKE Direct** (-2.8%, -9.9%, -4.5%, -6.4%, -6.0%), not wholesale.
2. **Nike's business with Foot Locker grew during the clean-out.** Hill, Q4 FY26 call (2026-06): "our revenue growth and retail sales comps with Foot Locker were positive for the first time in 4 years." That quarter (Mar to May 2026) came straight after Foot Locker's Q4 2025 inventory purge and during its first wave of 2026 closures.
3. **The peer control rules out a generic mall-channel drag.** In the same quarter Foot Locker comped -3.6% (May to Jul 2026), adidas North America grew +17% currency-neutral and Genesco's Journeys (a mall chain) comped +2%. The weakness follows **brand and silhouette mix**, not the channel.
4. **The partners themselves name an upstream cause.** Dick's 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-08-01 says inventory grew "particularly in certain legacy footwear silhouettes and apparel franchises that are not resonating with our athletes", and the Q2 release says Foot Locker "has greater exposure to legacy footwear silhouettes". That is the classics and Sportswear glut (see `franchise_oversupply.md`) passing through the partner's balance sheet.
5. **Door closures are small and mostly not Nike-heavy formats.** Since acquisition (2025-09-08), Foot Locker's North America owned stores fell from 1,637 to 1,531 (-106, -6.5%). Just under half of that (52) was **WSS**, a value family-footwear banner. Foot Locker, Champs and Kids Foot Locker together fell from 1,486 to 1,432 (-54, -3.6%) (my arithmetic on Dick's 10-Q store tables).

**Where the hypothesis does hold:** Nike says FY27 sell-in is being cut while partners work off aged Sportswear. Hill (Q1 FY27): aged Sportswear sell-through "has impacted our future order books as we proactively work with our wholesale partners to work through excess inventory." Foot Locker (-3.6% comp, FY guide cut) and Hibbett (Q2 LFL -8.4%) are the most exposed accounts. So partner destocking is a real **transmission channel** for FY27, but the thing being destocked is Nike's own over-supplied lifestyle product.

**Size (estimate):** about $0.1B to $0.3B of FY27 revenue from reduced Nike buys at Foot Locker North America and Hibbett. That is roughly 0.3% to 0.6% of FY26 revenue, or about 3% to 9% of the guided FY27 decline. The part that is truly **independent** (doors closed for real-estate or balance-sheet reasons, not to clear Nike product) is about $20M to $60M. Effectively zero in H2 FY26.

## 3. Evidence for the hypothesis

1. **Foot Locker's clean-out was large and real.** Dick's FY2025 10-K: $390.0M of acquisition charges in FY2025, "including $217.9 million of charges from the write-down and liquidation of inventory". A further $42.7M (Q1 2026) and $40.4M year to date by Q2 2026 (the Q2 10-Q figure is year to date) "to write down and liquidate inventory". Q3 FY25 release guided Foot Locker Q4 2025 gross margin down "1,000 to 1,500 basis points" with comps "down mid- to high-single digits".
2. **Doors are closing.** 67 Foot Locker stores identified in the review of unproductive assets; 110 Foot Locker Business closures in H1 2026 against 27 openings (DKS 10-Q, quarter ended 2026-08-01).
3. **Foot Locker is deteriorating again.** Proforma comp -3.6% in Q2 2026, FY2026 guide cut to -2% to 0% with a segment loss of $(80)M to $(40)M; Q2 segment loss $31.9M on $1.7B sales (DKS 10-Q).
4. **Hibbett is weak and very Nike-dependent.** Q2 LFL -8.4% (SGB, OPENED (repo)); Nike 73.7% of Hibbett purchases (FY2024 10-K).
5. **Nike's own guidance points to lower partner sell-in.** Order books impacted (Q1 FY27), buys tightened (Q4 FY26), Q2 FY27 NA sell-in comparison headwind.
6. **Headline NA wholesale growth overstates sell-in.** Friend (Q4 FY26): "we didn't sell in up 10%. There was a meaningful amount of that revenue growth that was associated with lower returns or sales-related reserves and returns, lower discount and lower cancellations." The 10-Qs attribute Q2 and Q3 FY26 NA wholesale growth partly to "marketplace management actions in both the current and prior year" (i.e., an easy base after Nike's FY25 returns and cancellations). Q1 FY26 was helped by "higher liquidation volume to value channels". So underlying full-price sell-in was weaker than +10%.

## 4. Evidence against (or that it is not an independent cause)

1. **No slowdown in NA wholesale** after the reset (P1). The drag in NA total is NIKE Direct.
2. **Foot Locker revenue grew for Nike** in Q4 FY26, the first time in four years (P2).
3. **No co-movement** between Foot Locker comps and Nike NA wholesale across six quarters (P3).
4. **Peers through the same doors grew** (adidas NA +17% c.n.; Journeys +2%) (P4).
5. **Partners blame legacy silhouettes**, i.e., Nike's own over-supplied classics, and Nike's own site promotions (P5).
6. **Closures are a continuation, not a shock.** Foot Locker announced about 400 closures in March 2023 (Retail Dive, OPENED (repo) in `dtc_wholesale.md`), long before the Dick's deal. The Dick's closures continue a multi-year shrink of low-productivity mall doors, and nearly half the NA closures are WSS.
7. **Nike-side caveat:** the Q4 FY26 NA wholesale +10% includes lower returns and reserves (Friend). Even so, any shortfall comes from Nike tightening supply of aged Sportswear, which is Nike's choice.

## 5. Independent cause or symptom?

**A symptom.** The causal chain is:

Nike over-supplied classics (AF1, Dunk, Jordan retro) through FY2022 to FY2024. Lifestyle demand then faded (Circana H1 2026: sport lifestyle down; Hill: "a lack of energy in the lifestyle space"). Inventory piled up at retailers most exposed to "legacy footwear silhouettes" (Foot Locker, Hibbett). Brands, Nike included, promoted on their own sites (Stack). Those partners comp negative and close weak doors. Nike cuts sell-in to let them clear.

The only independent link is the real-estate pruning of low-productivity doors. That would happen with or without Nike's product problem, but it is small (section 6). Foot Locker's balance-sheet distress is now moot as a channel risk, because Dick's owns it and has funded the reset.

## 6. Contribution estimate (estimates, my arithmetic)

**Exposure.**
- **Foot Locker:** FY2024 cost of sales $5,666M (Foot Locker XBRL; includes occupancy). Assume merchandise is 80% to 85% of that, and Nike is 59% of purchases (Foot Locker 10-K). That gives about $2.7B to $2.8B of Nike buys globally. With North America at an assumed 65% to 75% of Foot Locker, Nike sales to Foot Locker NA are about **$1.8B to $2.1B**, or 15% to 18% of Nike NA wholesale ($11,958M in FY26).
- **Hibbett:** FY2024 cost of sales $1,145M (includes freight and occupancy). With Nike at 73.7% of purchases, Nike buys are about **$0.7B**, or about 6% of NA wholesale.
- **DTLR:** not quantified. No filing data, and it is smaller.

**Scenario.** If FY27 Nike buys fall 5% to 10% at Foot Locker NA and Hibbett, the hit is about **$0.13B to $0.28B**. That is:
- 0.3% to 0.6% of FY26 revenue ($46,398M);
- about 3% to 9% of the guided FY27 decline (high single digits, about $3.2B to $4.2B);
- about 1.5% to 3% of the peak-to-FY27 decline ($51.4B to roughly $42B to $43B).

**Independent share.** The door-closure-only portion is about 3.6% of Nike-heavy Foot Locker NA doors. These are the least productive doors, and Dick's expects some sales to transfer. That gives about **$20M to $60M**, or under 0.15% of revenue.

**H2 FY26.** About zero. NA wholesale grew about 10% and Foot Locker revenue grew.

## 7. Can management fix it?

**Partly.** The inventory-driven part disappears once Nike stops over-supplying aged Sportswear and gives partners newness. Hill is already cutting Dunk (about -50% in Q1 FY27) and Jordan retro volume, with "North America will feel the biggest impact". The door-closure part is outside Nike's control but is too small to matter. It does mean "buying back the shelf" has a slightly smaller mall-door base to buy back into. Performance-led accounts (DICK'S, Academy, run specialty) are where shelf is actually being regained.

## 8. Quick challenge pass (kept short)

- *Could the Foot Locker hit simply show up later (Q2 FY27, Sep to Nov 2026)?* Possible. Nike flags a Q2 FY27 NA sell-in headwind. But Nike attributes it to lapping its own Q2 FY26 shipment timing ("timing of North America wholesale shipments", Q4 FY26 call), not to partner closures. Test this when Q2 FY27 reports (about late Dec 2026): if NA wholesale falls while DICK'S, Academy and JD banner comps are positive and Foot Locker comps are negative, the Foot Locker channel is doing some of the work.
- *Is the +9% NA wholesale an illusion?* Partly. It is flattered by lower returns and reserves and an easy FY25 base. But that flattery comes from Nike's own FY25 actions, not from partners.

## 9. Gaps

- Nike does not disclose revenue by account. The Foot Locker and Hibbett exposure figures are estimates from retailer filings with an assumed merchandise share of cost of sales and an assumed NA share.
- I did not open JD's H1 2026 release directly. JD figures rely on SGB (OPENED (repo)), and searches found no other copy.
- No DTLR data. No Hibbett store-count series under JD.
- Foot Locker comp by brand is not disclosed. The Q4 FY26 Foot Locker statement is Nike's own claim.

## Challenge: REFUTER

Date: 2026-10-13. Kept short on purpose. The investigator's own verdict already rejects the hypothesis as an independent cause, so a full adversarial pass would add little. I re-checked the load-bearing numbers and looked for gaps in the refutation.

**Number check (OPENED (repo), `data/nike_channel_split.csv`, Nike 10-Q and 10-K segment tables):**
- Q1 FY27 NA wholesale was $2,981M against $2,736M in Q1 FY26, or +9.0% (my arithmetic). NA total was $5,127M against $5,020M, or +2.1%. NA Direct was $2,146M against $2,284M, or -6.0%. This confirms the hypothesis's "+2%" series is **total** NA, and the slowdown comes from Direct, not wholesale.
- FY26 NA wholesale was $11,958M (10-K p. 83). That matches the denominator used in the exposure estimate.

**Where the refutation is weakest (steelman, so the verdict is not overstated):**
1. The Q4 FY26 two-year stack for NA wholesale was only about +0.5%, in the quarter right after Foot Locker's purge. Taken alone, that is consistent with a partner drag. But Q1 FY27 recovered to about +7.5% on a two-year basis, so the drag did not persist. Friend's "we didn't sell in up 10%" also points to Nike's own reserve and returns accounting, not to partner doors.
2. Nike's account-level Foot Locker revenue for Jun to Aug 2026 is unknown. Q2 FY27 (due about late Dec 2026) is the real test, as section 8 says.
3. The exposure estimate rests on two assumptions (merchandise share of cost of sales and NA share of Foot Locker). Even if Nike's buys fell 20% instead of 5% to 10%, the hit would be about $0.5B, roughly 1% of FY26 revenue. That is still a minor share of the decline. The verdict does not depend on these assumptions.

**What would revive the hypothesis:** NA wholesale turns negative in Q2 FY27 while the DICK'S banner, Academy and JD banner comps stay positive, and Foot Locker comps stay negative. Until then, the timing, account, peer and attribution predictions all fail.

**Refuter verdict:** The independent-cause version does not survive. It is a real transmission channel for Nike's own classics oversupply in FY27, and it is minor in size. I agree with "symptom of another cause". No new web research was needed beyond the repo's primary-source data.
