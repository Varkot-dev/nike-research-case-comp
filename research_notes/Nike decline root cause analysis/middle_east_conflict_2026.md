# Hypothesis: the 2026 Middle East conflict as an outside shock to EMEA

Date: 2026-10-13. Scope: test whether the conflict that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran on 28 Feb 2026 explains the Q3 FY26 EMEA store break and the elevated EMEA inventory, separately from the global Sportswear fade. Builds on `emea_converse_jordan.md` (R1 sizing: EMEA FY26 currency-neutral loss about $0.37B).

## Bottom line

- **Verdict: minor.** The conflict is real and management named it, but it cannot explain the Q3 FY26 EMEA store break, because the quarter ended on 28 Feb 2026, the day the strikes began. EMEA inventory was already up double digits on that same date.
- The one quarter fully exposed to the war (Q4 FY26, Mar to May) shows EMEA stores **improving**, from -20% to about -9%. If the war were driving store traffic, Q4 should have been worse, not better.
- Nike's Gulf stores are mostly run by partners (Sun & Sand Sports, GMG), so a Middle East traffic hit would land mainly in **EMEA wholesale**. EMEA wholesale was flat for FY26 and about flat in Q4 FY26.
- Size: about **$0.02B to $0.08B** of revenue in the FY24 to FY26 window (well under 1% of the $4.96B peak-to-FY26 fall), and perhaps another $0.02B to $0.06B in Q1 FY27. Upper bound $0.1B. This is below the $0.05B to $0.2B prior range, and most of it falls outside the window.
- The Q3 FY26 EMEA break away from North America is better explained by what was already in the data before 28 Feb: double-digit Sportswear declines, heavy promotions in Europe (adidas and JD Sports say the same), Nike's own digital promotions, and EMEA's later sales-organisation reset. The Middle East is at most a small add-on and a convenient label on the call.
- Fixable by management: **no** for the shock itself (it is exogenous and fading; not mentioned on the Q1 FY27 call). The inventory and promotion problem it was blamed for is fixable and predates the war.

## Evidence for

1. Management named it twice: Q3 FY26 call (31 Mar 2026), "recent disruption in the Middle East" as a reason for elevated Q4 EMEA inventory and "This quarter, we also experienced traffic disruption from the Middle East" (VectorShift transcript, OPENED); Q4 FY26 call (30 Jun 2026), "disruption in the Middle East" (REPO `emea_converse_jordan.md`, OPENED).
2. Gulf mall traffic did collapse in March 2026 (Retail Insight Network, OPENED).
3. adidas, Puma and Under Armour all cite the conflict in 2026 (see prediction 7).
4. The stock fell from $62.18 (27 Feb) to $58.64 (4 Mar), then from $52.82 (31 Mar) to $44.63 (1 Apr) on the Q4 outlook (REPO `data/nke_daily_prices.csv`); the press tied part of the guidance cut to the Middle East (Bloomberg via BNN, 1 Apr 2026, SNIPPET; Digital Commerce 360, 1 Apr 2026, OPENED).

## Evidence against

1. **Timing:** the conflict starts on the last day of the quarter in which the EMEA store break happened. The break is pre-war.
2. **Inventory predates it:** EMEA inventory up double digits at 28 Feb 2026.
3. **Wrong direction in the war quarter:** EMEA stores improved from -20% to about -9% in Q4 FY26, the first full war quarter.
4. **Wrong channel:** Nike's Gulf retail is largely partner-run, so it is wholesale for Nike; EMEA wholesale did not dent.
5. **Management's own ranking:** the Middle East comes third in Friend's list after Sportswear and European traffic and promotions; the Q1 FY27 call (new CFO Denton) drops it entirely.
6. **Peer magnitude:** adidas grew Emerging Markets 10% in the quarter the conflict began; UA's whole FY27 Middle East headwind is about $35M of profit.
7. **No filing mention:** the 10-Q, 10-K and Q1 FY27 10-Q EMEA discussions do not name the conflict.

## Independent cause or symptom?

It is an independent, exogenous shock, but a small one. It is **not** the cause of the Q3 FY26 EMEA decoupling from North America. That break is a symptom of the global Sportswear and classics fade (EMEA has the highest Sportswear mix), amplified by a promotional European lifestyle market and Nike's own digital promotions, as already set out in `emea_converse_jordan.md`. The Middle East disruption added a little to Q4 FY26 and possibly Q1 FY27, and gave management a convenient external reason for an inventory problem that existed before.

## Sizing (my arithmetic, assumptions stated)

- Nike does not disclose Middle East revenue. Assume the Middle East is about 4% to 8% of EMEA revenue (about $0.5B to $1.0B a year on $12.6B FY26 EMEA). This is an assumption, not a disclosed number.
- Assume Nike's Gulf sell-in fell 15% to 30% for the affected quarters (Gulf mall footfall -15% to -50% in March; adidas still grew Emerging Markets, so sporting goods held up better than luxury).
- Q4 FY26 (inside the FY24 to FY26 window): $0.125B to $0.25B quarterly base times 15% to 30% = about **$0.02B to $0.08B**.
- Q1 FY27 (outside the window), smaller as the shock faded: about $0.02B to $0.06B.
- Share of the $4.96B FY24 to FY26 revenue fall: about **0.4% to 1.5%**; of EMEA's FY26 c-n loss of about $0.37B, roughly 5% to 20%.
- Any extra markdown cost from clearing EMEA inventory falls on margin, not revenue, and cannot be separated from the pre-war inventory build.
