# Root cause test: did an innovation and product pipeline drought cause Nike's decline?

Research date: 2026-10-13. Hypothesis under test: *An innovation and product pipeline drought (no new hit franchises, falling behind in running super-shoes and max-cushion, weak newness) caused the decline.*

Source status:
- **OPENED**: I read the page or document myself in this session.
- **REPO**: a verified number already in `research_notes/04_financial_data_pack.md` or `data/nike_filing_facts.csv`, with the SEC filing and printed page given.
- **REPO-NOTE**: taken from an earlier repo research note, carrying the status that note recorded.
- **SNIPPET**: seen only in a search-result summary. Treat it as a lead, not a fact.

Nike earnings-call quotes come from Nike's own official transcripts (investors.nike.com, "webcast_transcript" PDFs, asset IDs 9384 to 9591). These were downloaded and converted to text in an earlier session, and I searched them in full text in this session. Status is **OPENED** for every quarter from Q1 FY21 to Q1 FY27 (25 calls). Caveat: no SEC filing breaks out "Running", "Performance" or "Sportswear" revenue. All category figures below are management statements on calls, made under Reg FD and safe-harbor language. They are not audited.

Companion note: `franchise_oversupply.md` in this folder, whose numbers I reuse and do not re-derive.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Verdict: a contributing cause, and largely a symptom of the same upstream decision that produced the franchise oversupply. It is not the primary cause of the FY24 to FY26 revenue decline.** The drought was real and Nike admits it. Donahoe (Apr 2024): "What's been missing is the kind of bold, disruptive innovation that Nike's known for." Hill (Dec 2024): traffic "softened because we lack newness in product." Nike lost the No. 1 US adult performance running position to Brooks from Q1 2022, and acknowledged losing share in run specialty. Its share of the elite marathon podium fell from 31 of 36 (2019) to fewer medals than Adidas (2023).
- **The decisive test fails, though.** If innovation were the main cause, the revenue decline should sit in the innovation-led business (Performance, especially Running). It does not. Performance "grew double digits" in Q4 FY24, the very quarter Lifestyle first declined. It grew in Q2 FY25 while Sportswear fell double digits, and it grew mid-single digits in FY26 to "$16 billion". Running added "roughly $1 billion" over five quarters. Yet total revenue was still down 4% in Q1 FY27. The hole is in Sportswear, Jordan retro, Greater China and Converse.
- **Where the hypothesis does bite** is the lifestyle "newness" leg. Nike had no new lifestyle hit big enough to backfill the more than $4B classics reduction in FY25. Digital traffic fell for lack of newness. The new retro-running styles (Vomero 5, P-6000, V5 Runner) scaled from a small base. This is the same failure as the oversupply, seen from the other side: with a thin pipeline, Nike milked Dunk, AF1 and AJ1.
- **Upstream cause:** the 2020 Consumer Direct Acceleration reorganization. It replaced sport and category teams with a "consumer construct" of Men's, Women's and Kids, and pushed resources toward digital. Donahoe also blamed 2.5 years of remote work and COVID factory closures. Hill's fix ("Sport Offense", sport-specific teams) is the explicit reversal of that reorganization.
- **Estimated share of the decline:** about **10% to 25%** of the $4,964M net FY24 to FY26 revenue decline, or **roughly $0.5B to $1.2B**, almost all through weak lifestyle newness. The Running and super-shoe leg contributes roughly zero net to that decline window, because Running is now well above its FY24 level. Separately, the drought cost Nike forgone growth before the decline began: running share lost to Hoka, On and Brooks from FY21 to FY24, while total Nike revenue was still rising. That is not part of the measured decline.
- **Fixable by management: yes. This is the most demonstrably fixable cause.** Once Nike shipped Vomero 18, Pegasus Premium and Vomero Plus and re-entered run specialty:
  - Running grew more than 20% for several quarters.
  - Vomero 18 became a "$100 million-plus franchise" in about 90 days.
  - Nike "nearly tripled" its max-cushioning share in a year.
  - Nike run-specialty sales rose 35.4% while On fell 19.7% (12 months to Sept 2025).
  - Nike gained "5 points of running market share in statement footwear" in Western Europe and North America in FY26.

  The lifestyle-newness gap is harder to fix, because lifestyle hits are fashion bets, and the lifestyle category is shrinking industry-wide.

## 3. Evidence in detail

### 3.1 Management admissions of the drought (official transcripts, OPENED, and press)
- **Q4 FY23 (Jun 29, 2023):** "Running has been a competitive battlefield lately, with more and more brands joining the market. We saw roughly 10% growth for our Running footwear business in FY23." Running was still growing in FY23.
- **Q1 FY24 (Sept 2023), Donahoe:** "over the past few years, we have launched new product innovations in Running but we need to drive more meaningful consumer connections among everyday runners and scale these innovations more effectively across the marketplace."
- **Q2 FY24 (Dec 21, 2023), Donahoe:** "The second half of fiscal '24 represents the start of a multiyear product innovation cycle that will introduce new franchises, concepts and platforms ... this new innovation cycle will take some time to fully ramp up." Also: "we know we must be faster, increasing the pace of innovation."
- **Q3 FY24 (Mar 21, 2024):** "we are in the midst of shifting our product portfolio toward newness and innovation ... we are pulling back supply of classics such as the Air Force 1; and we are reducing supply of Pegasus ahead of launching new innovation in the Pegasus 41." Also: "we're back on our front foot with growing confidence in our innovation pipeline."
- **CNBC interview with Sara Eisen, Apr 12, 2024 (via NBC New York, OPENED):** Donahoe said "What's been missing is the kind of bold, disruptive innovation that Nike's known for", and "it's really hard to do bold, disruptive innovation, to develop a boldly disruptive shoe on Zoom." Teams "came back together 18 months ago in person" and Nike is "ruthlessly focused on rebuilding our disruptive innovation pipeline." He disputed that Nike was losing share, saying it was "gaining share." [NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/nike-ceo-blames-remote-work-for-innovation-slowdown-saying-its-hard-to-build-disruptive-products-on-zoom/5314938/?amp=1)
- **Fortune, Phil Wahba, Jun 28, 2024 (OPENED):** reports that Donahoe told the WSJ that Nike had lost its "sharp edge" in sports and must reestablish its "disruptive innovation pipeline" (WSJ original not opened). [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/06/28/nike-ceo-under-pressure-innovate-stocks-drop)
- **Q4 FY24 (Jun 27, 2024):** "we've been accelerating our innovation pipeline, including pulling forward several innovations, some more than a year. We're moving aggressively to reestablish our innovation edge." Running: "our overall Running business was impacted by our proactive actions to manage the Pegasus portfolio transition." Pulling launches forward by more than a year is an admission that the pipeline was thin for the period it was meant to cover.
- **Q1 FY25 (Oct 1, 2024), Friend:** "we've acknowledged that we've lost market share in the Running specialty channel." Also: "women's running footwear was up and it was the first time we've got positive growth in several quarters." This dates Running weakness to roughly FY24.
- **Q2 FY25 (Dec 19, 2024), Hill:** "What I've seen is traffic in NIKE Direct, digital and physical, have softened because we lack newness in product and we're not delivering inspiring stories." Running: "men's was flat, and women's, up low to mid-single digits." This is the strongest single quote for the hypothesis.
- **Q3 FY25 (Mar 20, 2025), Hill:** "While we added innovation across our 5 key Fields of Play this quarter it's not enough to offset the continued headwinds of our classic franchises." This quote is direct evidence that newness was too small to backfill the classics.

### 3.2 The upstream organizational cause
- **Nike leadership release, Jul 22, 2020, via Retail Dive (OPENED, published Jul 23, 2020):** new leaders for "Men's, Women's and Kids"; "a simpler approach to product categories"; Amy Montagne moved from "vice president and general manager of global categories" to Men's; at least 500 headquarters job cuts (Oregon WARN). [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/nike-shuffles-leadership-structure-details-layoffs/582191/)
- **Bloomberg via BNN, Jun 2020 (SNIPPET):** CDA simplifies the "consumer construct" of men's, women's and kids' businesses, with more investment in e-commerce and technology.
- **Q3 FY24 transcript (OPENED; quoted in `franchise_oversupply.md`):** Donahoe: "we brought the best of the category offense right back in", an admission that the 2020 construct had removed it.
- **Q2 FY25, Hill:** "empowering more nimble cross-functional teams to obsess in the needs of sports-specific athletes by gender"; "we're going to focus in on 5 sports to start with, running, basketball, training, football and sportswear." This is the Sport Offense, which reverses the 2020 construct.
- **Matt Powell (BCE Consulting), BBC via Yahoo, Oct 2, 2026 (REPO-NOTE, opened):** Nike spent R&D on digital instead of product, which "shut down" innovation, and it will not come back quickly.
- **Massimo Giunco essay (REPO-NOTE, opened via FashionUnited, ~Aug 2024):** dismantling category teams caused a loss of product expertise.
- Nike does not disclose R&D spending, so I could not test a spending cut directly. A patent-count test was attempted, but the PatentsView API was unreachable through the proxy (502).

### 3.6 The decisive category test (calls, OPENED, plus REPO)
- **Q3 FY24:** "Performance footwear grew high-single digits, with double-digit growth from $100-plus franchises, including ... Structure and Vomero in running."
- **Q4 FY24:** "Performance grew double digits in the quarter ... after double-digit growth over the past several years, our Lifestyle business declined in Q4 across Men's, Women's, and Jordan, more than offsetting strong growth in our sport Performance business."
- **Q1 FY25:** "Our men's and women's lifestyle business was planned down double digits in Q1."
- **Q2 FY25:** "Overall, our sport performance fields of play grew year-over-year, offset by a double-digit decline in sportswear."
- **Q4 FY25 (North America):** "Sportswear declined in the quarter, driven by a near 40% reduction in our classic footwear franchises. Performance also declined. However, we saw strong sell-through for new products offered in running and training." This is one counter-example: performance did dip in NA in Q4 FY25, during heavy liquidation.
- **Q2 FY26:** "Sportswear growth was driven by apparel, with footwear flat, as a mid-20s percent decline in Classic footwear franchises was offset by growth in Air Max and the look of Running styles." By then, lifestyle newness was backfilling the classics.
- **Q1 FY27:** Performance "up high single digits ... if you were to exclude the reset in Greater China, NIKE Performance would have been up low double digits"; "the performance growing mid-single digits in FY '26 to $16 billion"; Sportswear down low double digits; "there's a lack of energy in the lifestyle space right now" (Hill, quoted in `franchise_oversupply.md`).
- **Derived arithmetic (my estimate, assumption-laden):**
  - Performance was about $16B in FY26, up mid-single digits, which implies about $15.2B to $15.4B in FY25. Performance grew in most FY25 quarters but dipped in NA in Q4, which suggests FY24 Performance of roughly $15B.
  - NIKE Brand revenue fell from $49,322M to $45,222M, FY24 to FY26 (REPO).
  - So non-Performance NIKE Brand revenue (Sportswear plus Jordan) fell by roughly $5B, while Performance rose by roughly $1B.
  - Converse fell a further $908M (REPO).
  - The decline is therefore entirely outside the innovation-led business.

Mentions of "Dn" in calls: 12 in Q3 FY24, 6 in Q4 FY24, then 0 in every later call (my counts). No sales figure was ever given. I found no press data on Dn sell-through. Silence after launch is suggestive, not proof.
- **Q1 FY25:** "what we call our look of running business, led by Vomero 5, V2K and P-6000 has grown more than 4x over the past year." It is growing fast but from a small base. Q4 FY24 had guided to "nearly triple our retro running business by the end of Fiscal 25."

- **Q1 FY27 (quoted in `franchise_oversupply.md`):** "a handful of Sportswear Footwear franchises ... grew by strong double digits ... led by our Running-inspired silhouettes, the P-6000 and the V5 Runner", but "some aged, higher volume, sportswear footwear sold through below expectations." - **Category confound (REPO-NOTE):** sport-lifestyle footwear is declining industry-wide (Circana H1 2026, snippet). Adidas Lifestyle was +2% in Q2 2026 in a "high promotional" market. Lifestyle newness is now fighting a category headwind that no pipeline would fully overcome.

## 4. Strongest evidence for
1. Nike's own leaders admitted the drought in plain words: "What's been missing is the kind of bold, disruptive innovation that Nike's known for" (Donahoe, Apr 2024), "reestablish our innovation edge" (Jun 2024), "we lack newness in product" (Hill, Dec 2024). Nike also "pulled forward several innovations, some more than a year."
2. Running share loss came before the decline and was large: Brooks No. 1 in US performance running from Q1 2022, Hoka and On combined adding about $3B of revenue from 2021 to 2024, and Nike admitting run-specialty share loss.
3. A clean within-Nike natural experiment shows the mechanism works. Max-cushion innovation (Vomero 18) plus specialty re-entry produced more than 20% Running growth, "$1 billion" added, a tripled max-cushion share and +5 share points in statement running.
4. Newness was explicitly "not enough to offset" the classics decline in FY25 (Q3 FY25). Weak lifestyle newness left a hole when the classics were cut.
5. The fix is the reversal of the 2020 consumer-construct reorganization, which ties the drought to an identifiable management decision.

## 5. Strongest evidence against (or limiting)
1. Category test: Performance grew while Lifestyle collapsed (Q4 FY24, Q2 FY25), and Performance grew mid-single digits to $16B in FY26. The decline sits in Sportswear, Jordan retro, China and Converse.
2. Reversal test: Running has recovered strongly since Q1 FY26, yet total revenue fell 4% in Q1 FY27 and is guided down high-single digits for FY27. Fixing innovation where it was most lacking did not fix the top line.
3. Geography: Nike China Running grew for six consecutive quarters while Greater China fell 26% c-n.
4. Timing: the running share loss of FY21 to FY24 coincided with Nike revenue *rising* $6.8B. The drought cost growth and share, not absolute revenue, until the classics bubble burst.
5. Peers: the lifestyle winners (Adidas Samba, New Balance, Onitsuka Tiger) won with retro product, not innovation. The lifestyle loss was a fashion-cycle and oversupply event more than an innovation event.
6. Measurement: Circana share excludes DTC, so part of Nike's "lost share" in 2022 to 2024 is a channel artifact of its own DTC pivot.

## 6. Quantification (my estimate; method shown)
- **Decline to explain:** FY24 to FY26 revenue -$4,964M (REPO: FY24 10-K p. 57; FY26 10-K p. 55).
- **Running and super-shoe leg:** about $0 net. Running was weak from roughly Q2 FY24 to Q1 FY25: women's negative "several quarters", men's flat in Q2 FY25. On a business I estimate at roughly $4B to $5B, that dip is unlikely to exceed a few hundred million dollars. The estimate is derived because Nike never discloses Running revenue; Hill's "Running grew $5 billion -- $1 billion" is a garbled line in the Q4 FY26 transcript. By FY26 Running was about $1B above its prior level, so the net contribution to the FY24 to FY26 decline is roughly zero or positive.
- **Lifestyle-newness leg:** classics fell by more than $4B from peak (Q2 FY26 call). Newness had almost fully offset the classics decline in Sportswear footwear by Q2 FY26, but had not in FY25. If a "normal" Nike pipeline had backfilled 15% to 30% more of the FY25 classics cut (assumption), the newness gap is about $0.5B to $1.2B.
- **Total:** about $0.5B to $1.2B, or **10% to 25% of the net decline**. Before the decline, the drought also cost Nike forgone growth, plausibly $1B to $2B a year by FY24. That figure assumes Nike would have kept a share of the roughly $3B HOKA-plus-On gain. It is not part of the measured decline.
- **Valuation:** the innovation narrative likely contributed to multiple compression (BofA cited "innovation pipeline concerns" in its Underperform call, REPO-NOTE), but I cannot isolate it from the other news on each earnings day.

## 7. Fixability
- **Running and performance innovation: yes, already being fixed.** The evidence is listed in Section 1. Timescale: about 12 to 18 months from the Vomero 18 launch (early 2025) to share gains.
- **Lifestyle newness: partly.** New franchises (P-6000, V5 Runner, Air Max) grow but need several seasons to reach classics scale. The sport-lifestyle category is shrinking industry-wide, and Hill himself describes a "sea of sameness" across brands.
- **Overall:** fixing innovation is necessary but clearly not sufficient. The FY26 to FY27 evidence shows it cannot offset the Sportswear, Jordan, China and Converse headwinds on its own.

## 8. Gaps
- Nike discloses no Running, Performance or Sportswear revenue in filings, and the call figures are unaudited.
- No pre-2022 Circana share for Nike running was found. Brooks' 21% (2023) is a snippet; the 11-quarter streak is opened.
- No Air Max Dn sales data exists in any source I found.
- No Nike R&D disclosure exists, and the patent-count test was not possible (PatentsView unreachable).

### R1. Verdict of the challenge
The hypothesis does **not** survive as a cause of the measured decline. I would downgrade it from "contributing cause" to **"symptom of another cause"**, with a share of the FY24 to FY26 decline of about **0% to 10% (at most about $0.5B)**, not 10% to 25%. Both legs of the investigator's mechanism trace back to other decisions that Nike's own CFO and CEO named:
1. The running share loss was attributed by Nike's CFO to a **distribution pullback**, not a product gap (R2).
2. The "lifestyle newness" leg is really the classics over-indexing and oversupply story (already scored in `franchise_oversupply.md`), plus an industry-wide lifestyle slump that hit the strongest peer too (R4, R5).
3. The decline today is concentrated where the innovation fix is already working (China Running grows, Performance grows), so the fix demonstrably does not move the total (R3).

### R2. The running leg: a quote the investigator truncated changes the causal story
- The notes quote Friend (Q1 FY25, Oct 1, 2024): "we've lost market share in the Running specialty channel." The **next sentences** in the official transcript (OPENED, saved text, lines 660 to 663) are: "**More than 4 years ago, we pulled back on our engagement with that channel. The investigator's own caveat that Circana excludes DTC points the same way: the measured share loss is partly where Nike chose not to sell.
- Timing check: Q4 FY23 call (OPENED via the notes): "roughly 10% growth for our Running footwear business in FY23", i.e. during the Brooks No. 1 streak that began Q1 2022. Running revenue grew through most of the supposed drought.

## Challenge: ROOT-CAUSE

Challenge date: 2026-10-13. Lens: accept that the symptom is real (Nike lost running share, and lifestyle newness was too thin to backfill the classics cut). Then ask whether the "innovation drought" is itself a downstream effect of a different upstream cause. Status labels as above. Nike call quotes were re-read by me in full text from the official Nike IR transcript PDFs (local text copies in the session scratchpad, `tx/*.txt`): **OPENED**. Deckers and Nike filings were downloaded by me from EDGAR in this session: **OPENED**. All arithmetic is mine. Note: the shared web-search budget for this run was exhausted before this pass started. I therefore relied on primary filings, the official transcripts and existing repo notes, and did not work around the limit.

### RC0. Verdict
**Symptom of another cause. As an independent cause the hypothesis does not survive.** The two legs of the "drought" trace to different upstream decisions, and neither is a failure of product R&D as such:
1. **Running leg → a distribution decision.** Nike's own CFO attributes the run-specialty share loss to Nike pulling back from that channel, not to missing product. Nike had a max-cushion line in market from early 2021, and Running revenue grew about 10% in FY23. The Running revenue dip in FY24 to Q1 FY25 followed a deliberate Pegasus supply cut.
2. **Lifestyle leg → the Consumer Direct merchandising doctrine.** From 2017, "newness" was defined as refreshing proven models with data (Express Lane "update"), and from 2020 the digital model was fed with classics. Newness was then physically crowded out by classics inventory until it was liquidated. The lifestyle product that worked once room was made (Vomero 5, P-6000) came from the archive, not from new technology. That is a merchandising and allocation gap, not an innovation-pipeline gap.

The innovation-drought label also fails a timing test that Donahoe's own explanation (remote work, "hard to ... develop a boldly disruptive shoe on Zoom") depends on: HOKA's wholesale surge began two years before COVID.

### RC1. Nike's own attribution of the running share loss is a channel decision
- **Q1 FY25 call, Oct 1, 2024 (OPENED), CFO Matt Friend, answering Citi on how far run specialty had fallen:** "we've acknowledged that we've lost market share in the Running specialty channel. **More than 4 years ago, we pulled back on our engagement with that channel. And as a result of that, we saw market share losses.**" The investigator quotes the first sentence. The causal sentence that follows it points upstream to distribution, which dates the pullback to before Oct 2020, in the Consumer Direct Offense (CDO) era.
- **Q2 FY24 call, Dec 21, 2023 (OPENED), Donahoe on everyday running:** "this is the area where we have the most work. **And so we have good product.** We had some nice wins in the quarter, the Structure 25 and the Vomero 17 ... But we're very focused on building out our ground game with everyday runners, and that means getting into the RSGs, **back into the RSGs**." In other words, the CEO located the gap in presence, not product.
- **Nike had max-cushion product before the share loss.**
  - Q3 FY21 call, Mar 18, 2021 (OPENED): "The ZoomX Invincible Run offers a brand-new look for NIKE Running with increased foam in the mid-sole."
  - Q3 FY23 call, Mar 21, 2023 (OPENED): the Invincible 3 "is designed to provide maximum cushioning and comfort".
  - Q4 FY23 call, Jun 29, 2023 (OPENED): "Since its Q3 launch, Invincible has already doubled its retail sales vs. last year."
  - The max-cushion gap that the Fleet Feet CEO describes was therefore partly a gap in specialty-door presence and in product breadth, not the absence of a platform.
- **Running revenue grew through the period when Brooks led national retail.**
  - Q1 FY23 (Sept 2022, OPENED): "Running delivered solid growth."
  - Q3 FY23 (OPENED): "running up double-digits, led by the Invincible 3."
  - Q4 FY23 (OPENED): "Running grew double-digits" in the quarter and about 10% in FY23 (already in the notes).
  - Brooks' No. 1 position from Q1 2022 is measured by Circana, which excludes brand DTC. Nike's own running sales were rising in the same window, so this is consistent with a channel shift in where Nike sold, not with a product collapse.
- **The Running revenue dip was partly a supply decision.**
  - Q3 FY24, Mar 21, 2024 (OPENED): "we are reducing supply of Pegasus ahead of launching new innovation in the Pegasus 41."
  - Q4 FY24, Jun 27, 2024 (OPENED): "our overall Running business was impacted by our proactive actions to manage the Pegasus portfolio transition, ... recent new releases in Vomero, Invincible, Infinity, and Structure all grew high double-digits."
  - Taken together: in the very quarters cited as the drought, the dip came from cutting Nike's largest running franchise. The other running franchises grew.

### RC2. The "natural experiment" bundles three treatments, so it cannot isolate innovation
- **Q3 FY26 call, Mar 31, 2026 (OPENED), Hill:** "NIKE Running was the first team to move into the Sport Offense. They created a clear product construct based on athlete insights, segmented and differentiated assortments across an integrated marketplace and elevated our presence in story [and] talent at retail."
- The recovery that started in 2025 combined three changes at the same time:
  1. new product (Vomero 18, Pegasus Premium);
  2. re-entry to run specialty: NA run-specialty partners "up double digits" in Q1 FY25 (OPENED), and the Fleet Feet full rollout (REPO-NOTE);
  3. a reorganization into a sport team.
- The investigator reads the recovery as proof of the innovation mechanism. It is equally strong proof of the distribution mechanism (RC1) and the org mechanism (`org_talent.md`).
- The one channel datapoint that discriminates favours distribution. Nike's run-specialty growth of +35.4% (12 months to Sept 2025, REPO-NOTE) is the growth expected from re-entering doors Nike had left.
- In fairness, the Fleet Feet CEO's "better cushioning" quote and Vomero 18's "$100 million-plus" in about 90 days show a product component too. I cannot split the three treatments with public data.

### RC3. The challenger surge predates both the 2020 reorg and remote work (new data)
HOKA brand wholesale net sales, Deckers fiscal years ending Mar 31 (Deckers FY2020 10-K, filed 2020-06-01, five-year table, OPENED):

| FY | HOKA wholesale ($M) | YoY (my arithmetic) |
|---|---|---|

| FY17 | 93.1 | +24% |
| FY18 | 132.7 | +43% |

- **Timing implications:** HOKA's wholesale engine was compounding at 40% to 50% a year from FY18, that is, from the year after Nike's June 2017 CDO and its exit from "less differentiated and less productive points of distribution" (Q4 FY17 8-K, already OPENED in `dtc_wholesale.md`).
  - This was before the July 2020 consumer-construct reorganization.
  - It was before any COVID remote work.
  - Donahoe's remote-work explanation (Apr 2024) and the investigator's 2020-reorg explanation cannot be the origin of the running share loss. Both arrive after the challenger's take-off.
- **Deckers' own attribution mixes product and doors:**
  - FY2020 10-K (OPENED): "continued global growth through new customer acquisitions, as well as higher sales driven by key franchise updates and new product launches."

  - These are self-reported and promotional, but they confirm that HOKA's product was pulling demand. Nike's channel pullback opened the doors; HOKA's product won them. This matches the two-cause chain in `dtc_wholesale.md` RC5.

### RC4. The lifestyle newness gap is a product of the Consumer Direct doctrine
- **The doctrine redefined newness as refreshing proven models.** Q4 FY17 call, filed as 8-K Ex. 99.1 on 2017-07-05 (OPENED, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018717000059/q4fy17erccexhibit991.htm):
  - Express Lane has three parts: "update, and that's editing and refreshing materials, colors or prints on popular existing models based on consumers' insights", alongside "create" in "half the time".
  - Consumers "see something they love, they want more versions of it."
  - Q1 FY21 (Sept 2020, OPENED, quoted in `franchise_oversupply.md`): "By continuously adding new styles, we expand these popular platforms."
  - From 2017, the operating model made it rational to grow by versioning AF1, Dunk and AJ1 rather than by building new lifestyle platforms.
- **Newness was physically crowded out by classics inventory, then allowed back in.**
  - Q2 FY25, Dec 19, 2024 (OPENED): "We are creating capacity in the marketplace to sell in seasonal newness and innovation for fall and holiday '25."
  - Q3 FY25, Mar 20, 2025 (OPENED), Hill: "we're bringing the product that's not selling out of the market and then we will liquidate that ... to make room for the new innovation."
  - If the binding constraint was shelf and open-to-buy occupied by classics, the "not enough to offset" quote from Q3 FY25 is a capacity statement as much as a pipeline statement.
- **Nike was also cutting assortment while needing newness.** Q2 FY24, Dec 21, 2023 (OPENED): the $2B savings plan includes "simplifying our product assortment."
- **What worked in lifestyle was archival, not innovation.** The "look of running" business (Vomero 5, V2K, P-6000) "has grown more than 4x over the past year" (Q1 FY25, OPENED). These are retro silhouettes reissued from Nike's archive. Lifestyle rivals (Samba, New Balance, Onitsuka) also won with archival product. The lifestyle shortfall therefore measures a failure to read and allocate to a fashion rotation, which is a merchandising and portfolio failure. It is not a technology pipeline failure, so "innovation drought" mislabels it.
- **The residual is a category headwind no pipeline fixes.** Q1 FY27 (Oct 1, 2026, OPENED): "there's a lack of energy in the lifestyle space right now" and a "sea of sameness that's hurting the lifestyle marketplace across our brands and our competitors."

### RC5. A different explanation fits the same data better
**Chain:**
1. **2017 CDO:** Nike adopts own-channel economics, pulls back from "less differentiated" wholesale and from run specialty, and defines newness as data-driven updates of proven models.
2. **2018 to 2022:** HOKA and On fill the vacated running doors with product runners want. Nike Running still grows in its own channels, so the loss appears in Circana share, not in Nike revenue.
3. **2020 CDA:** classics feed Digital, the consumer construct removes category ownership, and Nike's lifestyle line concentrates in three silhouettes.
4. **2023 to 2025:** the fashion cycle rotates away from court classics. Inventory of classics blocks newness until it is liquidated, and Nike cuts Pegasus supply ahead of a launch.
5. **2025 to 2026:** Nike re-enters doors, reorganizes by sport and ships product. Running recovers. Lifestyle stays weak because the category itself is soft.

**Test against the investigator's predictions:**
- **P2 category** fits: the R&D-led business (Performance) grew, and the merchandising-led business (Sportswear) shrank.
- **P3 reversal** fits: fixing running did not fix the total, because the total was never driven by R&D.
- **P6 geography** fits: China Running grew while China fell.
- **P1 timing** fits better than the drought story: HOKA's take-off follows the 2017 CDO, not the 2020 reorg or 2020 to 2022 remote work.
- **The management language spike (P9) fits a narrative choice.** "Innovation" became the stated explanation in FY24, while Donahoe was disputing share loss ("gaining share", Apr 2024). By Oct 2024 his CFO was attributing it to the channel pullback.

### RC6. Contribution re-estimated under this lens
- **Running and super-shoe leg:** the investigator puts this at about $0 net in the decline window, and I agree. Its share-loss history belongs mostly to the CDO channel decision, already counted in `dtc_wholesale.md` and `timeline_forensics.md`.
- **Lifestyle-newness leg:** the investigator puts this at $0.5B to $1.2B. Most of it is the same CDA merchandising and classics-concentration mechanism already counted in `franchise_oversupply.md` (whose ROOT-CAUSE pass itself pointed at "the newness pipeline", meaning that the two notes point at each other) and in `timeline_forensics.md` (CDA chain 50% to 60%). The only part that is independent of those mechanisms is a genuine shortfall in creating new lifestyle platforms (the Air Max Dn went silent after launch). I estimate this part at **about $0 to $0.5B, or 0% to 10% of the $4,964M decline**. This is a judgment, with wide error bars. It is not additive with the companion notes.
- **Fixability:** the upstream decisions (channel pullback, consumer construct, digital classics model) have all been reversed. That is why Running came back. What remains (lifestyle category softness, China, Converse) is not an innovation problem.
