# Root cause test: did governance and incentives drive Nike's bad decisions?

Research date: 2026-10-13. Hypothesis under test: *Governance and incentives (founder dual-class control via Swoosh, LLC and Phil Knight, board choices, executive compensation tied to digital and DTC metrics, buybacks over investment) drove the bad decisions behind the FY2024 to FY2027 revenue decline.*

Status labels: **OPENED** = I downloaded or read the document myself in this session. **OPENED (repo)** = opened by an earlier researcher and recorded in this repo; reused, not re-fetched. **SNIPPET** = seen only in a search-result summary. Every Nike proxy (DEF 14A) from 2017 to 2026 and every annual-meeting vote 8-K (Item 5.07) from 2019 to 2026 was downloaded from sec.gov in this session and searched in full. Proxy page numbers are the printed page numbers, located from the page footers in the EDGAR text. All arithmetic is mine and labelled.

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: symptom of another cause, with one real but small independent channel (the digital-revenue bonus metric as an amplifier).** Governance is where the bad decisions were *made*, but the structural features the hypothesis names (dual-class control, buybacks) did not *cause* them, and the pay metrics followed the strategy rather than leading it.

1. **The incentives came after the strategy.** Nike's bonus plans had no digital or DTC metric in FY2017 (pre-tax income), FY2018 to FY2020 (EBIT) or the LTIP (revenue and EPS). The wholesale account cuts began in FY2018 under those plans. "Digital Revenue" entered the annual bonus only in June 2020, the month Consumer Direct Acceleration (CDA) was announced, and the proxy says explicitly it was there "to support digital growth in connection with our Consumer Direct Acceleration strategy" (2021 proxy p. 35). The pay plan was built to fit the strategy, not the other way round.
2. **The incentive was real but small at the top and broad below.** Digital revenue was one-third of the annual bonus for FY2021, FY2022 and FY2023, then dropped for FY2024. For the CEO that was about **$1.0M of about $20M to $21.5M target pay, roughly 5%** (my arithmetic on 2022 and 2023 proxies). But the same metrics applied to "all global employees who are eligible ... through annual incentive bonuses" (2023 proxy p. 35), so a company-wide bonus pool rewarded digital revenue for three years.
3. **The behavioral signature is there in FY2023.** Digital revenue hit 137% of its bonus target in FY2023 ($13.7B adjusted) in the same year NIKE Direct margins fell from "higher promotional activity to liquidate inventory" (FY2023 10-K p. 33). In March 2024 CFO Matt Friend admitted: "we've been more focused on trying to achieve mix of marketplace targets, then we have serving consumer demand" (Q3 FY2024 call, OPENED). That is direct evidence that targets, and a pay plan built on one of them, distorted decisions.
4. **Dual-class control fails the discriminating tests.** (a) It has been constant since the 1980 IPO, through 40 years of outperformance, so it cannot explain a change on its own. (b) The outside investors it supposedly overrode *cheered* the strategy: NKE rose 15.5% on June 25, 2021, the day the 60% direct and 40% digital targets were set (price CSV, my arithmetic), and the 2021 proxy says Class B holders were "broadly supportive." (c) A widely held peer with no controlling shareholder, adidas, ran the same DTC pivot and put "Success of the direct-to-consumer business" in its CEO's 2021 bonus (adidas 2021 compensation report, OPENED). (d) Nike's Class A shares carry one vote per share. Founder control applies only to the election of 8 of 11 directors.
5. **Buybacks did not starve investment.** Nike spent $13,744M on buybacks in FY2022 to FY2024 while demand creation rose from $3,114M (FY2021) to $4,285M (FY2024) and cash plus short-term investments never fell below $10.7B at a year end (data pack; my arithmetic). The buybacks destroyed shareholder value (about $7.7B at today's price, my arithmetic) but they are not a revenue cause.
6. **Where governance did fail:** the board's own judgment. It chose an outsider digital CEO (a director since 2014), approved CDA, paid a FY2023 bonus of 119% in the year the damage was being done, and the founder publicly backed the CEO ("unwavering confidence," June 28, 2024) 83 days before replacing him. That is the CDA decision restated at board level, not a separate cause.

**Contribution estimate:** about **0% to 10% of the $4,964M FY2024 to FY2026 revenue decline (about $0 to $0.5B)**, entirely through the incentive-amplification channel and possible delay. It is **not additive**: it overlaps with what `dtc_wholesale.md`, `franchise_oversupply.md` and `digital_tech.md` attribute to the CDA operating model. Dual-class control and buybacks: about 0% directly.

**Fixable?** Partly, and mostly already fixed: the digital metric was removed for FY2024, FY2026 to FY2028 PSUs now use revenue and EPS with a relative-TSR modifier (2026 proxy p. 32), and the board was refreshed toward brand expertise (Knudstorp, Arnault; a new "Brand/Marketing" skill line first appears in the 2025 proxy). The dual-class structure cannot be changed without Class A approval (2025 proxy p. 21), but it is not the cause.

## 3. Evidence for the hypothesis (strongest first)

1. **Pay was explicitly wired to the strategy, company-wide.** 2021 proxy p. 35: 1H FY2021 metrics were "(1) revenue generated through sales on digital platforms ('Digital Revenue') ... to support digital growth in connection with our Consumer Direct Acceleration strategy; (2) ... SG&A ...; and (3) units ... ('Inventory Units')". Goal: digital up "28% to 42%" vs the prior-year half. 2H (p. 36): digital goal "25% to 45%". FY2022 (2022 proxy p. 33): digital target "+31%" vs FY2021, "with additional stretch built into the maximum goal". FY2023 (2023 proxy p. 35): digital target +15%. The same structure applied to the broad-based employee bonus (2022 proxy p. 34: payout "consistent with the payout of the similarly-structured annual bonus for employees below the executive officer level"; 2023 proxy p. 35).
2. **Management admitted target-chasing.** Friend, Q3 FY2024 call, Mar 21, 2024 (Motley Fool transcript, OPENED): "we've been more focused on trying to achieve mix of marketplace targets, then we have serving consumer demand"; also "we don't like the way our brand is showing up in wholesale, and we own that." The public targets were 60% direct and 40% NIKE digital by FY2025, set June 2021 (Motley Fool, June 26, 2021, SNIPPET).
3. **The FY2023 payout rewarded the damage.** Digital earnout 137%, overall PSP calculated 145%, paid 119% (2023 proxy p. 35), in the year the 10-K blames "higher promotional activity to liquidate inventory" in NIKE Direct (FY2023 10-K p. 33). Sibling notes (`franchise_oversupply.md`, `pricing_discounting.md`) show this was when classics were over-supplied and discounted through Nike's own app.
4. **Confidential-witness allegations tie pay to behavior.** First Amended Complaint, *In re Nike, Inc. Securities Litigation*, No. 3:24-cv-00974 (D. Or., filed Feb 10, 2025; OPENED), para. 293: CW-1 "articulated that Donahoe wanted to boost his personal bonuses by cutting costs for innovation and relying on NIKE's classic products only." Paras. 779 to 784 argue the digital metric motivated pushing DTC "even if that growth were unsustainable." **Caveat:** these are allegations. The court (Judge Adrienne Nelson, Mar 31, 2026) found scienter adequately pleaded for only one of seven surviving statements (Donahoe's Sept 28, 2023 "innovation pipeline is strong"), and dismissed the claims against Friend, Parker and others (The Fashion Law, Apr 6, 2026, OPENED). I could not open the opinion, so I do not know how it treated the compensation-motive argument. The complaint also mis-dates the metric: it says digital first entered bonuses for FY2022, but the 2021 proxy shows it in FY2021.
5. **Founder control insulated pay decisions.** In 2020, excluding Class A votes, say-on-pay support was about 38.8% (my arithmetic on the Sept 18, 2020 8-K: 671,411,282 For, 571,342,132 Against, 308,915,859 Class A votes cast). The 2021 proxy (p. 30) calls 54% "much lower than our historical approval rate". Every Class A director in every year 2020 to 2026 got 0 votes withheld (8-Ks), so the founder bloc's choices are never contested.
6. **Board composition lacked industry operators.** 2019 skills matrix: Digital/Technology 6 of 12, Retail Industry 4 of 12, no brand or sport category (2019 proxy). A "Brand/Marketing" line appears only from the 2025 proxy (p. 5; 6 of 11 in 2026), and "Sport/Media/Entertainment" in 2026. Read as the board's own later judgment of what was missing (inference).
7. **Founder backed the CEO late.** Phil Knight, June 28, 2024, the day NKE fell about 20% ($94.19 to $75.37, price CSV): "John Donahoe has my unwavering confidence and full support" (Gulf News/Bloomberg, OPENED). Donahoe's exit was announced Sept 19, 2024 (8-K, OPENED (repo)).
8. **Academic prior.** Kim, Levit and Michaely, "The Dynamic Trade-Off of Dual-Class Shares" (ECGI Finance WP 590/2019, Jan 28, 2019; abstract OPENED): dual-class valuations "rise following dual-class recapitalizations but decline over time" and "Investment by mature dual-class firms is less sensitive to opportunities." Nike is a mature dual-class firm. The study's sample is mostly super-voting structures; Nike's is weaker (one vote per share, board-election split).

## 4. Evidence against (strongest first)

1. **Timing: strategy preceded the metric.** No digital metric in any Nike bonus or LTIP from FY2017 to FY2020 (2017 to 2020 proxies; text search, 0 hits for "digital revenue"). The account cuts started in FY2018 under an EBIT bonus. The metric was introduced in the CDA month and dropped in June 2023, a three-year window inside a strategy era that ran 2017 to 2024.
2. **A widely held peer did the same thing.** Adidas, no controlling holder: Rorsted's 2021 individual bonus criterion was "Average target achievement of Success of the direct-to-consumer business and Cash flow" (100%); Global Sales head Auschel's was "Success of the direct-to-consumer business" (0%) and "Attracting new members" (200%) (adidas 2021 Compensation Report, OPENED). Adidas also cut "thousands of non-strategic wholesale accounts" (`dtc_wholesale.md`, OPENED (repo)). The DTC thesis was an industry belief, independent of ownership structure.
3. **Outside shareholders endorsed the strategy.** +15.5% on June 25, 2021; +1.3% on CDA day, June 25, 2020 ($100.08 to $101.40) (price CSV). 2021 proxy p. 31: in the engagement with holders of about 33% of Class B, "Shareholders were broadly supportive" and concerns were about award size; Donahoe had "forfeited approximately $79 million" at ServiceNow. The 2025 proxy (p. 21): most holders "did not have specific concerns" with dual class.
4. **Structure is a constant; the decline is a change.** Dual class since 1980 (2026 proxy p. 20). Swoosh's Class A stake moved only from 245,000,000 shares (77.7%, 2019 proxy) to 233,500,000 (76.6%, 2021 to 2023 proxies) to 221,750,000 (78.8%, 2026 proxy p. 70). Nothing about control changed around 2020 to 2024.
5. **Performance pay paid nothing during the decline.** Three straight PSU cycles at 0%, FY2025 bonus 0%, CEO FY2022 bonus 0% (proxies cited in P9). The FY2026 bonus was cut from 101% to 74% by "negative discretion" (2026 proxy). Pay was not rewarding the collapse.
6. **Buybacks were not funded at the expense of operating investment.** Demand creation and overhead rose; cash stayed above $10B (data pack). Nike's FY2021 buyback was only $608M, the low point, during the CDA launch year. The largest buyback year (FY2023, $5,480M) came before revenue fell.
7. **Geography contradicts a firm-wide cause.** NA recovered in FY2026 under the same board; China and Converse collapsed (data pack). Sibling notes tie those to China-specific execution and the franchise cycle (`china.md`).
8. **The board acted on its own data before the market forced it.** The digital metric was removed in June 2023 (2024 proxy p. 33), before the Dec 2023 guidance cut, and the board replaced the CEO within about 9 months of that cut. This is not obviously slower than adidas, which replaced Rorsted in 2022 after its own 2021 to 2022 deterioration (`adidas_gulden.md`).

## 5. Independent cause or symptom?

**Symptom.** The causal chain the evidence supports is:

*Industry-wide belief in DTC and digital (shared by Nike's board, its outside investors and adidas)* -> *board picks a digital outsider CEO (Oct 2019) and approves CDA (June 2020)* -> *board builds pay to reward the strategy (digital revenue in every bonus, FY2021 to FY2023)* -> *organization chases the 60/40 mix targets, feeds the app with classics and discounts to hit them* -> *franchise oversupply, brand erosion, digital unwind (FY2024 to FY2026)*.

Governance sits at the decision node, so "board choices" is true by definition, but it is the CDA cause restated. The specific structural mechanisms named in the hypothesis do not discriminate:
- **Dual class:** constant; the market it would have shielded the board from agreed with the board; the widely held peer made the same call.
- **Buybacks:** value-destroying, not revenue-destroying.
- **Incentives:** real amplifier, downstream of the strategy, removed in 2023.

The one thing governance plausibly added is **speed and depth**: a company-wide bonus on digital revenue likely made the organization push harder on the app and on promotions in FY2022 and FY2023 than it otherwise would have, and a founder-controlled board with a founder publicly backing the CEO in June 2024 may have delayed the change by a few months. Neither can be measured cleanly.

## 6. Contribution estimate

- Base: FY2024 to FY2026 revenue decline of $4,964M; NIKE Brand Digital fell about $3.5B (FY2024 $12.1B to FY2026 $8.6B; `dtc_wholesale.md`, OPENED (repo)).
- **Incentive amplification:** if the digital bonus metric raised digital push by even a few percent of the FY2023 digital base ($12.6B), the extra promotional and classics volume that later had to unwind is in the low hundreds of millions of dollars. Upper bound I can defend: about 10% of the decline (about $0.5B), and it overlaps fully with the CDA mechanism already counted in sibling notes. Lower bound: zero, since adidas shows the same behavior with smaller incentive weight and Nike's targets were public commitments anyway.
- **Dual-class and board delay:** if a widely held board had moved 3 to 6 months earlier, some FY2025 damage might have been avoided. I cannot quantify it; Hill's reversal has not yet produced growth after two years, so earlier action would not obviously have changed FY2024 to FY2026 revenue much.
- **Buybacks:** about $0 of revenue. About $7.7B of shareholder value lost on the $18B program (124.4M shares x ($97.57 - $35.35); my arithmetic).
- **Total, non-additive:** about **0% to 10% (about $0 to $0.5B)**.

## 7. Can management fix it?

Partly, and most of what is fixable has been done:
- Digital metric dropped for FY2024 (2024 proxy p. 33). FY2026 to FY2028 PSUs: "Adjusted Revenue (50%) and Adjusted EPS (50%) ... with a Relative TSR modifier" (2026 proxy p. 32). FY2026 non-CEO bonus adds 20% individual objectives (2026 proxy).
- Board refresh toward brand expertise: Knudstorp (2025), Arnault (Sept 2026), Brand/Marketing skills line (2025 proxy p. 5).
- **Watch item (new risk created by the fix):** the new PSUs reward EPS, and with Pace targeting about $2.5B of cumulative savings (Q1 FY2027 release), EPS can recover through cost cuts and buybacks without revenue recovering. Revenue is weighted 50%, which limits this.
- Dual class cannot be changed by management or the board (2025 proxy p. 21). It is not a cause, so this does not matter for the turnaround.

## 8. What would change my mind

- An internal document or testimony showing the 60/40 targets or the account cuts were set *to hit* a pay metric (rather than the metric set to fit the targets).
- The court's Mar 31, 2026 reasoning, if it found the compensation-motive allegation persuasive.
- Evidence that Class A holders or Knight personally pushed CDA against board or management resistance, or blocked an earlier CEO change.
- Evidence that buyback funding cut product or innovation budgets (Nike does not disclose R&D).
