# Root cause test: is a durable consumer taste shift to comfort, performance running and "quiet" or heritage brands taking share from Nike regardless of execution?

Research date: 2026-10-13. Hypothesis under test: *A durable consumer taste shift toward comfort, performance running and "quiet" or heritage brands (On, Hoka, New Balance, Asics, Salomon, Adidas terrace) is a structural force taking share regardless of Nike execution.*

Status labels: **OPENED** = I fetched and read the page or filing in this session (curl or WebFetch). **OPENED (repo)** = opened by an earlier researcher and recorded in this repo's notes; reused, not re-fetched. **SNIPPET** = seen only in a search-result summary. Nike numbers come from `data/nike_filing_facts.csv`, `research_notes/04_financial_data_pack.md`, or the FY2024 and FY2026 10-Ks I downloaded from EDGAR in this session (page numbers from the "2026 FORM 10-K nn" footer that follows each table). All arithmetic is mine and labelled "my arithmetic". Currency conversions are approximate and labelled.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: contributing cause. The taste rotation is real and partly exogenous, but it is not the primary cause, and the "regardless of Nike execution" part of the hypothesis fails.**

What holds:
- **The rotation is real, large and broad.** In US athletic footwear (Euromonitor Passport, Dec 18, 2025, via HGI, OPENED), On went from outside the top 10 in 2019 to 6.3% share in 2025, Deckers (Hoka) to 3.8%, Brooks to 2.8%, New Balance from 3.6% to 6.4%, Asics from 2.6% to 3.6%, Skechers from 7.5% to 9.0%. That is roughly 12 to 16 points moving to running, comfort and heritage brands in six years (my arithmetic).
- **It hits every incumbent that sat in the fading silhouette families, not just Nike.** VF Corp (Vans) lost *more* US share than Nike (9.4% to 3.9% vs 34.1% to 28.9%). Vans revenue fell from $3,682.9M (FY2023) to $2,149.1M (FY2026), -42%; Converse fell from $2,427M to $1,174M, -52%, with units -31% "in all territories" in FY2026. Nike's classic franchises (Dunk, AF1, AJ1) fell by "more than $4 billion" from peak. Three different canvas, skate and court-derived lifestyle franchises collapsed in the same window. That is the signature of a category fashion cycle, not of one company's mistakes.
- **The challengers' gains dwarf Nike's loss.** From roughly FY2024 to FY2026, On (+CHF 1,222M, about +$1.6B), Hoka (+$780M), New Balance (about +$2.7B, unaudited), Asics (+¥240B, about +$1.3B) and Amer's Outdoor Performance segment (+$568M in 2025 alone) added roughly $7B of revenue (my arithmetic), against Nike's -$4,964M. In Greater China, Amer Sports (Arc'teryx, Salomon) alone grew from $844.8M (2023) to $1,861.9M (2025), equal to about 60% of Nike's $1,698M China decline (my arithmetic).
- **Nike's decline is a footwear decline.** Footwear (NIKE Brand plus Converse) explains $4,689M of the $4,964M FY2024 to FY2026 revenue fall (94%, my arithmetic on FY2026 10-K p. 37 to 41). NIKE Brand apparel fell only $326M and equipment rose $124M. A sneaker-taste shock predicts exactly this.

What fails (the discriminating tests):
1. **Geography is inverse to challenger intensity.** North America is the epicenter of On, Hoka, Brooks and New Balance (On books 51% of its net sales in the US: CHF 1,550.5M of CHF 3,014.0M in 2025). Yet Nike North America had the *smallest* footwear decline (-8.4% FY24 to FY26) and footwear grew +5% in FY2026, faster than a flat US market (Circana: $90B, flat in 2025). Greater China, where the Western running challengers are smallest, had the largest decline (-24.6% in footwear).
2. **The broad categories were not shrinking when Nike fell hardest.** Nike NA footwear fell 13% in FY2025 (Jun 2024 to May 2025). Over roughly the same months US sport-lifestyle footwear *grew* (+3% in H1 2025, +4% for 2025) and running grew (+7%, then +9%) (Circana). Nike lost share *within* growing categories. That is a silhouette rotation plus Nike's deliberate classics cut, not a category collapse.
3. **Incumbents diverged under the same taste regime.** Adidas, an incumbent of Nike's type, grew North America brand revenue +10% currency-neutral (ex-Yeezy) in 2025 and +17% in Q2 2026; Puma (North America -16.7% in Q2 2026) and Under Armour (North America -8% in FY2026) shrank. Same consumers, same trend, opposite outcomes. Execution decided who rode the rotation.
4. **Nike wins where it re-executes, inside the trend itself.** In run specialty, the challengers' home channel, Nike dollars rose 35.4% in the 12 months to Sept 2025 while On fell 19.7% and Hoka 7.4% (Circana and Karnan via SGB, OPENED (repo)). NIKE Running added "roughly $1 billion" over five quarters and Performance reached $16B in FY2026 (Nike calls, OPENED (repo)). A force that takes share "regardless of execution" should not reverse in its own core category within four to six quarters of a product and channel fix.
5. **Durability is mixed, and the evidence points to rotation, not a one-way shift.** The US running challengers are decelerating sharply: Hoka from +23.6% (FY2025) to +15.9% (FY2026) to +7.7% (Apr to Jun 2026); On Americas +4.5% reported in Q2 2026; Adidas footwear +1% in Q2 2026 with Terrace cresting. Meanwhile the next wave (Asics SportStyle +~90% in North America and +100%+ in Europe in H1 2026; Salomon; Onitsuka Tiger) is accelerating. Euromonitor shows the same pattern a decade earlier: Nike lost 6.4 US share points in 2016 to 2019 to the Adidas Boost and Originals wave, then held roughly flat in 2021 to 2023 (GlobalData) on its own Dunk and AF1 retro wave. Taste rotates; incumbents gain or lose depending on whether their portfolio and pipeline sit on the rising wave.

**Independent cause or symptom?** Both. The direction of consumer taste (running, comfort, wellness, outdoor, retro-running and terrace, logo-light) is exogenous to Nike and would have happened anyway. But Nike's *exposure* to it is a symptom of the Consumer Direct Acceleration portfolio choices (classics concentration on Nike Digital, exit from run specialty, dissolved category teams), and the *speed* at which consumers abandoned the Dunk was partly caused by Nike's own oversupply (Berger and Heath 2008: tastes are abandoned when a signal spreads to unwanted groups; OPENED (repo)).

**Contribution (Section 6):** about **25% to 45% of the $4,964M FY2024 to FY2026 decline (central about 35%, roughly $1.7B)** is associated with Nike sitting on the losing side of the taste rotation, net of the roughly $1B Nike captured on the winning side. The part that a well-executed Nike would still have lost, the truly "regardless of execution" share, is lower: about **15% to 25%**. This overlaps heavily with `franchise_oversupply.md` (the classics fade) and is not additive with it.

**Fixable by management: partly.** Nike can and does participate in the rising families (Vomero, Pegasus Premium, P-6000, V5 Runner, Air Max, run specialty doors), and that is working. It cannot revive Dunk, AF1 or Chuck Taylor demand on command, cannot re-concentrate a market where On, Hoka, Asics, New Balance and Salomon now have permanent shelf and DTC businesses, and its scale is itself a handicap for "quiet" positioning.

## 3. Evidence in detail

### 3.1 The rotation: US share data (Euromonitor)
- **Euromonitor Passport (Dec 18, 2025), US athletic footwear company shares**, as reproduced by HGI (Marie Driscoll, "Have athletic footwear industry titans lost their step?", Jan 13, 2026, OPENED via curl). Parsed from the table (my reading of an interleaved table; the column order checks out as descending in each year):

| Company | 2016 | 2019 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nike | 40.5 | 34.1 | 28.9 |
| adidas | 8.6 | 11.0 | 10.1 |
| VF Corp (Vans etc.) | 8.8 | 9.4 | 3.9 |
| Skechers | 8.4 | 7.5 | 9.0 |
| New Balance | 4.0 | 3.6 | 6.4 |
| On AG | n/a (not top 10) | n/a | 6.3 |
| Deckers (Hoka, UGG) | n/a | n/a | 3.8 |
| Asics | 4.0 | 2.6 | 3.6 |
| Puma | n/a | 2.5 | 3.0 |
| Brooks | 1.6 | n/a | 2.8 |
| Under Armour | 3.4 | 2.7 | n/a |
| Top 10 total | 83.6 | 77.7 | 77.8 |

- HGI text: US athletic footwear "grew at a 6.6% CAGR 2011-2024, slowed to a 4.0% pace 2019-2024 and is projected to slow further to 2.9% in the 2024-2030 period according to Euromonitor"; Nike "has lost more 1160 basis points of market share in the past 10 years in the U.S.; globally Nike's share loss was 540 basis points, to 22.9%". Salomon "sits at #20 ... holding a 0.3% share".
- **Inferences (mine):**
  - 2019 to 2025: Nike -5.2 pts (-15% of its share), VF -5.5 pts (-59% of its share), adidas -0.9 pts. Gainers: On about +6, New Balance +2.8, Deckers about +2 to +3.8, Brooks about +1 to +2.8, Skechers +1.5, Asics +1.0. Roughly 12 to 16 points to running, comfort and heritage.
  - Proportionally, Nike lost *less* than VF. The legacy lifestyle incumbent most exposed to the fading silhouettes (Vans Old Skool, Authentic, Sk8-Hi) lost the most. That is consistent with a category force, and it means Nike was not uniquely punished.
  - Top-10 concentration fell in 2016 to 2019 (83.6 to 77.7) but was flat in 2019 to 2025 (77.8): the challengers replaced Under Armour, Wolverine and Fila inside the top 10 rather than fragmenting the market further.
  - Nike's biggest US share loss (6.4 points) came in 2016 to 2019, to the Adidas Boost, NMD, Stan Smith and Superstar wave, before On and Hoka were material. Nike then held about 35% in 2021 to 2023 (GlobalData, OPENED (repo) `dtc_wholesale.md`) on its own retro wave (Dunk, AF1, AJ1). Taste waves have moved share in both directions within a decade.

### 3.2 Category data (Circana, US)
- **H1 2025** (Retail Dive, Aug 19, 2025, OPENED): total footwear dollars -1%, units -2%; "Running-inspired shoes drove dollar sales up 3% in sport lifestyle, the largest footwear segment"; running and cross-training dollars +7%, units +9%.
- **Full year 2025** (Circana Q4 2025 Footwear Industry Pulse via MR Magazine, Feb 5, 2026, OPENED): US footwear "held steady in 2025 at $90 billion", units down low single digits. Sport lifestyle "concluded the year up 4% in dollar sales", "driven primarily by running-inspired and soccer/football-inspired styles" (the second phrase was in the search summary of the same article; the fetched text confirmed the +4%). Performance +6%, running +9%, walking +9%, fashion footwear -2%.
- **H1 2026** (Circana press release, Aug 17, 2026, OPENED): total +1%; performance +6% "coupled with an increase in units"; "Running shoes continued to lead gains, with category dollar and unit sales both climbing +13%"; "Growth in running-inspired styles was not enough to offset an overall decline in sport lifestyle footwear"; full-year 2026 expected "relatively flat".
- **Inference:** the broad taste trend (performance, running, walking, comfort) is unmistakable and accelerating. But sport lifestyle as a category *grew* through 2025. Nike's FY2025 NA footwear collapse (-13%) therefore came from losing share inside lifestyle, as demand rotated from basketball, court and canvas silhouettes to running-inspired and terrace ones, and from Nike's own deliberate classics cut. Only in 2026 did the lifestyle category itself shrink.

- The sequence is telling: Vans peaked first (FY2022) and fell first; Converse peaked in FY2023 and fell from FY2024; Nike classics turned in Q4 FY2024. The skate, canvas and court silhouette family faded over 2022 to 2026 across owners with different channel strategies. Vans had no SNKRS-style hype machine and still lost 42%.
- Within the same window, VF's own heritage outdoor brands grew (The North Face $3,612.7M FY2023 to $4,005.8M FY2026; Timberland roughly flat to up in FY2026), matching the "gorpcore" and outdoor leg of the taste shift.
- **Inference:** a meaningful part of the classics and Converse decline would have happened under any owner. This is the strongest evidence *for* the structural hypothesis.

### 3.5 Natural experiment 2: incumbents under the same taste regime
- **Adidas** (2025 annual report, OPENED): North America €5,087M (2024: €5,128M), +4% currency-neutral, +10% for brand adidas excluding Yeezy; Greater China €3,623M, +9% c-n; footwear €14,232M, +7% c-n (+12% brand adidas). 2024 (annual report, SNIPPET): revenue €23,683M vs €21,427M (2023); North America -2% in euros (Yeezy and cautious sell-in). Q2 2026 (EQS release, OPENED (repo)): NA +17%, Greater China +15%, footwear only +1%, Lifestyle +2%, Performance +39%.
- **Puma** Q2 2026 (TheIndustry.fashion, OPENED (repo)): organic -9.4%, North America -16.7%, footwear -11.7%.
- **Under Armour** FY2026 (8-K, OPENED (repo)): revenue -4%, North America -8%.
- **Inference:** under one taste regime, the incumbent that executed onto the rising family (Adidas Samba, Gazelle, Spezial, then Performance running) grew double digits, while incumbents that did not (Puma, Under Armour, Vans) shrank. The taste regime did not decide incumbents' outcomes. Execution did. Adidas's own footwear slowing to +1% in 2026 shows it is now exposed to the next rotation.

### 3.6 Natural experiment 3: the challengers' home turf
- **Run specialty, 12 months to Sept 2025** (Karnan and Circana via SGB, Dec 3, 2025, OPENED (repo)): Nike +35.4%; On -19.7%; Hoka -7.4%; Brooks -6.8%.
- **Nike calls (OPENED (repo) `innovation_drought.md`):** Vomero 18 "$100 million-plus franchise" in about 90 days (Q4 FY25); "5 consecutive quarters of double-digit growth in NIKE Running ... added roughly $1 billion" and "In FY '26, across Western Europe and North America, we gained 5 points of running market share in statement footwear" (Q4 FY26); "nearly tripled our share of the Max cushioning category" (Q1 FY27).
- **Asics in the same channel** (Asics H1 2026 consolidated summary, Aug 14, 2026, OPENED): "Sales for run specialty stores in North America increased +24.2% YoY on a currency-neutral basis." Both Nike and Asics grew there while On and Hoka slowed: the specialty channel rewards current product, not a fixed brand preference.
- **Inference:** the running and comfort taste shift is not hostile to Nike. Once Nike shipped competitive product into the channel, it took share from the very challengers the hypothesis says are structurally winning.

- **Asics** (H1 2026 summary, OPENED): H1 net sales ¥534.4B vs ¥402.7B (+32.7%, +22.0% cc). - **Inference:** the first wave (On and Hoka in US wholesale) is maturing; the second wave (Asics SportStyle, Onitsuka, Salomon, New Balance) is still accelerating. The direction (running heritage, outdoor, comfort, less logo) is persistent; the winners rotate. For Nike this means the hole left by the 2020 to 2023 lifestyle mix will not refill by itself, but it also means share is contestable each cycle.

### 3.8 Greater China: a different substitution
- Nike Greater China FY2024 $7,545M to FY2026 $5,847M (-$1,698M); footwear -24.6%, apparel -16% (FY2026 10-K p. 39).
- Premium specialist and outdoor brands grew through the slump: Amer Greater China +$1,017M (2023 to 2025; OPENED); On APAC CHF 141.1M to 511.1M (OPENED; includes Japan and Australia); Brooks China +245% (2025, SNIPPET); Descente and Kolon "close to 60%" and lululemon mainland China +29% (2025; Yicai, OPENED (repo) `china_market.md`). Mass domestic brands slowed (Anta main brand +3.7%, Li-Ning +3.2% in 2025; OPENED (repo)), and Adidas grew +9% c-n.
- Nike China Running had its "sixth quarter of growth" by Q1 FY27 while Greater China fell 26% c-n (OPENED (repo) `innovation_drought.md`). So the China hole is lifestyle, Jordan and price discipline, not running.
- **Inference:** a premium outdoor and specialist shift is real in China and is consistent with the hypothesis. But the Adidas control, the stalling of the mass guochao brands, the documented price war (industry discounts at about 50% to 70% of list), and Nike's own inventory and channel resets mean taste explains only part of China. I assign 20% to 35%.

### 3.9 "Quiet" and comfort apparel
- If a "quiet" or logo-light shift were hurting Nike broadly, apparel should fall too. NIKE Brand apparel fell only 2.4% FY24 to FY26 ($13,775M to $13,449M, data pack), and NA apparel grew. The "quiet" leg shows up in footwear branding (logo-light running and terrace shoes, Asics and Onitsuka), not in a broad apparel loss.
- Brand-level signals are consistent with a scale handicap: Kantar BrandZ 2026 has Zara overtaking Nike as the most valuable apparel brand; YouGov has Skechers (comfort) at 35.5 consideration vs Nike 36.3; Tim Derdenger (BBC, Oct 2, 2026) argues athletes now prefer smaller "change and growth" brands (all OPENED (repo) `analysts_and_consumer_data.md`). Piper Sandler: Nike teen footwear mindshare 61% (Fall 2023), 57% (Fall 2024), 46% (Fall 2025), "stabilizing sequentially"; Adidas among upper-income females rose from 3% to 14% (Fall 2024) on Samba and Gazelle (OPENED (repo)).

### 3.10 Is the taste shift partly caused by Nike?
- Berger and Heath (2008, *JPSP* 95(3)): people abandon tastes when the signal spreads to groups they do not want to be identified with (OPENED (repo) `academic_literature.md`). The Dunk went from scarce (Panda Dunk >$300 resale in 2021) to below retail by 2023 as Nike kept "continuously adding new styles" (OPENED (repo) `franchise_oversupply.md`). StockX: Dunk trades -41% YoY and Asics sales +589% in the first seven months of 2024 (Fashion Dive, OPENED (repo)). Over-distribution sped up the abandonment that the fashion cycle would have produced more slowly.
- Nike's absence from the rising categories was a choice: it exited about half its retail accounts and most run-specialty doors by 2021 (OPENED (repo) `dtc_wholesale.md`), Brooks held No. 1 in US adult performance running from Q1 2022 (OPENED (repo)), and Nike's head of men's running left to run Hoka in Feb 2024 (OPENED (repo) `org_talent.md`).

## 4. Strongest evidence for and against

**For (the taste shift is real, broad and partly outside Nike's control):**
1. Euromonitor US share 2019 to 2025: about 12 to 16 points to On, Deckers, New Balance, Brooks, Asics and Skechers; VF (-5.5 pts) lost more than Nike (-5.2 pts).
2. Vans -42% (FY2023 to FY2026), Converse -52% and Nike classics ">$4 billion" down: three owners, one silhouette family, one window.
3. Circana: running +7%, +9%, +13% (H1 2025, 2025, H1 2026); sport lifestyle growth "driven primarily by running-inspired" styles, then declining in 2026; Hill: "there's a lack of energy in the lifestyle space right now."
4. The decline is 94% footwear, the product where the challengers compete.
5. Second-wave challengers still accelerating in 2026 (Asics forecast +29.5%; SportStyle NA about +90%; Amer +32%), so the pressure is ongoing.
6. China: Amer Greater China +$1.0B in two years against Nike -$1.7B, while the mass domestic brands stalled.

**Against (it is not the primary cause and not execution-independent):**
1. Geography inverse to challenger intensity: NA footwear -8.4% (and +5% in FY2026) vs Greater China -24.6%.
2. Nike regained share in the challengers' own channel once product returned: run specialty +35.4% vs On -19.7% and Hoka -7.4%; +5 points in statement running in W. Europe and NA (FY2026).
3. Under the same taste regime, Adidas grew double digits (NA brand +10% c-n 2025, +17% Q2 2026) while Puma and Under Armour shrank: execution sorted the incumbents.
4. Sport lifestyle was a *growing* category (+3%, +4%) during Nike's sharpest fall (FY2025): within-category share loss and deliberate supply cuts, not a category collapse.
5. Timing: On and Hoka scaled in 2020 to 2023 while Nike set revenue records; the break in Q1 FY2025 coincides with Nike's own classics cut, not with a step change in challenger growth.
6. Rotation, not a one-way shift: On and Hoka are decelerating in US wholesale in 2026; Adidas footwear +1%; Nike itself lost 6.4 US points in 2016 to 2019 and then held share in 2021 to 2023.

## 5. Independent cause or symptom?

- **Exogenous part:** the direction of taste (performance running as everyday wear, comfort and wellness after COVID, outdoor and "gorpcore", retro-running and terrace silhouettes, logo-light design, Japanese heritage). Nike did not cause it and could not stop it. The Vans and Puma outcomes, Euromonitor's VF share collapse and the Circana category data show this.
- **Endogenous part (symptom of the Consumer Direct Acceleration model):**
  1. *Exposure.* Nike entered the rotation over-weighted in the fading family because the digital model was fed by Dunk, AF1 and AJ1 ("highest share of business" on Nike Digital, Q4 FY24).
  2. *Absence from the rising family.* The 2020 consumer construct dissolved the running category team; Nike exited run specialty and half its accounts; it ceded Fleet Feet, JD and Foot Locker shelf to Hoka and On.
  3. *Acceleration.* Oversupply turned a gradual fashion fade into a fast abandonment (Berger and Heath).
- So the hypothesis describes a real exogenous force whose *damage to Nike* was set by Nike's own portfolio and channel decisions. The upstream cause for the damage is the same one identified in the companion notes: the CDA strategy and its classics-heavy digital engine.

## 7. Can management fix it?

**Partly.**
- **Fixable (and already working):** participating in the rising families. Running is the proof: +$1B in five quarters, run-specialty share regained, Vomero 18 at $100M+ in 90 days, max-cushion share nearly tripled. Running-inspired lifestyle (P-6000, V5 Runner, Air Max) grew "strong double digits" in Q1 FY27. Nike's scale in R&D, athletes and distribution is an advantage in performance categories where product, not cool, decides.
- **Not fixable on demand:** reviving Dunk, AF1, AJ1 retro or Chuck Taylor demand; reversing a lifestyle category that Circana now shows declining; reclaiming the 12 to 16 US share points that On, Hoka, New Balance, Asics and Brooks have built with their own DTC and specialty businesses (Hoka DTC grew 9x and On's 14x over 2019/2020 to 2024, OPENED (repo) `dtc_wholesale.md`); and the scale handicap in "quiet" or insider positioning.
- **Timing:** the Adidas precedent (Gulden from 2023) shows an incumbent can ride a rotation within 12 to 24 months if the product exists. Nike's next lifestyle wave (Hill: more than a dozen new Sportswear styles in 2H FY27) is the test. Given that the current challenger leaders are already decelerating in US wholesale, the window is open, but the next wave (Asics SportStyle, Onitsuka, Salomon) is a moving target.

### R3. Where the hypothesis's predictions fail

**R3.1 The "one silhouette family, one window" natural experiment is actually staggered, and in its first phase Nike was the winner.** VF FY2023 10-K (OPENED): Vans fell from $4,161.9M (FY ended Apr 2, 2022) to $3,682.9M (FY ended Apr 1, 2023), -12%, Americas $2,640.8M to $2,380.5M (-10%), "driven by the performance in the direct-to-consumer channel". Over almost the same months (Jun 2022 to May 2023) Nike's NA footwear grew 22% and NIKE Brand footwear rose from $29,143M to $33,135M (data pack, FY2022 and FY2023 10-Ks), on the Dunk, a skate-derived silhouette that competes directly with Vans. Converse *grew* in FY2023 ($2,346M to $2,427M, +3.5%, data pack) while Vans fell. So Vans peaked in early 2022, Converse in mid-2023, Nike classics in 2024. A single exogenous taste wave against the whole skate/canvas/court family predicts a synchronized fall; the data instead show substitution *within* the family (Vans to Dunk) followed by brand-by-brand collapses that line up with each owner's own supply and marketing cycle. This weakens the investigator's strongest pro evidence.

**R3.2 Vans is a contaminated control.** VF's own FY2023 10-K blames DTC performance in the Americas and a 36% Greater China drop during COVID lockdowns (OPENED). The investigator concedes Vans "had its own execution problems" (Section 8) but still uses it to justify 50% to 75% taste attribution for Converse. A control with its own execution failure cannot separate taste from execution.

**R3.3 Converse: Nike's own filing calls it a reset, not a taste loss.** Nike FY2026 10-K (OPENED): "Converse is in the midst of a strategic reset of the brand and marketplace"; gross margin fell 490 bps on "brand and marketplace reset costs, including higher inventory obsolescence reserves"; "Lower ASP primarily reflects higher discounts and channel mix"; demand creation -39%. A brand whose owner cut marketing by 39% and is deliberately pulling inventory out of the marketplace will lose units in all territories with or without a taste shift. Also, 2025's fastest-growing lifestyle trend was low-profile, thin-sole shoes ("soccer/football-inspired styles", Circana, OPENED), which is the Chuck Taylor's own archetype; a pure taste story struggles to explain why a slim canvas classic lost 31% of units in the year slim classics were growing. I put Converse at 30% to 50% taste, not 50% to 75%.

**R3.4 The "comfort" and "quiet" legs are not durable in 2025 to 2026.** If a durable shift to comfort and quiet brands were structural, the leading comfort and quiet incumbents should be gaining in Nike's core market. They are not:
- Crocs Brand North America revenue **-6.8%** in 2025 ($1,710M) and HEYDUDE **-13.3%** ($715M), with $737M of HEYDUDE impairments (Crocs Q4 2025 release, 8-K Ex. 99.1, Feb 12, 2026, OPENED). Q2 2026: Crocs NA +0.4%, HEYDUDE -5.7% (Crocs Q2 2026 release, Jul 30, 2026, OPENED).
- lululemon (the archetypal logo-light "quiet" premium brand): Americas revenue **-1%** in FY2025, comparable sales **-3%** (release Mar 17, 2026, OPENED); Q2 FY2026 Americas **-8%**, comps **-12%** (release Sep 3, 2026, OPENED).
- Hoka +7.7% and On Americas +4.5% reported in mid-2026 (investigator's own data).
Taste is rotating through comfort and quiet brands as well, which contradicts a one-way structural shift and fits a fashion cycle in which any brand, Nike included, can be on the wrong or right side.

**R3.5 The winning "heritage" styles are incumbent classics, so the axis is product, not brand type.** Circana says 2025 sport-lifestyle growth came from "running-inspired and soccer/football-inspired styles" (OPENED). The terrace shoes (Samba, Gazelle, Spezial) are decades-old archives of a 75-year-old incumbent sold through the same wholesalers as Nike. The hypothesis lists "Adidas terrace" among the challengers, which quietly concedes that an incumbent with the right archive and supply discipline captured the shift. Puma, which also has terrace product, shrank (NA -16.7% Q2 2026, investigator's data). Same taste, same product archetype, opposite outcomes: execution decided, not a force acting "regardless" of it.

**R3.6 Correction to one of the investigator's "against" points (it is weaker than stated).** SGB (Dec 3, 2025, OPENED): the run-specialty channel grew only 3.1% (Karnan), the top five brands combined fell 7.4%, and Nike's +35.4% took it only from No. 9 to No. 8. So the run-specialty figure is a small-base gain, not proof that Nike took material share from On and Hoka. The more relevant figure in the same article is that in Circana all-channel running (excluding DTC) Nike became the **third-largest seller, passing On**, at an $83 ASP versus On $143 and Hoka $137, with Brooks and Hoka at about 23% each. That still refutes "regardless of execution" (Nike can win running volume when it ships product), but it also shows Nike's running recovery is at lower price points, so the dollar offset is smaller than the share gain implies.

**R3.7 Geography and timing still point away from the challengers.**
- Q1 FY2027 (data pack, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 22): NA $5,127M vs $5,020M (+2.1%), EMEA -4.7%, APLA -1.8%; Nike Brand ex-China about -0.8% (my arithmetic). Greater China -22% reported (-26% c-n) and Converse -28%. The live decline is concentrated in the market where Western challengers are smallest and in a brand under deliberate reset.
- NIKE Brand footwear: FY24 $33,427M, FY25 $29,510M, FY26 $29,525M (data pack). The entire NIKE Brand footwear decline happened in one year (FY25), the year Nike cut classics supply, and then stopped while running challengers and Asics SportStyle kept growing. A structural force that keeps operating should keep taking revenue.
- Nike FY2026 10-K (OPENED) attributes Greater China to "declining store traffic, elevated promotional activity and higher levels of inventory across the marketplace" and wholesale cuts "to prioritize marketplace health"; none of this is a taste mechanism.

**R3.8 China substitution comparison is mismatched by product.** Nike's China decline FY24 to FY26 is 80% footwear (-$1,364M of -$1,698M). Amer's Greater China growth is led by Arc'teryx Technical Apparel (Amer 20-F FY2025, OPENED: Arc'teryx's geographic growth is "grounded in its historical momentum in North America and Greater China", 79 owned stores there; Salomon footwear "brand awareness is steadily developing" in Greater China). An apparel-led gain is not evidence that Chinese consumers replaced Nike shoes, and Nike China apparel fell only $293M. The "about 60% of Nike's China decline" equivalence is a coincidence of magnitudes, not a measured substitution. Nike China running also grew for six straight quarters (repo) while total China fell, so in China Nike is on the *winning* side of the running leg.

**R3.9 Euromonitor share timing.** The confirmed table shows Nike lost more US share in 2016 to 2019 (-6.4 pts, before On and Hoka were material) than in 2019 to 2025 (-5.2 pts). Bridged with GlobalData's flat ~35% for 2021 to 2023 (repo, `dtc_wholesale.md`), most of Nike's post-2019 loss falls in 2024 to 2025, after On and Hoka had already scaled and while they were decelerating. Of the roughly 17 points gained by On, Deckers, New Balance, Brooks, Skechers, Asics and Puma in 2019 to 2025, Nike supplied about 5 (my arithmetic on the table); VF, Under Armour, Wolverine and Fila supplied most of the rest. The challengers fed mostly on weaker incumbents, not on Nike.
