# Root cause test: cross-cutting timeline forensics, FY2017 to Q1 FY2027

Research date: 2026-10-13. Hypothesis under test: *A quarter-by-quarter timeline of Nike decisions against metric inflection points shows which proposed causes the TIMING supports and which it rules out.*

Status labels: **OPENED** = I read the document myself in this session. **OPENED (repo)** = opened by an earlier researcher and recorded in this repo's notes (file named); reused, not re-fetched. **SNIPPET** = seen only in a search summary. Numbers for Q3 FY2023 onward and all annual figures come from `data/nike_filing_facts.csv`, `data/nike_channel_split.csv` and `research_notes/04_financial_data_pack.md` (verified there with filing and page). Quarterly figures for FY2016 Q4 to FY2023 Q2 I extracted myself from the earnings-release exhibits (8-K Ex. 99.1) downloaded from EDGAR in this session (list in section 9). Nike also filed its earnings-call transcripts as 8-K exhibits ("ercc") from Q1 FY2017 to Q4 FY2018; I downloaded and searched all eight. All growth rates labelled "my arithmetic" are computed from those reported dollar values.

## 1. Bottom line

**The timing identifies one primary chain and rules out most of the popular single-cause stories.**

1. **The first metric to break was NIKE Brand Digital, four quarters before total revenue.** Digital growth (currency-neutral) went +34% (Q2 FY23), +24% (Q3 FY23), +14% (Q4 FY23), then **+2% (Q1 FY24, Jun to Aug 2023)**, +1%, -4%, -10% (Q4 FY24), -20% (Q1 FY25). In the same window Nike-owned stores were still growing (+24% Q4 FY23, +6% FY24) and wholesale was flat to up (+1%, -3%, +3% c.n. in Q1 to Q3 FY24). Total revenue only went negative in Q4 FY24 (-1.7%) and fell off a cliff in Q1 FY25 (-10.4%). A cause must therefore be one that hits Nike's own digital channel first, in mid-2023.
2. **The signal sequence matches the classics-oversupply chain inside the Consumer Direct Acceleration (CDA) model, in the right order and with plausible lags:** CDA launched (June 2020, "lead with NIKE Digital") and classics fed digital ("continuously adding new styles", Sept 2020) -> COVID scarcity hid oversupply (FY21 to FY22, Friend: demand "significantly exceed[s] available inventory supply", Mar 2022) -> resale premiums collapse (Panda Dunk from >$300 in 2021 to <=$150 by Feb 2023; AJ1 premium 61% to 4%, 2020 to 2023) -> inventory glut and liquidation (inventory +44% at Aug 31, 2022; FY23 gross margin -250 bps; North America +27% to +30% in Q2 and Q3 FY23 on liquidation) -> digital decelerates (summer 2023) -> management first flags "managing our franchises" (Dec 21, 2023) and "pulling back supply of classics" (Mar 2024) -> Lifestyle declines for the first time (Q4 FY24) -> cliff (Q1 FY25, classics on Digital "down nearly 50%"). No other candidate has an onset that lines up with every step.
3. **Timing rules out, as the trigger:** macro and the COVID hangover (the industry fell in calendar 2022 to 2023 while Nike grew 10% in FY23); the 2021 Xinjiang boycott (Greater China grew +8% c.n. in FY24 before breaking in Q1 FY25); tariffs (April 2025, three quarters after the cliff); Elliott Hill (started Oct 14, 2024, after the June 27, 2024 guidance cut and the Q1 FY25 quarter itself); wholesale account cuts as a direct revenue trigger (cuts ran 2017 to 2022, wholesale grew in FY23 and Nike was already reopening accounts in Oct 2023 before the decline); and the 2020 marketing shift as a leading cause (teen mindshare peaked at 61% in 2023, three years after the shift).
4. **Timing supports, as secondary and partly independent causes:** a canvas and classic-lifestyle fashion cycle (Converse turned negative in Q1 FY24, one year before NIKE Brand, matching Vans which fell from 2022); Nike-specific execution in Greater China (break synchronous with the global classics break, then a separate, deepening slide to -26% c.n. in Q1 FY27 while North America grows); and the innovation gap from the 2020 reorganization (running share lost from Q1 2022, a 2 to 4 year product lag, but it sits in categories that are now growing).
5. **A 2017 to 2018 precedent shows what a pure "classics tightening" costs and how long it lasts.** After the June 15, 2017 Consumer Direct Offense, North America fell -3%, -5%, -6% for three quarters (Q1 to Q3 FY18), which management tied to "undifferentiated wholesale" and "our quick and deliberate tightening of the distribution of select styles within the Jordan Brand" (Q3 FY18 call, filed as 8-K exhibit Mar 27, 2018). It returned to growth in Q4 FY18 (+3%) on new innovation (VaporMax, Air Max 270, React). **The same thing happened this time in North America:** four negative quarters (Q1 to Q4 FY25), then growth from Q1 FY26 (+4.0%, +8.8%, +3.3%, +2.7%, +2.1%). So the classics reset behaves like a 4-quarter shock where Nike's model is otherwise intact. **The persistence of the decline into FY27 is a Greater China and Converse phenomenon, plus EMEA promotions, not a North America one.**
6. **The stock decline is a separate, earlier story.** NKE fell from $177.51 (Nov 5, 2021 peak) to $83.12 (Sept 30, 2022), -53%, while revenue was still rising. Only after Dec 2023 does the price track the revenue breakdown. Roughly half the 80% drawdown predates any revenue decline (multiple compression, the inventory glut and China lockdowns).

**Verdict for the method:** timing points to the CDA model's classics-on-digital chain as the primary cause of the FY25 onset (my estimate 50% to 60% of the FY24 to FY26 decline), with Greater China execution (20% to 25%) and the canvas cycle at Converse (about 10%) as the main independent contributors. Macro, tariffs, leadership change, the boycott and the account cuts cannot be the trigger because they come at the wrong time.

## 5. Strongest evidence for and against the timing reading

### For
1. **Digital leads by four quarters, inside a growing company.** Digital went from +24% to +2% between Q3 FY23 and Q1 FY24 while stores grew 24% and wholesale was flat; this channel signature is unique to the classics-on-digital mechanism (Q4 FY24 call: classics had "their highest share of business" on Nike Digital, OPENED (repo)).
2. **Gross margin rose 120 bps in Q1 FY25, the quarter revenue fell 10.4%.** The cliff was therefore not driven by discounting; it was a volume pullback (planned classics cuts and lost digital traffic). Discounting and the GM collapse (to 40.3% in Q4 FY25) followed the cliff, which makes discounting a symptom in timing terms.
3. **Inventory was lean when the cliff came** (-10% to -14% YoY through FY24). An inventory overhang cannot explain the FY25 onset; it explains the FY23 episode, which Nike grew through.
4. **The FY18 precedent and the FY25 to FY26 North America path match** (three to four negative quarters, then growth), consistent with a supply reset in an otherwise working machine.
5. **Converse leads NIKE Brand by a year**, which separates the canvas cycle from Nike's DTC choices (Converse sat outside the Men's/Women's/Kids construct).

### Against (or limits)
1. **Timing cannot separate two causes that start together.** The classics chain, the innovation gap and the 2020 reorg all date to mid-2020 and all predict a 2023 to 2024 symptom. The category test (Lifestyle fell, Performance grew in Q4 FY24) helps, but within Lifestyle "fatigue" and "no newness" are the same event seen from two sides.
2. **The resale collapse was partly market-wide** (rates rose, COVID scarcity ended; Axios Feb 2023). Part of the 2022 to 2023 signal is exogenous, so the chain's first link is shared with the market.
3. **Management's own supply cuts are a decision, not a measured demand loss.** From Q3 FY24 the revenue line partly records Nike's choice to remove $4B+ of classics. Timing proves the order of events, not the size of the counterfactual decline.
4. **Greater China's post-FY25 path breaks from the global pattern** and needs China-specific explanation (`china.md`); timing alone cannot apportion it.
5. **Pre-FY2019 quarterly totals for EMEA and APLA in the old geography structure** were not reconstructed, so the FY18 precedent rests on North America and Greater China only.

## 6. Independent cause or symptom?

Timeline forensics is a method, not a cause. What it shows is that **the proximate events (digital collapse, discounting, teen mindshare loss, executive churn, layoffs, margin squeeze) are all downstream in time of one upstream decision set taken in 2020: the CDA operating model** (lead with Digital, feed it with high-volume classics, shift marketing to paid capture, dissolve category teams), which COVID scarcity masked for two years. Two causes have onsets that do not depend on it: the canvas fashion cycle (Converse) and, after FY25, Greater China's deeper execution and market problems.

## 7. Contribution estimate (FY2024 to FY2026, -$4,964M, plus the Q1 FY27 leg)

Inference, wide error bars, non-additive overlaps netted:
- **CDA classics-on-digital chain (incl. its promotion and innovation-newness components): about 50% to 60% (about $2.5B to $3.0B).** Basis: Digital -$3.5B FY24 to FY26 (about 71% of the decline), classics ">$4B" from peak, partly offset by Performance growth; the North America segment, where the chain was purest, recovered once supply reset.
- **Greater China execution beyond the shared chain: about 20% to 25% ($1.0B to $1.2B)**, and about 65% of the Q1 FY27 decline ($332M of $507M).
- **Converse canvas cycle: about 10% ($0.45B to $0.6B of Converse's -$908M).**
- **Macro: about 5% to 15%**, rising in FY27.
- **Account cuts, tariffs, leadership change, boycott as revenue triggers: about 0% by timing** (they matter for share, margin or China's ceiling, not the onset).

## 8. Fixable by management?

**Partly.** The timing evidence says the core mechanism is fixable and has been fixed once already in the market where it was purest: North America returned to growth four quarters after the classics reset, exactly as in FY18. What timing shows is not yet fixed: Greater China (still accelerating down, Nike says "beyond fiscal 2027"), Converse (a fashion cycle Nike cannot reverse, only wait out or reposition), the Jordan retro reset (only started Oct 2026), and EMEA, which relapsed when it lapped promotions. The new risk the timeline highlights is reorganization fatigue: there has been a structural reset in every year from 2020 to 2026, and Pace runs to FY2031.
