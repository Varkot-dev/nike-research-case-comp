# Root cause test: do China-specific forces explain the Greater China collapse?

Research date: 2026-10-13. Hypothesis under test: *China-specific forces (guochao national-tide preference, Anta/Li-Ning/Xtep competition, the 2021 Xinjiang cotton boycott, the Chinese consumption slump, and Nike China channel and discount problems) explain the Greater China collapse.*

Source status labels:
- **REPO**: verified number already in `data/nike_filing_facts.csv` or `research_notes/04_financial_data_pack.md` (filing and printed page given there).
- **REPO-NOTE**: taken from an earlier repo note (`Nike decline execution vs structural/china_market.md`), with the status that note recorded.
- **OPENED**: I read the document text myself. Many primary documents (Nike IR call transcripts, Nike 10-K text, HKEX filings, adidas and ASICS results) had already been downloaded to this session's scratchpad by earlier steps of this workflow; I read them in full text in this session. National Bureau of Statistics pages and the adidas annual report page were fetched today.
- **SNIPPET**: seen only in a search-result summary. Treat as a lead.

Method note: the hypothesis bundles two different kinds of cause. I test them separately, because they have opposite implications:
- **(A) External China forces**: guochao, local brand competition, the 2021 boycott, the consumption slump. Management cannot fix these.
- **(B) Nike's own China channel and discount problems**: distributor online price war, aged inventory, an unrefreshed partner store fleet, reduced local staff. Management can fix these.

---

## 1. Bottom line

1. **Greater China is a big piece of the decline, but not because of China's market.** Greater China fell from $7,545M (FY24) to $5,847M (FY26), down 22.5% (REPO, FY24 10-K p. 38, FY26 10-K p. 36). That $1,698M is **34%** of Nike's $4,964M revenue decline, from a segment that was only 14.7% of revenue. In Q1 FY27 it was **65%** of the decline ($332M of $507M). Excluding Greater China, the NIKE Brand fell 5.7% over FY24 to FY26 and only 0.8% in Q1 FY27 (my arithmetic on REPO figures).
2. **The external China forces (A) fail most of their tests.**
   - *Timing:* the 2021 boycott hit Nike for about two months. Nike China then grew 4% (FY23) and 8% (FY24) currency-neutral, and the collapse began three years after the boycott.
   - *Macro:* the China market grew while Nike shrank. Sports and entertainment goods retail rose 11.1% (2024) and 15.7% (2025). Apparel and footwear retail rose 0.3% and 3.2%. Nike China fell 12% and 13% currency-neutral in FY25 and FY26.
   - *Guochao:* foreign brands are the winners, not the losers. adidas China grew 13% (2025) and 16% (H1 2026), and ASICS China grew 19.9% (2025). The domestic mass brands grew only low single digits (Anta brand +4.8%, Li-Ning +2.8% in H1 2026).
   - *Local competition:* Nike China Running, the category where Xtep, Li-Ning and Anta are technically strongest, has grown for six straight quarters while Sportswear and Jordan collapsed.
3. **Nike's own China channel and discount problems (B) pass every test.**
   - *Digital:* Greater China digital sales fell 22% (FY25), 29% (FY26) and 36% (Q2 FY26).
   - *Margin:* Greater China gross margin fell from 50.2% to 45.7%, while adidas China's rose from 49.6% to 52.6%.
   - *Partners:* Nike's largest partner, Topsports (86.7% of revenue from Nike and adidas), cut gross margin from 41.8% to 38.0% and saw revenue fall 6.6%, 4.7%, then low teens.
   - *Self-diagnosis:* Hill said in Dec 2025: "we've become a lifestyle brand competing on price in China. We also reduced the feet on the ground... our stores aren't compelling."
   - *Fix:* Nike's chosen fix is to cut distributor online sales from 2027-01-01, which is a channel fix, not a geopolitical one.
4. **Channel B is mostly a symptom, not an independent cause.**
   - It is the China version of Nike's global errors: overreliance on lifestyle classics and Jordan, Consumer Direct Acceleration headcount cuts that "reduced the feet on the ground", digital discounting, and an aged store fleet.
   - China's structure amplified those errors. Nike's China business runs through more than 5,000 partner-operated monobrand stores, and distributors run their own online storefronts on a digital-first, promotion-heavy marketplace.
   - The current -26% figure is also partly self-inflicted on purpose: management is deliberately reducing sell-in.
5. **Estimate (section 5):**
   - External China forces explain about **$0.3B to $0.6B** of the FY24 to FY26 decline, about **6% to 12% of Nike's total decline**.
   - Nike's China channel, discount and inventory reset explains about **$1.1B to $1.4B**, about **22% to 28%**. Part of that overlaps with the global classics and Jordan oversupply estimate in `franchise_oversupply.md`.
   - **Fixable: partly.** The channel and discount part is fixable, but slowly, and Nike says the drag extends "beyond fiscal 2027". The external part (a structurally lower ceiling for foreign incumbents since 2021) is not fixable.

**Verdict: contributing cause.** As stated, the hypothesis is half right. China is a real and large part of the decline. But the *China-specific external* forces it names are minor. The part that works is Nike's own execution in China, which is a symptom of upstream Nike-wide errors.

## 3. Evidence in detail

### 3.1 Timing: the boycott recovered, and the collapse is a 2024 event

- **Q4 FY21 call (June 2021, OPENED):** "After a strong March, our business in Greater China was impacted in April and we adjusted our operations by suspending marketing activities and product launches. We then began to see a recovery trend, improving to a single digit decline in May and sequentially improving into June, with month to date retail sales trends approaching prior year levels." Greater China grew 9% c-n in that quarter, and Nike said the region "delivered its 7th consecutive year of double-digit growth."
- **Q1 and Q2 FY22 calls (OPENED):** FY22 weakness was attributed to "regional closures and lower levels of foot traffic due to COVID containment" and to lost production in Vietnam, because Greater China "experienced a decline in units sold in the second quarter due to lost production and lower available inventory supply." Q2 FY22 was -24% c-n.
- **SCMP, 2021-03-26 (OPENED, archived copy, headline and lead only):** "Anta, Li Ning shares surge as Chinese consumers back domestic brands in Xinjiang cotton row."
- **Boycott size, Morningstar estimate (SNIPPET, REPO-NOTE):** in April 2021, Tmall sales fell 59% for Nike and 78% for adidas, while Li-Ning rose more than ninefold.
- **Recovery (REPO):** Greater China grew +25% c-n (Q4 FY23), +12% (Q1 FY24), +8% (Q2 FY24), +6% (Q3 FY24) and +7% (Q4 FY24). FY24 revenue was $7,545M with EBIT of $2,309M, close to FY23's $2,283M.
- **Collapse (REPO):** -3% (Q1 FY25), -11%, -15%, -20%, -10%, -16%, -10%, -17%, -26% (Q1 FY27), c-n.
- **Inference:** the boycott and the 2021 to 2022 guochao peak caused a sharp but temporary shock. Nike then rebuilt to within 9% of its FY21 peak. The 2024 to 2026 collapse needs a different trigger.

Caveats:

- Even so, no reading of this data shows a market that fell 12% to 26% a year.

Supporting data:
- Total retail sales of consumer goods grew 3.5% (2024, Topsports FY24/25 results, OPENED), 3.7% (2025, STCN, OPENED) and 1.3% (H1 2026, Xtep and Anta interims, OPENED). This is a slow consumer economy, but not a collapsing one.
- Nike's own Q1 FY25 call (OPENED) did cite macro: "retail sales moderated across the industry, and NIKE was not immune." That macro factor is real at the margin.

### 3.3 Guochao and domestic competition: foreign premium brands are winning

- **adidas:**
  - Greater China Q1 2026 €1,135M, +17% c-n; Q2 2026 €953M, +15% c-n, "reflecting continued strong market share gains"; H1 2026 +16% (adidas Q1 and Q2 2026 results PDFs, OPENED).
  - 2025: €3,623M (2024: €3,459M), adidas brand +13% c-n, gross margin 52.6% vs 49.6%, "due to reduced discounting" (adidas Annual Report 2025, Greater China page, OPENED today). In 2025 Greater China grew faster than Europe (+10%) and North America (+10%) (adidas FY2025 release, OPENED).
- **ASICS:** Greater China net sales +19.9% to ¥120,514M in FY2025. "despite concerns about economic weakness, sales in Greater China remained robust." The FY2026 forecast is +16.2% (ASICS FY2025 summary, 2026-02-13, OPENED).
- **Anta Group, H1 2026:** revenue +12.9% to RMB43,507M, but the main ANTA brand grew only +4.8% to RMB17.77B, while FILA grew +6.1% (Anta interim 2026, 2026-08-26, OPENED). For FY2025, the ANTA brand grew +3.7% and FILA +6.9% (Anta FY2025 annual results, OPENED). Anta itself writes that consumption pressure is "weighing on growth for certain international sportswear brands" and that "international brands accelerate their expansion in the Chinese market."
- **Li-Ning, H1 2026:** revenue +2.8% to RMB15,235M (Li-Ning interim, 2026-08-20, OPENED).
- **361°, H1 2026:** revenue +8.0% to RMB6.16B (361° interim, 2026-08-18, OPENED).
- **Xtep, H1 2026:** professional sports segment (Saucony) +11.4%. Xtep holds the top marathon wear rate in China, for example 54% in Xiamen (Xtep interim, 2026-08-25, OPENED).
- **Historical (OPENED, Chinanews 2022-06-17):**
  - adidas Greater China fell 15.9%, 14.6% and 24.3% in Q2 to Q4 2021, and 35% in Q1 2022.
  - Anta's 2021 revenue of RMB49.32B was close to Nike Greater China's $8.29B.
  - A consultant was quoted: "年轻人也不再迷恋'洋品牌'，'国潮'兴起" ("young people no longer idolise 'foreign brands'; guochao is rising").
- **Inference:**
  - Guochao was the 2021 to 2022 story, and it hit adidas harder than Nike.
  - In 2024 to 2026 the growth in China goes to differentiated premium and specialist brands, foreign or Anta-owned (adidas, ASICS, lululemon, Descente, Kolon, Saucony), not to the mass guochao brands.
  - Nike is losing share to *both* foreign and local players. Hill (Q3 FY25 call, OPENED): "The competition is a bit more aggressive than I had... when I remembered it 4.5 years ago."
  - That is a competitive-position failure, not a nationalist rejection of foreign brands.

### 3.4 Category: performance grows, lifestyle collapses (the same pattern as everywhere else)

- **FY25 10-K (OPENED, REPO p. 40):** "Greater China revenues decreased 12% on a currency-neutral basis due to lower revenues in Men's, the Jordan Brand, Women's and Kids'... Footwear revenues decreased 13%... Unit sales of footwear decreased 11%."
- **FY26 10-K (OPENED, REPO p. 39):** "Greater China revenues decreased 13%... Wholesale revenues decreased 14%, reflecting our actions to prioritize marketplace health. NIKE Direct revenues decreased 12% due to declines in digital sales of 29%... Footwear revenues decreased 15%... Unit sales of footwear decreased 14%."
- **Footwear vs the rest (my arithmetic on REPO):** footwear $5,552M to $4,188M (-24.6%) vs apparel plus equipment $1,993M to $1,659M (-16.8%), FY24 to FY26.
- **Calls (OPENED):**
  - Q3 FY26: "Running grew double digits... growth in tennis, golf and ACG... Sportswear declined double digits as expected."
  - Q1 FY27: "if you were to exclude the reset in Greater China, NIKE Performance would have been up low double digits."
  - Q1 FY25: "there was definitely strong demand for the classic franchises in China. Jordan is a big business in Greater China."
- **Resale (REPO-NOTE, 21jingji 2026-10-05, opened):** Jordan retros trade on Dewu at about 53% to 55% of retail.
- **Inference:** China shows the *same* category pattern as North America and EMEA: performance up, sportswear and classics down. That points upstream to the global franchise and lifestyle problem (`franchise_oversupply.md`), amplified in China by deeper Jordan and lifestyle exposure and a sneaker resale market that has deflated.

### 3.5 Channel and discount: Nike's own diagnosis

**Hill, Q2 FY26 call, Dec 2025 (OPENED):**
> "the reality is we've become a lifestyle brand competing on price in China. We also reduced the feet on the ground, people on the ground. As you know, it's a mono-b[rand marketplace] ... we weren't making the investment in our store fleet, so our stores aren't compelling... the cycle that it started to feed itself, soft demand leading to consistent promotions and then impacting profitability."

**Other Nike calls (OPENED):**
- **Friend, Q2 FY26:** "These challenges resulted in a higher mix of off-price sales with higher markdowns, higher sales-related returns, higher wholesale discounts and higher obsolescence charges."
- **Friend, Q4 FY25:** "Greater China executed a deeper reset of inventory relative to our other geographies with higher sales-related reserves, higher discounts and supply reductions."
- **Friend, Q3 FY25:** "given that it's a monobrand market... it's going to take us time."
- **Hill, Q1 FY26:** "With over 5,000 mono-brand stores in China, this will take investment, and it will take time." He also said "Digital remains a highly promotional marketplace in Greater China with consumer shopping moments extending longer on local platforms with deeper discounts."
- **Hill, Q1 FY27:** "The majority of our partners' physical doors in Greater China have not been refreshed in the past 7 years." On distributor online sales: "We are eliminating distribution through channels that are not aligned with our marketplace strategy, which will decrease the deep discounting of our brands."

**Partner filings (OPENED):**
- **Topsports HKEX filings:**
  - FY2024/25: revenue RMB27,012.9M, -6.6%; gross margin 41.8% to 38.4%; 5,020 stores, down 18.3%.
  - FY2025/26: revenue RMB25,739.9M, -4.7%; gross margin 38.0%. "Principal brands" (Nike and adidas) were 86.7% of revenue, down 4.2%. "fierce promotional activities in China's online sports footwear and apparel market... led to deeper year-on-year discounts, which served as the main drag on gross margin."
  - Q1 FY2026/27 (Mar to May 2026): "low-teens decline"; selling area -11.2% YoY.
  - 2026-07-22: Nike's online termination is "significant", because Nike online was "approximately 22% of the total revenue of the Group."
- **Pou Sheng, 2026-07-22:** Nike online was "approximately 15% of the total revenue" but its "contribution to the Group's profit was insignificant". That is consistent with Nike online being sold at clearance margins.

**Press:**
- **Xinhua, reprinting Beijing Youth Daily, 2026-07-24 (OPENED):**
  - "一双鞋在同一电商平台的不同旗舰店售价相差400多元" ("one shoe's price differs by more than ¥400 across flagship stores on the same platform").
  - Consumers could always find ¥600 to ¥700 running shoes in the Topsports and Pou Sheng online stores.
  - A long-time Nike buyer said it now "只觉得不值" ("just feels not worth it").
- **Shidai Caijing via Winshang, 2023-09-27 (OPENED):** Nike outlets in China were already clearing at 55% of list in 2023, and a distributor said new-product discounts had deepened from 90% of list (2019) to 85%. The discount problem predates the revenue collapse.

**Contrast with adidas:**
- adidas China gross margin rose 3 points in 2025 on "reduced discounting" (OPENED).
- Nike China's fell 4.5 points over FY24 to FY26 (REPO).
- Same market, same partner (Topsports sells both), opposite outcomes.

### 3.6 Where the -26% in Q1 FY27 comes from

- **Q3 FY26 call (OPENED):** "declines in Greater China driven by intentional reduced sell-in and marketplace management actions."
- **Q4 FY26 call (OPENED):** the decision "to sell in less inventory into the marketplace in order to create a more full-price business."
- **Q1 FY27 call (OPENED), Denton:** the guidance "assumes that China actually gets worse from a revenue perspective for the balance of this year."
- **Inventory (REPO):** Greater China inventory was $951M (May 2025), $793M (May 2026) and $853M (Aug 2026).
- **Inference:** the latest quarters overstate consumer demand loss. Sell-through proxies (Topsports -4.7%, comparable stores -6% in FY26) suggest consumer offtake of Nike in China fell mid-to-high single digits a year, while sell-in fell in the low teens to mid-twenties. That gap is destocking plus the planned exit from distributor online sales. Both are management choices.

## 4. Is this independent, or a symptom?

**Independent China-only forces (real, but small for 2024 to 2026):**
- A post-2021 structural ceiling for foreign incumbents. Both Nike and adidas are still 21% to 29% below their 2021 China peaks.
- A 2026 consumption softness of a few points.
- Mass-tier local brands with improved technology.

**Symptoms of upstream, Nike-wide causes (most of the 2024 to 2026 collapse):**
1. **Lifestyle and classics overreliance.** China had heavy Jordan and lifestyle exposure, and the resale bubble deflated. This is the same mechanism as `franchise_oversupply.md`.
2. **Consumer Direct Acceleration era choices:**
   - "reduced the feet on the ground" (Hill).
   - Digital-led growth that, in China, ran through distributor storefronts on Tmall, JD and Douyin. This created a price war Nike did not control.
   - Partner doors not refreshed for 7 years.
3. **A slow inventory response.** Elevated inventory was first flagged in Q1 FY25, but the deepest reset came in Q4 FY25 to Q4 FY26.

China's structure (more than 5,000 partner-run monobrand stores, distributor-run online stores, and a digital-first, promotion-heavy marketplace) turned those global errors into a bigger and longer decline than elsewhere. It also slows the fix, because Nike must act "with and through partners" (Hill, Q2 FY26).

## 5. Quantification

Reference figures (REPO plus my arithmetic):
- Greater China FY24 to FY26: -$1,698M, which is 34.2% of the -$4,964M total decline.
- Q1 FY27: Greater China -$332M of a -$507M total decline (65%).
- Greater China EBIT: $2,309M to $1,278M (-45%).

Counterfactuals for Greater China, FY24 base $7,545M:

| Counterfactual for Nike China FY26 | Implied gap vs actual $5,847M | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Flat (market roughly flat for Nike-type products) | $1,698M | All of the decline is underperformance vs the market |
| Grow with domestic mass brands (+3.5% a year) | about $2,235M | Underperformance larger than the decline |
| Track Nike's other regions (ex-China NIKE Brand -5.7% over two years) | about $1,268M Nike China-specific excess; about $430M shared with global Nike issues | |
| Add a structural foreign-brand headwind of 2 to 4 share points a year | about $0.3B to $0.6B attributable to external China forces | My estimate of the external part |

My estimate of the FY24 to FY26 decline:
- **External China forces (guochao, local competition, boycott legacy, 2026 macro softness):** about **$0.3B to $0.6B**, which is **6% to 12% of Nike's total decline** (18% to 35% of China's).
- **Nike China channel, discount and inventory reset, including deliberate sell-in cuts:** about **$1.1B to $1.4B**, which is **22% to 28% of Nike's total decline**.
  - Overlap warning: roughly the China share of Jordan and classics shrinkage is also counted in `franchise_oversupply.md`'s $2B to $3B. Do not add the two estimates.
- **Error band:** wide. Nike does not disclose China sell-through or category splits. The NBS categories are broad. adidas's growth is partly recovery from a low 2022 base.

Fixability:
- **Channel and discount: partly fixable.** The exit from distributor online sales on 2027-01-01, store resets, local product creation (first collection October 2026, a full local pipeline from holiday 2027) and a new GM (Cathy Sparks, January 2026) are all controllable levers. But Nike guides that China gets worse through FY27 and that the drag lasts "beyond fiscal 2027" (REPO, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 20). Removing 22% of Topsports' revenue base also cuts Nike's sell-in mechanically.
- **External ceiling: not fixable.** Even a successful reset probably returns Nike to a lower share than its 2020 position (Euromonitor 25.6%, SNIPPET).

## 6. Strongest evidence

**For the hypothesis (China-specific forces explain the collapse):**
1. Greater China fell 22.5% vs 5.7% for the rest of the NIKE Brand, so something China-specific is happening.
2. Hill's own diagnosis ("lifestyle brand competing on price in China"), together with the distributor online price war (Topsports gross margin down 3.8 points; ¥400 price gaps for the same shoe), is a China-specific channel failure.
3. Both foreign incumbents are still 21% to 29% below their 2021 China peaks, which fits a structural guochao ceiling.
4. 2026 sports goods retail is negative (-3.7% Jan to Aug).

**Against (the external China forces):**
1. adidas China +13% (2025) and +16% (H1 2026), and ASICS China +19.9%, in the same market. The difference-in-differences gap is about -20 points, specific to Nike.
2. Nike China grew 8% c-n in FY24, three years after the boycott. The collapse starts in mid-2024.
3. The China sports goods market grew 11% to 16% in 2024 and 2025 while Nike fell 12% to 13%.
4. Nike China Running grew for six straight quarters, in the category where Chinese brands are technically strongest.

### R3. The Asian control group: Japan and Korea

### R5. Where the external story gets some support (stated honestly)

- Topsports (OPENED, HKEX 2026052701005) blames "the sluggish macro consumption environment and declining offline foot traffic", says "discretionary goods including sports footwear and apparel have borne the brunt", and says "brands focusing on professional sports and outdoor niche tracks" are the growers. Its "other brands" line (Puma, Converse, Vans, TNF, Timberland, ASICS, Onitsuka, Skechers, Li-Ning, HOKA and others) fell 9.9% (FY24/25) and 7.4% (FY25/26), with store count down 18% in FY24/25. So the foreign mall-based lifestyle channel in China was genuinely weak, and the NBS "sports and entertainment goods" series (+11% to +16%, which includes non-sportswear goods and subsidy-boosted items) overstates the market Nike actually sells into. The investigator's "15 to 25 point gap vs the market" is therefore too large as a measure of the market gap.
- Under Armour Asia-Pacific net revenue fell 4.8% in its fiscal 2026 (Apr 2025 to Mar 2026) on lower wholesale (UA FY2026 10-K, OPENED). A soft foreign-brand wholesale channel in Asia is real.
- Nike's own first explanation in June 2024 was macro: Greater China "brick-and-mortar traffic declined as much as double-digits versus prior year" (Q4 FY24 call, OPENED).

But none of these rescues the hypothesis on magnitude. The correct benchmark is the peer in the same channel: adidas grew 9% to 17% a quarter through the same partner network, and On's APAC net sales rose 106.7% constant currency in 2025, with China named its fastest-growing market (On 2025 annual report, OPENED). Offline traffic weakness is a market-wide condition that a brand with the right product overcame.

### R6. The sell-in vs sell-through argument is weaker than claimed

The notes say consumer offtake fell only "mid-to-high single digits" and the rest of the -13% to -26% is deliberate destocking. Topsports' principal-brand revenue (Nike plus adidas) fell 4.2% in FY25/26 (HKEX 2026052701005, OPENED). If adidas, which grew 13% c-n in China in 2025, grew roughly 10% inside Topsports and was 30% to 40% of principal-brand sales (my assumption; Topsports does not split the two), then Nike sell-through at Nike's largest partner fell roughly 10% to 14% in that year (my arithmetic). That is about the same as Nike's FY26 Greater China -13% c-n. So through FY26, sell-in tracked consumer offtake fairly closely; the deliberate destocking story mainly applies to Q3 FY26 onward. That makes the consumer loss larger, but it is a loss to a competitor in the same store, so it adds to the Nike-specific share, not to the external share.

### R7. The external-force estimate has no measured basis

The notes allocate $0.3B to $0.6B to external forces by assuming "a structural foreign-brand headwind of 2 to 4 share points a year." Every measured foreign peer in 2024 to 2026 (adidas, ASICS, On, lululemon per REPO-NOTE) gained, not lost, share in China. The measured foreign-brand headwind for 2024 to 2026 is about zero or negative. The only external factor with evidence is the 2026 category softness (NBS sports goods -3.7% Jan to Aug 2026) and offline-traffic weakness, which overlaps only the last five months of FY26. A defensible range is about $0B to $0.3B, 0% to 6% of Nike's total decline. The 2021 boycott and guochao belong to a structural step-down that ended by FY24 (Nike China +8% c-n, EBIT $2,309M above FY23, REPO), so they explain none of the FY24 to FY26 change.

### R8. Verdict from this lens

- **External China forces (guochao, local rivals, boycott, macro):** do not survive. Their timing, peer, category and channel predictions all fail; at most a few points of 2026 softness. Minor.
- **"Nike China channel and discount problems":** real and large ($1.1B to $1.4B stands as an order of magnitude), but the evidence in R2 to R4 shows they are the China expression of Nike's global classics-franchise and lifestyle overexposure plus Consumer Direct Acceleration staffing and store under-investment, made worse by a partner-run channel Nike did not reset as it did in Japan and Korea. Calling them "China-specific forces" mislabels them.
- **Overall:** as worded, the hypothesis does not survive. China is the place where a large share of the decline shows up (34% of FY24 to FY26, 65% of Q1 FY27), but it is a **symptom of other causes** (franchise oversupply, lifestyle dependence, channel and store under-investment), not an independent China-specific cause.

## Challenge: ROOT-CAUSE

Challenger research date: 2026-10-13. Lens: accept that the Greater China collapse is real, then ask whether the investigator's two buckets ((A) external China forces, (B) Nike channel and discount problems) are themselves downstream of something else, and whether a different chain fits the same data better.

Source labels as above. "OPENED today" means I downloaded and read the document in this challenge. Note: the shared web-search budget for this run was exhausted before this challenge began, so I worked from primary documents I could fetch directly (HKEX, SEC copies, Nike transcripts) and could not run new searches; items that needed a search are listed under gaps.

### C1. The FY24 "recovery" was a shipment recovery, not a consumer recovery

The investigator's timing test (P1) rests on Greater China growing +4% (FY23) and +8% (FY24) currency-neutral, which is read as proof that the 2021 boycott shock had healed and that the collapse is a fresh 2024 event. The channel split says otherwise.

| FY24 | +8% | **+15%** | +1% | -8% | FY24 10-K MD&A (OPENED) |
| Q2 FY24 | +8% (reported revenue growth as stated on call) | **+19%** | -4% | -22% | Q2 FY24 call (OPENED) |

- For five straight quarters (Q2 FY24 to Q1 FY25), sell-in to partners grew 10% to 19% while Nike's own consumer-facing channel shrank 1% to 16%. Nike Digital in China fell in FY22, FY23, FY24, FY25 and FY26: it never recovered from the boycott year.
- Nike's own Greater China inventory rose from $973M (May 2023) to $1,070M (May 2024), +10%, while total NIKE, Inc. inventory fell 11% ($8,454M to $7,519M). Greater China was the only large segment whose inventory grew in FY24 (FY25 10-K segment supplemental table, OPENED).
- Q1 FY25 call, Friend (OPENED): "Wholesale grew 10%... traffic decelerated in our channels with lower sell-through rates. This has resulted in elevated inventory in the marketplace in an already promotional environment."
- Sell-through benchmark, Topsports (HKEX, OPENED today): revenue RMB31,876.5M (FY ended Feb 2022), 27,073.2M (Feb 2023, -15.1%), 28,933.2M (Feb 2024, +6.9%), 27,012.9M (Feb 2025, -6.6%), 25,739.9M (Feb 2026, -4.7%). Over FY21/22 to FY23/24 Topsports fell 9.2%, while Nike Greater China moved -2.3% currency-neutral over FY21 to FY24 and +12.3% over FY22 to FY24 (my arithmetic). Nike's FY24 wholesale growth (+15%) was roughly double its largest partner's sell-through growth (+6.9%, both principal brands).

**Inference:** the FY24 base is inflated by channel fill. The "recovery" that the investigator uses to rule out the boycott and guochao is visible only in shipments; the consumer-facing series (digital every year, total Nike Direct in FY24) was flat to falling. P1 does not cleanly refute the external forces.

### C2. The distributor online price war is downstream of that channel fill

The investigator treats the distributor online price war as a root Nike channel problem. The partner's own filings put it after the inventory overhang, not before:

- Topsports FY2024/25 results (HKEX 2025052101059, OPENED): "To address inventory pressures stemming from sales declines, we collaborated with brand partners... ramping up overall promotional activities, making tactical optimization of online and offline sales mix. Notably, the pace and scale of these adjustments were amplified in the second half of the financial year" (Sep 2024 to Feb 2025). Gross margin fell 3.4 points to 38.4%.
- Topsports online share of retail sales: "low-20%" in FY2022/23 (HKEX 2023052300939, OPENED today) to "mid-30%" in FY2024/25 with "double-digit growth" online (HKEX 2025052101059, OPENED). Inventory turnover days 130.4 (Feb 2022), 149.5 (Feb 2023), 136.1 (Feb 2024), 134.9 (Feb 2025).
- Sequence that fits the dates: Nike sells in +10% to +19% (Q2 FY24 to Q1 FY25) while its own traffic falls, then partners clear the surplus online from H2 calendar 2024, then Nike Digital collapses (-34% Q1 FY25, -36% Q2 FY26) as the same shoes appear cheaper on distributor storefronts (the Xinhua ¥400 gap, 2026-07-24, cited above), then Nike cuts sell-in (wholesale -13%, -14%) and finally exits distributor online (2027-01-01).

**Inference:** bucket (B) is not an independent cause. Its proximate upstream trigger is Nike's own FY24 to Q1 FY25 sell-in decision, made while the consumer signal was already weak, at a time when Nike was cutting guidance elsewhere (Q2 FY24 call: "Total retail sales across the marketplace fell short of our expectations"). The deeper upstream is the CDA-era marketplace design that let distributors run their own online storefronts alongside Nike's.

### C3. Over the full post-boycott cycle, the Nike-specific gap shrinks from "20 points a year" to single digits

The investigator's difference-in-differences compares FY24 to FY26 for Nike with 2025 to 2026 for adidas. That window starts at Nike's (inflated) high and adidas's post-reset trough. Measured from the 2021 peak, the incumbents converge:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nike Greater China, currency-neutral | FY21 | FY26 | **-25%** (0.87 x 1.04 x 1.08 x 0.88 x 0.87) | 10-K c-n rates (OPENED, REPO) |
| Nike Greater China, reported USD | FY21 $8,290M | FY26 $5,847M | -29% | REPO |

- adidas took its China reset in 2021 to 2022 (-36% c-n in 2022) and has rebounded since. Nike shipped through 2022 to 2024 and took its reset in FY25 to FY27. On a five-year view the two boycott-targeted incumbents and their shared distributor sit within about 4 to 6 points of each other (reported-currency basis; adidas's currency-neutral decline is probably a little smaller than -21% because the yuan weakened against the euro, so the true gap may be closer to 6 to 10 points).
- **Implication for the attribution.** If the structural post-2021 level for a boycott-hit foreign incumbent is 15% to 21% below its 2021 peak (adidas and Topsports benchmarks), then from Nike's FY24 level (-2.3% c-n vs FY21) the expected structural catch-up over FY24 to FY26 is -13% to -19%. Nike's actual was -23.4% c-n. On that benchmark, the deferred structural step-down explains about 56% to 82% of China's FY24 to FY26 decline, roughly **$0.9B to $1.4B, or 19% to 28% of Nike's total $4,964M decline**, and the Nike-specific excess is only about **$0.3B to $0.75B (6% to 15%)**. This roughly inverts the investigator's split ($0.3B to $0.6B external, $1.1B to $1.4B Nike channel).
- The "structural" bucket here is not purely external in its *timing*: Nike chose when to recognise it (by shipping into the channel in FY24). But its *level* matches what the market did to adidas, which argues the level is external.

### C4. What the alternative chain predicts, and where it fails

Alternative explanation: *the 2021 boycott and guochao lowered the ceiling for the two targeted incumbents by roughly a fifth; adidas recognised it in 2022; Nike deferred it through classics and Jordan demand and FY24 wholesale loading; the FY25 to FY26 collapse is mostly the deferred reset plus the unwind of that loading, and the distributor price war is the mechanism of the unwind.*

| Running and performance hold up while lifestyle resets | Running up six straight quarters | Consistent with both explanations |

### C5. Objections to the investigator's analysis

1. P1 (timing) is measured on shipments. The consumer-facing data show no full recovery after 2021, so "the boycott lasted two months" is true for the April to June 2021 shock but not for Nike's China digital demand.
2. The -20 points a year difference-in-differences is a window artifact: it compares Nike's post-loading reset with adidas's post-reset rebound. On a 2021 base the gap is about 4 to 10 points cumulatively, not per year.
3. Bucket (B) is mislabelled as a cause. The online price war and the margin collapse follow the FY24 sell-in and the partner inventory overhang in time; they are the unwind mechanism.
4. The FY24 base used for the $1,698M decline contains channel fill. If FY24 wholesale had grown at Topsports's +6.9% rather than +15%, FY24 Greater China revenue would have been roughly $0.3B lower (8 points on a $3.9B wholesale base, my estimate). That slice of the "decline" is Nike undoing its own pull-forward, not demand lost in FY25 to FY26.
5. The investigator is right that external macro and mass-tier guochao brands are not the 2024 to 2026 driver (NBS data, Anta and Li-Ning low single digits). Where they underweight the external side is the boycott-era *level reset* for targeted incumbents, which shows up in both adidas and Topsports.

### C6. Revised view

- China remains a large part of Nike's decline (34% of FY24 to FY26, 65% of Q1 FY27), so the hypothesis survives as a **contributing cause**.
- But the true upstream chain is: (1) a post-2021 structural step-down for boycott-hit foreign incumbents (external, level about -15% to -21% vs 2021); (2) deferred in Nike's P&L by FY24 to Q1 FY25 sell-in that ran 10 to 19 points ahead of Nike's own sell-through (Nike-made, timing); (3) unwound through distributor online clearance and a margin spiral (mechanism); (4) overshot in FY27 by deliberate sell-in cuts and the distributor online exit (Nike-made, intentional).
- Revised split of the $1,698M FY24 to FY26 Greater China decline (wide band): deferred structural reset about $0.9B to $1.4B; Nike-specific excess about $0.3B to $0.75B, of which roughly $0.3B is the unwind of FY24 channel fill. As a share of Nike's total decline: structural 19% to 28%, Nike-specific China excess 6% to 15%. The overlap warning with `franchise_oversupply.md` still applies to the Nike-specific slice.
- Confidence: medium-low. adidas is a single peer, its currency-neutral 2023 and 2024 China rates were not re-verified here, Topsports mixes Nike and adidas, and Nike does not disclose China sell-through.
