# Root cause test: did the shift from brand building to digital performance marketing weaken the Nike brand?

Research date: 2026-10-13. Hypothesis under test (the Massimo Giunco thesis): *Shifting marketing from brand building and sport storytelling to digital performance marketing and data-driven demand creation weakened the brand, and that weakening drove the decline.*

Status labels: **OPENED** = I read the page, PDF or filing myself in this session. **OPENED (repo)** = opened by an earlier researcher and recorded in this repo's notes; reused, not re-fetched. **SNIPPET** = seen only in a search-result summary. Filing numbers come from `data/nike_filing_facts.csv` and `research_notes/04_financial_data_pack.md` unless I say I read them in the 10-K text I downloaded from EDGAR in this session. Transcript quotes come from Nike's official earnings call transcript PDFs, which I downloaded from Nike's IR CDN (s1.q4cdn.com/806093406 and investors.nike.com/_assets) and converted to text. All arithmetic is mine and labelled.

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: symptom of another cause (a component of the Consumer Direct Acceleration operating model), with a real but modest contributing effect. It is not the primary cause.**

- **The shift itself is proven, by Nike's own words, not just by Giunco.** In Sept 2020 Nike told investors it was making "fewer, but significantly more impactful Brand campaigns" while increasing the return on "performance marketing investment", and that it would "move deeper into the funnel, moving from attracting and acquiring new members to retaining members" (Q1 FY21 call). In Dec 2024 the new CEO said: "we've shifted investments away from creating demand for our brand to capturing the brand demand through performance marketing for our digital business" (Q2 FY25 call). Demand creation fell from a 9.7% of revenue baseline (FY2017 to FY2019) to 7.0% to 8.3% in FY2021 to FY2024. That is about $3.5B less marketing over four years than the old ratio would have funded (my arithmetic). Athlete endorsement commitments fell from about $10.2B (May 2019) to $7.6B (May 2022 and May 2023).
- **The mechanism is also proven, but for a narrower claim.** Performance marketing captured demand rather than creating it. Nike Digital became about "50% full price and 50% off price" (Friend, Dec 2024). Its off-price mix rose high single digits while performance marketing rose. Cutting paid media later cut digital traffic by double digits. This matches the field evidence on paid search (Blake, Nosko and Tadelis, Econometrica 2015) and adidas's own 2019 econometric finding.
- **The claim that this "weakened the brand" fails most of its discriminating tests:**
  1. **Timing (P1):** brand-health measures *improved* for three years after the shift. Piper Sandler teen footwear mindshare was 56% (Spring 2021, implied), 60% (Spring 2022), 60% (Fall 2022), 61% (Spring 2023) and 61% (Fall 2023). Interbrand rank peaked at No. 9 in 2022. Axios Harris reputation rose from No. 56 to No. 21 in 2023. Brand erosion shows up only from Spring 2024 (59%, then 57%, then 49% in Spring 2025). That is the same window as the classics oversupply hangover.
  2. **Geography and brand (P4):** the decline is concentrated in Greater China (-22.5%, FY2024 to FY2026) and Converse (-43.6%). Both have causes specific to them. North America, where the digital performance machine was biggest, fell least (-4.1%).
  3. **Peers (P5):** adidas ran a mix of about 77% performance and 23% brand in the late 2010s (Peel, Oct 2019), and still grew fast and gained US share in 2016 to 2019 on Boost and Yeezy. Under Armour kept spending on brand and declined anyway. On and Hoka grew on product and specialty retail. Marketing mix does not sort winners from losers; product does.
  4. **Reversal (P6):** Hill restored brand marketing quickly. FY2025 demand creation was +9% to $4,689M (10.1% of revenue), and Nike returned to the Super Bowl in Feb 2025. Revenue kept falling: FY2026 was flat and Q1 FY2027 was -4%. Organic traffic "has slowed" (Sept 2025), and brand values fell again (Interbrand 2025 -26%; Kantar BrandZ 2026 -17%). The quick fix did not produce a quick result. That fits either a long brand-rebuild lag or a cause that sits elsewhere (product, China).
- **Best reading:** the marketing shift was the marketing arm of the DTC strategy. Its job was to drive paid, promotion-led traffic to Nike Digital, and much of that traffic would have bought Nike anyway, often at wholesale partners. It wasted money and trained consumers to expect discounts. Its unwinding mechanically removes digital revenue. It probably made the brand less able to resist when the product cycle turned (the lifestyle classics fatigue of 2023 to 2025). But the timing, geography and peer evidence say it was not the trigger. My estimate is about 5% to 15% of the FY2024 to FY2026 revenue decline, with low confidence (Section 6).

## 3. Evidence for the hypothesis

1. **Management confirms the shift in its own words, at both ends.**
   - Sept 22, 2020 (Q1 FY21, Friend): "we drove significant leverage in our Demand Creation spending versus prior year in the last two quarters, creating fewer, but significantly more impactful Brand campaigns. We were also able to increase the return performance marketing investment, driving accelerated digital demand." Also: "We have got to move deeper into the funnel, moving from attracting and acquiring new members to retaining members." This matches Giunco's "from CREATE DEMAND to SERVE AND RETAIN DEMAND" almost word for word.
   - Dec 18, 2020 (Q2 FY21): "SG&A declined 2% in the quarter as disciplined expense management and lower marketing spend on brand and sports events was partially offset by increased investments in digital marketing." Friend: "we're investing a lot of money in digital marketing today"; "a member-focused digital marketing model, will enable greater return on investment over time."
   - Mar 18, 2021 (Q3 FY21): "full-frontal digital marketing activities"; Datalogue acquired to "inform products marketing".
   - Mar 21, 2024 (Q3 FY24, Donahoe): "Our brand marketing must become bolder and more distinctive."
   - Dec 19, 2024 (Q2 FY25, Hill): "we've shifted investments away from creating demand for our brand to capturing the brand demand through performance marketing for our digital business"; "centralization has impacted the resources we have in key countries and key cities"; "We're starting to shift dollars from performance marketing to brand marketing." Friend: "reducing investment in performance marketing, which will reduce paid traffic"; "being less reliant upon paid media and performance marketing."
2. **The money moved.** Demand creation intensity fell 1.4 to 2.7 points below its pre-2020 level for four years (FY2021 to FY2024). The cumulative shortfall is about $3.5B (my arithmetic). Within the smaller budget, 10-Ks name digital marketing as the growing line in FY2020, FY2021, FY2022 and FY2024. Endorsement commitments fell about 25% from May 2019 to May 2022 and 2023 ($10.2B to $7.6B; my arithmetic from the 10-K obligations tables). Commitments are lumpy with renewal cycles, so this is suggestive, not conclusive.
3. **Giunco's account is detailed and insider-sourced.** Giunco worked at Nike for about 22 years. He says the brand team "shifted from brand marketing to digital marketing and from brand enhancing to sales activation". He says programmatic and performance spend reached "the double or more of the share of resources usually invested in the other brand activities", that local marketing teams were cut, and that content was centralised (sfin.ro republication, Jul 31, 2024, OPENED). Hill's "centralization" remark independently confirms the local-resource point.
4. **Mechanism supported by causal research.** Blake, Nosko and Tadelis (Econometrica, 2015, eBay field experiments): "brand keyword ads have no measurable short-term benefits", and for non-brand keywords "average returns ... are negative" (abstract, OPENED via Crossref/OpenAlex). adidas's 2019 econometrics showed brand media drove both retail and e-commerce sales more reliably than performance, and an AdWords outage in Latin America did not reduce search traffic or sales (Horizont, Oct 25, 2019, OPENED). The Binet and Field IPA work (2013) puts the optimum near 60:40 brand to activation (SNIPPET, secondary summaries).
5. **Discount dependence on the platform the marketing fed.** Digital off-price mix rose high single digits while performance marketing rose (Q2 FY25). Digital was about half off-price. Paid media was "largely driving bottom of funnel traffic" (Q1 FY26).
6. **Brand-health deterioration is real (from 2024).** Piper teen footwear mindshare fell from 61% (Fall 2023) to 49% (Spring 2025). Upper-income teens' athletic footwear mindshare was -510 bps Y/Y in Spring 2024 (Piper PDFs, OPENED). Tracksuit US tracking, Apr to Sept 2024: consideration 79% to 73%, preference 39% to 33%, usage 81% to 77%, awareness 94% (Tracksuit, Oct 21, 2024, OPENED via Wayback; a vendor with no pre-2024 baseline). YouGov: Skechers consideration rose from 33.4 to 35.5 (2024 to 2025), close to Nike's 36.3 (YouGov, Mar 20, 2026, OPENED).
7. **Outside marketing scientists and the trade press agree on the diagnosis.** Mark Ritson (Brandweek 2024) listed "overemphasis on performance marketing" among Nike's four mistakes (SNIPPET, Adweek). WARC (Aug 21, 2024): reliance on performance marketing and easily measured metrics "distorts the vision" (OPENED). The Digiday headline (Oct 2, 2025) reads Nike's "return to brand plan is working" (OPENED).

## 4. Evidence against (or that it is not the main cause)

1. **The timing is wrong for a trigger** (P1). For three years after the documented shift, Nike posted record revenue (FY2021 to FY2024), the teen mindshare peak (61% in 2023), its Interbrand rank peak (2022) and a reputation rebound (Axios Harris No. 21 in 2023). Nike also still produced landmark brand work in this period. "You Can't Stop Us" (July 2020) won a Cannes Lions Film Grand Prix in 2021 and an Emmy (SNIPPET: Campaign Asia, Adweek). That contradicts Giunco's claim of no landmark campaign since 2018. Brand erosion starts in 2024, together with the classics fatigue and digital traffic declines.
2. **Geography and brand concentration** (P4). About half of the decline is Greater China and Converse, which have their own causes. North America, the heart of the paid-digital model, held up best and grew in FY2026.
3. **Natural experiment at adidas** (P5). A heavier performance skew (77:23) coexisted with fast growth when the product was hot. adidas's later drop and recovery followed product. Under Armour shows that brand spend without product heat does not save a brand.
4. **The fix is applied, but demand has not turned** (P6). Demand creation went back above 10% of revenue in FY2025 and FY2026, which is above the pre-2020 ratio. Endorsement commitments are now about $15.5B to $16.2B, double the 2022 level. Yet revenue fell 10% in FY2025, was flat in FY2026 and fell 4% in Q1 FY2027. Brand-value rankings fell further in 2025 to 2026. If marketing mix were the main lever, a two-year, roughly $1B-a-year swing back toward brand and sport should be showing up more clearly by now. A long rebuild lag is possible, but the evidence cannot separate the two.
5. **Much of the "damage" is the unwind, not the brand.** Revenue lost when paid media is cut is revenue that performance marketing had bought, and part of it would have been bought at wholesale anyway. Management's own explanation for the FY2026 double-digit digital traffic decline is self-imposed: fewer promo days, lower markdowns, fewer classics and launches, and less paid media.
6. **Disclosure does not let anyone measure the mix directly.** Nike's 10-K defines "brand marketing expense" as including "digital marketing expense" (FY2026 10-K). The filings' "brand marketing" is therefore not "brand building" in Giunco's sense, and the brand-vs-performance split has never been disclosed. Third-party percentages found online (for example "65% digital in 2023") are unsourced and are not used here.
7. **Brand-value indices are partly circular.** Kantar BrandZ (-32% in 2023, -17% in 2026) and Interbrand (-26% in 2025) feed on financial results and market value, so they cannot show that brand weakness *caused* the financial decline.

## 5. Independent cause or symptom?

**Symptom.** The upstream cause is the Consumer Direct Acceleration operating model (June 2020 onward):

- digital-first commerce with membership as the organizing principle;
- category teams dissolved into a Men's, Women's and Kids' consumer construct;
- product allocated to NIKE Direct;
- marketing re-tasked to feed nike.com and the app, through performance media, member retention, personalisation and centrally produced content.

The marketing shift was not a separate decision by a marketing department. It was the demand engine the DTC plan required. Management described it in 2020 to 2021 as a way to raise return on demand creation and margin, and it funded the shift partly by spending less in total. Its effects run through the same channel as the other CDA symptoms (dtc_wholesale.md, franchise_oversupply.md):

- digital revenue inflated by paid, discounted traffic;
- wholesale partners undercut;
- the brand's emotional "reasons to believe" left to decay while product newness stalled.

When the classics cycle turned in 2023 to 2024, there was little brand capital and little new product to cushion it.

## 6. Quantification (honest, low confidence)

- **Size of the shift:** about $3.5B cumulative demand-creation shortfall vs the pre-2020 ratio over FY2021 to FY2024 (about $0.9B a year). An unknown but rising share of the remainder went to performance and digital marketing. Endorsement commitments were about $2.6B lower at the trough (May 2022 and 2023 vs May 2019).
- **Channel 1, the mechanical unwind of paid traffic:** NIKE Brand Digital fell $1.0B from FY2025 to FY2026 ($9.6B to $8.6B). Management attributes the FY2026 traffic drop to several self-imposed actions at once (promo days, markdowns, classics, launches, paid media). If paid media is a third to a half of that, about $0.3B to $0.5B, part of which shifted to wholesale and is not net lost (assumption).
- **Channel 2, brand-equity erosion reducing full-price demand:** this cannot be measured directly. Brand-health metrics fell about 12 points among teens (Piper, Fall 2023 to Spring 2025) and about 6 points on US consideration in six months of 2024 (Tracksuit). Those moves coincide with classics fatigue, China and competition from On and Hoka. Attributing a quarter to a third of that erosion to the marketing mix (my judgment) implies a few hundred million dollars of lost demand.
- **Total:** roughly $0.25B to $0.75B, or **about 5% to 15% of the $4,964M FY2024 to FY2026 revenue decline**. A wide error band: it could be lower if the post-2024 brand fall is entirely product-driven, or higher if brand neglect is what made the product fade so steep. For comparison, the classics oversupply unwind accounts for roughly 40% to 60% (franchise_oversupply.md), and Greater China plus Converse are about 52% gross.
- **Market value:** I cannot isolate any share-price effect.

## 7. Fixability

**Yes, partly, and the fix is already under way.**

- Marketing mix is the most controllable lever Nike has. Hill reversed it within 60 days of taking over: performance media was cut, brand and sport marketing raised to 10%+ of revenue, sports marketing commitments raised to about $16B, and key-city and local teams rebuilt.
- The adidas precedent (Peel's 2019 rebalance) shows the mix can be corrected without lasting damage.
- But brand equity rebuilds slowly, and marketing alone does not create demand without new product. adidas's recovery came on the Samba cycle, and Under Armour's storytelling push has not yet turned its revenue.
- The near-term cost is visible: less paid traffic, digital down double digits, and higher demand creation weighing on margins.

### R2. The 2020 to 2021 "shift" in dollars was mainly COVID, by Nike's own 10-K wording

The deepest point in the intensity series (FY2021, 7.0%) is the one the hypothesis leans on most. The 10-K explains it as event cancellation, not strategy:
- FY2020 10-K (filed 2020-07-24, OPENED): demand creation fell "as sporting events were postponed or canceled and a majority of stores were closed globally during the fourth quarter of fiscal 2020."
- FY2021 10-K (filed 2021-07-20, OPENED): "Demand creation expense decreased 13% for fiscal 2021, due to lower marketing and advertising expenses for our brand events and retail operations, as well as lower sports marketing expense as sporting events were postponed due to COVID-19."
- FY2022 10-K (filed 2022-07-21, OPENED): +24% "primarily due to higher advertising and marketing spend against brand campaigns as we experienced marketplace closures in the prior year due to COVID-19".
- FY2023 10-K (OPENED): +5% "primarily due to higher advertising and marketing expense and higher sports marketing expense" (digital is not named as the driver for the full year).
- FY2024 10-K (OPENED): +6% due to "higher advertising and marketing expense, digital marketing and sports marketing expense".

In absolute dollars, demand creation set records in FY2022 ($3,850M), FY2023 ($4,060M) and FY2024 ($4,285M), all above FY2019 ($3,753M). The FY2022 to FY2024 "underspend" exists only as a ratio to a revenue base that had jumped 31% (FY2019 $39,117M to FY2024 $51,362M). The 10-K text shows digital was one growing line among several, not a substitution for sport and brand. The investigator's P2 is real in direction but overstated in size once the COVID year is set aside: excluding FY2021, the FY2022 to FY2024 gap is about $2.3B over three years, and some of that is the denominator effect of a pandemic demand boom.

### R3. Category and channel test at the moment the decline began fails the hypothesis

Brand neglect through loss of sport storytelling predicts that sport performance and all channels weaken first. Q4 FY24 call (2024-06-27, official PDF s1.q4cdn.com/806093406/files/doc_financials/2024/q4/NIKE-Inc-Q4FY24-OFFICIAL-Transcript_FINAL.pdf, OPENED) shows the opposite pattern in the first quarter of decline:
- "declines in Lifestyle ... more than offsetting strong growth in our sport Performance business."
- "Nike Digital declined 10% ... Wholesale grew 8%."
- The digital miss was on "lower sales of certain classic footwear franchises ... even as they continue to drive retail sales growth at high full-price realization in multi-brand retail."

So at the turn, sport performance grew, wholesale grew, and the damage sat in one channel (Nike Digital) and one product family (classics). That is the signature of franchise oversupply and channel saturation, not of a brand that consumers had stopped valuing.

### R4. Brand storytelling was restored before Hill, at record spend, and revenue still fell 10%

The same Q4 FY24 call (Donahoe, Friend) announced "bigger, bolder brand campaigns, starting with EC24 and the Paris Olympics", "Awaken Your Madness", and "a brand campaign that you won't be able to miss". Q1 FY25 10-Q (filed 2024-10-07, OPENED): "Demand creation expense increased 15% primarily due to an increase in brand marketing expense, reflecting investment in key sports events", to a then-record $1,226M. Revenue that quarter fell 10% ($12,939M to $11,589M). The largest brand-building quarter in Nike's history coincided with the steepest drop. This weakens both the cause claim and the "long lag" escape route: the lag argument would need brand equity to be insensitive to a record campaign for over two years.

### R7. Mechanism direction: the 2024 rise in performance marketing looks like a response to the inventory problem, not its cause

Friend's Dec 2024 statement ties the rise in performance marketing to "the same period" as the rise in off-price digital mix, i.e. the year to late 2024. That is after the 2022 inventory glut and during the classics oversupply. Paid traffic was being bought to clear discounted classics on Nike Digital. Hill's plan in the same call lists cutting performance marketing next to "short-term liquidation of excess inventory". The paid-media dependence is best read as a symptom of the product and inventory cause, consistent with the investigator's "symptom" verdict but pointing to a different upstream cause (franchise oversupply) than the CDA strategy alone.

### R8. Problems with the quantification

- Channel 1 ($0.3B to $0.5B of digital decline from cutting paid media in FY2026) is a self-inflicted cost of the cure, booked two years after the decline started. It is not evidence that the 2020 shift "weakened the brand"; if anything it shows the paid traffic was low-value revenue being turned off. Part of it moved to wholesale (NA grew 4.8% in FY2026). Counting it as brand damage double counts the franchise and DTC unwind already attributed elsewhere.
- Channel 2 (a quarter to a third of the brand-health fall) is an assumption with no test behind it. The brand-health fall begins in Spring 2024, the same window as the classics collapse, and R3 shows sport performance was growing then. I find no data that isolates a marketing-mix share.
- Net, I would put the share attributable to the marketing shift as a cause of the FY2024 to FY2026 decline at about 0% to 5% (roughly $0B to $0.25B), with the paid-traffic unwind classified as a consequence of the turnaround rather than a cause of the decline.

### R9. What would change my mind

A pre-2024 brand-tracking series (YouGov BrandIndex, Morning Consult, Harris EquiTrend) showing Nike consideration falling in 2021 to 2023 while revenue was still rising, or disclosed media-mix data showing sport and brand spend cut in absolute terms in FY2022 to FY2024. Neither was found. I could not run new web searches this session (budget exhausted), so creative-award and TV-spend data (iSpot, Vivvix) remain unchecked.

### Refuter verdict

The hypothesis as a cause of the decline **does not survive**. Every discriminating prediction fails on primary data: the dollar "shift" was mostly COVID event cancellation; absolute marketing set records in FY2022 to FY2024; at the turn, sport performance and wholesale grew while classics on Nike Digital fell; a record brand campaign in Q1 FY25 came with a 10% revenue drop; segments that got the biggest marketing increases in FY2025 fell as much as the rest; and peers with higher marketing intensity (Under Armour) declined while a peer at Nike's "low" intensity (Deckers) boomed. What remains true is narrow: Nike Digital became dependent on paid, discounted traffic in 2023 to 2024, and that dependence was a symptom of oversupplied classics. Revised verdict: **minor**, about 0% to 5% of the decline.

## Challenge: ROOT-CAUSE

Challenger date: 2026-10-13. Lens: assume the symptom (weaker brand, paid-traffic dependence) is real, and ask what sits upstream of it. Research limits: this turn's shared web-search budget ran out before I could search, so I did no new keyword searches. Everything below comes from primary documents already downloaded in this session's scratchpad (10-K text from EDGAR, official Nike call transcripts, Piper Sandler infographics) plus one direct page fetch. Arithmetic is mine and labelled.

### C1. Verdict

**The "symptom of another cause" verdict survives, but the share is overstated. I would cut it to about 2% to 8% (roughly $0.1B to $0.4B) of the $4,964M FY2024 to FY2026 decline.** Three upstream causes explain the same data better than "marketing mix weakened the brand":

1. **COVID and channel-mix accounting** created most of the measured "underspend".
2. **The June to July 2020 CDA reorganisation** produced both the marketing shift and the product drought. Giunco himself ranks the marketing change third of three decisions.
3. **Sportswear product fatigue, after the classics oversupply,** drives the brand-health fade. Under one and the same marketing envelope, performance categories grew while Sportswear fell.

### C2. The "$3.5B underspend" is mostly COVID and a denominator artifact (new evidence)

- **The 10-Ks blame the FY2020 to FY2021 cut on COVID, not a strategy choice.**
  - FY2021 10-K: "Demand creation expense decreased 13% for fiscal 2021, due to lower marketing and advertising expenses for our brand events and retail operations, as well as lower sports marketing expense as sporting events were postponed due to COVID-19."
  - FY2020 10-K: the decrease came from "lower retail brand presentation costs and lower sports marketing investments ... as sporting events were postponed or canceled and a majority of stores were closed globally".
  - The FY2022 10-K reverses it: demand creation "increased 24% ... primarily due to higher advertising and marketing spend against brand campaigns as we experienced marketplace closures in the prior year".
  - Source: EDGAR 10-K text, OPENED in this session (the FY2020, FY2021 and FY2022 10-Ks, URLs as in `data/nike_filing_facts.csv`).
  - FY2021 alone is $1,213M of the $3,523M FY2021 to FY2024 gap against the 9.72% FY2017 to FY2019 baseline (my arithmetic on repo demand creation and revenue figures).
- **Sports marketing was rising, not starved, in FY2023 and FY2024.** The FY2023 10-K cites "higher advertising and marketing expense and higher sports marketing expense". The FY2024 10-K cites "higher advertising and marketing expense, digital marketing and sports marketing expense" (OPENED). In absolute dollars, FY2022 to FY2024 demand creation averaged $4,065M, against $3,557M in FY2017 to FY2019 (+14%; my arithmetic).
- **The ratio fell partly because the DTC pivot inflated the denominator.** A dollar of NIKE Direct revenue is booked at retail price, while a wholesale dollar is booked at roughly half of retail. The DTC share of revenue rose from 30.0% (FY2019) to 41.9% (FY2024) (repo channel split). That lowers demand creation as a share of revenue even when spend per pair sold is flat.
  - Method: I restated revenue on a wholesale-equivalent basis, multiplying NIKE Brand Direct revenue by an assumed 0.55 to 0.65 (my assumption).
  - Result: the baseline is 10.8% to 11.1%, and FY2022 to FY2024 run 9.3% to 10.3%. The FY2022 to FY2024 shortfall is $1.35B to $1.59B over three years, about $0.5B a year, not about $0.9B a year.
  - Conclusion: the "starved brand" claim rests on a number that is about two-thirds artifact.

### C3. The marketing shift sits downstream of the operating-model decision, which predates Donahoe's personal style

- **The FY2019 10-K (filed July 2019, under Parker) already frames the strategy as the Consumer Direct Offense / Triple Double.** It describes "investments in data and analytics capabilities, digital commerce platforms ... to accelerate our end-to-end digital transformation". It also records "Lower demand creation expense ... primarily due to lower advertising and marketing costs" in Global Brand Divisions (FY2019 10-K, OPENED).
- **CDA was announced in June 2020 as "a new digitally empowered phase" that would "lead with NIKE Digital and our own stores".** The operating-model and leadership changes followed on July 22, 2020 (FY2020 10-K, OPENED).
- **The re-tasking of marketing to performance and retention came after both of these.** It was first described in Sept 2020 (Q1 FY21 call, repo).
- **Giunco's own essay orders the causes.** He says (Sfin.ro republication, fetched 2026-10-13, OPENED; the republication page does not name the author) there were "three key decisions":
  1. eliminating categories;
  2. becoming DTC-led;
  3. changing the marketing model.

  He writes that "the brand team shifted from brand marketing to digital marketing and from brand enhancing to sales activation". On product he says: "If today, we talk about lack of innovation and energy in product creation, well, we know exactly what originated all of that." Read correctly, the Giunco thesis is a CDA thesis in which marketing is one output. It is not a stand-alone marketing thesis.
- **Implication:** the true upstream cause is the 2017 to 2020 digital-first operating model (data-led commerce, then category dissolution and centralisation). The same decision produced the marketing shift (feed nike.com) and the product drought (no category engine). Both are siblings, and the product sibling is the one that drives revenue (see C4).

### C4. Within-Nike natural experiment: same marketing, opposite outcomes by category (new evidence)

From FY2025 onward, Hill's brand and sport marketing push (demand creation 10.1% to 10.2% of revenue) applied to every category. If brand marketing were the binding constraint, categories should move together. They split by product freshness:

- **Q4 FY26 call (2026-06-30, official transcript, OPENED):**
  - "Running grew ... $1 billion over the last 5 quarters, and we gained 5 market share points."
  - In North America, running, global football and golf "all of which grew double digits", while "Sportswear declined double digits".
  - The performance business "grew mid-single digits this fiscal year", while "NIKE Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear, where sell-through remains challenged".
- **Q1 FY27 call (2026-10-01, OPENED):**
  - "a lack of energy in the lifestyle space".
  - "where we have introduced newness at scale, we've seen early success": the P-6000 and V5 Runner "grew by strong double digits", and Studio Fleece was "the best-performing apparel collection of the quarter".
- **Same brand, same media budget, same sport storytelling.** Categories with new product grew and categories with tired classics fell. This is the signature of a product cause with marketing as an amplifier. It is not a brand-mix cause.

### C5. Discount dependence was a channel and inventory decision, reversible in about two quarters (new evidence)

- **Q4 FY26 call (OPENED):** "off-price was down over 50% following aggressive actions to reduce promotions. This resulted in a 15-point improvement in full price realization" on digital.
- If performance marketing had damaged pricing power at the brand level, full-price realisation would not snap back within two quarters of a channel policy change.
- So the "50% off-price" digital mix (Dec 2024) is evidence of a DTC traffic-and-inventory choice, not of brand-equity erosion. The investigator's Channel 1 (the $0.3B to $0.5B paid-traffic unwind) belongs under `dtc_wholesale.md`. Counting it here double-counts the DTC cause.

### C6. Brand-health timing points at product, with one caveat in the hypothesis's favour

Piper Sandler teen mindshare (infographics OPENED in this session from the scratchpad copies):

| Season | Footwear (favorite brand) | Apparel (favorite brand) |
|---|---|---|
| Fall 2022 | 60% | 31% |
| Spring 2023 | 61% | 33% |
| Fall 2023 | 61% | 35% |
| Spring 2024 | 59% | 34% |
| Fall 2024 | 57% | 33% |
| Spring 2025 | 49% | 28% |

- **Against the hypothesis:**
  - Both series peak in Fall 2023, three years after the 2020 shift. That is after the 2022 resale collapse and at the peak of the classics supply.
  - The steepest drop (Fall 2024 to Spring 2025: -8 points in footwear, -5 points in apparel) came *after* Hill began moving money back to brand marketing (Dec 2024). It also coincided with the classics supply cut ("down more than 30%", Q4 FY25, repo).
  - A marketing cause would show the damage while the bad mix was in place and the repair after it was fixed. The data show the reverse.
- **For the hypothesis (caveat):** apparel fell roughly as much as footwear in relative terms (35% to 28% against 61% to 49%, both about -20%). So the fade is brand-wide, not only a Dunk effect. Sportswear apparel had its own classics problem (fleece; see Studio Fleece above), but a general loss of brand heat cannot be ruled out.
- The Fall 2025 and Spring 2026 Piper files in the scratchpad are 404 HTML pages, not PDFs, so they add no data.

### C7. Alternative explanation that fits the same data better

The CDA operating model (2017 to 2020) did two things:

1. It dissolved the category engine, which starved Sportswear of new product and pushed it to scale Dunk, AF1 and AJ1.
2. It re-tasked marketing to harvest that demand on nike.com.

When the classics saturated (2022 to 2024), teen favoritism, full-price sell-through and digital traffic fell together. Restoring brand spend has worked only where new product exists (running, football, P-6000, Studio Fleece). On this reading the marketing shift is a co-symptom of the operating-model decision. Its independent effect is the marginal amount by which a stronger emotional brand would have slowed the classics fade. That cannot be measured, and the category split suggests it is small.

### C8. Revised quantification

- **Drop Channel 1** ($0.3B to $0.5B paid-traffic unwind). It is a DTC channel decision already counted in `dtc_wholesale.md`.
- **Channel 2 (brand-equity erosion):** the investigator credits a quarter to a third of the 2024 to 2025 brand fade to marketing mix. Three findings push that down:
  - the C2 artifact, which shrinks the real underspend to about $0.5B a year;
  - the C4 category split;
  - the C6 timing.

  I would use 10% to 25% of a brand fade that is itself mostly downstream of Sportswear fatigue. That gives **about $0.1B to $0.4B, or about 2% to 8% of the $4,964M decline**, low confidence.

## Challenge: ROOT-CAUSE (second pass, scaled down)

Challenger date: 2026-10-13. A full ROOT-CAUSE challenge already appears above (C1 to C9). It traced the marketing shift upstream to the 2017 to 2020 Consumer Direct operating model, and it showed that restored brand spend lifted only the categories with new product. I agree with it and do not repeat it. This pass adds one new data point. It addresses the gap the REFUTER flagged (R9): is there a brand-tracking series that shows erosion before 2024, while revenue was still rising?

- **New evidence, Cowen survey (July 2022, 2,700 US consumers), reported by SGB Media on 2022-10-24 (OPENED):**
  - **Athletic footwear, ages 25 to 34:** Nike's preference share was "moving from 45 percent in 2017 to 36 percent in 2022."
  - **Athletic performance apparel, ages 25 to 34:** 39% in 2022, "down year-over-year from 41 percent in 2021 and from 43 percent pre-pandemic."
  - **Athletic footwear, ages 18 to 24:** share "increased year-over-year ... but decreased versus its pre-pandemic share."
  - **Casual lifestyle apparel, ages 18 to 24:** a 41% share, "flat with 2019 through 2022."
- **What it means for the root-cause question:**
  - **This is real pre-2024 erosion, so the timing objection (P1) is weaker than the investigator stated.** Adult preference was sliding while revenue was setting records. The Piper teen series missed it.
  - **The erosion starts in 2017, three years before the documented 2020 marketing shift.** So it cannot be a downstream effect of that shift. It lines up with the upstream decisions dated in C3: the Consumer Direct Offense (2017), the cut in wholesale accounts and the category consolidation. Over the same years, preference was moving to the performance-running challengers.
  - **The lifestyle share among 18 to 24 year olds was flat over 2019 to 2022.** That fits C4: classics demand held up until supply saturated it, and the erosion was concentrated in performance footwear among adults. That segment lost its product engine and its specialty-retail presence.
- **Assessment:** the new data move the start of brand erosion earlier, which helps the "brand weakened" symptom. They do not help the claim that the marketing mix caused it, because the slide predates the mix change. The best upstream explanation is still the operating-model decision: DTC-first distribution plus the dissolved category engine. The marketing shift is one of its outputs. I keep the first challenger's share of about 2% to 8% of the $4,964M decline, low confidence.
- **Limits:** I did not open the underlying Cowen report, only SGB's summary. It is one survey wave with recalled prior-year comparisons, the methodology is not given, and it covers the US only.
