# North America wholesale: sell-through or sell-in?

> **Status: DRAFT (Oct 13, 2026).** Produced by the multi-agent investigation (one investigator, two adversarial reviewers, one revision round). Sources are cited inline; independent spot-check against source documents is in progress and results will be logged at the end of this file. Treat figures as provisional until marked verified.


**Question.** Nike North America (NA) wholesale grew +11%, +24%, +11%, +10% and +9% from Q1 FY26 to Q1 FY27. Was that growth retail sell-through, or sell-in (channel fill)?

**Prepared:** October 13, 2026. Companion to `research_notes/05_channel_split.md` (Section 8.3 asks this exact question).

**Labels used:** [filing] = SEC filing; [Nike release] = Nike earnings call prepared remarks PDF (Nike calls them "not an official transcript"); [filing via mirror] = an 8-K exhibit read from a third-party copy of the filing; [XBRL] = value from the SEC XBRL company-facts API (data.sec.gov), tied to the accession that first reported it; [secondary] = press or news; [snippet only] = search-result text, not usable as evidence; [my arithmetic]; [opinion].

**Short answer (Revision 2).** Both, and the split now differs by period:
1. **About a third to a half of FY26 growth is lapping, not new demand.** FY26 NA wholesale was $11,958M, only 8.7% above FY24 and 6.1% above FY23 [my arithmetic]. Q1 FY26 (+11%) was still 1.3% *below* Q1 FY24. Q4 FY26 (+10%) was only 0.5% above Q4 FY24 [my arithmetic].
2. **Q2 FY26 (+24%) contained sell-in that Nike itself now calls a "higher comparison of North America sell-in activities."** It also included liquidation to off-price "value channels." It is the only quarter where NA wholesale ran far above its FY24 level (+22%) [my arithmetic]. Units carried it: after removing channel mix, wholesale like-for-like price/mix was about 0% to +3% (T6) [my arithmetic, model].
3. **Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 (+10%, +9%) are a mix of four things:**
   - **Price/mix.** Wholesale-only like-for-like price/mix in Q1 FY27 was about +2% to +3.4%, which is 23% to 38% of the +9% (T6) [my arithmetic, model]. Revision 1's "price ruled out" was wrong for Q1 FY27.
   - **A one-time World Cup sell-in.** It largely sold through at sporting goods: Dick's credits its +4.9% Q2 2026 comp partly to "strong results from the 2026 FIFA World Cup," and DICK'S Business inventory grew only 6% against sales of +5.6% after the tournament. It is real but not recurring, and its size is not disclosed.
   - **Channel fill at Foot Locker.** Foot Locker inventory at August 1, 2026 was +14.1% to +15.5% year on year, while its sales fell 6.5% and its cost of goods sold fell 4.3% [my arithmetic, corrected from +17%]. On a cost basis, inventory/COGS rose 20%. Tariff or price inflation in landed cost explains only about 2 points of that (Foot Locker gross margin fell 1.7 points). The build happened between May 2 and August 1, 2026: Foot Locker inventory was +3.6% to +5.1% at May 2. Nike says aged Sportswear "sold-through below expectations," it has been "oversupplying our iconic retro product," and "North America will feel the biggest impact."
   - **Real performance sell-through** (Running, Basketball) at sporting goods.
4. **Not drivers:** liquidation in Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 (it was a headwind then), currency, pre-buying ahead of the price increase, reserve-estimate releases (not material in FY26 per the 10-K), and a Q4 FY26 to Q1 FY27 shipment shift.

**Still unresolved:**
- How the roughly $954M of FY26 NA wholesale above FY24 splits between new doors, channel shift, liquidation and fill.
- Nike's share of the Foot Locker build. Nike is 59% of Foot Locker purchases, but the build is not split by brand.
- The size of the World Cup sell-in.

No public filing gives these (Section 4).

### T6. Wholesale-only like-for-like price/mix: removing channel mix from Nike's blended ASP [my arithmetic, model]

Method:
1. Hold each channel's price per unit fixed, with the Direct price per unit = r x the wholesale price per unit (r = 1.3, 1.6, 2.0, because Direct sells at retail and wholesale at the wholesale price; r is my assumption).
2. The channel-mix effect on blended ASP = (revenue index) / (unit index) - 1, using NA wholesale and Direct revenue growth from T1.
3. Like-for-like price/mix = (1 + Nike's observed ASP effect) / (1 + mix effect) - 1, assumed equal in both channels.
4. Footwear and apparel are assumed to share NA's total channel split, which Nike does not disclose by product.

| Period | NA wholesale | NA Direct | Mix effect on ASP (r 1.3 to 2.0) | Footwear like-for-like | Apparel like-for-like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 FY26 | +10.5% | -2.8% | -0.8% to -2.1% | -4.2% to -3.0% | -4.2% to -3.0% |
| Q2 FY26 | +23.9% | -9.9% | -1.9% to -4.7% | -3.1% to -0.3% | +6.1% to +9.1% |
| Q3 FY26 | +10.8% | -4.5% | -1.0% to -2.4% | +3.0% to +4.5% | 0.0% to +1.4% |
| FY26 | +14.1% | -5.9% | -1.2% to -3.0% | +0.2% to +2.1% | +3.2% to +5.1% |
| Q1 FY27 | +9.0% | -6.0% | -0.9% to -2.3% | -0.1% to +1.3% | +7.0% to +8.5% |

Results:
- **Q1 FY27, revenue-weighted** (footwear $3,259M, apparel $1,566M, equipment $302M at zero price effect): wholesale like-for-like price/mix +2.1% / +2.8% / +3.4% for r = 1.3 / 1.6 / 2.0. Wholesale units are then +6.7% / +6.0% / +5.3%, and price/mix is 23% / 31% / 38% of the +9.0%.
- **Q2 FY26** (same weights, an assumption): price/mix -0.1% to +2.6%, so units were about +21% to +24%.

Conclusions:
- Revision 1 said price was "ruled out as the main driver." That holds for Q2 FY26 and FY26.
- In Q1 FY27, price/mix is a material minority share, led by apparel, which includes the World Cup kits (high ASP).
