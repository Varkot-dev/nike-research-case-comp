Reading (DERIVED):
- The cost of the reset showed up in NA footwear units (-11% and -9% in Q2 and Q3 FY18) and NA EBIT (-14% in both Q2 and Q3 FY18, 10-Q pp. 27 and 28).
- The cost did not show up in NA gross margin, which moved only -40 to +30 bps.
- The payback was lower discounts. NA footwear ASP added 3 to 8 points in every FY19 quarter, and NA gross margin rose 20 to 50 bps.
- The filings attribute these moves to all of NA, not to Jordan. The Jordan share cannot be separated.

### 4b. Similarities

1. **Same mechanism and same language.** Both resets are deliberate supply cuts of retro product, with no price cut, framed as protecting scarcity. In 2017: "managing the balance between scarcity and scale" (Edwards). In 2026: "get back to leading the scarcity model that we created" (Hill). Nike's own CFO invoked FY18 as the template in June 2024.
2. **North America is the epicentre, with wholesale partners consulted.** FY18 cuts were explicitly North America. Q1 FY27: "North America will feel the biggest impact."
3. **The playbook names the same growth vectors.** FY18 named women's, apparel, international and performance. Q1 FY27 names dimensionalizing "through sport ... cleated business, our golf business, our training business" (Hill, p. 23). The offset is again expected from outside retro footwear.

### 4c. The three most important differences

1. **Scale and duration.** Today's decline is about 7 times larger in dollars ($1.67B vs $0.24B, different bases). Relative to Nike it is about 4.6 times larger (3.2% vs 0.7% of revenue). It has already lasted about 10 quarters vs 3. Today is not one reset but two waves: AJ1 from FY25, then broad retro in FY27.
2. **No international offset engine.** In FY18, China Jordan grew 30% to 50% while North America was cut. Today Greater China is -26% in Q1 FY27 and is being restructured itself. FY18's global growth came back before North America did: global growth by Q2 FY19, North America only in Q1 FY20. That sequencing is unavailable now.
3. **Demand backdrop, not just supply.**
   - In FY18 inventory was clean within 2 quarters, and Nike said hot styles were undersupplied on SNKRS.
   - Today Nike's own 10-Q cites "higher levels of discounting and broader marketplace pressure".
   - Resale data show most Jordan releases trading below retail.
   - North America footwear ASP is falling on discounts (Q1 FY27), whereas it rose 3 to 8 points in each FY19 quarter.
   - Competitors such as HOKA (about 17x larger than in 2018) take share of the same footwear spend.
   - The filings cannot separate how much of Jordan's mid-teens decline is planned supply versus lower sell-through.

## 5. What the analog implies for timing (DERIVED, conditional)

FY18 milestones, measured from acknowledgement in Sep 2017:
- Inventory "clean": +2 quarters.
- "Pull market": +3 quarters.
- Jordan a named global growth driver: +4 to 5 quarters.
- North America growth: +8 quarters.
- Full-year growth: FY19, the first full fiscal year after the reset.

Mechanical application to the broadened retro cut announced Oct 1, 2026:
- Clean by about Q3 FY27 (Dec 2026 to Feb 2027).
- Global Jordan growth by about Q1 to Q2 FY28 (Jun to Nov 2027).
- North America growth by about Q1 FY29 (mid-2028).

Management's own guidance is already slower than this mechanical path. Denton: pressure "probably bleed in a little bit into fiscal '28". The 10-Q: actions "extend beyond fiscal 2027". Hill (Q4 FY26): improvement in the "back half" of FY27.

Given the three differences above, the analog supports a Jordan return to growth **no earlier than FY28, with risk to FY29**. The FY18 template's six-year growth run relied on China, collab hype and an AJ1 that was undersupplied. Today none of these is present in the record.

The AJ1-specific wave offers a partial in-episode test:
- Named Oct 2024 (Q1 FY25).
- "Inventory levels are returning to health" Sep 2025: +4 quarters.
- "Stabilized" with improving full-price realization Mar 2026: +6 quarters.

That is slower than FY18's 2 to 3 quarters to clean. It is the better base rate for the broadened retro cut.
