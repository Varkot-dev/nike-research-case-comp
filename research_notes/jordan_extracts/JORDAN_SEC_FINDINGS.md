# Jordan Brand: every data point in Nike SEC filings, FY2017 10-K to Q1 FY2027 10-Q

Prepared 2026-10-13 for the Nike stock-pitch case. Source: 219 documents downloaded from sec.gov (Nike CIK 0000320187) into `jordan_sec/dl/filings`, converted to paged text with `scripts/sec_pipeline/topages.py` (`jordan_sec/text`). Coverage: every 10-K for FY2017 to FY2026, every 10-Q from Q1 FY2017 to Q1 FY2027 (filed 2026-10-02), every earnings 8-K (item 2.02) with Exhibit 99.1 since June 2016 (including the FY2016 to FY2018 conference-call transcripts Nike filed as 8-K exhibits), the 2025 and 2026 DEF 14A, and every 2023 to 2026 8-K with items 5.02, 7.01, 8.01 or 2.05. 77 of these documents mention "Jordan" (662 mentions in total); all of them are listed in the Appendix.

Page conventions: "Printed p." is the page number in the filing's footer (for example "2025 FORM 10-K 33"). Earnings releases and other 8-K exhibits have no printed page, so they are cited as "p. N of exhibit", counted from the exhibit's first page. In quotes, Nike's em dashes are shown as hyphens. Nothing is typed from memory: every value below was read off the cited page. Anything I calculated is labelled **derived**.

## 1. Key findings

1. **The Jordan revenue series breaks twice. Do not chain the numbers across a break.**
   - **Break 1 (FY2022 10-K).** Nike moved to the Men's / Women's / Kids' consumer construct, and Jordan stopped being one of several product "categories". FY2021 Jordan went from $4,711M to $4,780M, and FY2020 from $3,609M to $3,687M. Both series are still on a wholesale equivalent basis.
   - **Break 2 (Q1 FY2025 onward).** Nike "removed the non-GAAP financial measure of wholesale equivalent revenues" as its new ERP system rolled out. Jordan is now reported in GAAP NIKE Brand revenue terms, which include NIKE Direct revenue at retail prices. The restatements were:
     - FY2024: $6,988M (wholesale equivalent) became $8,701M.
     - FY2023: $6,589M became $8,460M.
     - The FY2024 growth rate went from +6% to +3% reported. **Derived:** 8,701 / 8,460 - 1 = +2.8%.
2. **Jordan on the current basis.** FY2023 $8,460M, FY2024 $8,701M (the peak), FY2025 $7,270M (-16% reported and currency-neutral), FY2026 $7,034M (-3% reported, -5% currency-neutral). **Derived:** FY2026 is 19.2% below FY2024.
3. **Quarterly Jordan figures.** No 10-Q and no Q1 to Q3 earnings release in the window gives Jordan dollars or a Jordan growth rate. 10-Qs only name Jordan qualitatively as a driver. The only quarterly numbers are management remarks in FY2017 and FY2018 call transcripts filed on 8-K (Section 3).
4. **Q1 FY2027.** The 10-Q (printed p. 20) says "additional actions related to NIKE Sportswear and Jordan Brand are expected to extend beyond fiscal 2027". The release headline lists "Taking actions to reposition NIKE Sportswear, Jordan Brand, and Greater China". No Jordan revenue is given.
5. **Michael Jordan's own arrangement.** No filing names Michael Jordan's endorsement or royalty deal or treats it as a related-party item. Jordan sits inside the aggregate endorsement obligations: $15.5B as of May 31, 2026, covering "base compensation and minimum guaranteed royalty fees". The 2026 proxy's related-person section names Knight and Parker family members and Craig Williams' son-in-law, nobody connected to Michael Jordan.
6. **Sarah Mensah.** The only filing that names her is the 2023-05-24 8-K Ex. 99.1: "Sarah Mensah, Vice President/GM, North America, will become President, Jordan Brand." She does not appear in the 2026 DEF 14A or in any 10-K executive officer list.

## 3. The restatements, explained from the filings' own words

### 3a. FY2025: wholesale equivalent removed. This explains FY2024 going from $6,988M to $8,701M.

**What the old number measured.** FY2024 10-K, "Use of Non-GAAP Financial Measures", printed p. 32:
> "References to wholesale equivalent revenues are intended to provide context as to the total size of our NIKE Brand market footprint if we had no NIKE Direct operations. NIKE Brand wholesale equivalent revenues consist of (1) sales to external wholesale customers and (2) internal sales from our wholesale operations to our NIKE Direct operations, which are charged at prices comparable to those charged to external wholesale customers. Beginning in fiscal 2025, with the continued rollout of a new Enterprise Resource Planning Platform, the Company will replace wholesale equivalent revenues and gross margin drivers with a comparable U.S. GAAP metric."

The FY2024 10-K revenue table (printed p. 34) puts Jordan $6,988M under the heading "NIKE Brand Wholesale Equivalent Revenues by:". That block totals "TOTAL NIKE BRAND WHOLESALE EQUIVALENT REVENUES | 40,767", against reported "Total NIKE Brand Revenues | 49,322".

**The change, first disclosed.** Q1 FY2025 10-Q (filed 2024-10-07), printed p. 19:
> "We have completed the implementation of a new Enterprise Resource Planning ("ERP") Platform in our Greater China and North America geographies as part of a global rollout integrating wholesale and NIKE Direct operations and enhancing supply chain and finance capabilities. As a result, beginning with the first quarter of fiscal 2025, we have removed the non-GAAP financial measure of wholesale equivalent revenues. There is no change to our reported revenues or gross margin."
URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018724000065/nke-20240831.htm

**The recast, shown.** FY2025 10-K, footnote (4) to the revenue table, printed p. 33. The same wording is footnote 3 of the Q4 FY2025 release, p. 5 of exhibit:
> "Beginning in fiscal 2025, with the continued rollout of a new Enterprise Resource Planning Platform, we have removed the non-GAAP financial measure of wholesale equivalent revenues. There is no change to our reported revenues or gross margin. Prior year amounts have been recast to conform to fiscal 2025 presentation."

In that table the heading for Men's, Women's, Kids', Jordan Brand and Others changes. It is now "NIKE Brand Revenues by: (4)", and the rows sum to "TOTAL NIKE BRAND REVENUES | 44,714 | 49,322", which is GAAP revenue. Before, the same rows summed to the wholesale equivalent total.

**What this means (my interpretation, based on the definition above).** Under the old measure, Jordan sold through NIKE Direct counted at the internal wholesale-type transfer price. Jordan sold to wholesale customers counted at the wholesale price. Under the new basis, Jordan's NIKE Direct sales count at the GAAP retail revenue NIKE Direct actually books. So the recast adds the Direct markup over wholesale price. It does not reflect any change in what counts as a Jordan product. No filing says the scope of Jordan products changed in FY2025.

**Derived:**
- FY2024 uplift: 8,701 - 6,988 = $1,713M (+24.5%).
- FY2023 uplift: 8,460 - 6,589 = $1,871M (+28.4%).
- FY2024 growth falls from +6% (WE) to +3% (GAAP, as printed).

Practical warning: if you compare FY2025 $7,270M with the FY2024 10-K's $6,988M, you get +4% (derived). That figure is wrong. The filed number is -16%.

### 3b. FY2022: consumer construct. This explains FY2021 going from $4,711M to $4,780M and FY2020 from $3,609M to $3,687M.

FY2022 10-K, footnote (4) to the revenue table, printed p. 32. The FY2023 10-K repeats it on p. 32, and the Q4 FY2022 release on p. 5 of exhibit:
> "As a result of the Consumer Direct Acceleration strategy, announced in fiscal 2021, the Company is now organized around a new consumer construct of Men's, Women's and Kids'. Beginning in the first quarter of fiscal 2022, unisex products are classified within Men's, and Jordan Brand revenues are separately reported. Certain prior year amounts have been reclassified to conform to fiscal 2022 presentation. These changes had no impact on previously reported consolidated results of operations or shareholders' equity."

How the tables changed:
- **Before (FY2017 to FY2021 10-Ks).** Jordan was one row in a category table: Running, NIKE Basketball, Jordan Brand, Football (Soccer), Training, Sportswear, Others. It sat alongside a separate gender table (Men's, Women's, Young Athletes'/NIKE Kids', Others).
- **After (FY2022 10-K).** The category table is gone. Jordan is a fifth line in the consumer table next to Men's, Women's and NIKE Kids'.

The filing does not quantify the +$69M (FY2021) and +$78M (FY2020) changes (derived). They come from reclassifying Jordan "separately" from the gender lines, and the filing gives no product-level detail.

### 3c. FY2018: a $1M revision to FY2017

FY2017 was $3,099M in the FY2017 10-K and $3,098M in the FY2018 10-K. The FY2018 10-K footnote (1) (printed p. 23) says "Certain prior year amounts have been reclassified to conform to fiscal 2018 presentation." The FY2018 10-K also folded Men's Training and Women's Training into one "Training" category. The $1M change is immaterial.

## 10. Method and limits

- **Download.** `jordan_sec/bin/dl.py`, an adaptation of `scripts/sec_pipeline/dl.py` with a wider date range, fetched the data.sec.gov submissions index and every matching filing with a User-Agent that carried a personal contact address (since stopped; see CLAUDE.md on the other branches: filings now come from the investors.nike.com mirror). The manifest with every URL is at `jordan_sec/dl/manifest.json`.
- **Text conversion.** `topages.py` converted the HTML to paged text. Printed pages were taken from the footers. Where the footer parser failed (FY2019 10-K seq. p. 30 and DEF 14A seq. p. 8), I checked the footer by hand: 28 and 7.
- **What was read.** Every Jordan mention in every document was extracted with its page (Appendix) and read. The revenue tables, their footnotes and the non-GAAP definition pages were read in full for each 10-K and each Q4 release. I did not read every page of all 219 documents end to end. Statements that something is "not in any filing" rest on full-text searches across all 219.
- **XBRL.** Not used. Nike does not tag Jordan revenue as a separate XBRL fact; it appears only in an MD&A table and footnote.
