# Why Converse collapsed, FY2023 to Q1 FY2027

Prepared Oct 13, 2026. Scope: Nike's own explanations (10-K and 10-Q MD&A, earnings releases, earnings call transcripts) plus outside context checked under the `verify-non-sec-claims` rules. Headline figures (revenue $2,427M FY23 to $1,174M FY26, EBIT $676M to $18M, Q1 FY27 -28%) are taken as established and not re-derived here.

**Sourcing notes.**
- www.sec.gov rejected requests without a declared contact User-Agent, and no contact was set. I did not invent one. ## (a) Summary of drivers

1. **Unit volume explains about 85% of the revenue decline (my arithmetic, see note 06 Section 5): units fell 12%, 12%, 31% and 34% in FY24, FY25, FY26 and Q1 FY27, first in North America and Western Europe, then in all territories.** In FY2024 Nike blamed declines "in North America and Western Europe". From FY2025 on, every filing says "all territories". The number of pairs sold fell 12% in FY24, 12% in FY25, 31% in FY26 and 34% in Q1 FY27. Fewer shoppers came to Converse's own stores and websites ("reduced traffic"), which Nike cites in every period.
2. **Price cuts in FY24 and FY25 reduced gross margin by 510 basis points over two years, and costs fell more slowly than revenue.** In FY24 and FY25 Nike tied lower selling prices to "increased promotional activity" and "higher discounts in direct to consumer". Gross margin fell 130 basis points in FY24, 380 in FY25 and 490 in FY26. Costs below gross profit fell 21% against a 52% revenue decline, rising from 25.9% to 42.3% of revenue (my arithmetic), so EBIT went from $676M to $18M.
3. **In mid-2025 Nike changed course to a "strategic reset", which shrank the brand further on purpose.** Nike replaced Converse's CEO (Jared Carver out, Aaron Cain in, July 2025). It said the Chuck Taylor was "in the early stages of a global market reset". It then cut shipments to retailers, wrote down old inventory, laid off staff (February 2026) and cut brand marketing by 39% for FY26 and 58% in Q1 FY27. U.S. Converse stores fell from 81 to 60 between May 2024 and May 2026. Nike does not split the FY26 decline between deliberate supply cuts and lower retail sell-through, so the share caused by the reset cannot be quantified from filings.
4. **Outside commentators attribute the volume decline to product concentration in the Chuck Taylor and a lifestyle-category slowdown. The measurable evidence: Nike cut Dunk revenue by nearly 50% in Q1 FY27, and Vans revenue fell 14% in VF's Q1 FY26 (secondary). The analyst and column statements below are opinion, not data.** A BNP Paribas analyst said Converse "didn't bring new innovation" and had "an over-dependency on the Chuck Taylor shoe". An October 2025 opinion column noted that shoppers moving on from Adidas's Samba were not switching to Chucks. Nike's own CEO said in October 2026 that "there's a lack of energy in the lifestyle space right now". Vans (owned by VF Corp.) shows a similar pattern in the same category.
5. **Nike has given no date for a return to growth. It does see early signs of less discounting.** Nike says the Converse actions "are expected to continue throughout fiscal 2027". Q1 FY27 was the first quarter in this run in which average selling price rose (by about 6 points, from "product mix and lower discounts"), but pairs sold fell 34%. Converse will now focus on the Chuck Taylor and Jack Purcell lines for "creators". Its basketball star, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, moved to Nike Basketball in June 2026. Nike has publicly rejected sale speculation (Authentic Brands Group was reported as an interested buyer) and says more detail will come at its November 2026 Investor Day.

**What Nike's own documents do and do not say.**
- **Geographies:**
  - FY24 declines were in North America and Western Europe. Asia DTC was still growing.
  - In FY25, wholesale fell in Western Europe and Asia while North America wholesale grew, and DTC traffic fell "in all territories".
  - From Q1 FY26 every channel declined in all territories.
- **Units vs price:**
  - FY24 and FY25 combined falling units with falling ASP, which was discount-driven.
  - FY26 was almost entirely units: down 31%, with ASP costing only about 1 point.
  - Q1 FY27 is the first period in this run with ASP up, from mix and lower discounts.
- **Inventory clean-up:**
  - Obsolescence reserves first appear in Q2 FY25 and again in Q3 FY26 and FY26.
  - The company-wide marketplace actions (higher markdowns, wholesale returns and discounts) are described at the Nike, Inc. level, not separately for Converse.
- **What is never stated:**
  - Nike does not quantify Converse inventory in any filing.
  - No Converse-specific growth date or revenue target is given. The only timing is "throughout fiscal 2027".
