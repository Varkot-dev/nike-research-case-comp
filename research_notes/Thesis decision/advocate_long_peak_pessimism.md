## 1. Thesis in one sentence

Nike's stock now prices a business that is permanently impaired, and the evidence for that is measurable (the lowest EV/sales in at least 11 years, short interest up about 7x, 19 of 21 target cuts, estimates down 25% in a month, a 21% shrink in the number of institutional holders), while the next dated events (Investor Day Nov 16 to 17, the Dec Q2 print, the January China channel reset) are being judged against a bar that management has cleared on every quarterly guide item under Hill (22 of 22), so the odds of a positive surprise are better than the price implies.

## 2. Variant perception: what the market believes vs what we believe

**What the market believes (measured, not inferred):**
- The sell side has capitulated. FY2027 consensus EPS fell from $1.712 to $1.288 in 30 days (-25%); FY2028 from $2.221 to $1.646 (-26%), with 23 down revisions and 0 up. Sell plus strong-sell ratings went from 2 to 7 in three months. The median price target ($34.40) is **below** the share price (REPO `consensus_and_valuation.md`, Yahoo, fetched Oct 13).
- Holders have given up. The number of 13F filers holding NKE fell from 2,200 (Dec 2024) to 1,727 (Jun 2026), -21%. Growth and quality funds exited: Pershing Square (18.8M shares), Viking (9.0M), Jennison (11.8M), Fundsmith (8.0M), Janus Henderson (7.4M), Longview (5.1M) (REPO `17_insider_and_institutional_flows.md`, SEC 13F data sets).
- Index money has left. Nike was removed from the S&P 100 before trading on Sep 21, 2026 (REPO REPORT.md, secondary: Daily Tidings/AP; corroborated by a search snippet citing the Sep 21 date, SNIPPET).
- Short sellers are pressing. 99.2M shares short on 2026-09-30, 8.2% of Class B and 6.7% of all shares, up from 14.5M in Dec 2023 (6.9x) and +73% in the two months from 57.4M on 2026-07-31 (REPO note 17, FINRA).

**What we believe:** the price assumes that the margin and revenue damage of FY2025 to FY2027 is mostly permanent. The repo's own evidence says most of the revenue damage came from Nike's own decisions (about 65% to 75% of gross headwinds, per the root-cause ledger in `00_root_cause_synthesis.md` on `claude/stoic-shannon-dmkmyu`) and that the fix has already worked where it was applied first: North America grew in five straight quarters (+4.0%, +8.8%, +3.3%, +2.7%, +2.1%, Q1 FY26 to Q1 FY27; REPO, Nike 10-Qs). Sentiment is priced for the bad regions (Greater China -26% c-n, Converse) to define the whole company.

**Why we could be right (the mechanism, not a feeling):** when expectations are cut this hard, the next data point only needs to be "less bad" to move estimates up. Management has beaten its own near-term guide on all 22 items under Hill, with revenue landing 2.3 points above the guide midpoint on average (REPO `19_management_track_record.md`). Consensus for Q2 FY27 EPS has already fallen from $0.523 to $0.381 and revenue to $11,176M (-10.1% y/y) (REPO `consensus_and_valuation.md`).

**Honest qualifier:** the market does not reward Nike beats. 13 of the 18 tracker-period earnings events fell on day one, and Hill-era beats produced day-one moves from -15.5% to +15.2% (REPO note 19). This is why the thesis leans on Investor Day and positioning rather than on "they will beat."

**Alternatives ruled out (and how)**
- *"Short interest is just option hedging, not bearish bets."* Partly true: 13F filers held puts on 77.7M shares at Jun 2026, mostly market makers (Susquehanna 17.4M, Citadel 14.4M, Jane Street 14.0M) (REPO note 17). - *"A squeeze will do the work."* Ruled out as a pillar: days to cover is only 2.59 (FINRA), so there is little squeeze fuel (REPO note 17). The positioning argument is about who is left to sell, not about forced buying.

**What one lever is worth (simple arithmetic already in the repo):**
- Each 100 bps of gross margin on $43B to $44B of revenue is about $430M to $436M of operating profit, about $0.22 to $0.23 of EPS (REPO `16_full_price_recovery.md`).
- Pace savings: "approximately $2.5 billion in cumulative savings through fiscal 2031" (8-K filed Oct 1, 2026, p. 2, REPO). That is about $0.5B a year on average, about $0.25 of EPS a year at a 25% tax rate (REPO note 19, *arithmetic in note*).
- The FY24 to FY26 margin bridge is decomposable: gross margin -1.7 pts, demand creation -1.9 pts (spending choice: $4,285M to $4,754M while revenue fell), overhead deleverage -0.6 pts (overhead dollars actually fell $931M) (REPO `14_profit_bridge_and_thesis_scorecard.md`, FY24 and FY26 10-Ks). Two of the three big lines (marketing as a choice, overhead deleverage) are not structural by construction.

**Alternatives ruled out (and how)**
- *"It is not cheap: 27x forward earnings."* Conceded on FY27 EPS. But FY27 EPS of $1.288 implies a 5.7% EBIT margin, below Nike's 10-year low of 8.0% (FY20, a COVID year) (REPO consensus note). A trough P/E on trough margin is the normal shape of a cyclical bottom, which is why the sales and EBITDA multiples are the better yardstick here.
- *"It is only cheap vs its own history, not vs peers."* True: on EV/Sales (1.20x on a lease-inclusive basis) Nike is at the peer median of 1.18x (REPO consensus note). The long case does not need Nike to trade at a premium; it needs margins to recover toward the 10-year average while the sales multiple stays at a peer-median level.
- *"Some of the de-rating is sector-wide and will not come back."* Accepted: about 45% of the log price fall is sector de-rating (adidas P/E fell by a similar 52%) (REPO root-cause synthesis). This caps the upside; it does not change the direction. I do not assume a return to historical multiples anywhere in this brief.

### Pillar 4. The dated catalysts are set against a low bar, and the mechanical comparisons ease from here

**Evidence on the bar**
- FY27 guide: revenue down high-single digits; adjusted EPS $1.15 to $1.35. Consensus $1.288 sits just above the $1.25 midpoint (REPO consensus note). Q2 FY27 consensus revenue $11,176M is -10.1% y/y and EPS $0.381 (down from $0.523 30 days earlier) (REPO consensus note).
- Nike has already told the market about a one-off Q2 headwind of "approximately 400 basis points" from lapping EMEA Cyber Week promotions and higher North America sell-in a year earlier (Q1 FY27 call, REPO REPORT.md). It is in the numbers already.
- Under Hill, quarterly guidance has been met or beaten on 22 of 22 items, revenue beat in 6 of 7 quarters (REPO note 19).
- Nike promised to "return to providing full year and long-term guidance at our Investor Day in the fall" (Q3 FY26 call p. 12 to 13, REPO note 19). A company that has just cut, restructured (Pace, $1.0B charges) and replaced its CFO (Denton from Aug 17) has every incentive to set targets it can beat.

**Evidence on easier comparisons (units and channel, not mood)**
- Greater China: c-n declines of -16% (Q2 FY26), -10% (Q3), -17% (Q4) and -26% (Q1 FY27) mean the FY27 quarters are comped against a base already cut by double digits (REPO Gulden/Plank report table, Nike 10-Qs). From Jan 1, 2027, Nike ends all distributor online sales in mainland China; that channel was about 22% of Topsports' revenue (Topsports HKEX notice, Jul 22, 2026, per REPO report). Partner online sold more Nike at retail than Nike Digital did (REPO `investigations/02` on `zealous-mccarthy`). If Nike's own channels recapture part of it, the reset is a revenue headwind followed by a price and margin tailwind.
- Classics: management said the classics franchises were "on track to decline from peak levels by more than $4 billion by fiscal year-end" FY26 (Q2 FY26 call, REPO report); Dunk revenue was cut by "nearly 50%", about a $200M Q1 FY27 headwind (Q1 FY27 call, REPO report). The smaller the remaining classics base, the smaller the drag it can create.
- North America footwear units +6% in FY26 (FY26 10-K p. 37, REPO); NA revenue up five straight quarters.
- Performance about $16B in FY26 and up high-single digits in Q1 FY27, "up low double digits" ex Greater China (Q1 FY27 call, REPO report). Nike run-specialty dollars +35.4% in the 12 months to Sep 2025 while On -19.7% and Hoka -7.4% (Karnan/Circana via SGB, REPO report, OPENED by that lane).
- Options price a one-day Q2 move of about 6.4% to 7.2% (1 sd), against a 14-quarter average absolute move of 8.3% (REPO note 17). The event is not priced as a tail.

**Peer analog: sentiment bottoms before fundamentals (with the counter-example)**
- adidas: monthly close low of EUR 98.98 in Sep 2022; EUR 159.44 by Mar 2023 (+61%) and EUR 184.00 by Jun 2023 (+86%) (Yahoo monthly chart ADS.DE, fetched Oct 13, *my arithmetic*, OPENED). Over the same window adidas's currency-neutral growth was 0% in Q1 and Q2 2023 and operating margin 1.1% and 3.3% (REPO Gulden/Plank report). The stock moved roughly four quarters ahead of the growth.
- Puma: monthly low EUR 18.42 in Sep 2025; EUR 27.50 by Jun 2026 (+49%), then EUR 22.98 by Sep 2026 (Yahoo monthly chart PUM.DE, OPENED). A sentiment bounce without a fundamental follow-through gave much of it back.
- Under Armour: monthly low $4.46 in Sep 2026, no rebound yet, nine quarters of decline (Yahoo UAA, OPENED; REPO report). This is the bear's analog, and Nike's Greater China and guidance slippage look like it.

**Alternatives ruled out (and how)**
- *"The catalysts are negative: Investor Day will kitchen-sink."* That is possible, and a kitchen sink is part of how a bottom forms (adidas guided to an operating loss in March 2023, after its stock had already risen 61% from the low). The risk is a cut that the market did not expect, mainly the dividend (see Section 6).
- *"The January China change will push revenue down further."* Agreed for revenue, and Denton's guide "assumes that China actually gets worse" (Q1 FY27 call, REPO report). It is already guided, so it should not surprise.

## 5. What proves this thesis wrong

1. **A dividend cut or a skipped raise at the Nov declaration.** The $1.64 rate is 121% to 143% of guided FY27 adjusted EPS; FCF covered 0.91x the dividend in FY26 and was -$64M in Q1 FY27 against $610M of dividends; the "consecutive years of increasing dividend payouts" line was dropped from the last two releases (REPO note 18). Note 18 puts about 15% on a cut and 40% on a freeze. Put-call parity implies a dividend roughly 45% to 60% below the current rate (REPO note 17, qualified). A cut would tell us management expects a long trough and would reset the yield-support argument.
2. **Investor Day with no dated, measurable targets**, or a long-term EBIT margin target below about 10%. The price already discounts about 11% to 14% depending on WACC (REPO consensus note); a lower target removes the "price assumes too little" argument.
3. **Short interest keeps rising after Oct 1 while the price falls** (Oct 15 and Oct 31 prints). That would mean shorts are adding into weakness, not running out of sellers.
4. **Capital Group or Sixth Street cut in the Q3 13Fs**, or any Knight-family sale (watch Phil Knight Form 4s for "by spouse" changes on the 4.5M Class B shares given to Penelope Knight; REPO note 17).
5. **North America turns negative** in Q2 FY27, which would show the five-quarter run was channel fill. Foot Locker inventory was +14% to +16% against sales -6.5% (REPO `investigations/01`, `retailer_sell_through.md`).
6. **Feng Tay monthly revenue in Oct and Nov falls double digits**, which (with a correlation of about 0.68 to Nike footwear growth at about a one-quarter lead) would point to Q3 FY27 weakness (REPO `DEEP_DIVE_DEFINITIVE_TESTS.md` Test 1).

## 6. Weakest points (stated plainly)

1. **The base rates are against us on the one variable that mattered most.** Among idiosyncratic 75%+ drawdowns, 6 of 11 with EV/sales at or below 1.0x recovered, against **0 of 9 above 1.0x** (Fisher p = 0.014). Nike is at 1.13x. In the Nike-like valuation subset, only 45% beat the S&P 500 over 3 years (median -34%) and 0 of 10 got back within 25% of the peak (REPO `20_base_rates.md`). The broad idiosyncratic median from the 80% crossing is still +52% at 1 year with 67% positive, but survivorship bias inflates it. This is the single strongest argument against a long.
2. **Sentiment is not a thesis by itself.** None of the flow signals is strong enough to carry a pitch alone (REPO note 17's own verdict). Contrarian setups need a fundamental turn to hold, as Puma's give-back in 2026 shows.
3. **Insiders went quiet exactly when the contrarian case needs them.** No purchase since Oct 1 at $32 to $36, after buying at $42 and $61.
4. **The near-term fundamentals still point down.** Q1 FY27 revenue -4.3%; Greater China -26% c-n for a ninth straight decline; the NIKE Brand ASP line turned negative again (-30 bps) in Q1 FY27, and the Sportswear and Jordan clean-up now runs "beyond fiscal 2027" (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 20, REPO notes 15, 16). Nike's past clean-ups took 3 to 4 quarters; this one is at 7 and counting.
5. **North America's growth may be partly sell-in.** About a third to a half of FY26 NA wholesale growth restored FY25 holdbacks; the FY26 NA gross margin is -350 bps excluding the $965M IEEPA refund (REPO `investigations/01`, `05`, `09`).
6. **Management's multi-year record is poor.** Every 2017 and 2021 long-term profit target was missed; the first full-year gross margin guide missed in all three years that had one (REPO note 19). An Investor Day target is not credible on its own.
7. **Part of the de-rating is permanent.** About 45% of the log share-price fall is sector-wide (REPO root-cause synthesis), so the upside is capped well below historical multiples.
8. **The biggest dated event that tests the fundamentals (Q2 FY27, about Dec 17) falls after the Nov 19 finals.** Before finals, only Investor Day, the dividend, short-interest prints, 13Fs and Feng Tay's October number will be public.
9. **The market does not reward beats.** 13 of 18 tracker-period earnings days were down (REPO note 19).
10. **On earnings, the stock is not cheap.** 27.4x FY27 and 21.5x FY28 consensus vs a peer median of 15.5x and 12.9x (REPO consensus note).

## 7. Self-assessment

Confidence that this is the right pitch: **4 out of 10.** The positioning evidence is real, current and well sourced, and the setup (low bar, dated catalysts, flat skew) is internally consistent. But the long rests on events that have not happened, the base rates point the other way on valuation, and the strongest fundamental evidence in the repo (China, ASP, sell-in, dividend coverage) leans short. If the team chooses long, it should be framed as "expectations reset below what the next events will show", with the dividend declaration and Investor Day as explicit go or no-go tests, not as "the stock is cheap".

### B. Factual errors and misquotes

1. **The reverse DCF range is quoted selectively.** The brief and summary say the price discounts "about 11 to 14%" and "needs only a return to roughly Nike's 10-year average margin". The repo's base case uses a 10.0% WACC (10-year UST 5.24%, beta 1.10, 5% ERP) and gives **13.8%** (13.4% to 14.1%). That is above FY24's 12.7% and the 11.8% 10-year average. The 11% to 12% figure only appears at a 9% WACC, which with a 5.24% risk-free rate implies an equity risk premium of only about 4.0% (*my arithmetic*: (9.0 - 0.13 x 4.7) / 0.87 = about 9.6% cost of equity; (9.6 - 5.24) / 1.10 = about 4.0%, against the 5% the note uses). The note's own per-share check: a 12.5% margin with 4% growth is worth **$33.55 at 10% WACC**, below $35.35; a 10% margin is worth $24.52 to $26.19 (`consensus_and_valuation.md` lines 212, 239, 243). **Correct statement:** at the repo's base WACC, today's price already requires Nike to beat its 10-year average margin and match or beat FY24.
2. **The summary's "11 to 14%" pairs a 9% WACC with 11% to 12% and a 10% WACC with 12% to 14%.** The note says 9% WACC gives 11% to 12% and 10% gives about 12% to 14% (base 13.8%). The pairing is right, but only the low end appears in the pillar headline.
3. **"The known Q2 400 bps one-off headwind is already in the -10.1% consensus."** That is plausible, but it is not verified. The repo has no consensus build that shows it. `investigations/01` sizes the 400 bps at about $497M of Q2 FY26 revenue. That does not show what consensus assumes.
4. **Investor Day date sourcing.** The brief says "Investor Day dates from Nike's Oct 1, 2026 release (REPO)". Note 18 (line 120) says the date comes from REPORT.md citing an Oct 1 *employee memo* that was not reopened, and note 16 (line 83) calls Nov 16 to 17 "snippet-only". The Q1 FY27 transcript says only "November". Do not put Nov 16 to 17 on a slide as filing-sourced.
5. **IEEPA refund figure.** The brief (weakest point 5) uses $965M. That is the North America share. Nike's total recovery was $986M ($302M received plus $684M receivable, FY26 10-K p. 30, per `investigations/05` line 20 on `zealous-mccarthy`). Both are right in context, but label which is which.
6. **adidas analog dates.** Yahoo monthly bars are stamped at month start in UTC, so the bar shown as 2022-09-30 is the **October 2022** close (OPENED). The low came in the month adidas cut guidance and ended the Yeezy deal, not in September. "+61% by Mar 2023" is really the April 2023 bar. This is small, but it matters for C4.

### C. Pillars where the evidence does not support the claim

**C1. Pillar 2 (valuation) contradicts the variant perception it is meant to support.**
- The brief says "what the market believes: Nike is permanently impaired." The repo's measured evidence says the opposite. Nike trades at **27.4x FY27 and 21.5x FY28 EPS against peer medians of 15.5x and 12.9x**. The consensus note's own reading is that "the P/E premium is the margin-recovery bet" (`consensus_and_valuation.md` line 199). The root-cause synthesis says Nike "still trades at about 28x guided FY27 EPS, so the market already prices a recovery" (`00_root_cause_synthesis.md` line 220, `stoic-shannon`, REPO).
- On current-year metrics there is no mispricing against peers: EV/Sales 1.20x vs 1.18x, EV/EBITDA 11.3x vs 11.6x, with a similar EBITDA margin (Nike 10.7%, adidas 10.2%, VF 9.4%) (same note, peer table). The EV/sales discount to Nike's own history is just the margin collapse: 1.13x / 3.06x = 37%, while the EBIT margin went from 11.8% to 8.2% (*my arithmetic*).
- "FY27 consensus implies a 5.7% EBIT margin, below the 10-year low" is not market pessimism. Consensus ($1.288) sits **above** management's own guidance midpoint ($1.25) (`consensus_and_valuation.md` line 143). The sell side has only adopted the company's guide. The relevant base rate for full-year guides is note 19's: only 1 of 4 first full-year revenue guides was met, and 0 of 3 first full-year gross margin guides (note 19 line 94).
- **The margin bridge does not prove reversibility.** The brief says demand creation (-1.9 pts) and overhead deleverage (-0.6 pts) are "not structural by construction". Overhead deleverage reverses only if revenue comes back, so that argument is circular. Demand creation reached a record 11.2% of revenue in Q1 FY27, and revenue still fell 4.3% (`00_root_cause_synthesis.md` line 218). If Nike needs that spend to hold share, cutting it back costs revenue. The bridge cannot tell "choice" from "cost of staying in the game".
- **Pace cannot close the gap in the relevant window.** The $0.5B a year average is about 110 bps of revenue, and "the majority" falls in FY29 to FY30 (note 19 line 52, line 210). The 2023 $2B plan was never scored, and margins fell during it (note 19 section D). Going from a 5.7% consensus margin to the 13.8% the base reverse DCF needs takes about 8 points. Pace supplies about 1 point, late.

**C2. Pillar 1 (positioning) is "extreme" only against Nike's own short history, and part of it argues the other way.**
- **Holder count fell, institutional shares did not.** Total 13F shares rose from 954.6M to 977.1M (79.3% to 81.2% of Class B), and top-5 concentration rose from 28.0% to 32.9% (note 17 line 25 and the breadth table). Small holders sold to large ones. That is consolidation, not "holders have given up".
- **Short interest of 6.7% of shares at 2.6 days to cover is not shown to be extreme against other stocks.** The repo has no cross-sectional percentile. The "6.9x" multiple partly reflects the 71% lower share price: dollar short only doubled, from $1.76B to $3.51B (note 17). The repo's cited literature (Boehmer, Jones and Zhang 2008) says heavily shorted stocks *underperform*. The +73% rise in two months came while the price fell from about $39 to $35, so shorts have been right on the recent move.
- **The skew argument is inverted from the source.** The brief says flat skew means "upside is cheap to express". Note 17's bottom line says the opposite: "The one actionable asymmetry ... downside protection costs no more than upside calls ... **A bearish view can be expressed cheaply in puts**" (note 17 line 277). The same note says call richness "can be fully explained by a market-expected dividend cut, which is bearish for income holders" (line 275). Flat skew is neutral at best.
- **The options move is not cheap.** The brief compares the 6.4% to 7.2% implied move with the 8.3% *mean*. The *median* historical move is 6.7% (note 17 line 246), so options price an ordinary print, as note 17 concludes. That does not support a long.
- **19 of 21 target cuts after a guide-down is mechanical.** Targets follow price and estimates. The ratings mix is not capitulation: Yahoo shows 1 strong buy and 9 buys, with a recommendation mean of 2.81, and MarketBeat shows 8 buys out of 39 (consensus note lines 75, 146). A real sentiment extreme would show a buy share near zero.

**C3. Pillar 3 ("smart money") has a negative record in this same stock.**
- Capital Group added at Q4 2025 (quarter-end $63.71), Q1 2026 ($52.82) and Q2 2026 ($41.05). Harris/Oakmark started in Q2 2025 at about $71 (note 17 flows table). Every tranche is under water at $35.35. Their buying did not mark a bottom; the stock fell a further 14% after the latest buying quarter (*my arithmetic*: 35.35 / 41.05 - 1). Insider purchases are -16% to -54%. The signal has been early three times. The brief needs a reason it is right this time, and none is given.
- The Q3 13Fs (due Nov 16) cover holdings as of Sep 30, *before* the Oct 1 guide cut, so they cannot show how this capital reacted to the new information.
- The repo's own literature note says the insider effect concentrates in small firms (Lakonishok and Lee 2001), and Nike is $52B (note 17 line 268).

**C4. Pillar 4 (catalysts and easier comparisons) is contradicted by the repo's own channel work.**
- **The dividend falsifier is more likely than not to trigger before finals.** Note 18 puts about 45% on a token raise, **40% on a freeze and 15% on a cut** for the November declaration (note 18 line 47). The brief lists "a dividend cut *or a skipped raise*" as its first falsifier. On the repo's own odds, that falsifier triggers about **55%** of the time, inside the competition window. Put-call parity also leans toward a lower dividend (note 17 F3, qualified).
- **"Hill beats 22 of 22" is the wrong base rate for Investor Day.** The 22 items are next-quarter guides. Investor Day sets *long-term* targets, and every 2017 and 2021 profit and growth target was missed (note 19 finding 4). The beat record is also known and priced: 13 of 18 earnings days were down.
- **China comparisons do not get easier.** Q1 FY27's -26% c-n came on top of an FY26 base that was already -13% c-n (`investigations/02` finding 2). An easy base did not stop the decline from speeding up. The Jan 1, 2027 exit removes partner online sales worth about **$743M at wholesale value, 12.7% of FY26 GC revenue** (`investigations/02` row 5). That is a new headwind for H2 FY27 to H1 FY28. Recapture by Nike Digital is an assumption with no evidence: Nike's own GC Digital fell about 28% c-n FY23 to FY25 while partner online grew about 59%.
- **"Less classics left to drag" conflicts with the units evidence.** North America footwear units rose 5% to 14% in FY26 at ASP about 5 points lower, "mainly from channel mix toward wholesale". The supply-cut signature (fewer units, stable price) shows up only in Greater China and Converse (`DEEP_DIVE_DEFINITIVE_TESTS.md` lines 13, 155 to 156). Nike moved the clean-up date from "by the end of December 2026" to "beyond fiscal 2027" (note 16 line 19). Jordan retros sit in full size runs, and Foot Locker sells them 18% to 24% off (`cross_branch_scan_oct13_evening.md` row 3 and lead 1). The drag is not ending on Nike's own timetable.
- **The North America "five growth quarters" proof is partly refill and sell-in.** 35% to 54% of FY26 NA wholesale growth only restored FY24 or FY23 levels. Q2 FY26 includes sell-in that Nike now calls a "higher comparison of North America sell-in activities". Foot Locker inventory is +14% to +16% against sales of -6.5% (`investigations/01` findings 1 and 2; cross-branch scan row 1). Excluding the refund, the NA FY26 gross margin is -350 bps vs FY24 (`investigations/05`).
- **The adidas analog had a catalyst that Nike lacks.** adidas's October 2022 low came days before it named a new CEO (Gulden, from Puma, early Nov 2022; widely reported, not opened here). China then reopened in Dec 2022. Its flat headline growth also hid a quantified one-off: excluding the roughly EUR 400M Yeezy drag, Q1 2023 sales grew about 9% in euros (GNA/dpa via search, SNIPPET; adidas release not opened). So the market looked through a known, sized hole to a growing core. At Nike, the CEO has been in place since Oct 2024. NIKE Brand outside China was still -0.8% in Q1 FY27 (`00_root_cause_synthesis.md` line 218). There is no new-management catalyst, and no growing core masked by a single sized one-off.

### D. Another explanation that fits the same data

Every pillar fits a "slow grind" reading equally well. Shorts added into a falling price and were right. Value holders averaged down and are under water. Insiders bought twice too early. Estimates were cut to management's own guide, which has a poor full-year record. The stock still carries a P/E premium to peers that only a margin recovery to FY24 levels or better justifies. In that reading the "extreme" positioning is a lagging description of a falling stock, not a leading signal. Under-Armour-style base rates apply: note 20 subset B gives only 45% beating the S&P 500 over 3 years, a median of -34% excess, and 0 of 10 back within 25% of peak. The repo cannot tell these two readings apart before Nov 19. The only fundamental print that could (Q2 FY27, about Dec 17) lands after finals.

### E. Is this already consensus?

Partly. The bearish view is crowded (shorts and target cuts). But "buy the trough on a broken blue chip" is itself a widely held stance: 10 buy ratings, Capital Group, Oakmark and Sixth Street on the long side, and above all a 27x forward P/E that already pays for recovery. A long pitch based on sentiment is not a variant view unless it shows margins recovering *above* the roughly 13.8% the base case already discounts. The brief does not argue that.

### F. What would rescue the long (for the team's decision)

A long survives only if it is rebuilt on a fundamental, measurable claim, not on positioning. For example: "GC sell-through stabilizes after Jan 2027 and NA ASP turns positive, giving gross margin of at least 44% by FY28." It would then need its own evidence. Positioning can be a supporting slide at most. As written, this thesis is the weakest of the long options. The repo's own data on the dividend, China channel and the NA units-vs-price split leans short.

### Verdict in one line

The long rests on "expectations are low", but the only expectation that is low is the next quarter. The share price itself still requires Nike to more than double its EBIT margin from the FY27 consensus 5.7% back to roughly 12% to 14% by FY31 (REPO `thesis_tests/consensus_and_valuation.md` row 122 and lines 243 to 246). That is a recovery thesis already in the price, not a pessimism thesis. Score: 3 of 10.

### 1. The "low bar" is mis-framed: the price embeds a full margin recovery

- The repo's own reverse DCF base case (10% WACC, 2.5% terminal growth, 3% revenue CAGR) needs a 13.8% steady-state EBIT margin by FY31, above FY24's 12.7% and the 11.8% 10-year average (REPO consensus note rows 122, line 243). The advocate quotes the 9% WACC case (11% to 12%) but the same note says the market-implied WACC is near 10% with a 5.2% 10-year yield (REPO line 243). A 9% WACC implies only about 3.8 points over the risk-free rate (ARITH), which is generous for a stock with 39% to 41% implied vol (REPO rows 87 to 91).
- Current margins: FY26 8.3%; FY27 consensus 5.7%; FY28 7.2% (REPO rows 120, 121, line 167). So the price needs roughly +6 to +8 points of margin from the FY27 trough (ARITH). The advocate calls this "only a return to the 10-year average"; the bear calls it the entire bull case, already paid for.
- On earnings, NKE trades at 27.4x FY27 and 21.5x FY28 against peer medians of 15.5x and 12.9x, and 24.9x NTM vs 13.2x (REPO line 199). The repo's own reading: "The P/E premium is the margin-recovery bet." A stock that holds a 70% to 90% P/E premium to peers after an 80% fall is not priced for despair.
- EV/sales of 1.13x is "lowest in the series" only against Nike's own 2016 to 2026 history; it equals the peer median of 1.18x (REPO line 199) and the median EV/sales of the whole busted-brand base-rate sample at the 75% crossing (1.10x; REPO `20_base_rates.md` item 5). It is ordinary, not extreme.

### 2. Base rates point the other way on the exact variable the thesis leans on

- Among idiosyncratic 75%+ drawdowns, 0 of 9 with EV/sales above 1.0x recovered vs 6 of 11 at or below 1.0x (p = 0.014); in the Nike-like valuation subset, 0 of 10 got back within 25% of the peak and only 45% beat the S&P 500 over 3 years, median excess -34% (REPO note 20 items 5 and 8).

### 3. High short interest is, on the academic evidence the repo already cites, a bearish signal

Using rising short interest as a contrarian buy signal runs against that literature (also Asquith, Pathak and Ritter 2005; Dechow et al. 2001; KNOWLEDGE, not opened).

"Record" only means record within a FINRA series that starts in Dec 2023 (REPO note 17 line 193); the S3 87M figure and the FINRA 99.2M figure for the same date disagree, which the advocate does not reconcile.
- Part of the short count is market-maker delta hedging (13F puts 77.7M underlying, top holders Susquehanna, Citadel, Jane Street) and "the data cannot split the two" (REPO note 17 lines 187, 217). That cuts both ways: it means the "extreme bearish positioning" is itself overstated.
