## 1. Thesis in one sentence

Nike's FY24 to FY26 decline was caused by its own 2017 to 2023 Consumer Direct strategy, the reversal has already worked where it was applied first (North America and Running), and the Q1 FY27 relapse that drove consensus EPS down about 25% in a week is 86% two deliberately shrunk pieces (Greater China and Converse), so the market is extrapolating a contained, dated reset as a permanent problem.

## 2. Variant perception

**What the market believes.** After the Oct 1, 2026 print the market concluded the turnaround has stalled. FY27 consensus EPS fell from $1.71 (30 days earlier) to $1.29, and FY28 from $2.22 to $1.65; 24 downward revisions in 7 days (Yahoo earningsTrend, fetched Oct 13, 2026, `consensus_and_valuation.md`). Short interest rose from 14.5M shares (Dec 2023) to 99.2M (Sept 30, 2026) (`17_insider_and_institutional_flows.md`). The stock is $35.35, 80.1% below its $177.51 peak (market data, `data/nke_daily_prices.csv`).

**What we believe.** The relapse is not spreading. It is concentrated in two businesses that Nike is shrinking on purpose, on a published timetable:
- Q1 FY27 revenue fell $507M. Greater China -$332M and Converse -$103M are $435M, or **86%** of it (REPO, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 25; my arithmetic).
- **NIKE Brand excluding Greater China was $9,772M vs $9,850M, -0.8%** (REPO; my arithmetic), while North America grew 2% and its EBIT rose 3.2% to $1,170M (REPO, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 25).
- Greater China's fall is mostly a channel decision: Nike ends distributor online sales on Jan 1, 2027 (Topsports and Pou Sheng HKEX inside-information notices, July 22, 2026, OPENED (repo note `china.md`)); GC wholesale fell 31% c-n versus GC Direct -18% in Q1 FY27 (REPO, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 28).

**Why we can be right when 35+ analysts disagree.** Consensus is revising the total, not the parts. The parts show the same 4-quarter "classics reset then growth" pattern in North America that Nike went through in FY2018, and the drag that remains has a known end date (the China distributor exit) and a known size (Dunk about $200M a quarter, Converse about $100M a quarter). Once those annualize in FY28, the growing Performance business (about $16B, +high-single digits, +low double digits ex China) becomes the visible total.

**Honest qualifier the team must say out loud.** The reverse DCF in `consensus_and_valuation.md` finds $35.35 already implies an 11% to 14% steady-state EBIT margin by FY2031 (FY24 was 12.7%). So the long is not "the market thinks Nike is dead." It is a **timing** bet: the market discounts a recovery that starts late (FY29 to FY31) and is uncertain; we argue the recovery is visible in the parts now and the total inflects in FY28 once two dated drags roll off. If that timing argument fails, the long has little edge.

## 3. Pillars

### Pillar 1. The decline was self-inflicted and Nike-specific, not a market or brand collapse

**Claim.** About 90% or more of the currency-neutral FY24 to FY26 decline is Nike-specific, and its root is one dated decision set: the Consumer Direct Offense (June 15, 2017) and its escalation, Consumer Direct Acceleration (June 25, 2020).

**Evidence**
- Revenue FY24 $51,362M to FY26 $46,398M, -$4,964M; currency-neutral about -10.8%, about $5.5B, because FX was a net +$0.6B tailwind (REPO, FY24 10-K p. 57, FY26 10-K p. 55, FY25 10-K p. 45, FY26 10-K p. 44; `00_root_cause_synthesis.md`).
- Over the same window, the peer pool grew +15.4% and the incumbent cohort +6.2% in constant FX; 9 of 12 peers grew (SEC XBRL and peer reports, OPENED (repo note `peer_differential.md`); researcher's arithmetic).
- Market was not shrinking: US footwear PCE +1.8% (2024) and +4.4% (2025), above pre-COVID trend (FRED, OPENED (repo note `macro_covid_cycle.md`)). Holding share in a market up about 6% would have put FY26 near $54B (synthesis, researcher's arithmetic).
- The decline sits in Nike's own channel: NIKE Direct -$3,799M versus NIKE Brand wholesale -$305M, FY24 to FY26 (REPO). NIKE Brand Digital fell from $12.1B (FY24) to $8.6B (FY26) (REPO via synthesis).
- The mechanism is stated by management: "we've been oversupplying our iconic retro product" (Hill, Q1 FY27 call, OPENED (repo note `franchise_oversupply.md`)); Digital ran "50% full price and 50% off price" and investment shifted "to capturing the brand demand through performance marketing" (Q2 FY25 call, OPENED (repo note)). Digital revenue was one third of the company bonus FY21 to FY23 "in connection with our Consumer Direct Acceleration strategy" (2021 proxy p. 35, OPENED (repo note `governance_incentives.md`)).

**Alternatives ruled out (and how)**
- *Macro or COVID payback:* Nike grew 10% in FY23 while adidas NA fell 16% c-n and Vans fell; the market kept growing through FY25 to FY26 (synthesis section 4; filings OPENED (repo)).
- *Tariffs:* the revenue decline began in Q1 FY25 (-10.4%), about eight months before 2025 tariffs; North America, the only tariff-exposed segment, grew 4.8% in FY26 (REPO).
- *The 2021 Xinjiang boycott:* Greater China grew +4% (FY23) and +8% (FY24) c-n and broke only in Q1 FY25, the same quarter as the global classics break (REPO).
- *Hill's reset as the cause:* the June 27, 2024 guidance cut and the Q1 FY25 -10.4% quarter both precede his Oct 14, 2024 start (REPO; synthesis).
- *Brand or logo fatigue:* NIKE Brand apparel, the most logo-dependent category, fell only 2.4%; NA apparel hit a record $6,075M in FY26 (REPO, FY26 10-K p. 37).
- *Lost wholesale shelf:* NA wholesale was already above FY19 in FY23 ($11,273M) and a record $11,958M in FY26 (REPO).

### Pillar 2. The fix already worked in North America, on the same clock as the FY2018 reset

**Claim.** In the market where the reset started first and was purest, revenue returned to growth after four negative quarters, as it did in FY2018, and regional profit has now turned.

**Evidence**
- North America quarterly growth: Q1 to Q4 FY25 -11.0%, -7.9%, -4.1%, -10.9%; then Q1 FY26 to Q1 FY27 +4.0%, +8.8%, +3.3%, +2.7%, +2.1% (REPO; `timeline_forensics.md`).
- FY2018 precedent after the 2017 Jordan tightening: NA -2.7%, -4.5%, -5.6%, then +3.3% in Q4 FY18 on new product (VaporMax, Air Max 270, React); "Jordan inventories are now clean" (Q3 FY18 call, 8-K exhibit filed Mar 27, 2018, OPENED (repo note `timeline_forensics.md`)).
- NA revenue FY26 +5% to $20,511M; NA EBIT $4,735M (FY25) to $5,376M (FY26) (REPO, FY26 10-K p. 36). Q1 FY27 NA EBIT $1,170M vs $1,134M, +3.2%, the first year-on-year NA EBIT increase since FY24 apart from Q4 FY26, which carried the IEEPA refund (REPO; my arithmetic from the quarterly series).
- Q1 FY27 NA growth was "driven by performance led by Running, Global Football and Basketball," with "good sell-through" at DICK'S, Academy, SCHEELS, JD and Foot Locker (Hill, Q1 FY27 call, OPENED (repo note `nike_standing.md`)). DICK'S Business comps were +4.9% with inventory in line (Dick's Q2 2026 release, OPENED (repo note `investigations/01`)).
- NA apparel +6% in Q1 FY27 while lululemon US fell 8% (REPO, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 26; lululemon 10-Q OPENED (repo note `apparel_women.md`)).

**Alternatives ruled out (and how)**
- *"NA growth is just channel fill."* Partly true, but bounded. `investigations/01` finds about 35% of FY26 NA wholesale growth only restores FY24 levels and Q2 FY26 contained sell-in. However, (a) at the measured Direct-to-wholesale price ratio of 1.654x, channel shift is only about 22% of FY26 NA wholesale growth (`investigations/09` A4, researcher's arithmetic); (b) Nike NA inventory was $3,491M at Aug 31, 2026, -0.9% YoY (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 17, OPENED (repo note `investigations/01`)), so Nike is not building stock to ship; (c) the Foot Locker build (+14.1% to +15.5% inventory vs sales -6.5%) is real but sits at one banner, while DICK'S own banner sold through. The fill risk reduces the size of the NA recovery, not its direction.
- *"NA margin only rose because of the IEEPA refund."* Correct for the reported figure (ex-refund FY26 NA EBIT margin 21.5% vs 27.2% in FY24, `investigations/09` A2). But on a no-new-tariff basis NA gross margin was +100 to +190 bps vs FY24, driven by lower cost per unit (`investigations/05`, researcher's estimate). The margin damage in NA is tariff policy, not demand.

### Pillar 3. Nike wins inside the "structural" trend when it executes: Running proves the brand still sells at scale

**Claim.** The bear case says challengers (On, Hoka) took share structurally. In their own home channel, Nike took it back within four to six quarters of re-entering, which a structural force should not allow.

**Evidence**
- Run specialty, 12 months to Sept 2025: Nike dollars +35.4%, On -19.7%, Hoka -7.4%, Brooks -6.8% (Circana and Karnan via SGB Media, Dec 3, 2025, OPENED (repo note `challengers_taste.md`)).
- NIKE Running: "5 consecutive quarters of double-digit growth ... added roughly $1 billion"; "gained 5 points of running market share in statement footwear" across Western Europe and North America in FY26 (Q4 FY26 call, OPENED (repo note)). "Nearly tripled our share of the Max cushioning category over the past year"; running "up double digits again" (Q1 FY27 call, OPENED (repo note `nike_standing.md`)).
- Performance was about $16B in FY26 and grew "high single digits" in Q1 FY27, "up low double digits" excluding Greater China (Hill and Denton, Q1 FY27 call, OPENED (repo note)). That is about 35% of FY26 revenue growing while the total fell (my arithmetic: $16B / $46.4B).
- Football "strong double-digit growth in all 4 geos"; Basketball "up double digits in North America" (same call).
- The pullback was a choice, not a loss: "More than 4 years ago, we pulled back on our engagement with that channel" (CFO Friend on run specialty, Q1 FY25 call, OPENED (repo note `innovation_drought.md`)). Running's share of NIKE Brand wholesale-equivalent revenue fell from 15.9% (FY17) to 13.8% (FY19) (FY19 10-K p. 27, OPENED (repo note)).

**Alternatives ruled out (and how)**
- *"Running growth is just discounting."* Running Warehouse shows 49% of Nike listings marked down vs Hoka 10% (`marketplace_pricing_and_shelf_share.md`), and Pegasus colourways are discounted on nike.com. This is the strongest counter. But Nike's own NA footwear units rose with NA growth, and the NIKE Brand ASP line in the gross margin bridge was only -30 bps in Q1 FY27 (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 23, REPO via note 14), against -330 bps at the worst (Q2 FY25). Discounting is a margin issue at the margin, not the source of a $1B running gain.
- *"Lifestyle decline is permanent, so Running cannot offset it."* Not ruled out; see weakest points. Partial counter: within Sportswear, AF1 is "a stable full-price business," P-6000 and V5 Runner grew "strong double digits" (Q1 FY27 call, OPENED (repo note)): the decline is concentrated in Dunk, which Nike cut "by nearly 50%" (about $200M a quarter).

### Pillar 4. What is still falling is shrinking on purpose, on a dated timetable, and China's channel overhaul lands in calendar 2027

**Claim.** The FY27 drag is three deliberate cuts (China channel, Converse, Dunk and Jordan retro supply), each with a mechanism Nike controls and a start date that lets it annualize in FY28.

**Evidence**
- Q1 FY27 bridge by segment: GC -$332M, EMEA -$155M, Converse -$103M, APLA -$27M, NA +$107M (REPO; my arithmetic).
- **Greater China is a same-store natural experiment that points at Nike's channel, not the Chinese consumer.** Through the same Topsports doors, adidas Greater China grew +9% to +17% c-n every quarter from Q3 2024 to Q2 2026 while Nike fell -3% to -20% (adidas quarterly PDFs, OPENED (repo note `china.md`)). Nike's FY24 sell-in to partners grew 15% while its own Direct grew 1%, and GC inventory rose 10% while total Nike inventory fell 11% (FY25 10-K segment table, OPENED (repo note)). Hill: "we've become a lifestyle brand competing on price in China"; partner doors "have not been refreshed in the past 7 years" (Q2 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls, OPENED (repo note)).
- **The fix is dated.** Nike online sales were 22% of Topsports' revenue and 15% of Pou Sheng's, and both end on Jan 1, 2027 (HKEX inside information, July 22, 2026, OPENED (repo note)). Reset doors are seeing "sales increase high single digits" and House of Innovation Shanghai had "10 consecutive months of growth" (calls, OPENED (repo note `china.md`)). Nike China Running grew for a sixth straight quarter (Q1 FY27 call). Local product creation starts with a first collection in Oct 2026 (repo note `china.md`).
- **Sell-in is falling faster than sell-through.** Q1 FY27 GC wholesale -31% c-n vs Direct -18% (REPO, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 28). FY26 sell-through proxies (Topsports -4.7%, GC comps -6%) vs GC wholesale -14% (`china.md`, OPENED (repo)).
- **APLA shows what an early reset looks like.** Nike cut classics supply in Japan and Korea in early 2024 and "regained our #1 position in Korea in women's lifestyle footwear" (Q4 FY24 call, OPENED (repo note)); APLA fell only 7.2% FY24 to FY26 (REPO). China, which got the reset later and through distributors, collapsed.
- **Converse** fell a year before NIKE Brand and in all territories; demand creation was cut 39% in FY26 (FY26 10-K, OPENED (repo note `emea_converse_jordan.md`)). Converse is now $263M a quarter (REPO), about 2.3% of revenue (my arithmetic): its drag is shrinking mechanically.
- **The Nike precedent.** In FY2010, FY2018 and FY2024, gross margin turned up 3 to 4 quarters after the worst discount quarter (`16_full_price_recovery.md`, filings). The adidas precedent under Gulden: inventory clean-up via conservative wholesale sell-in in 2023, then growth (adidas Q2 2026 +14% c-n, NA +17%, GC +15%; adidas Q2 2026 release, OPENED (repo note `adidas_gulden.md`)).

**Alternatives ruled out (and how)**
- *"China is structural guochao."* Foreign premium brands outgrew domestic mass brands in 2025 to 2026 (adidas +13%, ASICS +19.9% vs Anta brand +3.7%, Li-Ning +3.2%; HKEX and company filings, OPENED (repo note)). The external China slice is real but sized at $0.3B to $0.8B of $4.96B (synthesis).
- *"The Middle East conflict or World Cup explain EMEA and Q1."* The Q3 FY26 EMEA store break predates the Feb 28, 2026 strikes; Nike football grew in all four geographies in the World Cup quarter (synthesis section 4).

## 6. What would prove the long wrong (pre-registered)

1. **Q2 FY27 NIKE Brand ex-Greater China declines more than 3%** (Q1 FY27 was -0.8%). That would mean the weakness is spreading beyond the two deliberate cuts.
2. **Q2 FY27 North America revenue turns negative** by more than the guided sell-in lap, while DICK'S Business comps stay positive (the "NA was fill" reading wins).
3. **Running growth falls to single digits** or the ASP line in the gross margin bridge stays negative for Q2 and Q3 FY27 (Running bought with discounts).
4. **Post-cut Jordan retros (AJ1 Royal, AJ12 Fireside) still in full size runs at day 28**, meaning supply cuts are not restoring full-price sell-through.
5. **Greater China sell-through falls as fast as sell-in after Jan 1, 2027** (GC Direct and comps down 20%+), which would make China consumer rejection, not channel.
6. **Investor Day long-term EBIT margin target below 10%**, or a timeline that pushes growth past FY29.
7. **Feng Tay September to November revenue down more than 10% YoY**, pointing to a deeper Q2 footwear decline than guided.

## 7. Weakest points (stated plainly)

1. **The price already assumes recovery.** At about 28x FY27 guided EPS and a reverse-DCF implied 11% to 14% FY31 EBIT margin, the stock is not priced for "permanent." Our edge is timing only.
2. **North America is partly borrowed.** About a third to a half of FY26 NA wholesale growth only restores FY24; Q2 FY26 contained sell-in Nike itself flags; Foot Locker inventory +14% to +16% vs sales -6.5% (`investigations/01`). Q2 to Q4 FY27 NA may soften.
3. **Gross margin has not turned.** Seven quarters past the worst discount quarter with no sustained positive ASP line, longer than any Nike precedent (3 to 4 quarters); Nike moved the end of Sportswear and Jordan actions from "end of December 2026" to "beyond fiscal 2027" (FY26 10-K p. 30 vs Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 20, note 16).
4. **Lifestyle may be a down-cycle across all brands.** Hill: "a lack of energy in the lifestyle space" and "a sea of sameness" (Q1 FY27 call). Sportswear is just under half of revenue and fell low double digits. Running cannot offset that alone.
5. **Jordan demand may be falling, not just supply.** Retros sit in full size runs 4 to 10 weeks after launch; retailers discount 2026 retros 18% to 24%; the AJ1 resale difference-in-differences is -22.7 pts (interval includes zero) (`DEEP_DIVE_DEFINITIVE_TESTS.md`).
6. **Cash and dividend.** FCF covered 0.91x of dividends in FY26 and was -$64M in Q1 FY27; payout 121% to 143% of guided EPS; S&P cut to A, negative outlook (Oct 5, 2026, secondary via note 18). A dividend cut in the Nov 14 to 20 window would land the day before finals.
7. **Management credibility.** Every 2017 and 2021 profit target was missed; both of Hill's dated milestones slipped (note 19).
8. **Sector de-rating.** About 45% of the log stock decline is multiple compression shared with adidas, Puma, VF and lululemon (`valuation_vs_business.md`); no operating fix reverses that.
9. **China timing.** Management guides China "actually gets worse" for the rest of FY27; the Jan 1 exit mechanically removes revenue first.

### 2. Factual errors and misleading uses of correct numbers

**E1. "NA EBIT rose from $4,735M (FY25) to $5,376M (FY26)" is refund-inflated.** FY26 NA EBIT includes the $965M IEEPA tariff refund booked in Q4 FY26 (CSV: `ieepa_recovery_north_america`, Q4 FY26 release). Ex-refund, FY26 NA EBIT was $4,411M, **down 6.8%** from FY25 and down 24% from FY24's $5,822M (my arithmetic). Q4 FY26 NA EBIT ex-refund was $1,035M vs $1,045M, down 1% (my arithmetic). Pillar 2's "regional profit has now turned" rests on one quarter (+$36M in Q1 FY27).

**E2. The Q1 FY27 NA EBIT gain is plausibly tariff-rate relief, not demand.** Q1 FY26 carried full IEEPA tariffs; Q1 FY27 carried the 10% Section 122 replacement (REPO NOTE `investigations/05`, which itself marks the underlying Q1 FY27 margin change "unresolved"). NA gross margin was 43.4% in Q1 FY27, still **219 bps below Q1 FY25** (same note, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 26). The brief does not separate tariff relief from demand.

**E3. "86% of the decline is two deliberately shrunk businesses" is a net-versus-gross framing.** The 86% uses the net decline, in which NA's +$107M offsets other losses. On gross declines ($617M), GC plus Converse is 70.5% and **EMEA alone is 25%** (my arithmetic). EMEA fell 5% currency-neutral with EMEA Digital -26% (CSV, Q1 FY27 10-Q pp. 25, 27). EMEA is not a deliberate shrink in the brief's own taxonomy, and it is simply left out of the sentence. NIKE Brand outside both GC and NA (EMEA plus APLA) fell 3.8% reported (my arithmetic).

**E4. "Greater China's fall is mostly a channel decision" contradicts the repo's own China investigation.** `investigations/02` (REPO NOTE, branch `zealous-mccarthy`) finds: units explain -25.7 of the -26 point Q1 FY27 decline; Nike's own GC comps compounded to -12.6% over FY25 to FY26 while the NBS category grew +3.5% and adidas GC grew +20.3%; Nike-owned GC store sales fell 14% in Q1 FY27 (vs NA -6, EMEA -1, APLA +5); the deliberate destock is about 10 points of the 26 on the Nike-store benchmark and **1 to 8 points** on the Pou Sheng matched window. On those ranges the deliberate part of the -$332M is roughly $15M to $150M; the majority is lost sell-through (my arithmetic on Q1 FY26 GC of $1,512M). GC Direct, which the Jan 1, 2027 partner-online exit does not touch, fell 18% c-n with GC Digital -28% (CSV, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 28).

**E5. The adidas same-store "natural experiment" proves the opposite of what the brief says.** If adidas grew +9% to +17% through the **same Topsports doors** while Nike fell -3% to -20%, the door and channel are held constant, and the difference is the brand and product. That points at Nike's consumer demand, not at Nike's channel. The brief uses it to claim the opposite.

**E6. Converse is not "shrinking on purpose."** Converse fell from $2,427M (FY23) to $2,082M (FY24), $1,692M (FY25) and $1,174M (FY26), -52% in three years, and -28% c-n in both Q1 FY26 and Q1 FY27 with units -34% (CSV). The 39% demand-creation cut is a response to the collapse, not its cause. It began a year before the NIKE Brand decline, which the brief itself notes.

**E7. "Discounting cannot explain the Running gain" uses a company-wide line for a category claim.** The -30 bps ASP line in the gross margin bridge is NIKE Brand-wide; it nets Running discounts against lower discounts elsewhere, so it cannot bound Running pricing. Nike's own Q1 FY27 remarks say gross margin was "partially offset by **increased discounts** as well as channel mix" (REPO NOTE `investigations/05`, Q1 FY27 remarks pp. 10 to 11), and Running Warehouse shows 49% of Nike listings marked down versus Hoka 10% (REPO NOTE `marketplace_pricing_and_shelf_share.md`).

**E8. The "Dunk about $200M a quarter" drag overlaps the GC figure.** The Dunk cut is global; part of it sits inside GC's -$332M. `investigations/09` A5 (REPO NOTE) flags the same overlap. Adding Dunk to GC and Converse as separate, additive "deliberate" drags double-counts.

**E9. The valuation arithmetic uses a 21% tax rate; Nike guides FY27 to "mid-20 percent"** (REPO NOTE `04_financial_data_pack.md`, Q1 FY27 release p. 1). At 25%, the 10% margin earnings power is about $2.18, not $2.29 (my arithmetic: $43B x 10% x 0.75 / 1,481M), about 5% lower, and before interest.

**E10. "Performance about $16B, +HSD, +LDD ex-China" is an unaudited management category.** The Performance versus Sportswear split is not in the 10-Q or 10-K segment tables (CSV has no such line). It is a call-script number with no history to check against. It is the brief's main growth proof and should be labelled as management-defined.

**E11. Run-specialty data are a year old and a growth rate on a re-entry base.** The +35.4% is for the 12 months to Sept 2025 (REPO NOTE `challengers_taste.md`). Growth from a channel Nike had exited is not evidence of share level regained, and nothing in the brief is newer than Sept 2025 for that channel.

### 3. Logic: where another explanation fits the same data

**L1. Management's own guidance implies the relapse spreads (breaks Pillar 4 and pre-registered disproof #1).** FY27 revenue is guided to "decline in the high single-digit range" (REPO NOTE `nike_standing.md`, Q1 FY27 call and release). With Q1 FY27 at $11,213M, FY26 at $46,398M, Q2 to Q4 FY26 at $34,678M, GC falling 26% to 35% and Converse 28% for the rest of the year, the guide implies Q2 to Q4 NIKE Brand ex-GC changes of:

| FY27 total | GC -26% | GC -30% | GC -35% |
|---|---|---|---|
| -6% | -3.1% | -2.5% | -1.8% |
| -7% | -4.7% | -4.1% | -3.4% |
| -8% | -6.3% | -5.7% | -4.9% |
| -9% | -7.8% | -7.3% | -6.5% |

(My arithmetic. Base: Q2 to Q4 FY26 GC $4,335M, Converse $808M.) For any "high single-digit" reading (7% to 9%), NIKE Brand outside China falls 3.4% to 7.8%. The brief's disproof threshold (ex-GC worse than -3%) is therefore what management has already guided. The roughly 400 bps Q2 headwind Nike named (NA sell-in lap and EMEA Cyber Week) is entirely outside China.

**L2. North America is a channel shift plus fill, decelerating, still below FY24.** NA quarterly growth went +8.8%, +3.3%, +2.7%, +2.1% (decelerating, not the FY2018 acceleration). FY26 NA revenue was $20,511M, 4.1% below FY24; Q1 FY27 NA was 5.5% below Q1 FY24 (CSV, my arithmetic). NA Direct fell every quarter, -$138M in Q1 FY27, and NA footwear units grew only 2% with footwear revenue +1% (REPO NOTE `investigations/01` T1 and T3). At wholesale-equivalent value, 34% of the Q1 FY27 wholesale gain is channel shift, 23% to 38% is price/mix, and the rest is shared by World Cup sell-in, a Foot Locker inventory build (+14.1% to +15.5% vs sales -6.5%) and new doors (`investigations/01`, `09` A4). An equally good reading of the same data: Nike moved volume from its own channel into partners' stockrooms and the "fix" is a channel reshuffle whose payback comes in Q2 to Q4 FY27.

**L3. The FY2018 precedent is not like-for-like.** FY18 NA fell 2.7% to 5.6% for three quarters while total Nike grew; FY25 NA fell 4% to 11% for four quarters, total revenue fell 10%, and NA has not regained the prior level two years later. A shallower, shorter dip that recovered on new product is weak evidence for a deeper one.

**L4. "Self-inflicted" does not mean "reversible."** The brief proves Nike-specific cause (peer pool +15.4%, Nike -10.8% c-n). It does not prove reversibility. Self-inflicted damage can be permanent: shelf space ceded to On and Hoka, an innovation gap, and a lost hype audience. The 2017 to 2023 Consumer Direct period was also Nike's best growth period (revenue $34,350M FY17 to $51,217M FY23, CSV), so dating the cause to 2017 requires a lag argument the brief does not test against the simpler alternative: the classics franchise cycle (Dunk, AF1, Jordan retro) peaked and rolled over in FY25, which is a product-cycle problem with no fixed end date.

**L5. The "brand fatigue ruled out" test is too weak.** Apparel (-2.4%) is the least hype-driven category. Footwear, where brand heat lives, fell $3,902M or 11.7% FY24 to FY26 (CSV, my arithmetic), and NA apparel's record includes +6 pts of ASP from "product mix" and a World Cup kit year (`investigations/01` T3). Jordan is down mid-teens and Sportswear down low double digits (REPO NOTE `nike_standing.md`).

**L6. The variant perception is self-refuted.** The thesis says the market prices "a permanent problem." The repo's reverse DCF finds the price implies a **13.8% steady-state EBIT margin** at a 3% revenue CAGR (REPO NOTE `consensus_and_valuation.md`, base case), above FY24's 12.7%. The market is already pricing a full margin recovery. "80% below peak" anchors on a Nov 2021 price set at a much higher multiple, and about 45% of the log decline is sector de-rating (brief's own weakest point 8). What the stock offers is a timing bet on FY28, and the evidence for FY28 timing (L1, L2) points the other way.

**L7. The China "fix" is a further revenue cut before it is a recovery.** Partner online sales of Nike were about $743M a year at wholesale value, 12.7% of FY26 GC revenue (REPO NOTE `investigations/02`). Ending them on Jan 1, 2027 (Q3 FY27) removes that revenue unless Nike Digital captures it, and GC Digital is falling 28%. The lap of that exit is Q3 FY28, so H1 FY28 still carries it. Management itself says China "actually gets worse" and that pain will "bleed" into FY28 (REPO NOTE `nike_standing.md`).

**L8. APLA is not a success control.** APLA fell 3% (FY25) and 1% (FY26) currency-neutral (CSV) while adidas and Asian peers grew. "Fell only 7.2%" is a decline, not an early-reset recovery.

### 4. Is it consensus?

Partly. Ratings lean Hold (Yahoo: 1 strong buy, 9 buy, 25 hold, 4 sell, 3 strong sell; sells rose from 2 to 7 in three months; REPO NOTE `consensus_and_valuation.md`), so the long is not the crowded rating. But the specific argument (NA and wholesale stabilizing, new CFO resets at Investor Day, "de-risked") is already the published bull case of Jefferies ($75 Buy) and Bernstein (Outperform, $45) (REPO NOTE `reports/Nike decline execution vs structural.md`), and the price already implies margin recovery (L6). The brief's edge is not a fact the market lacks.

### 5. What survives

- Nike-specific cause versus peers (Pillar 1's diagnosis) survives; the attribution to the 2017 to 2020 strategy specifically is plausible but untested against the franchise-cycle alternative.
- Running and Performance growth is real on management's word, but unaudited and partly bought with discounts.
- Cash, dividend and catalyst dates are checkable and mostly correct.

### 6. What the long would need to fix to be defensible

1. Replace "86% is two deliberate cuts" with the gross bridge including EMEA, and state that FY27 guidance implies ex-China decline of about 3% to 8% for the rest of the year.
2. Use ex-refund NA EBIT everywhere; drop "NA profit has turned" unless Q2 FY27 shows it ex-tariff.
3. Restate China as sell-through share loss with a channel overlay (per `investigations/02`), and drop the adidas same-store test as support for "channel."
4. Move the disproof threshold so it is not already implied by guidance (for example, ex-GC worse than the guide-implied range), or accept that the base case trips it.

## Red team: opposite side

Prepared Oct 14, 2026. Role: a smart investor who is short NKE (or is refusing to own it) at $35.35, explaining what this long leaves out and whether it has any edge. This adds to the facts-and-logic red team above and does not repeat its items E1 to E11 and L1 to L8, except where noted. No modeling; arithmetic on reported figures only, labelled *my arithmetic*.

**Labels.** REPO NOTE = figure as recorded in the named repo note, which cites its own source; I re-read the note, not the underlying document. CSV = checked by me in `data/nike_filing_facts.csv`. SNIPPET = web search summary only, page not opened (one search run, Oct 14, 2026).

### Verdict in one paragraph

The long's diagnosis (self-inflicted, Nike-specific) is right, and that is the problem: it has been the published bull case, from the sell side and from insiders, at $61, at $42 and now at $35, and each time the "fix is working in North America and Running" evidence was already on the table. The thesis's only claimed edge is timing ("recovery visible in FY28, the market waits for FY29 to FY31"), and that claim is factually wrong: consensus already models FY28 as the inflection year (revenue +1.1%, EPS +28%). What the long ignores is that the stock already trades at about 1.7x to 1.9x the peer earnings multiple, so even a perfect fix that turns Nike into adidas (growing about 9%, at 12.9x next-year EPS) needs about $2.74 of EPS just to hold $35.35. Management's own FY27 guide implies the non-Performance 65% of the company falls about 15% to 18% this year, which is not "two deliberate cuts". Score: 3 out of 10. Not consensus in rating terms (Hold-heavy), but consensus in narrative terms; no informational edge.

### 1. The timing edge is already in consensus (breaks the variant perception)

- The brief's honest qualifier says the long is "a timing bet: the market discounts a recovery that starts late (FY29 to FY31) ... we argue ... the total inflects in FY28." Consensus says the opposite. FY28 consensus revenue is $43,645M vs FY27 $43,152M (+1.1%), and FY28 EPS $1.646 vs FY27 $1.288 (+28%); Zacks FY29 EPS $2.31 (+40% on Yahoo FY28) (REPO NOTE `thesis_tests/consensus_and_valuation.md`, Yahoo earningsTrend and Nasdaq/Zacks, fetched Oct 13, 2026).
- So the market is not waiting until FY29 to FY31. It already pays for an FY28 inflection. For the long to have a timing edge it must argue FY28 is **better** than +1% revenue and +28% EPS. The brief never states its own FY28 number, so its variant perception cannot be checked against consensus and cannot be won or lost. The FY27 guide already implies NIKE Brand ex-China falls about 3% to 8% for Q2 to Q4 (facts red team L1), so the FY28 base is still being lowered.

### 2. Even a perfect fix does not pay at this price (valuation the brief does not confront)

- NKE trades at 27.4x FY27 and 21.5x FY28 consensus EPS (24.9x NTM). Peer medians are 15.5x, 12.9x and 13.2x (REPO NOTE `consensus_and_valuation.md` table B3, Yahoo, Oct 13, 2026).
- adidas is the case the long holds up as proof a fix works (Gulden playbook, +14% c-n Q2 2026). It trades at 16.4x FY1 and 12.9x FY2 with consensus revenue growth of +9.3% and +6.5% (same table). Nike, shrinking 7%, trades at a 67% premium to adidas on next-year EPS (21.5 / 12.9, *my arithmetic*).
- The arithmetic of "success": if Nike's fix fully works and the market then values it like the adidas it is copying (12.9x), $35.35 needs EPS of about $2.74 (*my arithmetic*). That is 73% of FY24 diluted EPS of $3.73 (CSV, Q4 FY24 release p. 2) on revenue that consensus has about 15% lower ($43.6B vs $51.4B). At consensus FY28 EPS and the adidas multiple, the stock is about $21 (1.646 x 12.9, *my arithmetic*).
- The repo's reverse DCF already puts the implied steady-state EBIT margin at 13.8% (base case), above FY24's 12.7% and the 10-year mean of 11.8% (REPO NOTE `consensus_and_valuation.md` section C). The long's upside case (18x to 20x on $2.29 to $2.75) assumes Nike keeps a premium multiple after it stops being a premium grower. No peer in table B3 with negative or low growth (lululemon 9.8x, VF 14.5x, Puma) gets that.
- Base rates (REPO NOTE `20_base_rates.md`): in the Nike-like valuation subset (idiosyncratic, EV/sales above 0.8x) the median 3-year return vs the S&P 500 was **-34%**, and 0 of 10 completed episodes got back to within 25% of peak. The brief quotes the friendlier "+10% median excess" from the full idiosyncratic sample and omits the subset Nike belongs to.

### 3. "Concentrated and deliberate" fails on management's own segment arithmetic

- Management says Performance is about $16B and grew high-single digits in Q1 FY27 (brief, Pillar 3). FY27 revenue is guided down high-single digits. If Performance grows about 8% for the year (+$1.28B), the rest of the company (about $30.4B) must change by -$4.5B to -$5.5B for a -7% to -9% total, i.e. **-15% to -18%** (*my arithmetic* on FY26 revenue $46,398M, CSV).
- Greater China (FY26 about $5,847M, CSV: Q1 FY26 $1,512M plus Q2 to Q4 $4,335M) falling 30% and Converse ($1,174M) falling 28% together remove about $2.1B (*my arithmetic*). Even if every dollar of that sat outside Performance, at least $2.4B to $3.4B of FY27 decline is in Sportswear and Jordan outside China and Converse. That is about half or more of the non-Performance decline, in the businesses the long calls healthy except for the Dunk cut.
- The "Dunk about $200M a quarter" figure (about $0.8B a year) cannot fill that gap, and the facts red team (E8) already shows it overlaps the China figure.

### 4. Evidence the long leaves out on demand (the "brand fatigue ruled out" test is too narrow)

The brief rules out brand fatigue using apparel (-2.4%). It ignores the direct demand series already in the repo:

Demand fell **before** Nike cut supply, so "self-inflicted oversupply" may be the second cause, not the first.

- Cross-brand lifestyle: adidas footwear grew only 1% in Q2 2026 "as the terrace trend faded" (REPO NOTE `reports/Nike decline execution vs structural.md`). The lifestyle pool Nike needs to refill is shrinking for the brand the long uses as its control, so there is no category tailwind for the Sportswear rebuild.

### 5. The Running proof point is smaller and lower quality than presented

Management's "roughly $1 billion" in Running is therefore at or above the whole underlying non-classic gain; it cannot also carry a recovery elsewhere.
