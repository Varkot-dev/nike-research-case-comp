## 1. The thesis in one sentence

Nike's lifestyle and Jordan businesses, about 60% of revenue and historically its highest-margin dollars, lost consumer demand before Nike cut supply, the supply cuts have not brought pricing back, and the growth Nike is replacing them with (performance running, sold mostly through wholesale at lower prices and heavier markdowns) earns less per dollar, so the stock at about 28x guided FY2027 EPS is pricing a return of a profit pool that is not coming back.

|---|---|---|---|

| Valuation | $35.35 implies a steady-state EBIT margin of 11% to 14% by FY2031 (reverse DCF, `thesis_tests/consensus_and_valuation.md`), against 12.7% in FY2024 and 8.3% in FY2026 (note 14). | A smaller lifestyle pool plus a lower-margin replacement mix makes about 9% to 10% the more likely resting point. | The FY2024 margin was earned when Direct was 43.7% of NIKE Brand revenue and Jordan scarcity still worked (Pillar 4). The sharpest way to say it to judges: **the market is debating how long the supply reset takes. We are saying it is not a supply reset.**

**Claim.** The franchises that carried Nike's profit (Jordan retro, Dunk, AF1, AJ1) lost consumer demand in 2022 to 2023, while Nike was still growing their revenue. The supply cuts of FY2025 onward were a response to falling demand, not a voluntary sacrifice of healthy demand.

## 4. Pillar 2: The supply cut did not bring pricing back

**Claim.** If the problem were supply, fewer pairs would restore premiums. Nike has now cut the Air Jordan 1 High for two years and Dunk revenue by nearly 50% in a single quarter, and pricing has not come back anywhere outside Nike's own website.

**Evidence.**

| Test | Result | Source |

| AJ1 High OG resale by release year (GOAT, Oct 13, 2026) | -26% (2023), -28% (2024), -35% (2025), -30% (2026) vs retail; 40 of 41 below retail. No improvement after the cut | `data/jordan_resale_goat_20261013.csv`, my lane's arithmetic in note 12 |
| All Jordan general-release retros, median premium by year | +13% (2023), +6%, -5%, -19% (2026) | Same file |
| Where Nike moved the volume (AJ3, AJ4) | AJ4 +18%, +24%, +4%, -20%; AJ3 +24%, +10%, -17%, -22%. AJ4 releases rose from 7 to 33 a year. Premiums fall wherever volume goes | Same file; release counts note 12 |
| Difference in differences, AJ1 (cut) vs AJ3 (raised) | AJ1 did 22.7 points *worse* after its cut; 95% interval -48.3 to +3.5 (includes zero; thin coverage, no AJ4) | `DEEP_DIVE_DEFINITIVE_TESTS.md` Test 4 |
| Size runs weeks after launch (Oct 13, 2026) | AJ4 Rare Air Tour Yellow: all 13 sizes at nike.com, Foot Locker and Champs on day 38; AJ12 Bucks 10 to 11 of 13 on day 31; AJ1 Love The Game near-full on day 73 | Test 3, live product data (repo data, opened by lane) |

| Independent resale study | 70.7% of 605 Jordan items last sold below retail, median -20% | SneakerPing, Aug 18, 2026 (repo note; methodology not verified) |
| Nike's own words | "sell-through remains challenged, impacting both current discounting and future order books" (Q4 FY26); "some aged, higher volume, sportswear footwear sold through below expectations" (Q1 FY27); Sportswear and Jordan actions "expected to extend beyond fiscal 2027" | Nike transcripts; Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 20 |
| Units vs price in Nike's filings | North America, the target of the Jordan cut, sold 2% *more* footwear pairs at lower ASP in Q1 FY27. The cut-units-hold-price signature appears only in Greater China and Converse | Q1 FY27 10-Q pp. 26 to 28 (Test 5) |

**Why a held nike.com price is not evidence of full-price demand.** Nike shows 2 of 113 main-line retro colourways discounted on nike.com (Test 2), but the same shoes sit in full size runs, are discounted at Foot Locker and trade 26% to 35% below retail on resale. Nike holds its own list price and lets the markdown happen at partners. That flatters Nike's reported recovery and comes back as lower future orders, which Hill already flagged.

**Why this time differs from FY2018 (the market's analog).** FY2018 Jordan: -8% for one year, inventory clean in two quarters, Greater China growing 30% to 50%. Now: about -19% over two years, a further mid-teens drop, Greater China -26%, discounting continuing, and management says pain will "bleed in a little bit into fiscal '28" (note 12 section 3; `fy18_analog.md`; Q1 FY27 transcript). In FY2018 the resale ecosystem was booming and could absorb scarcity; today it has shrunk (Pillar 1).

**Alternatives ruled out, and how.**
- *"The cut is too new to judge."* True for the broad October 2026 cut. Not true for AJ1 High, cut from 2024, which shows no recovery after two release years. Dunk was cut "nearly 50%" in Q1 FY27 (Nike transcript), two years into the reset, which says demand is still falling to meet supply.
- *"Premiums fell because the resale market collapsed."* That is our point. Jordan's scarcity model monetized through a hype and resale ecosystem; if that ecosystem has shrunk (StockX search -57%, SNKRS search -73%), fewer pairs no longer convert into a full-price premium.

**Claim.** The shift away from court, basketball-retro and canvas silhouettes toward running and running-inspired styles is measured in market data and hit brands Nike does not run. That makes it a demand change, not only a Nike supply error.

**Simple arithmetic on the two realities (my arithmetic).** FY2026 revenue $46.4B; Performance about $16B; so Sportswear, Jordan and Converse are roughly $29B to $30B, about 62% to 65% of revenue. If that block falls 10% in a year, it loses about $2.9B. To offset that, Performance must grow about 18%, versus "high single digits" today. Total revenue cannot grow until lifestyle stops shrinking.

**Alternatives ruled out, and how.**
- *"It is all Nike's oversupply; a well-run Nike would not have lost it."* Vans and Converse (Converse runs its own reset, but the Chuck Taylor fade began a year before NIKE Brand's) faded 48% to 52% with no Jordan-style scarcity model. Three canvas, skate and court families collapsing at once is a category signature.
- *"It is a China story."* JD's Europe mix, Euromonitor US share and Circana US category data are all outside China.

## 6. Pillar 4: The profit pool Nike is rebuilding is smaller, and the stock prices it back

**Claim.** Lifestyle and Jordan were Nike's most profitable dollars because they sold heavily through NIKE Direct at full price with little R&D. The replacement is wholesale-led performance product at lower price points with heavy markdowns and record marketing. Even if revenue stabilizes, the EBIT margin the price needs is unlikely to return.

**Evidence.**

| What Direct earned over wholesale | FY2024 spread $8.5B, 39.5% of Direct revenue; peaked at 43.7% in FY2022 when scarcity was highest | `09_channel_margin_test.md`, FY22 10-K p. 32, FY24 10-K p. 34 |

**What the price needs (my arithmetic, illustrative, not a model).** At $35.35 and the $1.25 guidance midpoint, the stock trades at about 28x FY2027 and about 21x the $1.65 FY2028 consensus (Yahoo, Oct 13, via `consensus_and_valuation.md`). The lane's reverse DCF says the price implies an 11% to 14% steady-state EBIT margin. If lifestyle stays a smaller, lower-margin pool and FY2028 EPS lands near $1.30 to $1.40 instead of $1.65, at 20x that is $26 to $28, about 20% to 26% below today. Note 14's "stuck" case ($42B revenue, 6.5% margin) gives about $26 to $29.

**Alternatives ruled out, and how.**
- *"Tariffs, not mix, explain the NA margin."* Partly true and stated below as a weak point: on a no-new-tariff basis NA gross margin is about +143 bps vs FY24 (`investigations/05`). But the EBIT gap is mostly below gross margin (demand creation and overhead deleverage), which is the cost of buying replacement demand.
- *"Discounting is temporary."* Nike itself moved the end date from "by the end of December 2026" (FY26 10-K p. 30) to "beyond fiscal 2027" (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 20).

## 8. What would prove us wrong

1. Post-cut retros sell through at full price: AJ1 Royal in broken size runs by Nov 7, and holiday AJ4 Bred and AJ11 trade above retail within 60 days.
2. GOAT re-pricing of the same 247 shoes shows AJ1 High premiums rising while AJ1 supply stays low.
3. Investor Day discloses Jordan units falling faster than revenue (price per pair rising).
4. Sportswear decline narrows to mid-single digits in Q2 or Q3 FY27, with a positive NIKE Brand ASP line for two quarters.
5. nike.com men's sale share drops below 39% (45.7% today) and Nike's Running Warehouse markdown rate falls toward the store average (23%).
6. Piper Sandler Fall 2026 shows Nike teen footwear mindshare back above 46%.
7. Circana H2 2026 shows sport lifestyle growing again with Nike gaining share inside it.

## 9. Weakest points (honest)

1. **North America is growing.** Five straight quarters (+4.0%, +8.8%, +3.3%, +2.7%, +2.1%), NA footwear units +6% in FY26, NA apparel at a record. Part is sell-in (Foot Locker inventory up while comps fell), but not all.
2. **Outside China, Nike is nearly flat.** NIKE Brand outside Greater China fell only 0.8% in Q1 FY27 (`00_root_cause_synthesis.md`). Much of the live decline is China and Converse, which this thesis does not fully own.
3. **Nike wins when it executes inside the trend.** Running added about $1B in five quarters; run specialty dollars +35.4% vs On -19.7% and Hoka -7.4% (small base); "5 points of running market share in statement footwear." The challengers are decelerating (Hoka +7.7%, On Americas +4.5% mid-2026). That reads more like a rotation Nike can ride than a one-way shift.
4. **Some classics are stable.** AF1 is "driving a stable full-price business," "Air Jordan" search has been flat since 2024, StockX reported Jordan average resale price +6% in 2025, and AJ4 Black Cat drove "our largest Black Friday ever" (Q2 FY26). A lifestyle hit (Nike's Samba) would break the thesis fast.
5. **The supply and demand split cannot be measured in filings.** The Jordan lane puts it at 35% to 50% supply, 50% to 65% demand, low confidence. Nike discloses no Jordan units, price or inventory.
6. **The broad October 2026 retro cut is untested.** It lands mostly in calendar 2027, after finals.
7. **The profit-pool claim is an inference.** Nike does not report profit by franchise or channel after FY2024. On a no-new-tariff basis NA gross margin is up about 143 bps vs FY2024, which cuts against "lower-margin replacement" at the gross line.
8. **History and positioning are against shorts.** Nike lost 6.4 US share points to the adidas wave in 2016 to 2019 and won them back with its own retro wave. Base rates after 80% drawdowns: median +69% over three years. Short interest is already 99.2M shares (Sep 30, 2026), the dividend yield is a 4.6% carry cost, and about 45% of the stock fall is a sector de-rating already taken.
9. **The downside in plain arithmetic is moderate** (about 20% to 26% in our illustrative case), unless the multiple also compresses.

## 10. How this differs from the other candidate theses

This is the only short built on the *product and consumer* evidence (search, resale, size runs, retailer mix, Euromonitor) rather than on cash, the dividend or valuation alone. It pairs naturally with the dividend catalyst (Nov 14 to 20) and Investor Day (Nov 16 to 17), both before finals, and it is falsifiable on dates the judges can check.

**Self-assessed confidence: 5 out of 10.** The "demand fell first" leg is well evidenced for Jordan and AJ1. The "permanently smaller profit pool" leg is an inference from proxies, and the repo's own root-cause lane, using the same data, concludes the decline is mostly fixable.

## Red team: opposite side

Written Oct 14, 2026 by a reviewer arguing as an investor who is long NKE against this short. Labels: **REPO** = number taken from a repo note that carries its own filing citation (I read the note, not the filing); **OPENED** = I opened the page today; **my arithmetic** marked. No new modeling.

### Verdict in one paragraph

The "demand fell first" leg is real for the AJ1 silhouette, but the thesis stretches it into a claim about all of lifestyle and about Nike's profit pool, and the two pieces of evidence that carry that stretch (the North America profit gap and "the market prices FY24 margins") do not hold up. The variant perception is also not a variant: a named sell-side Sell published on Oct 6, 2026 already calls Nike's share loss "irreversible," and the Street is net bearish. Score: 3.5 out of 10.

### 1. The thesis is close to consensus, so the edge is thin

| Signal | Reading | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Berenberg downgrade, Oct 6, 2026 | Hold to Sell, target $49 to $27.50; Nike has "effectively conceded a reduced position", the change is "irreversible"; cites lifestyle down low double digits, Jordan down mid-teens, running up double digits | parameter.io write-up of the note, OPENED (https://parameter.io/berenberg-slashes-nike-nke-price-target-to-27-50-cites-structural-market-shift/) |
| Evercore ISI, Jun 23, 2026 | Outperform to In Line; reasons include weak US lifestyle and weak Jordan retro launches | Search summary only (SNIPPET, not opened) |
| Rating mix | MarketBeat 10 sell, 21 hold, 8 buy ("Reduce"); Yahoo sell plus strong sell rose from 2 to 7 in three months; JPM UW, BofA U/P, MS UW, BMO U/P | `thesis_tests/consensus_and_valuation.md` rows 72 to 76, 140, REPO |
| Price targets | Median $34.40, below the $35.35 price; 19 of 21 targets cut after Q1 FY27 | same note, REPO |
| Estimates | FY28 EPS cut 26% in 30 days (23 down, 0 up) | same note, REPO |

The advocate frames the market as believing a "supply reset". The Street that sets the price has already moved to "structural share loss in lifestyle and Jordan." The Berenberg $27.50 target is almost exactly the advocate's $26 to $28. A judge will see this as the bear consensus with better sneaker data, not as a differentiated call.

### 2. The valuation hook is overstated

- The thesis says the price assumes "the return of a profit pool that is not coming back" and "roughly FY24 margins." Consensus implies a **5.7% EBIT margin in FY27 and 7.2% in FY28** (`consensus_and_valuation.md` rows 120 to 121, REPO), far below FY24's 12.7%.
- The reverse DCF needs 12% to 14% only by **FY2031** at a 10% WACC; at 9% the bar is 11% to 12%, at 8% about 9% to 10%, near today's 8.2% (same note, line 243, REPO). Nike's 10-year average is 11.8%. So the price pays for a return to roughly the long-run average five years out, on an assumed WACC. "The price needs FY24" is one point on a sensitivity range, presented as a fact.
- A long would say: the short needs Nike to stay below about 10% EBIT margin permanently, and nothing in the lifestyle data proves that, because Nike reports no profit by franchise.

### 3. The North America "revenue back, profit not" bridge is mostly tariffs, not lifestyle

The advocate removes the $965M IEEPA refund but keeps the full FY26 tariff cost in NA EBIT, then attributes the gap to "demand creation and overhead." The repo's own tariff estimate for NA in FY26 is about **$1,011M** (range $920M to $1,111M) (`investigations/05_north_america_gross_margin.md`, zealous-mccarthy branch, REPO).

| North America | FY24 | FY26 ex-refund | FY26 no-new-tariff (my arithmetic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EBIT | $5,822M | about $4,411M | about $5,422M ($5,331M to $5,522M) |
| Margin | 27.2% | 21.5% | about 26.4% (26.0% to 26.9%) |

On a like-for-like tariff basis the NA profit gap shrinks from about 570 bps to about 80 bps, while NA gross margin is **up** about 143 bps (+98 to +192) vs FY24 (same note, REPO) with the mix already shifted toward wholesale and running. That is the opposite of "the replacement pool earns less." Ongoing Section 122 tariffs are a real cost, but they are a tariff thesis, not a lifestyle-demand thesis. **This is the pillar that breaks hardest.**

### 4. "Demand fell first" does not separate demand from supply

- Jordan revenue **rose 32%** FY22 to FY24 while "Jordan 1" search fell. Revenue rising while interest fades is what pushing supply into a market looks like: Nike released 700+ Jordan styles a year in 2022 and 2023 (SneakerPing via BallerAlert, `culture_demographics.md`, REPO). Oversupply dilutes the signal and then interest falls (Berger and Heath 2008, cited in `challengers_taste.md`, REPO). The sequence fits the supply story at least as well.
- Teen mindshare **peaked** at 61% in Spring and Fall 2023, after the AJ1 premium had already collapsed to 4% and while Dunk was oversupplied; it fell with revenue, not ahead of it (`culture_demographics.md` P1, REPO). A leading demand indicator that lags is not "demand first."
- The Feng Tay test (`DEEP_DIVE_DEFINITIVE_TESTS.md` Test 1, REPO) is about **total** Nike footwear, which includes the growing running business, and mixes price, currency and about 15% non-Nike business. Its own verdict says it "does not rule out a cut confined to Jordan and classic styles." It cannot carry a Jordan demand claim.
- "Air Jordan" search has been flat since 2024 and StockX reported Jordan average resale price +6% in 2025 and Jordan sneaker sales +6% in H1 2026 (`culture_demographics.md` P3, REPO). The decline is concentrated in one silhouette Nike over-released, not the franchise.

### 5. The "cut did not work" test did not test a cut

- The AJ1 High cut (about 20 to 10 releases a year) was a **reallocation inside Jordan**, not a franchise cut: AJ4 releases went from 7 to 33 a year and AJ3 and AJ4 premiums fell about 40 points as volume moved onto them (advocate pillar 2). Total Jordan retro supply was not cut until Hill's Q1 FY27 announcement (Oct 1, 2026), which the advocate concedes is untested.
- The difference-in-differences result is **not significant** (-22.7 points, 95% interval -48.3 to +3.5, 6 AJ1 and 2 AJ3 shoes seen both before and after, no AJ4 control) and the repo labels it "not decided" (`DEEP_DIVE_DEFINITIVE_TESTS.md` Test 4, REPO).
- Natural experiment in the same month: in Sept 2026 genuinely scarce Nike drops traded at **27% to 56% premiums** (Air Max 1000 56%, Book 2 43%) while oversupplied Jordan retros traded 40% to 47% below retail (Needham via FN/Yahoo, `culture_demographics.md`, REPO). Same consumer, same resale market, different supply. That is direct evidence that scarcity still converts into price when Nike actually restricts it.

### 6. "Lifestyle" is not one bucket, and outside China Nike is nearly flat

- Sportswear (just under half of revenue) **contains** the rising family: running-inspired lifestyle (P-6000, V5 Runner, Air Max, Vomero 5) grew "strong double digits" in Q1 FY27 (`challengers_taste.md`, Nike call, REPO). Treating all of Sportswear plus Jordan plus Converse (about 62% to 65% of revenue) as the fading pool double counts.
- NIKE Brand outside Greater China fell only **0.8%** in Q1 FY27 (`00_root_cause_synthesis.md`, REPO). If 60% of revenue were in a structural demand reset, the ex-China business could not be flat. China (-26% c-n) is an execution story: adidas grew +9% to +17% c-n every quarter through the same Topsports doors (`00_root_cause_synthesis.md` row 11, REPO). Converse's EBIT collapse ($676M to $18M) is the canvas cycle plus a 39% marketing cut, not Jordan.
- North America grew five straight quarters and NA footwear units rose about 6% in FY26 (advocate weak point 1). Part is sell-in (`investigations/01`, REPO), but the region where On, Hoka and New Balance are strongest had Nike's **smallest** footwear decline (-8.4% FY24 to FY26) (`challengers_taste.md`, REPO).

### 7. The taste shift is a rotation Nike is already winning back in

- The repo's own test of "taste shift regardless of execution" **failed its decisive prediction** (P7 reversibility): Nike run-specialty dollars +35.4% vs On -19.7% and Hoka -7.4% (12 months to Sept 2025, Circana and Karnan via SGB, REPO); +5 points of statement-running share in Western Europe and North America in FY26 (Nike Q4 FY26 call, REPO); running added about $1B in five quarters.
- The challengers are decelerating: Hoka +23.6% (FY25) to +15.9% (FY26) to +7.7% (Apr to Jun 2026); On Americas +4.5% reported in Q2 2026 with the guide cut (Deckers 8-K and On 6-K, `challengers_taste.md`, REPO).
- adidas, an incumbent in the same taste regime, grew North America +10% c-n in 2025 and +17% in Q2 2026 by executing onto the rising silhouette (`challengers_taste.md`, REPO). Execution, not the regime, decided outcomes.
- The repo puts the truly execution-independent share of the decline at **15% to 25%** (`challengers_taste.md`, REPO). The short needs it to be the majority.

### 8. The "lower-margin replacement" evidence has confounds

- Running ASP $83 vs On $143 and Hoka $137 is an all-channel average that includes Nike's kids and value tiers sold through family and value doors, where On and Hoka barely sell. It is a mix difference, not proof that Nike sells the same shoe for less (my judgment; no like-for-like SKU comparison in the repo).
- The 22 of 22 Pegasus 41 and 49% Running Warehouse markdowns fall in a model change-over year (Pegasus 41 superseded by the next version), a normal clearance pattern (my judgment, not tested).
- Demand creation at 11.2% of revenue in Q1 FY27 sits in a quarter (Jun to Aug 2026) that contained the 2026 FIFA World Cup (Jun 11 to Jul 19, 2026, public tournament schedule, not opened today). Nike's own FY13 10-K cited "higher demand creation expense for the Olympics" in a similar event year (`thesis_tests/marketing_payoff.md`, REPO). One event quarter is not a structural cost line.

### 9. Factual and framing errors found

1. "The price assumes a return to roughly FY24 margins": consensus implies 5.7% (FY27) and 7.2% (FY28); the 11% to 14% reverse-DCF figure is for FY2031 at a 10% WACC and falls to 9% to 10% at 8% WACC.
2. NA EBIT ex-refund (21.5%) is compared with FY24 (27.2%) while still carrying about $1.0B of FY26 tariffs; like for like it is about 26.4%. Attributing the gap to demand creation and overhead is wrong in size.
3. Weak point 8 says Nike "won back" the 2016 to 2019 share loss with its retro wave. The repo's Euromonitor and GlobalData data show Nike only **held** about 35% in 2021 to 2023 and then fell to 28.9% in 2025 (`challengers_taste.md`, REPO). Minor, and it cuts against the long, but it is misstated.
4. Variant perception says "the market ... treat[s] the decline as an FY2018-style oversupply reset." The rating mix and the Berenberg note show the Street has already moved to a structural framing.
5. "The cut has not restored price" is presented as a test result; the repo's own Test 4 calls it "not decided" and the cut was a within-Jordan reallocation.

### 10. What would make me (the long) wrong

- AJ1 Royal still in full size runs at nike.com, Foot Locker and Champs on about Nov 7, and the holiday AJ4 Bred and AJ11 Space Jam below retail within 60 days, **after** the franchise-wide cut.
- Investor Day (Nov 16 to 17) sets a long-term EBIT margin target below 10%.
- Q2 FY27 (about Dec 17) shows NIKE Brand ex-China turning clearly negative while running keeps growing, which would isolate a lifestyle drag.

### Bottom line for the team

Keep the sneaker-level evidence (size runs, retailer markdowns, the scarce-vs-oversupplied split): it is the best original work in the repo. But do not pitch "structural, permanently smaller profit pool" as the headline: it is the Berenberg call already in the price, its profit bridge is mostly tariffs, and the repo's own root-cause lane, the run-specialty reversal and the flat ex-China business argue against it. If the team goes short, the cleaner hook is a dated one (Jordan retro sell-through after the October cut, plus the dividend and Investor Day), not a permanent-demand claim.

### B. Factual and framing errors

1. **Search timing is shifted by a year.** "'Jordan 1' search fell 43% from 2023 to 2024 while Jordan revenue was at its FY24 peak" is wrong on dates. The search window is July to September. July to September 2024 is Q1 **FY25**, a year in which Jordan revenue fell 16%. FY24 (June 2023 to May 2024) contains the July to September **2023** reading. Matched windows: July to September 2021 (Q1 FY22) at 70 to July to September 2023 (Q1 FY24) at 51 is **-27%**, while Jordan revenue rose about 33% FY22 to FY24 (+29%, +3%). The divergence is real but about 40% smaller than stated, and the same mismatch inflates "-59% 2021 to 2024 while revenue rose 32% FY22 to FY24" (the search window runs a year past the revenue window). (Repo, re-read: WF `alternative_explanations.md` section 3, `12_jordan_brand_thesis.md` lines 26 to 29; my arithmetic.)
2. **"The cut" in Pillar 2 was a reallocation, not a cut.** The repo's own release counts show core adult retro releases of 98 (2023), 96 (2024), **113 (2025)** and about 91 (2026 including scheduled). AJ1 High went from about 20 to about 10, but AJ4 went from 7 to 33 and AJ3 from 8 to 15. Note 12 concludes: "Supply shifted between models, not cut in total; any cut was in pairs per release, which public data cannot show." The advocate's "two years of cuts" therefore tests a period in which total retro supply (by release count) rose. Release counts are colourways, not pairs, so even the AJ1 "halving" is not a measured pair cut. (Repo, re-read: WF `12_jordan_brand_thesis.md` line 68.)
3. **The quarter cited to show the cut failing predates the cut.** "North America, the target of the cut, sold 2% more pairs at lower ASP in Q1 FY27." Q1 FY27 is June to August 2026. The broad Jordan retro cut was announced on the Q1 FY27 call (October 1, 2026), and Hill said "North America will feel the biggest impact" in the near term, i.e. from now. NA footwear units also include running, which the thesis itself says is growing; total NA pairs cannot isolate Jordan. (Repo, re-read: WF Test 5; `12_jordan_brand_thesis.md` line 35.)
4. **The "highest-margin dollars" premise is asserted, not sourced.** Nike discloses no franchise or category margin. The only support is the estimate that about 50% of Jordan ran through NIKE Direct (range 39% to 66% depending on an assumed internal price ratio), and Direct-heavy is not the same as highest-margin after Direct's operating costs. (Repo, re-read: WF `12_jordan_brand_thesis.md` lines 44 to 57.)
5. **Weak point 8 contradicts Pillar 3's own source.** It says Nike lost 6.4 US share points to adidas in 2016 to 2019 "and won them back." The Euromonitor series the advocate cites shows 34.1% in 2019 and 28.9% in 2025: never won back. Either the series or the sentence is wrong. (Repo, re-read: SS `dtc_wholesale.md` line 24.)
6. **Selective reading of the search-share data.** The advocate says Nike's search share is "flat only since Sep 2026." The same note says US Nike share was **up year on year for five straight months (June to October 2026)**, rose from 36.5% (2026Q2) to 39.0% (2026Q3), and that "the lifestyle franchise drag (Dunk, AF1, Jordan 1) has faded from -20% to -45% YoY to roughly flat." The worldwide figure is the weaker one; the US figure, which is where the Jordan cut is aimed, is turning. (Repo, re-read: WF `consumer_interest_altdata.md` lines 41 to 44, 213.)
7. **Vans base year is cherry-picked.** -48% uses Vans' FY2022 peak ($4,161.9M); the repo lane uses FY2023 ($3,682.9M, -42%). Not wrong, but state the base. (Repo, re-read: SS `challengers_taste.md` line 15.)

### C. Pillars where the evidence does not carry the claim

**C1. Pillar 1 (demand fell first): the sequence fits the supply story just as well.** The thesis needs demand to fall *independently* of supply. The repo's own record is that supply rose first: 700+ Jordan releases a year in 2022 and 2023 (WSJ via InvestNews, SS synthesis line 59), Jordan revenue +29% in FY23, and Nike's own Q1 FY27 words "we've been oversupplying our iconic retro product." Hype indicators (search, SNKRS installs, resale premium) then fell *during* that oversupply. That is exactly what the scarcity model predicts when supply is flooded, so "demand fell before Nike cut" does not distinguish "taste changed" from "Nike burned the hype by oversupplying." Note 12 itself reads it as "Oversupply eroded demand first." Two smaller points: SNKRS weekly *downloads* measure new installs of a 2015 app, which fall as the installed base saturates, and are not a demand measure; and the AJ1 premium fell from 61% to 4% in 2020 to 2023, the years of peak volume.

**C2. The factory test does not test the Jordan claim.** Feng Tay and Yue Yuen are totals across all Nike footwear (Feng Tay is about 85% one customer; Yue Yuen is multi-brand). Test 1's own verdict: "This does not rule out a cut confined to Jordan and classic styles, because supplier totals cannot be split by franchise." What the test does show is that Nike did *not* cut factory volume in FY25 while revenue fell 11.7%, with NA ASP down 8 points on discounts in Q2 FY25. That is Nike clearing an oversupply, which is the supply-reset reading, not evidence against it. (Repo, re-read: WF Test 1 verdict and Test 5 table.)

**C3. Pillar 2 (cut did not restore price): the strongest data point argues for supply.** Where Nike *raised* volume (AJ4 releases 7 to 33, AJ3 8 to 15), premiums fell about 40 points within two release years (AJ4 +24% to -20%; AJ3 +24% to -22%). That is a textbook supply effect: more pairs, lower price. The AJ1 result (no recovery) is equally consistent with "premiums are set by total Jordan retro supply, which rose." Other measurement problems:
- The GOAT table is one snapshot (Oct 13, 2026) sorted by release year, not premiums at release. It shows 2023 pre-cut AJ1s (14 of 15 below retail) are as weak today as post-cut ones. It cannot show a before and after. (Recomputed.)
- The difference-in-differences (-22.7 points) has a 95% interval of -48.3 to +3.5, rests on 6 AJ1 and 2 AJ3 shoes seen both before and after, and has no AJ4 control. Test 4 labels it "not decided." Listing it as evidence overstates it.
- Size runs measure whether a size can be bought online, not how many pairs exist, and are a single date; the Vomero 5 control is in full runs at day 1,108 too.
- Foot Locker markdowns are survivor-biased: shoes that sold through are no longer listed, so "6 of 13 listed retros discounted" over-samples losers. The same note shows Foot Locker sale styles fell from 2,633 (Jan 2025) to 718 today, a much cleaner wholesale channel. (Repo, re-read: WF `marketplace_pricing_and_shelf_share.md` line 23.)
- The advocate's own rebuttal ("premiums fell because the resale market collapsed: that is our point") concedes the measure is driven by a market-wide resale contraction, so falling Jordan premiums cannot separately prove Jordan-specific demand loss.

**C4. Pillar 4 (smaller, lower-margin pool): the North America number is a tariff effect.** The advocate compares FY26 NA EBIT *excluding the $965M IEEPA refund but including about $1,011M of FY26 tariff cost* with FY24, which had no new tariffs. Investigation 05 estimates FY26 NA tariff cost at about $1,011M (range $920M to $1,111M). On a no-new-tariff basis: 5,376 - 965 + 1,011 = about $5,422M, a **26.4% NA EBIT margin (range 25.9% to 26.9%) vs 27.2% in FY24**, a gap of about 0.8 points, not 5.7 (my arithmetic on ZM `investigations/05` lines 53 to 55, 122 to 123). NA gross margin on that basis is up about 143 bps. So in the region where Direct share fell most (48.6% to 41.7%), and where Jordan is concentrated, the profit pool before tariffs is nearly intact. Tariffs are a real cost, but they hit every importer and say nothing about lifestyle demand. The "EBIT gap sits mostly in demand creation and overhead" claim is not supported once tariffs are treated consistently.

**C5. The running price comparison is mix, not markdown.** The $83 vs $143 (On) vs $137 (Hoka) all-channel ASP compares Nike's full ladder (Revolution, Downshifter, kids, Amazon at $49 to $75) with two brands that sell almost nothing under $130. At the same run-specialty shelf the median Nike road shoe lists at $154.95 vs Hoka $174.95, an 11% gap, not 42%. The 49% markdown share counts colourways, during a Pegasus 41 to 42 changeover; Saucony (31.7%), Brooks (32.9%) and Skechers (36.8%) are also well above Hoka, and Hoka halved its colourway range (16.6% to 8.2% share), which mechanically lowers its markdown share. Nike's running markdowns are real but do not prove "lower margin per dollar" for Performance overall; Nike discloses no category margin. (Repo, re-read: WF `runningwarehouse_brand_summary.csv`, `marketplace_pricing_and_shelf_share.md` lines 17, 59.)

### D. Another explanation that fits the same data

The live decline is mostly China and Converse, not US Jordan and lifestyle. In Q1 FY27, Greater China was about 65% of the revenue decline and Converse about 20%; NIKE Brand outside Greater China fell only 0.8% (SS `00_root_cause_synthesis.md` line 218, repo arithmetic). North America has grown five straight quarters, NA wholesale grew about 9% to 11% in Q3 FY26 to Q1 FY27, and Foot Locker comps turned positive (SS synthesis lines 140, 211). Euromonitor shows Nike's US share loss *slowed* from about 2.1 points a year (2016 to 2019) to about 0.9 (2019 to 2025), while VF lost more share than Nike (9.4% to 3.9%) (SS `dtc_wholesale.md` line 24, `challengers_taste.md` line 15). A rotation that hits Vans and Converse hardest, with Nike's own running up about $1B in five quarters, fits "Nike is an incumbent riding a fashion cycle, with a China problem" at least as well as "Nike's profit pool is permanently smaller." The repo's root-cause lane puts the fashion rotation at only 10% to 20% of the FY24 to FY26 decline.

### E. What survives

- Pillar 3 is solid on its own terms: JD Europe mix (retro basketball about 37% to 18%), Vans and Converse fading under different owners, Circana running +13% with sport lifestyle down in H1 2026, and Asics SportStyle growth all sit outside Nike's control and outside China.
- Converse (EBIT $676M to $18M) and Greater China (GM -450 bps) are real lifestyle-heavy profit-pool losses.
- The nike.com vs partner price gap is a genuine, checkable observation, even if survivor-biased.
- The pre-registered tests (AJ1 Royal day 14 and day 28 size runs, holiday resale) are the right way to decide this, but most of the decisive evidence lands after finals.

### F. Is it consensus?

Largely yes. "Nike lost the lifestyle cycle to On, Hoka, Asics and New Balance" has been the dominant narrative since 2024, and the price already reflects it: about 80% below peak, 99.2M shares short, and about 45% of the fall explained by a sector de-rating (SS synthesis line 17). The genuinely non-consensus part is narrow: that the October 2026 retro cut will not restore premiums. That claim cannot be tested with the evidence in this file, because the pre-cut period it relies on saw total retro supply rise.

### G. Fixes needed before this thesis could be pitched

1. Re-date the search evidence to matched fiscal windows (-27% from Q1 FY22 to Q1 FY24, not -43%).
2. Drop "two years of cuts" and say "a reallocation from AJ1 to AJ3 and AJ4, with total retro releases up in 2025." Present the AJ3 and AJ4 premium collapse honestly as supply evidence.
3. Restate the NA profit gap on a no-new-tariff basis (about 26.4% vs 27.2%) or remove it.
4. Replace the $83 vs $143 ASP comparison with like-for-like shelf prices, or drop it.
5. Remove "won them back" from weak point 8, or source it.
6. Either own China and Converse explicitly (they are about 85% of the Q1 FY27 decline) or concede that the US Jordan and lifestyle leg is not where the live decline is.
