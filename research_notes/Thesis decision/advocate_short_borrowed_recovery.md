## 1. Thesis in one sentence

Nike's North America "recovery" (the only region that grew in FY2026) was built on items that do not repeat: lapping its own FY2025 hold-backs, lower returns reserves, a one-off World Cup sell-in, retailers (above all Foot Locker) taking inventory ahead of sales while the markdown on that product happened at the retailer and in resale rather than on Nike's P&L, and a $965M tariff refund that made North America look like the one region with rising margins; that inventory has to be worked down through FY2027 and into FY2028, so the consensus FY2028 rebound (EPS $1.65, +28%) and a ~28x forward multiple are too high.

## 2. Variant perception

**What the market believes.** After the Oct 1, 2026 guide-down, consensus accepts a bad FY2027: FY2027 EPS was cut 25% in 30 days to $1.29 and 19 of 21 broker actions cut targets [market data; repo: consensus_and_valuation]. But it treats FY2027 as the **trough**: FY2028 consensus is EPS $1.65 (+28%) on revenue +1.1% [market data, Yahoo, Oct 13]. The implicit story is that North America shows the playbook works (five straight quarters of wholesale growth, "positive comps at Foot Locker for the first time in four years", and the highest regional margin), so once Greater China and Sportswear are cleaned up, growth resumes. The stock pays 27.4x FY2027 and 21.5x FY2028 EPS against a peer median of 15.5x and 12.9x [market data; repo: consensus_and_valuation B3], which is a margin-recovery premium.

**What we believe.** North America is not the proof; it is the next problem. Nike's NA wholesale growth of +9% to +24% a quarter ran against Nike-heavy specialty comps of -7% to +1%, and the gap went into retailer inventory (Foot Locker inventory +14% to +16% vs sales -6.5% at Aug 1, 2026). Nike has already admitted the mechanism ("oversupplying our iconic retro product", "North America will feel the biggest impact", Sportswear and Jordan actions "expected to extend beyond fiscal 2027"), but the market is pricing it as a **one-quarter Q2 timing item** (Nike's ~400 bps Q2 headwind) rather than a multi-quarter unwind of the one region that was carrying the company (NA = 44% of FY2026 revenue, *my arithmetic* 20,511 / 46,398).

**Why we could be right where the market is not.** The market reads Nike's own numbers (shipments). The evidence sits in **other companies' filings and on the shelf**: Dick's segment inventory, Foot Locker comps, JD and Shoe Carnival liquidation, nike.com vs Foot Locker price tags, size runs and resale. Few sell-side models reconcile Nike sell-in against partner balance sheets.

## 3. Pillars

### Pillar 1. NA wholesale growth outran consumer sell-through, and the gap is sitting in partner inventory

**Hard evidence**
- **Nike shipments vs partner sales.** Nike NA wholesale YoY: +10.5% (Q1 FY26), +23.9% (Q2), +10.8% (Q3), +9.8% (Q4), +9.0% (Q1 FY27) [filing, each 10-Q/10-K; repo: investigations/01 T1]. Over the same windows: Foot Locker pro forma comps -4.7%, -3.4%, +0.6%, -3.6%; JD North America LFL -1.8% (FY26) and -4.0% (HY27); Shoe Carnival Q2 2026 comps -7.1%; Academy footwear about -1% to +4% [retailer filing; repo: retailer_sell_through]. Only the DICK'S Business comped +3% to +6%.
- **Foot Locker channel fill, on a cost basis.** Foot Locker inventory at Aug 1, 2026: $1,950M to $1,974M vs $1,709M a year earlier, **+14.1% to +15.5%**, while sales fell 6.5% and cost of sales fell 4.3%. Inventory / quarterly COGS went from 1.27 to about 1.52 (+20%); tariff or cost inflation explains about 2 points (Foot Locker gross margin -1.7 pts) [retailer filing, Dick's Q2 2026 release pp. 3, 12; *my arithmetic* in repo: investigations/01 T4a, T4b]. The build happened May 2 to Aug 1, 2026 (it was +3.6% to +5.1% at May 2), overlapping Nike Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27.
- **Size of the Nike piece (my arithmetic).** At last year's ratio (1.27 x $1,291M COGS = $1,640M), excess Foot Locker inventory is $310M to $334M at cost; at Nike's 59% share of Foot Locker purchases [retailer filing, Foot Locker FY2024 10-K p. 29] that is **~$183M to $197M**. On the stricter May 2026 ratio (1.35) it is ~$122M to $136M. Either way it equals **roughly 50% to 80% of Nike's entire Q1 FY27 NA wholesale gain ($245M)**.
- **Growth concentrated in the accounts where fill shows up.** Top-3 U.S. customer dollars rose $772M to $1,173M in FY2026, i.e. 52% to 80% of the $1,474M NA wholesale increase [filing, 10-K concentration disclosure; *my arithmetic*, repo: investigations/09 (c)].
- **Nike's own words.** "Simply put we've been oversupplying our iconic retro product" and "In the near term, North America will feel the biggest impact" (Q1 FY27 remarks PDF pp. 4 to 5) [Nike release]. Aged Sportswear "sold-through below expectations ... impacted our future order books as we ... work through excess inventory" (p. 3). Q2 FY27 headwind from "a higher comparison of North America sell-in activities" (p. 13). Dick's: industry inventory "grew ... particularly in certain legacy footwear silhouettes", "an increasingly aggressive promotional environment for key athletic brands" (Dick's Q2 2026 10-Q p. 21) [retailer filing].
- **Non-recurring pieces inside the growth.** 35% of FY26 NA wholesale growth ($520M of $1,474M) only restores FY2024 volume Nike held back in FY2025 [filing, FY26 10-K p. 37 "fewer marketplace management actions"; *my arithmetic*]. Q2 FY26 (+24%) was the only quarter far above FY2024 (+22% vs Q2 FY24) and included off-price liquidation [Nike release, Q2 FY26 PDF p. 9]. Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 included a World Cup sell-in that Nike says doubled 2022 kit sales [Nike release, Q1 FY27 PDF p. 7]; it is lapped from Q2 FY27.

**Alternatives ruled out (and how)**
- *Tariff or price inflation in retailer inventory dollars:* ruled out by the inventory/COGS test (both at cost, so common cost inflation cancels); gross margin data show only ~2.3% relative cost rise [repo: investigations/01 T4b].
- *Dick's repositioning Foot Locker stores:* ruled out; owned stores fell 2,307 to 2,212 and square footage 12.4M to 11.6M, which lowers inventory; write-downs reduce carrying value [retailer filing, Dick's Q2 2026 release p. 5, pp. 13 to 14].
- *Back-to-school timing:* ruled out; both balance dates are the same fiscal week [repo: investigations/01 item 17].
- *A Q4 FY26 to Q1 FY27 shipment shift:* ruled out; both quarters grew about equally and the combined two quarters grew +9.4% [*my arithmetic*, repo T7].
- *Generic mall-channel weakness (not Nike):* ruled out in the other direction; adidas NA grew +17% c.n. and Journeys comped +2% in the same quarter, so the weakness follows Nike/Jordan-retro-heavy banners [secondary/retailer filing; repo: us_wholesale_partner_destocking P4].
- *Channel shift from Nike Direct explains the growth:* only partly; at wholesale-equivalent value channel shift is ~22% of FY26 NA wholesale growth and ~34% of Q1 FY27 growth [*my arithmetic*, repo: investigations/09 A4].

### Pillar 2. The markdown did not disappear; it moved off Nike's P&L to partners and resale, and it comes back to Nike as lower sell-in and higher allowances

**Hard evidence**
- **Jordan retros: full price on nike.com, discounted at Foot Locker.** 2 of 113 listed colourways in the main Jordan retro lines are discounted on nike.com, vs 22 of 22 Pegasus 41 colourways [product data, api.nike.com feed, Oct 13, 2026; repo: DEEP_DIVE Test 2]. 0 of 61 AJ1 High / AJ3 / AJ4 releases from 2024 to 2026 were seen discounted within 90 days on nike.com (Wayback checks). At Foot Locker on the same day, 6 of 13 Jordan retros from the sample were discounted: AJ3 IF4396-100 (2026) $169.99 vs $215 (21% off), AJ1 High OG IH4363-100 $139.99 vs $185 (24% off), women's AJ4s 18% to 23% off [product data, footlocker.com].
- **Unsold at full price.** AJ4 Rare Air Tour Yellow has all 13 sizes (7 to 13) in stock at nike.com, Foot Locker and Champs on day 38; AJ12 Bucks 10 to 11 of 13 sizes on day 31 [product data; repo: DEEP_DIVE Test 3]. AJ1 High general releases trade 26% to 35% below retail on resale; 86% of AJ1 High sizes on GOAT have a lowest ask below retail [product data, GOAT, Oct 13].
- **The cost shows up in partners' P&Ls.** Dick's wrote down and liquidated $217.9M of Foot Locker inventory in FY2025 plus $42.7M in Q1 2026 [retailer filing, Dick's FY2025 10-K p. 39; Q1 2026 10-Q p. 25]; Foot Locker segment gross margin 25.7% vs 27.4% [retailer filing]; Shoe Carnival gross margin -690 bps on "liquidation of aged and excess inventory" [retailer filing, Q2 2026 10-Q pp. 20 to 21]; Foot Locker cut its 2026 outlook to comps -2% to 0% and a segment loss of $40M to $80M [retailer filing, Dick's Q2 2026 10-Q p. 22].
- **How it returns to Nike.** Nike's own definition of marketplace management is "higher sales returns and discounts with our wholesale partners to reduce inventory and create capacity for new product" (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 19) [filing]. In FY2026 the reverse happened: Nike's sales-related reserves (returns, post-invoice discounts, claims) fell from $1,834M (May 2025) to $1,589M (May 2026) and $1,488M (Aug 2026); per dollar of quarterly wholesale, 0.28 to 0.21 [filing, FY26 10-K p. 84; Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 7; *my arithmetic*]. Then-CFO Friend on the Q4 FY26 call: "we didn't sell in up 10%. There was a meaningful amount of that revenue growth that was associated with lower returns or sales-related reserves and returns, lower discount and lower cancellations" [Nike release, Q4 FY26 call; repo: us_wholesale_partner_destocking]. The FY26 reserve decline ($245M) equals ~17% of the FY26 NA wholesale gain (global balance, upper bound; *my arithmetic*, repo: investigations/09 A7).
- **Illustration only (my arithmetic, not a forecast):** if the reserve ratio went back to 0.23 or 0.25 of quarterly wholesale (the Q2 FY26 and Q3 FY26 levels) on Q1 FY27 wholesale of $6,948M, that is $110M to $249M of revenue Nike would give back as allowances.

**Alternatives ruled out (and how)**
- *"Jordan retros are not discounted because they sell through":* ruled out on the tested sample; full size runs weeks after launch and sub-retail resale are the opposite of sell-through [repo: DEEP_DIVE Tests 3, 4]. Caveat: sold-out shoes drop out of the feed, which biases nike.com's discount share toward zero.
- *Reserve release flattering revenue via estimate reversals:* ruled out by Nike ("variances ... were not material", FY26 10-K p. 47) [filing]. What remains is lower gross provisions, which is still a lap that cannot recur.
- *Industry-wide promotions, not Nike-specific:* partly survives (Dick's calls it industry-wide). But Dick's ties it to "legacy footwear silhouettes" and "launch and retro product", where Nike is dominant, and adidas NA grew +17% in the same quarter.

**Honest narrowing.** "Nike held full price on its own site" is true for **Jordan retros**, not for nike.com overall: men's sale share on nike.com is 45.7% and the sale wall (5,102 items) is near a series high [product data; repo: 16_full_price_recovery D]. Nike discounts Running and Dunk on its own site; the off-P&L markdown is a Jordan/retro story, which is exactly the product Foot Locker over-holds.

### Pillar 3. North America's margin "recovery" is the tariff refund

**Hard evidence**
- NA gross margin FY2024 44.4% to FY2026 45.6% (+120 bps) reported. The $965M IEEPA refund booked in Q4 FY26 cost of sales is 470 bps of NA revenue; **ex refund, with tariffs, FY26 NA GM was 40.9%, -350 bps vs FY24**, worse than EMEA (-240 bps) and close to APLA (-390 bps) [filing, FY26 10-K pp. 30, 37; *my arithmetic*, repo: investigations/05, recomputed and verified per 00_INDEX log].
- NA EBIT margin 27.2% (FY24) to 26.2% (FY26) reported; **ex refund 21.5%, -5.7 pts, the same as EMEA (-5.7) and APLA (-5.8)** [*my arithmetic* on repo `data/nike_region_profitability.csv`, investigations/09 A2]. No segment actually grew profit margin over two years.
- Company level: FY26 diluted EPS $2.10 included a one-time $0.52 tariff refund benefit [filing, Q4 FY26 release p. 1]. Q1 FY27 NA GM 43.4% is 219 bps below Q1 FY25 [filing, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 26]. Nike's ASP line in the gross margin bridge turned negative again in Q1 FY27 (-30 bps) [filing, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 23].
- FY2027 carries replacement Section 122 tariffs (10%, then 15% from August per Nike's plan) with no refund [Nike release, Q4 FY26 PDF p. 12].

**Alternatives considered**
- *Underlying margin did improve:* **survives, and it cuts against this pillar.** On a no-new-tariffs basis FY26 NA GM was ~45.8% (range 45.4% to 46.3%), +100 to +190 bps vs FY24, driven by lower cost per unit, not price [repo: investigations/05 2b]. The honest claim is narrower: the reported "NA is the only region with rising margin" is a refund artifact, and the underlying gain is a cost story (freight, mix, logistics) that does not prove full-price demand. Pricing contributed only ~+0.8% to revenue per unit [*my arithmetic*, repo: investigations/05 row 8].
- *FX:* ruled out as a cause of NA's rise; it moved against NA [repo: investigations/05 row 11].
- *Restatement:* ruled out; FY24 NA gross profit identical in both 10-Ks [filing].

### Pillar 4. Estimates and the multiple assume FY2028 recovery that the channel unwind and Nike's own timetable do not support

**Hard evidence**
- **What is priced.** FY2028 consensus EPS $1.646 (+28%) and revenue +1.1% [market data, Yahoo, Oct 13]. P/E 27.4x FY27 and 21.5x FY28 vs peer medians 15.5x and 12.9x [market data; repo: consensus_and_valuation B3].
- **Nike's own timetable slipped past FY2027.** FY26 10-K: clean-up done "by the end of December 2026" (p. 30). Q1 FY27 10-Q: Sportswear and Jordan actions "expected to extend beyond fiscal 2027" (p. 20) [filing].
- **The comparison is hard.** Q2 FY26 NA wholesale was $3,550M. Merely returning to the Q2 FY24 level ($2,902M) would be -18% YoY [*my arithmetic*]. Nike's guided company-level Q2 headwind (~400 bps, ~$497M) combines NA sell-in and EMEA Cyber Week [Nike release, Q1 FY27 PDF p. 13]. After Q2, NA laps the World Cup sell-in (Q4 FY26 to Q1 FY27) and the Foot Locker build into FY2028's first quarter.
- **Leading indicator points down.** Feng Tay (83% to 86% one customer, inferred Nike) leads Nike footwear growth by about one quarter (correlation 0.68 ex-COVID, n = 24); its Q1 FY27 revenue was -8.8% [exchange filings via MOPS; *my arithmetic*, repo: DEEP_DIVE Test 1].
- **No cushion from cash.** Dividend $1.64 = 121% to 143% of FY27 adjusted EPS guidance; FCF covered 0.91x of the dividend in FY26 and was negative in Q1 FY27 [filing; *my arithmetic*, repo: 18_capital_and_dividend]. S&P cut to A, negative outlook, on Oct 5, 2026, expecting ~$1.2B of annual cash burn for two to three years [secondary: Reuters via capital.com].
- **Units, not price, in NA.** Q1 FY27 NA footwear units +2% with ASP -1 pt; the supply-cut signature (units down, price held) appears only in Greater China and Converse [filing, Q1 FY27 10-Q pp. 26 to 28; repo: DEEP_DIVE Test 5].

**Alternatives considered**
- *"It is all in guidance already":* partly survives. Guidance (revenue down high-single digits) and the Q2 headwind are public. Our claim is about **duration**: the guide frames NA as a Q2 lap; partner balance sheets and Nike's "beyond fiscal 2027" say it runs longer, which hits FY2028, where consensus still expects growth.
- *Low EV/sales (1.13x, lowest in the 10-year series) protects the downside:* survives as a risk to the short (see weakest points).

## 5. What would prove us wrong

1. Dick's Q3 2026 shows Foot Locker inventory growth at or below its comps (inventory near or below ~$1.85B) with comps flat or positive: the fill cleared through sell-through, not an unwind.
2. Nike Q2 FY27 NA wholesale declines no more than the guided lap (roughly ~$3.2B or better, i.e. about -10%) and Q3 FY27 NA wholesale grows again.
3. Sales-related reserves do not rise as a share of wholesale, and the NIKE Brand ASP line in the gross margin bridge is positive in Q2 FY27.
4. The AJ1 Royal and holiday retros show broken size runs within 14 to 28 days at full price at Foot Locker as well as nike.com.
5. Investor Day gives partner inventory data (weeks of supply) showing the channel is clean, plus an NA plan that does not cut wholesale.

## 6. Weakest points (stated plainly)

1. **Nike's share of the Foot Locker build is inferred, not measured.** Dick's does not split inventory by brand. The ~$120M to ~$200M Nike piece uses Foot Locker's 59% Nike share from 2024 [repo: investigations/09 (b)].
2. **The size may be small relative to the stock.** Even the high end (~$200M Foot Locker plus up to ~$250M of allowances) is ~1% of revenue. A separate repo estimate puts the FY27 hit from Foot Locker and Hibbett reduced buys at $0.1B to $0.3B, 3% to 9% of the guided decline [repo: us_wholesale_partner_destocking §6].
3. **Much is already disclosed and probably priced.** Nike itself said "oversupplying", "North America will feel the biggest impact" and flagged the Q2 sell-in lap; consensus FY27 EPS fell 25% and short interest rose from 75.6M to 99.2M shares in September [market data]. The variant rests on duration (FY2028), which cannot be proven before Nov 19.
4. **DICK'S, Nike's largest account, shows real sell-through** (comps +3% to +6%, inventory in line with sales), and Running and NA Basketball are up double digits with share gains [retailer filing; Nike release].
5. **The margin pillar holds only on one basis.** Ex-tariff NA gross margin rose ~+143 bps, and the market already treats the refund as one-off (FY27 EPS guidance is far below FY26).
6. **"Full price on its own site" is only true for Jordan retros.** nike.com runs a large sale wall; Nike's on-sale rate at Foot Locker (13.2%) is close to the store average (11.4%) [product data; repo: 16].
7. **Valuation floor and squeeze risk.** EV/sales 1.13x and EV/EBITDA 11.2x are the lowest in the 10-year series; the 4.6% yield attracts income buyers; a confident Investor Day two days before finals could squeeze shorts.
8. **Timing.** The decisive data (Dick's ~Nov 24, Nike ~Dec 17) arrive after finals. Before Nov 19 we have only the size tracker, Feng Tay, the dividend and Investor Day.

Confidence that it is **not already priced** and drives the stock lower from $35.35: moderate (about 5/10). The thesis is strongest framed as "FY2028 consensus is too high because NA must shrink to clear the channel", not "Nike is collapsing".

### Verdict in one paragraph

The mechanism is mostly real but it is small, already disclosed by Nike, already in consensus, and partly misattributed. Even on the advocate's own high-end sizing the channel unwind is worth about $0.02 to $0.16 of EPS against FY28 consensus of $1.65, while the sell side has already cut FY28 EPS by 26% in 30 days with zero upward revisions, the median target is below the share price, and short interest is at a series record. The returns-reserve leg is wrong in direction: reserves are still above FY24 and far above FY23, so the FY26 decline is normalization, not a one-off borrowed gain. The thesis has no clear edge over what the market already believes. Score: 3/10.

### 1. Size: the thesis explains a rounding error in FY28 EPS

- Advocate's high-end exposure: ~$200M Foot Locker Nike piece plus up to ~$250M of allowances, ~$450M of revenue; the repo destocking note puts the FY27 Foot Locker plus Hibbett hit at $0.1B to $0.3B [repo: `us_wholesale_partner_destocking.md` §6, stoic-shannon].
- *My arithmetic:* lost revenue x 40% to 43.4% gross margin (Q1 FY27 NA GM 43.4%, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 26, filing via repo) x (1 - 22.5% tax) / 1,487M shares:
  - $100M: about $0.02 of EPS
  - $450M: about $0.09 to $0.10
  - $700M (all channels, stretched): about $0.15 to $0.16
- Against FY28 consensus EPS of $1.646 that is **1% to 10%**, and inside the existing consensus range of $1.10 to $3.22 (39 analysts) [market data, Yahoo earningsTrend, repo: `consensus_and_valuation.md` lines 39 to 42]. At 21.5x, $0.10 of EPS is about $2.15 a share, about 6% of the price. That is not a short thesis; it is a footnote to someone else's.

### 2. Consensus already does not believe in North America

Consensus is not modeling an NA rebound in units; the +28% EPS is a margin story (lapping clearance and tariff costs). The short attacks revenue; the FY28 number is driven by margin, and clearing the channel helps margin.
- **The sell side is already bearish.** Recommendation mean 2.81 (hold); sell plus strong sell rose from 2 to 7 in three months; MarketBeat consensus "Reduce" with 10 sells; median target $34.40 below the $35.35 price; 19 of 21 targets cut Oct 2 to 5 [market data, repo: `consensus_and_valuation.md` lines 66 to 76; MarketBeat via repo].

A crowded short with a 4.6% yield to pay is a poor setup for a two-day-before-finals Investor Day.

**Conclusion on edge:** "Partner inventory is high and NA will feel the biggest impact" is Nike's own sentence, Dick's own sentence ("the industry is carrying too much inventory"), and the sell side's revision path. The only differentiated element is *duration into FY28*, and that cannot be tested before Nov 19.

### 3. Pillar 2 (reserves) is backwards

- Sales-related reserves: **$994M (May 2023), $1,282M (May 2024), $1,834M (May 2025), $1,589M (May 2026), $1,488M (Aug 2026)** [filing: FY23 10-K p. 84, FY24 10-K p. 85, FY25 10-K p. 83, FY26 10-K p. 84, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 7; repo `data/nike_filing_facts.csv` rows 5557 to 5560 and `thesis_tests/say_vs_do_execution.md` line 38; not reopened today].
- *My arithmetic:* the Aug 2026 balance is still **+16% above May 2024** and **+50% above May 2023**. FY25 was the abnormal year, when Hill paid partners to clear the channel. A decline from that spike is normalization, not a "borrowed" gain. The advocate's "give-back" illustration (returning to 0.23 to 0.25 of quarterly wholesale) uses the clean-up period itself as the norm. On a pre-clean-up base the reserve ratio would be expected to fall further, not rise. The repo's own say-vs-do test reads the same data as "easing in FY26 fits cleanup progress" [repo: `say_vs_do_execution.md` line 38].
- Friend's "we didn't sell in up 10%" quote is management *telling* the market the growth included lower reserves. Disclosed in June 2026; no edge.

### 4. Pillar 1 (Foot Locker fill) has alternatives the advocate did not rule out

- **The Foot Locker inventory is global, the thesis is about North America.** Dick's reports one Foot Locker Business inventory figure ($2.0B) covering Foot Locker's EMEA and APAC stores too [retailer filing, Dick's Q2 2026 release, search result opened today: s205.q4cdn.com]. On the Q2 2026 call, Ed Stack's "the industry is carrying too much inventory" was said **in the context of Foot Locker EMEA** [secondary transcript, Webull, opened today via WebFetch summary; verify against the Dick's PDF transcript before quoting]. The advocate never splits the build by region.

The advocate rules out "repositioning" using total store count and square footage, but a remodel-plus-new-assortment program raises inventory per door even as doors fall. Not ruled out.

Purchase-accounting step-up "had flowed through by Q2 2026" is labelled **opinion** in the repo [repo: `investigations/01` line 215].
- **Nike's Foot Locker sell-through was positive in the quarter before the build.** Hill, Q4 FY26 call: revenue growth and retail sales comps with Foot Locker "positive for the first time in 4 years" (Mar to May 2026) [Nike release via repo: `us_wholesale_partner_destocking.md` P2]. - **The 59% share is stale and whole-company.** Foot Locker FY2024 10-K, pre-acquisition, and the Jordan retro mix the advocate blames is exactly what Nike says it is cutting (Dunk about -50% in Q1 FY27) [repo: `us_wholesale_partner_destocking.md` line 79]. If Nike is cutting the retro supply, the fill shrinks Nike's FY27 revenue (guided) and improves FY28 pricing, which is the long case, not the short.

### 5. Pillar 3 (tariff refund margin) is already priced

- FY27 adjusted EPS guide of $1.15 to $1.35 versus FY26 diluted EPS of $2.10 shows the market and the company both strip the $0.52 refund [filing, Q4 FY26 release p. 1, via repo; Nike guide]. No investor is capitalizing the refund.
- The advocate's own alternatives test concedes that on a no-new-tariff basis NA gross margin rose about +100 to +190 bps, driven by lower unit cost [repo: `investigations/05` 2b]. A real cost improvement in the biggest region is evidence for the long side.

### 6. Factual and framing errors

6. **"Nike held full price on its own site" (the one-line summary)** is true only for Jordan retros; nike.com men's sale share is 45.7% [repo: note 16]. The advocate's body concedes this; the headline should not claim it.

### 7. Is it consensus already?

Largely yes. Each pillar is in a public source: Nike's Q1 FY27 remarks ("oversupplying", "North America will feel the biggest impact", Q2 NA sell-in lap), Friend's Q4 FY26 reserve comment, Dick's Foot Locker inventory, comps and loss guide, Nike's own one-off labelling of the $0.52 refund, and a 26% cut to FY28 EPS. ## Red team: facts and logic

**Prepared:** October 14, 2026. **Lens:** check the key numbers against the source notes and primary documents, and find places where the evidence does not support the claim or where another explanation fits the same data. **Opened status:** I re-read the repo notes named below on their branches (`git show`). I did not re-open Nike or Dick's filings; where I cite a filing page, it is as quoted in the repo note. Two web checks (Foot Locker and Dick's inventory accounting policy) were opened as search results only (snippets of the SEC 10-K pages), not the full filings. Arithmetic is labelled *my arithmetic*.

### Verdict in one paragraph

The arithmetic in the advocate case is mostly correct as transcribed. The problems are in what the numbers are made to prove. (1) Pillar 3 (margin) is built on a wrong comparison and does not survive: the "ex refund" North America figure keeps about $1.0B of FY26 tariffs that the refund itself reversed, so it is not an underlying margin. (2) Pillar 1 (channel fill) is real but only for one quarter at one retailer; the Foot Locker data the advocate cites show **no** build during the four FY26 quarters that made up most of the "recovery". (3) Pillar 2 (markdowns moved off Nike's P&L) has no pre-2024 baseline and an untested alternative (Nike's own factory stores). (4) Pillar 4 (FY28 estimates too high) does not follow from the sized evidence: the measured fill is worth roughly 2 to 4% of FY28 consensus EPS. The mechanism is real; the scale and novelty claimed are not.

### Pillar 3 breaks: the "ex refund" margin is not an underlying margin

- **What the advocate says:** ex refund, FY26 NA gross margin was 40.9% (-350 bps vs FY24) and NA EBIT margin 21.5% (-5.7 pts), "the same as EMEA (-5.7) and APLA (-5.8)".
- **Arithmetic check:** correct as arithmetic. FY24 NA EBIT 5,822 / 21,396 = 27.2%; FY26 ex refund 21.5% [repo `data/nike_region_profitability.csv`; investigations/09 A2; *my arithmetic* reproduces it].
- **Logic failure.** The $965M refund is the recovery of IEEPA tariffs that were themselves charged to NA cost of sales, mostly inside FY26. The advocate's own source says the refund is "at least as large as the IEEPA cost it reverses inside FY2026, never smaller", and that FY26 NA tariffs in cost of sales were about $1,011M (range $920M to $1,111M) [repo: zealous-mccarthy `investigations/05` Section 0 items 2 to 3 and table 2b, citing Q2 and Q3 FY26 remarks PDFs and FY26 10-K p. 30]. Removing the refund while keeping those tariffs charges NA for a cost that was later refunded. It measures "NA if tariffs had been lawful and never refunded", which is not a recurring state.
- **Correct comparison.** Adding back the ~$1,011M of FY26 NA tariffs: NA EBIT margin ≈ (4,411 + 1,011) / 20,511 = **26.4%, about -0.8 pts vs FY24**, not -5.7 pts [*my arithmetic*, an estimate that carries 05's tariff estimate]. On gross margin, 05 gives **+100 to +190 bps vs FY24 (central +143)** on a no-new-tariff basis. EMEA and APLA did not carry US import tariffs, so the "same as EMEA and APLA" comparison sets a tariff-burdened NA against tariff-free regions.
- **Cherry-picked base.** "Q1 FY27 NA GM 43.4% is 219 bps below Q1 FY25" picks the FY25 quarter; the year-on-year change was **+110 bps** and EMEA (+140 bps) and Greater China (+30 bps) also rose that quarter [repo: investigations/05 Section 0 item 7, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 26 as cited].
- **What survives:** FY27 carries Section 122 tariffs with no refund (Q4 FY26 remarks PDF p. 12, as cited). That is a forward cost, already inside Nike's FY27 guidance, not evidence that the FY26 recovery was "borrowed". The advocate's own Section 3 already concedes most of this; the pillar headline and the summary still state the broken version.

### Pillar 1 holds only for May to July 2026 at one retailer

- **Numbers check out:** Foot Locker inventory $1,950M to $1,974M at Aug 1, 2026 vs $1,709M (+14.1% to +15.5%); COGS $1,291M vs $1,349M; inventory/COGS 1.27 to ~1.52; 1.27 x 1,291 = $1,640M, so excess $310M to $334M; x 59% = $183M to $197M; at 1.35, $122M to $136M [repo: investigations/01 T4a, T4b; Dick's Q2 2026 release pp. 3, 12 as quoted; *my arithmetic* reproduces all of these].
- **The build is absent in FY26, the year of the "recovery".** The same source table shows Foot Locker inventory at Nov 1, 2025 +4.5% to +6.1%, at Jan 31, 2026 **-1.1% to +1.1%**, and at May 2, 2026 +3.6% to +5.1% [investigations/01 verdict row 5, T4a]. Nike NA wholesale grew +$1,474M in FY26, including +$684M in Q2 FY26, yet Foot Locker's balance sheet was flat to mid-single digits throughout, and DICK'S Business inventory tracked its sales (+6% inventory vs +5.6% sales at Aug 1, 2026) [investigations/01 row 12]. So the advocate's summary sentence "NA wholesale grew +9% to +24% a quarter while comps ran -7% to +1%. The gap went into retailer inventory" is **not supported for Q1 to Q4 FY26** by the data cited. The fill evidence covers only Nike Q1 FY27 (and May 2026).
- **Foot Locker is not all North America.** Foot Locker's 2024 U.S. sales were $5,328M of $7,971M (67%) [repo: investigations/06 Table D, Foot Locker FY2024 10-K as cited]. Dick's "Foot Locker Business" inventory includes Europe and Asia Pacific, where Nike's shipments book in EMEA and APLA, not NA. Scaling the advocate's range by ~0.70 (U.S. plus an assumed ~3% Canada) gives a Nike NA piece of **~$85M to ~$138M**, or **35% to 56%** of the $245M Q1 FY27 NA wholesale gain, not 50% to 80% [*my arithmetic*; the 0.70 assumes the build is spread like sales].
- **Brand attribution cuts both ways.** The 59% Nike share is from 2024 and fell from 65% in 2023 as Foot Locker diversified brands [Foot Locker FY2024 10-K p. 29 as cited]. The advocate uses adidas NA +17% c.n. to rule out generic mall weakness, but adidas sell-in to the same Foot Locker doors is equally a candidate for part of the inventory build. Nike's on-sale rate at Foot Locker (13.2%) is close to the store average (11.4%) [repo: 16_full_price_recovery], which does not suggest Nike product is disproportionately the aged stock.
- **Partner comps are counted, not weighted.** The list (Foot Locker, JD NA, Shoe Carnival, Academy footwear) is set against "only" DICK'S. By Nike purchase dollars DICK'S is the largest single account (Dick's Inc. Nike purchases ~$3.1B to $3.5B in FY2025, global; Foot Locker U.S. ~$1.9B; Academy largest vendor ~$0.5B) [repo: investigations/06 Table D]. Shoe Carnival's -7.1% comp also sits inside a rebanner, and its Nike share is not shown in the repo. A purchase-weighted sell-through figure would be much closer to flat than "-7% to +1%".
- **Top-3 customer dollars are partly mechanical.** The $772M to $1,173M top-3 increase is a U.S. figure (not NA wholesale), and the share rise from 25% to 29% partly reflects Dick's absorbing Foot Locker, which frees a top-3 slot for a new #3 account [repo: investigations/06 rows 1 to 3; investigations/09 A1, which flags this as a direct contradiction between notes]. It also contains DICK'S, where sell-through is real. It does not isolate fill.
- **Accounting check (opened as search snippet only):** I tested whether a change in inventory method could inflate Foot Locker's carrying value after the deal. Foot Locker used the retail inventory method with LIFO for domestic stock, and Dick's FY2025 10-K (year to Jan 31, 2026) keeps the retail method with LIFO for the Foot Locker segment while DICK'S uses weighted average cost [secondary: search snippets of SEC 10-Ks, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/850209/000143774925009620/floc20241213_10k.htm and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1089063/000108906326000007/dks-20260131.htm; not opened in full]. So a method change is **ruled out as a main explanation**. One residual: purchase accounting resets LIFO layers to fair value at Sept 8, 2025, so the Aug 2025 standalone figure carried any LIFO reserve and the Aug 2026 figure does not; size unknown, probably small given fair value ($1,718M) was close to the prior-year balance.

### Pillar 2: no baseline, an untested alternative, and the partner write-downs are mostly the wrong vintage

- **No pre-2024 baseline.** "0 of 61 AJ1 High, AJ3 and AJ4 releases from 2024 to 2026 discounted within 90 days on nike.com" has no comparison cohort from 2019 to 2023 [repo: DEEP_DIVE Test 2]. If Nike has never discounted retros on its own site within 90 days, the finding shows Nike's long-standing pricing policy, not a recent shift of markdowns onto partners. Likewise, full size runs at day ~30 are historically normal at Foot Locker: AJ1 Black White 2.0 (12 of 13 sizes, day 24), AJ3 Cement Grey (13/13, day 31), AJ5 White Black (13/13, day 22) [repo: DEEP_DIVE Test 3 "History says the same"]. That makes "unsold at full price" weak evidence of anything new in FY26 to FY27.
- **Untested alternative inside Nike's own P&L.** DEEP_DIVE Test 3 notes many 2025 to 2026 retros were taken off nike.com (status INACTIVE or HOLD) rather than discounted. Product pulled from nike.com can be sold through Nike Factory Stores, which are part of NIKE Direct, so the markdown would land on Nike's P&L. The test does not check factory-store prices.
- **Control choice.** The 22 of 22 Pegasus 41 comparison uses a superseded running model that Nike clears by design; it shows Nike discounts outgoing models, not that it shifted retro markdowns.
- **Small sample.** Foot Locker: 6 of 13 sampled retros discounted on one day [repo: DEEP_DIVE Test 2].
- **Write-downs are mostly the wrong vintage.** Dick's $217.9M FY2025 write-down and liquidation was the post-acquisition clean-up of inventory acquired from standalone Foot Locker; Dick's then said Foot Locker inventory was "well-positioned entering fiscal 2026" [Dick's FY2025 10-K p. 8 as quoted in investigations/01]. In Q2 2026, the quarter of the claimed build, the Foot Locker inventory adjustment was only **$2.3M** [Dick's Q2 2026 release p. 13 as quoted]. **Unreconciled figure:** the advocate gives $42.7M for Q1 2026, but investigations/01 gives $40.4M for the whole first half (implying ~$38M in Q1). One of the two is a different measure (possibly write-down plus liquidation margin) and should be checked before use.
- **Reserves are not abnormally low.** Sales-related reserves per dollar of quarterly wholesale in the **same quarter** were 0.18 (Aug 2024, before the clean-up), 0.25 (Aug 2025) and 0.21 (Aug 2026); in dollars $1,200M, $1,788M, $1,488M [investigations/01 T2, 10-Q p. 7 lines as cited; *my arithmetic*]. The current level is still **above** the pre-clean-up baseline, so "a lap that cannot recur" may already have largely recurred. The 0.23 to 0.25 "illustration" uses the two highest FY26 quarters; reverting to the Aug 2024 ratio would *raise* revenue. These balances are also global, and a balance change is not a revenue flow (investigations/09 A7).

### Pillar 4: the sized evidence cannot move FY28 EPS much

- **Price arithmetic checks:** 35.35 / 1.29 = 27.4x; 35.35 / 1.646 = 21.5x; 1.646 / 1.29 = +28%; dividend 1.64 / 35.35 = 4.6%; 1.64 / 1.35 to 1.15 = 121% to 143% [*my arithmetic*; consensus figures from repo consensus_and_valuation, Yahoo, not reopened].
- **Scale.** Take the advocate's own upper range: ~$250M of revenue given back (Foot Locker NA piece plus allowances). At a ~45% gross margin, a ~20% tax rate and ~1.48B diluted shares (my assumption, not checked today), that is about **$0.03 to $0.06 of EPS**, or **2% to 4% of FY28 consensus ($1.65)** [*my arithmetic*]. The separate repo estimate of a $0.1B to $0.3B FY27 hit from Foot Locker and Hibbett [stoic-shannon `us_wholesale_partner_destocking` Section 6] is the same order. FY28 consensus growth of +28% on revenue of only +1.1% is a margin call (tariffs, gross margin, costs), not an NA revenue call, so an NA channel unwind does not attack the main driver of the estimate.
- **Feng Tay is global, not North America.** Feng Tay's -8.8% Q1 FY27 tracks Nike footwear worldwide, including Greater China (-22% in Q1 FY27); it leads the Q2 FY27 that Nike has already guided down [repo: DEEP_DIVE Test 1]. It does not evidence an NA-specific or FY28-specific unwind.
- **Straw comparison.** "Return to Q2 FY24 ($2,902M) would be -18%" is not a forecast anyone holds; Q2 FY24 itself was -8.8%. Nike already guided a ~400 bps (~$497M) Q2 headwind tied to "a higher comparison of North America sell-in activities" [Q1 FY27 remarks PDF p. 13 as cited], against Q2 FY26 NA wholesale that was $648M above Q2 FY24 [investigations/01 row 2]. Most of the Q2 FY26 excess is inside guidance.
- **"Timetable slipped" is partly global.** The FY26 10-K "end of December 2026" sentence also said "North America has made the most progress against these actions, while Greater China and Converse will take more time" (FY26 10-K p. 30, as quoted in zealous-mccarthy note 05_channel_split). The "beyond fiscal 2027" language covers Sportswear and Jordan globally and Greater China [Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 20 as cited]. It is not an NA-specific admission.

### Is it already consensus?

Largely yes. Nike itself said "oversupplying our iconic retro product", "North America will feel the biggest impact", and guided the Q2 sell-in lap; the then-CFO said on the Q4 FY26 call "we didn't sell in up 10%"; consensus FY27 EPS fell 25% in 30 days and short interest rose from 75.6M to 99.2M shares in September (all as cited by the advocate). The only non-consensus piece is **duration into FY28**, and the measured evidence (one Foot Locker quarter, ~$85M to ~$138M of Nike NA product) is too small to carry it. The cross-branch scan already lists "where the markdowns went" as an open lead, which is a sign the idea is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated variant [stoic-shannon `cross_branch_scan_oct13_evening.md` Section 3 item 1].

### What would rescue the thesis

1. Evidence of fill outside Foot Locker in FY26 (JD North America or Hibbett inventory, Academy Nike-specific data), because Foot Locker alone shows no FY26 build.
2. A 2019 to 2023 baseline for nike.com retro markdowns and Foot Locker size runs, plus Nike Factory Store pricing on pulled retros.
3. Dick's Q3 2026 (estimated ~Nov 24, after finals) showing Foot Locker inventory still well above sales with rising write-downs.
4. Drop Pillar 3 as written, or restate it as "FY27 tariffs are unrefunded" (which is in guidance).
