## 1. Thesis in one sentence

Nike now pays a dividend ($2.44B a year) that its free cash flow did not cover in FY2026 (0.91x) and that its own FY2027 guidance does not cover on earnings (payout 121% to 143% of guided adjusted EPS), while S&P has just cut it to A with a negative outlook and expects about $1.2B of annual cash burn; the board has to choose, in the Nov 14 to 20 declaration window that overlaps Investor Day (Nov 16 to 17), between spending scarce cash to extend a 24-year streak or resetting it, and either path forces the market to price a longer earnings trough than the 21.5x multiple on FY2028 consensus ($1.65) assumes.

## 2. Variant perception: what the market believes vs what we believe

**What the market believes (measured, not inferred):**
- **The dividend is under pressure, but as a cash item.** The 4.64% yield is close to the 5.49% yield on Nike's own 2030 notes (REPO note 18, finanzen.net quote). Put-call parity on listed options implies dividends about 48% to 62% below the scheduled $0.41 rate through Jan 2028, though early-exercise bias inflates that gap (REPO note 17, section F3). So the market already prices some cut. I do not claim the market thinks the dividend is safe.
- **The earnings trough is short.** FY2028 consensus EPS is $1.646 and FY2029 is $2.31 (REPO consensus note, Yahoo and Nasdaq/Zacks). Those imply EPS rising 32% and then 40% from the FY2027 guidance midpoint of $1.25 (*my arithmetic*). The stock trades at 21.5x FY2028 and 15.3x FY2029, so the price leans on that recovery.
- **The balance sheet is a backstop.** Net cash of $475M, $11.4B of liquidity and an A rating (REPO note 18) are read as "Nike can afford to wait."

**What we believe:**
- The dividend is not just a cash line. It is the one number that tells the market how long management thinks the trough lasts. Nike needs adjusted EPS of about $1.26 (flat working capital, no restructuring cash) to $1.52 (with -$200M of working capital and about $190M of after-tax Pace cash) to cover the current dividend from operations (REPO note 18, section E). FY2027 is guided at $1.15 to $1.35.
- The recovery that would restore coverage rests on FY2028 to FY2029 targets. This management team and its predecessors have missed every long-dated profit target since 2017, and the FY2028 consensus itself fell 26% in 30 days with 23 cuts and 0 raises (REPO note 19; consensus note).
- Whatever the board does in November, it lowers the floor under the stock. A token raise spends cash S&P wants for debt and Pace, and keeps the downgrade path open. A freeze ends the streak and breaks the "25-year" story that dividend-growth funds hold it for. A cut admits the trough runs past FY2028 and puts FY2028 and FY2029 consensus at risk.

**Why we could be right (mechanism, not mood):** three independent constraints converge on the same $2.44B, and none of them depends on a view about the brand. (1) Operations: FCF below the dividend in FY2026 and on FY2027 guidance. (2) Credit: S&P's negative outlook and its FOCF-to-debt threshold. (3) Calendar: $2.0B of notes due by March 27, 2027 and the dividend decision falling inside Investor Day week.

**Alternatives ruled out (and how):**
- *"FY2026 FCF was depressed by the IEEPA tariff receivable, a timing item."* Tested. Adding back the full $684M receivable gives FCF of about $2,868M, only 1.19x the dividend (REPO note 18). And the $986M IEEPA benefit in FY2026 net income (about $0.52 of EPS) is non-recurring: excluding it, FY2026 payout of EPS was 103% (REPO note 18, Q4 FY26 release p. 1). - *"Q1 FY2027 was hit by one-off severance."* Tested. The $243M FY2026 severance accrual was "substantially all" paid in Q1 (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 17, REPO note 18). - *"Capex is temporarily high."* Ruled out: capex fell from $812M (FY2024) to $684M (FY2026), which helped FCF (REPO note 18).
- *"FCF overstates the problem because SBC is non-cash."* It understates it. SBC was $715M, 33% of FY2026 FCF. With buybacks paused, shares outstanding rose 0.63% from Mar 2025 to Sep 2026 (REPO note 18). Offsetting that dilution at $35.33 would cost about $330M (REPO note 18).

## 5. What would prove us wrong

1. Nike raises the dividend in November **and** Investor Day gives a dated FCF framework that covers the dividend by FY2028 (for example FCF above $2.5B), with the 2027 notes refinanced at a tight spread and S&P moving the outlook back to stable.
2. Q2 FY2027 (Dec 17) shows H1 FCF at or above H1 dividends (about $1.22B), and FY2028 consensus stops falling.
3. Nike announces a deep "reset" cut together with credible long-term targets. Precedents say this kind of cut hurt on the day but was followed by gains at 12 months (VF's second cut +51% vs reaction close; Estee Lauder +35%; Intel 2023 +69%; REPO note 18). A short held past such an event is on the wrong side of the base rate.
4. Insider purchases resume at current prices (Hill, Cook, Denton) before the window closes (about Dec 14) (REPO note 17).
5. Evidence that the North America and China declines are turning faster than guided, lifting EPS above the $1.26 to $1.52 break-even within FY2028.

## 6. Weakest points (stated plainly)

1. **The market already prices part of this.** A 4.64% yield sits near Nike's own bond yields, and options imply roughly half the current dividend through 2027 (with a known bias). If the cut is already in the price, a cut alone may not move the stock down.
2. **The board has a cheap reason to raise in November.** A 25th consecutive raise makes Nike eligible for Dividend Aristocrat status (OPENED articles, Jan 2026). A 1-cent raise costs only about $59M a year (REPO note 18). Note 18's own odds were 45% token raise, 40% freeze, 15% cut for November. The most likely near-term outcome may be a token raise, which is mildly bullish and works against a catalyst-driven short before finals.
3. **The balance sheet is strong.** Net cash, $11.4B of liquidity, no maturities between March 2027 and March 2030, and cash that covers a stress shortfall for about three years (REPO note 18). The dividend breaking is a choice, not a forced event. This is not Kraft Heinz or Walgreens.
4. **Base rates favour longs at an 80% drawdown.** Idiosyncratic episodes bought at the 80% crossing returned a median +52% at 1 year, with 67% positive (n = 30) (REPO note 20). The short case leans on the subset where valuation is higher (EV/sales above 1.0x), which predicts no **full** recovery, not a falling price: that subset's median 1-year return was still +44% (REPO note 20).
5. **The multiple is not high against Nike's own history.** Forward P/E of 27.4x is below the 10-year mean of 33.1x, and EV/EBITDA of 11.2x is half the 10-year mean (REPO consensus note).
6. **Q1 FCF is seasonal,** so "negative in Q1" is weaker evidence than it sounds. The FY2026 full-year 0.91x is the solid figure.
7. **The trade is crowded and sentiment has capitulated.** Short interest is up about 7x since Dec 2023, the median price target ($34.40) is below the price, and 19 of 21 targets were cut after Oct 1 (REPO notes 17 and consensus). A short is not a contrarian call here.
8. **Precedent reactions are confounded.** Every large day move on a cut came with an earnings miss, impairment or withdrawn guidance, so the dividend effect alone cannot be isolated (REPO note 18).
9. **The track-record pillar is about long-dated targets.** The current team's quarterly guidance record is perfect (22 of 22).

### C. Pillars where the data does not support the claim, or fits another explanation

**Pillar 1 ("not a timing artifact") is overstated.** The FY2026 shortfall is a working-capital story, not an earnings-power story:
- FY2026 CFO before working capital was $4,546M ($2,868M plus the $1,678M working-capital drain). Less capex, that is $3,862M, or 1.60x the dividend (*my arithmetic* on REPO note 18 table B). Even stripping roughly $0.75B to $1.0B for the non-recurring IEEPA benefit in net income, pre-working-capital FCF still covers the dividend at roughly 1.2x to 1.3x (*my arithmetic*, approximate because the IEEPA tax effect is not split out).
- So "0.91x" requires roughly $1.0B of non-IEEPA working-capital drain (falling taxes payable, falling payables, accrued liabilities) to repeat. Q1 FY2027's -$846M says it has not stopped yet, but a shrinking business normally *releases* working capital; whether it recurs is the open question, not a settled fact. The pillar should say "uncovered for two reporting periods because of working capital and lower earnings", not "structural".
- **Internal inconsistency on SBC.** The FY2027 bridge adds back about $650M of SBC as a cash source, while the "alternatives ruled out" box argues SBC overstates cash. Excluding SBC, the bridge gives 0.62x to 0.75x coverage (*my arithmetic*), which helps the short but shows the brief picks whichever SBC treatment suits each paragraph. Pick one.
- **Precedent payout comparison mixes bases.** Nike's 121% to 143% is on a guided trough year; Estee Lauder's 102% is trailing actual, VF's is a quarterly run-rate (REPO note 18 section F). Nike itself paid 104% of FCF in FY2020 and kept raising (REPO note 18 table C).

**The variant perception ("market prices a short trough") does not survive the consensus data.**
- FY2028 consensus EPS of $1.646 is only about 4% above FY2026 EPS excluding the IEEPA benefit ($1.58; REPO note 18 section C; *my arithmetic*). FY2028 consensus revenue is $43.6B, up 1.1% and still below FY2026's $46.4B, with an implied EBIT margin of 7.2% versus 12% to 13% in FY2023 to FY2024 (REPO consensus note; REPO note 19).
- That is not a "short trough" assumption; it is a market pricing no revenue recovery and margins near the bottom through FY2028. The "+32% then +40%" growth is measured off a guided trough year and inflated by the source mix above.
- Note 18's own base case already reaches 1.04x coverage in FY2028 *at* consensus. The thesis needs FY2028 EPS to fall below consensus, i.e. it is an earnings-miss thesis wearing a dividend costume; the dividend adds no independent information.

**The catalyst logic is unfalsifiable and mispriced against the short.**
- The brief says a raise, a freeze and a cut are *all* bearish. A thesis in which every outcome confirms it has no test.
- Note 18's probabilities are 45% token raise, 40% freeze, 15% cut (REPO note 18 section G). Options already imply dividends 48% to 62% below $0.41 (REPO note 17 F3; about -25% for Dec 2026 after the basis correction). So about 85% of the probability mass (raise or freeze) is *better* than what options price. On the advocate's own numbers, the base-case November event is a positive surprise.
- Swoosh, LLC collects about $364M a year from the dividend (REPO note 18 section G), and the board raised by 1 cent at a 73% payout in Nov 2025. Incentives favour continuity.

**The credit pillar has already been tested by the market, and the stock went up.** S&P's downgrade hit on Oct 5, 2026. NKE closed $33.96 that day versus $33.87 on Oct 2 (+0.3%) and rose to $35.35 by Oct 12 (+4.1%) (REPO `data/nke_daily_prices.csv`; *my arithmetic*). Reuters reported an intraday dip to about $32.83 (SNIPPET). A further one-notch cut from A to A- leaves Nike solidly investment grade, and revolver ratings changes "would not trigger acceleration" (FY26 10-K p. 46 via REPO note 18). Note 18's verdict is explicit: "Can Nike sustain the dividend? Through FY2029, yes, financially."

**Pillar 4 (holders exit) argues against itself.** Base rates in REPO note 20 give a median +44% 1-year return even in the expensive (EV/sales above 1.0x) subset; the "0 of 9 recovered to 75% of peak" statistic is about full recovery over years, not a 5-week short. SDY at a 1.57% weight is immaterial against a $52.5B market cap (*my arithmetic*), and index removal rules were never checked.

### D. Verdict from this lens

The arithmetic is clean; the inferences are not. The core facts (0.91x FY2026 coverage, 121% to 143% payout of guidance, S&P negative outlook) are real, but (1) the coverage gap is mostly working capital, (2) consensus already prices near-trough FY2028 earnings, so the "short trough" variant perception is not what the data shows, (3) the November event is more likely to beat option-implied expectations than miss them, and (4) the credit catalyst already passed with the stock rising. Best used as a risk factor or a dated test inside another thesis, not as the thesis. Score: 3 out of 10.

## Red team: opposite side

Prepared 2026-10-14 by a reviewer arguing as a holder of the opposite position (long, or at least "do not short this"). Labels follow the advocate's: REPO (repo note, with its own citation), OPENED (page opened in this session), SNIPPET (search summary only), *my arithmetic*. No modeling.

### Verdict in one paragraph

The facts in pillars 1 to 3 are mostly right, but almost all of them are already public and already priced, and the dated catalyst more likely goes against the short before the Nov 19 finals. The one differentiated claim ("the dividend tells you how long the trough lasts") is an interpretation, not a measurable driver, and the evidence for a long trough lives in other theses, not in this one. As a standalone short, this is a consensus trade with a catalyst that probably points the wrong way. Score: 3 out of 10.

### 1. The dividend-cut risk is consensus, not a variant view

| Evidence | What it shows | Source |

The advocate concedes most of this in weakest points 1 and 7. The opposite-side conclusion is stronger: if the market already prices about half the dividend away, then a **token raise** (note 18's most likely outcome, 45%) or even a **freeze** (40%) is a positive surprise versus what options imply. Only the cut path (15%) is clearly negative, and on the precedent data a telegraphed cut barely moves the stock (below).

### 2. The catalyst is more likely to help a long before finals

- Management said on the Oct 1 call that the dividend "remains a priority" (TIKR, Oct 5, 2026, OPENED; secondary paraphrase, not the transcript). A cut seven weeks after that statement, in the same week as an Investor Day meant to rebuild credibility, would be unusual.
- Swoosh, LLC (Knight family) owns 221.75M Class A shares, elects three-quarters of the board and receives about $364M a year at $1.64 (REPO note 18, 2026 proxy p. 70; note 17). It has sold no shares. The controlling holder's incentive runs toward continuity.
- A 1-cent raise costs about $59M a year (REPO note 18), 0.7% of the $8.4B cash pile, and makes Nike eligible for the S&P 500 Dividend Aristocrats at 25 years (advocate's own OPENED sources). That is a cheap, positive headline days before finals.
- Telegraphed cuts did not move stocks: VF's first cut -0.5%, Intel 2023 -2.3% on the day. The large drops (-14% to -28%) came with surprise earnings or guidance news on the same day (REPO note 18, F). Nike's risk is now as telegraphed as it gets. Deep "reset" cuts were followed by +35% to +69% at 12 months vs the reaction close (VF second cut, Estee Lauder, Intel 2023).

### 3. The cash shortfall is mostly working capital and tariffs, both of which the thesis does not decompose

- **FY2026 0.91x is a working-capital number.** FY2026 working capital was -$1,678M, against +$716M in FY2024 (REPO note 18, FY26 10-K p. 58). Of that, $684M was the IEEPA receivable collected in Q1 FY27. Inventories were flat (-$31M), so the inventory clean-up is done; the rest is payables and taxes falling as purchases shrank. *My arithmetic:* FY26 net income ex the IEEPA benefit (about $0.52 x 1.49B shares = about $0.78B after tax) is about $2.33B; plus $1,438M of non-cash items less $684M capex gives about $3.09B with flat working capital, or about **1.28x** the $2,407M dividend. Working capital does not drain $1B a year forever: it drains while revenue falls and reverses when it stops falling.
- **FY2025 coverage was 1.42x** ($3,268M FCF vs $2,300M), one year earlier (REPO note 18). One bad year, half explained by working capital, is not a trend line.
- **EPS payout overstates the cash problem.** D&A (about $760M) roughly equals capex (about $750M), so cash earnings before working capital are about adjusted net income plus SBC (about $650M, or about $0.44 a share). The 121% to 143% "payout" therefore maps to roughly 0.9x to 1.0x cash coverage before Pace and working capital. The advocate's own FY2027 arithmetic (0.89x to 1.01x) confirms that the dividend is about break-even on guidance, not deeply uncovered. The dilution argument (SBC shares rose 0.63% in 18 months, REPO note 18) is real but costs about $330M once, not $2.4B a year.
- **Tariffs are the largest single EPS drag, and they are lapping.** FY2026 North America tariff cost was about $1,011M (range $920M to $1,111M) (REPO zealous-mccarthy `investigations/05`). On a no-new-tariff basis NA gross margin was +100 to +190 bps vs FY2024 (same note). Management said Q1 FY27 would be "the final quarter where higher tariffs continue to be a material year-over-year headwind to gross margin" (SNIPPET, web search summary of pre-Q1 coverage; not opened). Q1 FY27 gross margin already rose 60 bps to 42.8% (REPO, Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 19, 23). The FY2028 consensus of +28% EPS growth on +1.1% revenue (REPO consensus note) is mostly a margin recovery against a tariff-inflated FY2027 base, plus Pace savings after the $243M of severance already paid. The thesis treats the FY2028 to FY2029 rebound as one more "multi-year target Nike will miss" without sizing how much of it is mechanical cost lapping.
- **Nike's own precedent.** In FY2020 dividends were 104% of FCF ($1,452M vs $1,399M). The board raised 12% in Nov 2020, and FY2021 coverage was back to 27% (REPO note 18, table C). A 1.1x shortfall at Nike has happened before without a cut. (Caveat: FY2020 was a COVID shock with a fast rebound; the analogy is about board behaviour, not about demand.)

### 4. The balance sheet removes any forcing event inside the competition window

- Net cash $475M, $8.4B cash and STI, $3.0B undrawn revolvers, no maturities from Mar 2027 to Mar 2030 (REPO note 18). Note 18's own verdict: "Through FY2029, yes, financially" the dividend is sustainable, even in the stress case.
- Refinancing both 2027 notes at about 5.5% costs about $59M a year pre-tax, about $0.03 of EPS (REPO note 18).
- S&P's "$1.2B cash burn a year" is after dividends, which means S&P itself models the dividend continuing (REPO note 18, section E reading). The rating agency's base case is not a cut.
- An A-rated issuer at 1.73x gross debt/EBITDA (REPO note 18) is not Kraft Heinz, VF or Walgreens, which cut to deleverage. The advocate concedes this (weakest point 3).

### 5. The "income holders exit" pillar is not supported by the flow data

- The exits cited (Pershing, Viking, Jennison, Fundsmith, Janus Henderson, Longview) are quality and growth managers, and they already left between Q1 2025 and Q2 2026 (REPO note 17). That selling is in the price; it is not a future flow from a dividend decision.
- The buyers are value and income-tolerant money: Capital Group doubled from 40.2M to 84.9M shares (7.1% of Class B), Oakmark built 14.4M, Sixth Street opened 12.4M (REPO note 17). These holders bought a cut risk, not a streak.
- The only identified dividend-index holder is SDY at a 1.57% weight (REPO note 18, secondary). Index rules were not opened. The advocate concedes forced selling is "probably too small to matter."

### 6. Valuation does not give a short room

- 27.4x forward vs a 10-year mean of 33.1x; EV/EBITDA 11.2x vs 22.3x (REPO consensus_and_valuation). The candidate summary's "high multiple on depressed earnings" is wrong against Nike's own history; the advocate's corrections section says so, but the summary sentence still uses it.
- Base rates: company-specific 80% drawdowns returned a median +52% at 1 year with 67% positive; even the EV/sales above 1.0x subset had a median +44% (REPO note 20). The 0 of 9 statistic is about not reaching 75% of peak, not about falling further.
- The $26.9 "illustration" holds the 21.5x multiple fixed while cutting EPS to trough, which is the opposite of how trough multiples behave (P/E tends to expand when EPS is depressed). It is not evidence of downside.

### 7. Factual and framing errors to fix before any slide

1. "Missed every multi-year target since 2017" (candidate summary) is wrong as written: the 75% international and 30% digital targets were met (REPO note 19). The advocate's correction ("every profit target set in 2017 and 2021") should replace it everywhere.
2. "High multiple" (candidate summary) is false against Nike's 10-year averages (see 6).
3. "Negative FCF in Q1 FY27" is seasonal: Q1 FY26 was +$15M. Presenting it as a deterioration signal overstates it.
4. Pillar 4 uses quality and growth fund exits as evidence that **income** holders are exposed. Those are different holders, and they have already sold.
5. The source table labels the capital.com page "OPENED (search result)". A search result is a SNIPPET, not an opened page. Re-label or open it.
6. "Payout above every precedent" compares Nike's guided-adjusted-EPS payout with precedents' trailing or guided EPS on mixed bases (REPO note 18, F: some trailing adjusted, some guide). The comparison is directionally fair but not like for like.

### 8. Is anything here genuinely differentiated?

Only one thing: the claim that the November board decision plus Investor Day is the first **dated** read on how long management thinks the trough lasts. That is useful as a **test** inside whichever thesis the team picks (long or short), not as a thesis on its own. The advocate's own self-assessment (4/10, "best not as a standalone short") is, if anything, generous.
