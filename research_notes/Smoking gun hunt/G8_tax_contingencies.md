# G8: Tax rate, EU state aid, IEEPA refund and other contingencies (smoking-gun hunt)

Research date: 2026-10-15. Question: FY28 EPS depends on a tax rate and on contingencies nobody models. Tags: OPENED = read in the primary document; SNIPPET = seen only in a search result or secondary page; DERIVED = my arithmetic from stated inputs. Filings were downloaded from the investors.nike.com mirror (never www.sec.gov). Pages are printed pages ("FY26 10-K p. 74"). Data: `G8_tax_series.csv` (annual FY2015 to FY2026), `G8_tax_series_quarterly.csv`, `G8_tax_scenarios.csv` (54 scenario rows), regenerated by `G8_tax_scenarios.py`. Overlap: `Nike decline root cause analysis/effective_tax_rate_drift.md` (decline attribution) and `Deep dives Oct 14/D1_fy28_bridge.md` (FY28 bridge); this note extends both and corrects one argument in the first (section 3.4).

## 1. Bottom line

1. **No smoking gun in the tax rate.** Consensus FY28 already embeds about 25% (D1: 25.2% implied) and Nike guides "mid-20 percent range" (Q1 FY27 8-K Ex. 99.1, p. 1 of exhibit, OPENED). At that rate there is no EPS gap from tax. The tax risk to a short is to the upside: each point of ETR below 25% is worth +$0.022 on consensus pretax ($3,234M) or +$0.016 on D1 Base pretax ($2,323M) (DERIVED). Reverting to the FY2019 to FY2024 mean of 14.1% would add +$0.24 (consensus) or +$0.17 (Base). The filings give mechanical reasons it cannot fully revert (section 3.3).
2. **The one unmodeled, quantifiable contingency is not the Dutch state aid case. It is the Belgian customs claim.** Press reports (De Tijd, Sep 2024, SNIPPET via TaxLive and RTV) put the Belgian duty and VAT claim at EUR 1.5 billion. Nike's 10-K discloses the claim but gives no amount, says it "is unable to estimate the range of loss" and carries no stated accrual (FY26 10-K p. 88, OPENED). On my split (section 5) the duty part is EUR 0.39B to 0.61B ($0.45B to $0.71B pretax, $0.23 to $0.36 per share after tax) if the VAT is recoverable, and up to $1.06 to $1.08 per share if the VAT is not. It is a one-time charge in the year it is booked, with a possible recurring duty cost of $0.03 to $0.05 per share per year (DERIVED). The trial was set for 26 February 2026; I could not find the outcome.
3. **EU state aid (SA.51284) is dormant, not decided.** The Commission's register shows one decision only, the January 2019 opening decision; the only court case is T-648/19, which the General Court dismissed on 14 July 2021. No final decision, no recovery order, no Court of Justice appeal is registered (section 2). Amount at stake is not public. Scenario sizing: each 1 point of excess Dutch margin over the periods the rulings ran is about $0.27 per share one-time (no US offset), or $0.02 per share per year if prospective (DERIVED).
4. **IEEPA refund reversal risk is close to nil.** The $684M receivable was substantially all collected after May 31, 2026 (FY26 10-K p. 30, OPENED), and accounts receivable fell $695M in Q1 FY27 (10-Q p. 4). The cost is not reversal but the tax on it (about $231M, DERIVED) and the FY27 EPS comparison, not FY28.
5. **A pattern worth one question to Nike: the FY27 tax guide moved from "low 20% range" (June 2026 call, p. 13) to "mid-20 percent range" (Oct 1 release) in 3.5 months with no stated reason.** Implied Q2 to Q4 FY27 rate is 25% to 28% against 19.6% in Q4 FY26 (section 3.2). The filings cannot separate the candidate causes (section 3.4).

## 2. EU state aid investigation (item 1)

**What it is (OPENED, opening decision PDF from the Commission register).** Commission decision C(2019) 6 of 10 January 2019 (register adoption date 9 January) opened the formal procedure on five Dutch advance pricing agreements: Nike European Operations Netherlands BV (NEON) 2006, 2010, 2015 and Converse Netherlands BV 2010, 2015. The Commission's provisional concern: the royalties NEON and Converse Netherlands pay to Dutch-resident but tax-transparent Nike entities (NIL Bermuda and NI CV hold the IP) "appear to be higher than what independent companies negotiating on market terms would have agreed," so the Dutch tax base was too low. NEON was left taxable on only a "[2-5%]" operating margin on revenue (redacted bracket). NEON revenue was EUR 3.54B (FY07) and EUR 8.40B (FY16); royalty to NIL / NI CV was "[8-23%]" of revenue; NEON EBIT per Table 1 was EUR 992M in FY16. Amount of aid: not stated; the letter contains no recovery figure.

**Sizing (DERIVED; assumptions in `G8_tax_scenarios.py`).** Revenue base = NEON net turnover FY07 to FY16 (EUR 49.1B, opening decision Table 1, x 1.30 USD/EUR assumed = $63.8B) plus reported EMEA revenue FY17 to FY22 ($60.3B) = $124.1B; through FY26 $176.0B. Dutch rate 25%. Interest adds 30% of principal (assumption). Excess margin = arm's-length margin minus the ruled "[2-5%]"; unknown, so scenarios: 1 point on $124.1B = $310M tax, $403M with interest = $0.27 per share (no US offset); 2 points $0.54; 3 points $0.81; 5 points $1.36. A 50% US foreign-tax-credit offset halves each. Recurring: 1 point on FY28 EMEA revenue ($12.6B) = $31M = $0.021 per share. Nike's Dutch cash taxes were only $77M in FY26 (10-K p. 75), so a 1-point excess margin ($310M) equals about 4 years of current Dutch cash tax. Note a data tension I cannot resolve: NEON's Table 1 EBIT margin was 11.8% in FY16 while the ruling base is 2 to 5%, so the taxed profit definition differs from NEON's reported EBIT.

### 3.2 What the FY27 guide implies

Q1 FY27 tax was $209M on $921M (22.7%). If the full year is 25% of GAAP pretax of about $2,178M (D1 adjusted pretax $2,475M less $297M Pace), Q2 to Q4 must run 26.7%; if 25% applies to adjusted pretax, 26.4%; at the 24% to 26% edges, 25.0% to 27.9% (DERIVED). The last five reported quarters were 20.7, 20.0, 19.6 (Q4 FY26), 22.7 (Q1 FY27). So the guide needs a step of about 4 to 7 points in the remaining quarters.

### 3.3 Why a reversion to 14% is blocked by the filings (DERIVED from the reconciliations)

FY2019 to FY2024 mean line items versus FY2026: stock comp -3.6 pts average versus +1.1 now (a 4.7-point swing; it follows the share price: awards vest below grant value); FDII -4.5 average versus -2.9; FY22's one-time Subpart F deferred benefit -4.7 and FY24's one-time foreign tax credit relief and FY25's $133M Section 987 benefit (-3.4) will not repeat (FY26 10-K p. 72 to 73). Stock comp alone is worth +$0.10 per share on consensus pretax if the swing fully reversed (4.7 pts x $0.0217). Mix also moved: US share of pretax went from 12% (FY19) to 69% (FY26).

## 4. IEEPA tariff refund (item 4)

- Facts (OPENED): Supreme Court ruling 20 Feb 2026; Nike deemed recovery probable in Q4 FY26 and booked $986M in cost of sales (NA $965M, Converse $21M); received $302M by 31 May; $684M receivable in accounts receivable; "Subsequent to May 31, 2026, we received substantially all of the remaining IEEPA tariff receivable" (FY26 10-K p. 30 and p. 61). Q1 FY27 balance sheet: accounts receivable $5,242M versus $5,931M; cash flow shows a $695M decrease (10-Q p. 3, 4).
- Tax treatment (DERIVED): no disclosure. US income without FDII, so about 23.4% (21% federal plus 2.4% state per the FY26 reconciliation) = $231M of FY26 tax expense; FY26 ETR excluding the benefit is 19.3% ((792 - 231) / (3,900 - 986)), so the benefit pushed reported FY26 ETR up by about 1 point. Cash tax falls due in FY27. After-tax benefit $0.51 per share, which D1 labels $0.52.
- Reversal risk: ruled out. Alternatives considered: clawback by Customs (no filing language; cash is in hand), litigation reopening (the ruling is final), interest income (not disclosed). The 10-K says only that Nike "will continue to monitor developments ... pertaining to tariff refunds and litigation." No Q1 FY27 mention of IEEPA in the 10-Q. The FY27 and FY28 issue is the replacement tariff rate (D1 and `risk_tariffs.md`), not the refund.

## 5. Other contingencies (item 5; FY26 10-K Note 16, p. 88)

- **Belgian customs claim** (first disclosed FY23 10-K p. 88; wording unchanged FY23 to Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 17): "claims for certain years from Belgian Customs ... for alleged underpaid duties related to products imported beginning in fiscal 2018 ... unable to estimate the range of loss ... could have a material adverse effect." Bank guarantees issued to appeal.
  - Outside the filing (SNIPPET, De Tijd 14 to 18 Sep 2024 via TaxLive, RTV, Made In, RetailDetail): EUR 1.5B total; customs duties plus VAT claimed by customs and the Special Tax Inspectorate (BBI); three Nike companies linked to the Laakdal logistics centre; dispute is "first sale" versus "last sale" valuation (the EU Union Customs Code of 2016 moved the basis to last sale); Nike "is fighting them"; merits trial set for 26 February 2026 in the Limburg court of first instance. No split between duties, VAT and penalties was published.
  - Consistency check (DERIVED): Nike's fiscal 2018 begins 1 Jun 2017, matching the end of the first-sale transition; bank guarantees and letters of credit rose from $289M (FY22) to $588M (FY23, the year the claim first appears and "legal matters" is added to the purpose list), $768M, $884M and about $1.3B (FY26 10-K p. 88). Not separable from real estate guarantees, so it is correlation only.
  - Sizing (DERIVED, `G8_tax_scenarios.csv`): total = duty + 21% VAT on (value gap + duty). At a 12% blended duty rate the undervalued customs value is EUR 4.2B (about EUR 0.6B a year over seven fiscal years, about 11% of EMEA product cost; plausible for a first-sale gap) with duty EUR 0.51B and VAT EUR 0.99B. One-time EPS: duty only, VAT recoverable -$0.23 (8% rate) / -$0.29 (12%) / -$0.36 (17%); VAT treated as lost -$1.04 to -$1.08. Penalties and interest excluded. Recurring if Nike must stop using first-sale values: -$0.03 / -$0.04 / -$0.05 per year. Whether Nike already moved to last-sale values is not disclosed.
  - Status: trial outcome unknown. Routes tried: 10-K FY23 to FY26 (worked, no amount), Q1 FY27 10-Q (same text), Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 call transcripts (no mention), six web searches in English and Dutch (nothing after Sep 2024), Flemish parliament angle (nothing), Belgian court judgment portal (Juportal) not tried, it needs a case number. Six routes tried, none found a 2026 outcome. What would settle it: the Hasselt court judgment or a Nike 10-Q Note 16 change in the Q2 FY27 10-Q (due about Jan 2027).
- **Section 301 and other customs**: no mention in the FY26 10-K (searched "Section 301", "Section 232", "Section 122": zero hits). Risk factors cover tariffs generally.
- **Transfer pricing**: IRS "certain transfer pricing adjustments" excepted from closed years (every 10-K since FY18); resolution for FY17 to FY19 largely prepaid (section 3.5).
- **General**: "customs, tax, intellectual property" legal proceedings accrued only when probable and estimable (Note 16). Bank guarantees and letters of credit about $1.3B.

## 6. Smoking gun verdict

- Tax rate: **No.** Consensus and guide agree (about 25%). Direction of risk is to the upside for EPS.
- Pillar Two: **No.** Already in force since 1 Jun 2024 with no material effect; a 1 to 3 point add-on costs $0.02 to $0.07, which is smaller than the $0.09 to $0.19 filing-based FY28 gap in D1 only at the high end.
- State aid: **No** for FY28 as a base case (dormant, no amount, no accrual); **tail risk** of $0.27 per excess point one-time.
- Belgian customs: **Not a smoking gun for recurring FY28 EPS, but the largest unmodeled contingency found.** A $0.23 to $0.36 one-time item (duty only) against FY28 consensus $1.646 is 14% to 22%, if booked in FY28; it is dated to a trial that may already have happened, and nothing in Nike's filings or guidance addresses it.
