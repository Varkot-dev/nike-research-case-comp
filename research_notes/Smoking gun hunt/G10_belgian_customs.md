## 1. Bottom line

1. **There is no judgment and no appeal yet.** The case did open at the Hasselt court on Thursday 26 Feb 2026 (Belga dispatch dated 26 Feb 2026 05:34, OPENED via La Libre; it reports the opening, not the outcome). The merits are now on the Hasselt court agenda for **30 November 2026**: TV Limburg, 28 Aug 2026, "Op 30 november staat een geschil tussen sportgigant Nike en de Belgische douane en fiscus op de agenda. Inzet: een duizelingwekkende 1,5 miljard euro" (OPENED, press). So the date G8 gave (26 Feb 2026) slipped by about nine months, and 30 Nov 2026 is the last day of Nike's fiscal Q2 FY27 (quarter ends 30 Nov). The reason for the move is not published (section 4).
2. **The amount is still EUR 1.5B, press only, and no split between duties, VAT and penalties has ever been published.** Press says the total contains "een amende en achterstallige betalingen" (a fine and arrears) in the tax-inspectorate part (Gondola, repeating De Tijd, OPENED; RTV 14 Sep 2024 says no breakdown was given). Belga on 26 Feb 2026 calls the whole EUR 1.5B "arriérés de droits de douane" while the 2024 coverage says "invoerrechten en btw". The sources disagree on the composition, so the split in section 6 stays a DERIVED assumption.
3. **Nike's filings never changed their substance.** No accrual, no amount, no range, in every 10-K and 10-Q from the FY22 10-K to the Q1 FY27 10-Q (filed 2 Oct 2026, after the TV Limburg item). The wording did change in three small ways (section 5). The Nike Retail B.V. statutory accounts, signed 27 Jan 2026, repeat the same paragraph and carry no provision.
4. **Sizing (DERIVED, press EUR 1.5B, G8 method):** a one-time hit of **$0.23 to $0.36 per share after tax** if the loss is duty only and the VAT is recovered (14% to 22% of FY28 consensus EPS $1.646); $0.51 to $0.73 if 25% to 50% of the EUR 1.5B is non-deductible fines and interest; **$1.04 to $1.08** if the VAT cannot be recovered; **$1.16 ceiling** (all EUR 1.5B expensed, no tax shield). Cash out in any losing case is up to about **$1.7B** (19% of the $9.0B of cash and short-term investments at 31 May 2026), against bank guarantees and letters of credit of about $1.3B in total (all purposes). Recurring cost if Nike must stop using first-sale values: $0.03 to $0.05 per year.
5. **Smoking gun for recurring FY28 EPS: no.** It is a one-time, dated, unreserved event risk. It is a catalyst for the short only if the judgment lands inside FY28 (1 Jun 2027 to 31 May 2028) and Nike books it there. No peer faces the same first-sale claim (section 7).

## 3. The claim and the legal issue

- **Amount (press):** EUR 1.5B in total; the claimants are Customs and the BBI (tax). Split unknown. Belgian authorities treat three Dutch-registered Nike companies as debtors (Made in, Belga); Belga places all three in Hilversum, which is where NRBV sits (OPENED, NRBV reports). I could not identify the other two by name.
- **Legal issue (press plus UCC commentary):** goods move from an Asian factory to a Nike entity in Singapore (first sale), then to the Dutch distributor (last sale) and then enter the EU at Laakdal. Nike declared customs value on the lower first sale; Customs says the value must be the last sale before entry (UCC Implementing Regulation Article 128). Import VAT is computed on customs value plus duty, so the same undervaluation produces the VAT claim (DERIVED, G8 formula).
- **What EU law says (SNIPPET):** commentary cited Article 128 as having eliminated the "first sale for export" approach. The CJEU's Massimo Dutti (Inditex) judgment, C-500/24, reported as 30 Oct 2025, is the closest recent authority: under the old Community Customs Code (Article 147 IA) an earlier sale counts only if the EU is "undeniably" the destination when that sale is made; the importer failed that test. Law-firm commentary notes the issue "remains contested under the UCC" (Reed Smith, 3 Nov 2025, OPENED). The judgment itself I could not open (EUR-Lex blocked, section 9). Data cannot turn this into a win probability; the one structural point is that the burden sits on the importer and the UCC text is against first sale.
- **No Nike-specific legal commentary found.** No Loyens, Tiberghien, Deloitte, PwC or Baker McKenzie piece names the case. The only analysis is De Tijd, 16 Sep 2024, "Na claim van 1,5 miljard tegen Nike: Europese douanewetgeving lokt fiscale discussies uit" (headline SNIPPET, paywalled) and RetailDetail, 18 Sep 2024 (OPENED), which says the CJEU "has been asked to clarify" the interpretation.

## 4. Court status and appeal

- **Ruling: none.** Nothing on Juportal, the court, the press or Nike's filings reports a judgment. The 30 Nov 2026 hearing has not happened yet (today is 15 Oct 2026).
- **Appeal: the appeal that exists is the administrative one** (Nike "has engaged in the appellate process" since Q1 FY23, i.e. before the court case), and bank guarantees were issued "in order to appeal the claims". A first-instance loss would be appealable (Antwerp Court of Appeal is my understanding of the district; unverified). No timetable for appeal exists.
- **Why 26 Feb 2026 became 30 Nov 2026:** not published. Surviving explanations: (a) 26 Feb was an opening or case-management hearing (Belga calls it the trial's opening) and 30 Nov is the pleading hearing after written submissions; (b) a postponement requested by a party; (c) a court calendar effect. No source separates them. What would: the Hasselt court's roll entry (griffie, Limburg division) or a Belga dispatch from 26 Feb 2026 reporting the court's order. Not obtainable online.
- **When could a judgment land?** Unknown; I did not verify Belgian rules on delivery after pleadings. If pleadings are done on 30 Nov and judgment follows within months, Nike's fiscal Q3 or Q4 FY27 (Dec 2026 to May 2027) is likelier than FY28; if pleadings run over several sessions or the court orders a CJEU reference, FY28 or later. A CJEU reference would push the hit out by roughly the length of a preliminary ruling (typically well over a year; general knowledge, unverified).

Constant in all of them: "unable to estimate the range of loss and cannot predict the final outcome as it could take several years to reach a resolution"; "fines, penalties and other consequences ... could have a material adverse effect." The Q1 FY27 10-Q also says management does not expect any pending matter to have a material adverse impact "except as described below", and the Belgian paragraph is the first one below. Item 3 of the 10-K says no material pending proceedings and points to Note 16.

- **What changed and what it does not tell us:** the FY23 addition shows the claim moved from plan to appeal to guaranteed appeal within 15 months of first disclosure. "And other government authorities" (Oct 2023 to Jan 2025) lines up with the BBI (VAT) side of the press story; its removal in Apr 2025 has no stated reason. No filing uses "unfavorable", "ruling", "judgment" or "hearing" for this matter (searched all 18 filings plus FY21 and Q2 and Q3 FY22: only the paragraph above). Nothing in the Q1 FY27 10-Q (2 Oct 2026) mentions the 30 Nov hearing.
- **Restricted cash:** none. "Restricted cash" returns 0 hits in the FY23 and FY26 10-Ks and the Q1 FY27 10-Q, so any collateral is not shown as a separate balance (the guarantees are bank-issued and not necessarily cash-backed).
- **Guarantee series (DERIVED, correlation only):** $289M, $588M, $768M, $884M, about $1.3B. The increase since FY22 is about $1.0B. The purposes list is "real estate agreements, self-insurance programs, other general business obligations and legal matters" so the Belgian share is not separable. Alternatives for the rise, none ruled out: Belgian customs collateral; more store leases (NRBV operating lease commitments EUR 725M to EUR 752M FY24 to FY25, a small change, OPENED); self-insurance; other legal matters; FY26 US customs bonds during the tariff period (the FY26 step of about $0.4B coincides with it). What would decide: Nike's own breakdown, or the guarantee register at the Belgian customs office. Not public.
- **Where the guarantees sit (OPENED, NRBV statutory accounts):** NRBV "extended guarantees to banks" EUR 137.5M (May 2022), EUR 218.6M (May 2023), EUR 233.0M (May 2024), EUR 239.4M (May 2025). At about 1.07 to 1.13 USD per EUR (ASSUMPTION) that is roughly $0.15B, $0.23B, $0.25B, $0.27B, which is 51%, 40%, 33% and 31% of the group figure. NRBV took about 29% of the FY23 increase and under 20% of the FY24 and FY25 increases. So most of the increase is held outside NRBV (other Nike entities or the parent). Nothing in NRBV is labelled Belgian.
- **Subsidiary accounts:** NRBV (Hilversum, filed at Companies House as UK establishment FC019114; Dutch KvK copy not accessed) reproduces the Nike paragraph word for word in its note 19 (FY23, FY24, FY25 reports), has no provision line on its balance sheet (FY24: non-current liabilities EUR 10.5M, current liabilities EUR 1,149M with no provision caption), and its auditor (PwC) opinion is not qualified on this. FY25 figures: revenue EUR 10,977M, net result EUR 426M, equity EUR 3,561M. The EUR 1.5B press claim is 42% of NRBV equity and 3.5 times its FY25 net result (DERIVED); it would not be absorbed without a visible hit. Plausibility check (DERIVED): the 12% duty case implies EUR 603M a year of undervalued customs value over 7 years, 9.8% of NRBV's FY24 cost of inventories expensed (EUR 6,165.8M); a first-sale gap of that size is plausible, but no filing states the actual transfer-price markup.

- **Cash:** $1.725B is 19.2% of the $9.0B cash and short-term investments at 31 May 2026 (FY26 10-K, OPENED), and 75% of it could in principle be funded by the $1.3B of guarantees if all of them were Belgian (they are not). Share-count equivalent: $1.16 per share. The cash goes out only after the appeals are exhausted if the guarantees keep enforcement suspended (inference from the 10-K sentence, not stated).
- **Which case is most likely?** Data cannot say. The VAT question is the biggest swing ($0.23 to $0.36 versus $1.04 to $1.08): VAT paid on import is normally deductible by a VAT-registered importer, so case B (cash timing only) is the base; I have not verified the Belgian deduction rules for an assessment made after the fact, nor whether penalties apply. What would settle: the split of the EUR 1.5B in the court file.
- **Timing against FY28:** the hit is booked when a loss becomes probable and estimable (Note 16 policy, FY26 10-K). If that is a first-instance judgment before 31 May 2027 it lands in FY27, outside FY28 EPS and outside the consensus year, though it would reset the base. If it lands from 1 Jun 2027, it hits FY28 GAAP EPS. Whether sell-side FY28 consensus excludes one-time items is not verified.
- **Recurring tail** applies only if Nike still values on first sale. Whether Nike moved to last-sale values after 2018 is not disclosed ("beginning in fiscal 2018" and "certain years" suggests claims for several years, not a closed period).

## 8. Explanations tested

- **"Nike's FY27 and FY28 earnings are hurt by this":** only if booked in FY28 (section 6). Ruled out as a recurring driver unless Nike still uses first-sale values, which is undisclosed.
- **"The claim is already reserved":** ruled out. NRBV balance sheet has no provision line; Nike 10-K says no range can be estimated; no accrual language.
- **"Trial outcome is known":** ruled out. No judgment found in any of 8 route families; the hearing is 30 Nov 2026.
- **"The 2026 date was kept":** ruled out by TV Limburg (28 Aug 2026), which lists 30 Nov.
- **"The EUR 1.5B is duties only":** not ruled out (Belga 2026) or in (De Tijd 2024: duties plus VAT; fine inside the tax part). Survives; the split drives a four-fold EPS range.
