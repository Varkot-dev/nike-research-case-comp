# G4: Litigation, confidential witnesses and sworn testimony (smoking-gun hunt, as of Oct 15, 2026)

**Question.** Do Nike's securities, derivative, consumer or other court cases contain dated, quantitative factual allegations (confidential witnesses, exhibits, testimony) about demand, inventory, discounting, channel practices or China that the SEC filings do not, and that would change the FY28 short thesis?

**Answer in one line.** No smoking gun. The only CW-bearing complaint (the securities class action) is about the 2021 to 2024 DTC strategy, its allegations are unproven, a federal judge threw out 78 of 79 challenged statements, and the one fact pattern that touches inventory (a "Foot Locker took dead inventory in late 2022" allegation) is directionally consistent with Nike's own FY23 numbers but cannot be separated from ordinary restocking with public data. There is no CW account of China demand at all. No deposition or trial testimony about demand, inventory or China is public. Everything below is an ALLEGATION unless it is a filing fact, a court finding, or labelled DERIVED.

Tags: **OPENED** = I opened the document itself and read the text. **SNIPPET** = search result or secondary summary only, not verified. **DERIVED** = my arithmetic from OPENED inputs.

## 2. Court findings on the securities case (ECF 85, Mar 31, 2026; OPENED, 56 pp, govinfo.gov and RECAP copies agree)

- **CWs were held reliable "at this stage"** (Op. 44 to 45): the complaint gave titles, tenure and basis of knowledge. The court did not make factual findings; it assumed the allegations true.
- **Falsity adequately pleaded for 7 of 79 statements** (Nos. 19, 51, 53, 59, 71, 73, 78): "pace of innovation has not slowed" (Oct 6, 2021, Parker), "number one cool and number one favorite brand in North America" (Sept 29, 2022, Friend), "very strong innovation pipeline" (Sept 29, 2022 and Mar 21, 2023), "marketplace strategy remains the same" (Sept 2023, Donahoe), "innovation pipeline is strong" (Sept 28, 2023), and the July 25, 2024 10-K brand-image risk factor (Op. 36 to 42). The court's own dates for Statement 71 and 73 are internally inconsistent (Sept 12 vs Sept 29 for No. 71; Sept 28 vs Sept 29 for No. 73); I follow the conclusion page (Sept 28, 2023 for No. 73).
- **Falsity NOT adequately pleaded** for: "CDA is working" (puffery and opinion, Op. 28 to 29); supply chain statements (plaintiffs did show Nike had no separate DTC supply chain, but Nike never promised one, Op. 30 to 31); technology and Adobe (Op. 31 to 34); product organization (Op. 37); the "no signs of any softness" demand statement of Sept 2022 because Direct and Digital revenue were rising then (Op. 39); the 10-K risk factors other than July 2024 (Op. 40 to 41).
- **Scienter adequately pleaded for only Statement 73** (Op. 48, 55), on Donahoe's own April 12, 2024 words that Nike had been "ruthlessly focused on rebuilding our disruptive innovation pipeline" "over the last year." The court held the CW-based knowledge allegations (CW-1 audit reports and the 2023 VP summit, CW-4 meetings, Foot Locker deal) too vague, attenuated or non-defendant opinion (Op. 47 to 51). On the Foot Locker allegation specifically it said knowing about excess inventory "does not mean he knew NIKE's marketplace strategy had changed" and that Foot Locker "may well have been" one of the strategic partners (Op. 47 to 48).
- **Alternative inference (Op. 55):** "Revenue suggests that CDA worked for a time" and then faltered. This is the court's reading, not a finding of fact, but it matches the filings (NIKE Direct share of brand revenue 28% in FY17 to 44% in FY23 and FY24, per SAC ¶81 as recited in Op. 8).
- The court read the surviving claim narrowly: it concerns the innovation pipeline, not inventory, discounting, channel stuffing or China.

**What this means for the pitch.** Treat the CW material as color the court allowed to stand at the pleading stage, never as established fact. The court's ruling also undercuts any "management lied about demand" framing: the demand statement was held not actionable.

## 8. Smoking gun? **NO.**

Reasons, with the rule-outs:
1. **Not a demand disclosure the market lacked.** Nike's own filings disclosed the FY23 inventory build (+44.2% YoY), "liquidate excess inventory," and the Greater China obsolescence reserve in 2022. The CW rows 1 and 2 add color (named retailers, a coercion claim) not new quantities.
2. **Allegations only.** Nothing is sworn, tested or adjudicated; the one court review dismissed 78 of 79 statements and held the demand statement not false.
3. **Wrong period.** The CW accounts end at roughly May 2024 (latest CW tenure). They say nothing about FY26 to FY28 sell-in, which is the thesis question.
4. **No China content.** Zero CW accounts on China demand.
5. **Only the innovation-pipeline statement survives**, and it is not a financial-quantity claim.

**Weak support for the short, labelled as such:** the pattern in section 4 (inventory, receivables and Foot Locker stock all up in 2022 on flat retailer sales) shows Nike has done sell-in-heavy shipping before, and Foot Locker's inventory is again about +17% YoY at Aug 1, 2026 with pro forma comps -3.6% (repo note 15, retailer note). That is a pattern of behavior, not proof of repetition; the FY26 NA wholesale rise (+14%) has other explanations (restored distribution) that note 15 already catalogs.
