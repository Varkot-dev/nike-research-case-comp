# Academic Literature on Nike's Decline: Execution vs Structural

Scope: peer-reviewed papers, working papers, business-school cases and theses that go beyond the items already in hand (Caoui et al. NBER w30351; Delisia Matthews; Slaton & Pookulangara JFMM 2024; Benno Nigg; SMU CDO case). Verification status is flagged for each item. "Abstract or index only" means I read a bibliographic record or abstract, not the full text (most are paywalled). Searches ran 2026-10-13.

Side key: **EXEC** = supports "self-inflicted execution problem, fixable"; **STRUCT** = supports "structural problem"; **LEAD** = bears on whether a returning insider team can fix it.

## 1. DTC vs wholesale channel strategy (channel conflict, shelf presence, DTC pullback)

### Takeaway
The strongest new evidence is Van Crombrugge, Breugelmans, Gryseels & Cleeren (Journal of Marketing, 2025). When a supplier opened its own webshop, about 2,000 retailers ordered roughly 19% less, and wholesale prices rose about 21%. A companion JAMS study found retailers also cut the brand's shelf presence. This gives causal, non-Nike evidence for the mechanism behind Nike's wholesale losses, and it supports the EXEC side: the lost shelf space came from a channel choice, not from consumers leaving.

### Cited Findings
- **Van Crombrugge, M., Breugelmans, E., Gryseels, F., & Cleeren, K. (2025). "How Retailers Change Ordering Strategies When Suppliers Go Direct." *Journal of Marketing*, 89(2), 141 to 162. DOI 10.1177/00222429241266576.** Sample: nearly 2,000 retailers facing a toy supplier's webshop launch. On average retailers chose an "exit" response: orders fell 19% and wholesale prices rose 21%. The AMA release reports distinct SKUs ordered fell 18.75%. Smaller independent retailers cut back more than large ones. Side: **EXEC**, because channel conflict is a predictable cost of going DTC and the stance can be reversed. Verified via the [Erasmus University news summary](https://www.eur.nl/en/news/when-brands-sell-direct-how-do-retail-partners-respond), the [AMA press release](https://www.ama.org/press-releases/press-release-from-the-journal-of-marketing-how-retailers-change-ordering-strategy-when-a-supplier-starts-its-own-direct-channel/), the [author open-access PDF (EUR Pure)](https://pure.eur.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/167363444/van-crombrugge-et-al-2024-express-how-retailers-change-ordering-strategies-when-suppliers-go-direct.pdf) and the [Tinbergen listing](https://tinbergen.nl/publication/169928/how-retailers-change-ordering-strategies-when-suppliers-go-direct). Numbers come from the summaries; I did not read the full text.
- **Practitioner spin-off in HBR (2025), same author team:** recommends that brands such as Nike add visible store locators that send web traffic to retailers, and build relationships aimed at small retailers., [EBSCO record](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/social-sciences-and-humanities/0a71122f-c626-5c87-8904-86577c43288e/research-how-retailers-respond-when-brands-start-selling-direct)
- **Related JAMS study by overlapping authors:** after a consumer-electronics maker launched a DTC site, retailers "reduce the brand's shelf presence, raise prices and, as a result, lose sales." I saw this only in a search summary and did not open the article itself, so treat it as **unverified** until the JAMS DOI is found. Source of the description: [AMA Nov 2024 explainer (Sony example)](https://www.ama.org/2024/11/12/how-do-retailers-change-their-strategy-when-suppliers-like-sony-start-direct-channels-of-their-own/)
- **Osinga, E. C., Chandukala, S. R., & Mittal, S. (2025). "Nike's Consumer Direct Offense Strategy: A Hit or a Miss?" SMU case (distributed by HBP as 176SMU).** Set in 2024. It describes Nike's "radical cut back" of wholesale, early success in FY21 to 22, then a demand slowdown and inventory build-up in FY22 to 23. Students assess the effects on wholesale partners. Side: **EXEC**. This case was already in hand; it is listed to give the authors., [SMU InK](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cases_coll_all/520); [HBP listing](https://store.hbr.org/product/nike-s-consumer-direct-offense-strategy-a-hit-or-a-miss/176SMU)
- **Industry (not peer-reviewed), BMO Capital Markets (Simeon Siegel):** wholesale tends to earn higher pre-tax margins than DTC once fulfillment, logistics and marketing costs are counted., [SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/dtc-and-e-commerce/bmo-report-highlights-some-disadvantages-in-dtc-vs-wholesale/92197.article)

### Inferences
- The 19% order cut and 21% wholesale-price rise are a ready analogy for the shelf space Nike ceded to Hoka, On and New Balance in Foot Locker, Dick's and independent run stores. Because the cause was a supplier decision, partners can be won back. This supports EXEC, provided Nike rebuilds relationships, especially with small specialty run shops (the segment most hurt in the JM study).
- The JM finding is about a supplier's *entry* into DTC. No study I found measures whether retailers return after a brand *pulls back* from DTC. So the speed of Nike's wholesale recovery is an open empirical question.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed paper that uses Nike's own wholesale-exit data (for example, the 2017 to 2021 account cuts) to estimate lost sell-through or competitor share gains.
- The full citation for the JAMS DTC/shelf-presence study is unconfirmed.
- I found no academic study on how DTC pullbacks perform (that is, brands returning to wholesale).

## 2. Scarcity, limited editions, brand dilution, hype cycles and identity signaling

### Takeaway
The theory points two ways. Scarcity and limited editions can raise brand value (Balachander & Stock; Amaldoss & Jain). But when a signal spreads to the "wrong" groups, early adopters drop it (Berger & Heath 2008). Combined with Caoui et al., this predicts that over-supplying Dunk, Jordan 1 and AF1 erodes their signal value. That is a self-inflicted, EXEC-side cause, and it can be reversed by tightening supply. The STRUCT reading is that fashion cycles turn regardless of supply.

### Cited Findings
- **Berger, J., & Heath, C. (2008). "Who Drives Divergence? Identity Signaling, Outgroup Dissimilarity, and the Abandonment of Cultural Tastes." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 95(3), 593 to 607.** Across seven studies, people abandon tastes to avoid signaling an identity they don't want. In a field study, undergraduates stopped wearing a charity wristband after students from a neighboring "geeky" dorm began wearing it. Divergence is strongest away from *dissimilar* outgroups. Side: **EXEC/STRUCT, mixed.** Mass distribution of a status sneaker, such as the Dunk going to outlets and mass retail, triggers abandonment by core adopters, which is EXEC (a supply choice). But taste cycles are also endogenous, which is STRUCT. Verified: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18729697/), [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/marketing_papers/296), [author PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Divergence.pdf)
- **Amaldoss, W., & Jain, S. (2005). "Conspicuous Consumption and Sophisticated Thinking." *Management Science*, 51(10), 1449 to 1466.** A rational-expectations monopoly model with laboratory tests. Snobs can have upward-sloping demand, but only when (weakly) follower consumers are present. Exclusivity value depends on mass followers existing *without* owning the same item. Side: **EXEC**, because the mix of exclusive and mass supply is a managerial lever. Abstract only: [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0399); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/r/inm/ormnsc/v51y2005i10p1449-1466.html)
- **Amaldoss, W., & Jain, S. (2005). "Pricing of Conspicuous Goods: A Competitive Analysis of Social Effects." *Journal of Marketing Research*, 42(1), 30 to 42.** The citation was seen in a reference list; I did not read the content., [RePEc citations page](https://ideas.repec.org/r/inm/ormnsc/v51y2005i10p1449-1466.html)
- **Amaldoss, W., & Jain, S. "Reference Groups and Product Line Decisions: An Experimental Investigation of Limited Editions and Product Proliferation."** Duke repository record. It addresses directly whether limited editions or proliferation fits a reference-group structure. Year and venue not confirmed in what I saw (I believe it is *Management Science* c. 2010, but **unverified**)., [DukeSpace](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/items/59ed480c-f5ae-4d4b-b9fb-35c5c2e4cad5); [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/757483)
- **Balachander, S., & Stock, A. (2009). "Limited Edition Products: When and When Not to Offer Them." *Marketing Science*, 28(2), 336 to 355. DOI 10.1287/mksc.1080.0401.** Bibliographic record only. I could not see the findings, so treat the specific conclusions as unverified. The same pair published on scarcity as a quality signal (*Management Science* 2005) and on scarcity strategies in autos (*Management Science* 2009)., [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mksc.1080.0401); [DBLP](https://dblp.org/pid/01/7879)
- **"A Model of the 'It' Products in Fashion," *Marketing Science* (DOI 10.1287/mksc.1120.0742).** Models fashion hits, and appeared in the same search cluster. I did not confirm the authors from the page I saw., [INFORMS references page](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/references/10.1287/mksc.1120.0742)
- **German-language dissertation: "Wirkung von Limited Editions für Marken: theoretische Überlegungen und empirische Überprüfung"** ("The Effect of Limited Editions on Brands: Theoretical Considerations and Empirical Testing"). TUM library record (BV035647095). Author and year not shown in the snippet., [TUM catalog](https://search.ub.tum.de/vufind/Record/DE-604.BV035647095)

### Inferences
- Caoui et al. (NBER w30351; adding supply lowers willingness to pay by 7 to 15%) plus Berger & Heath give a two-step EXEC story. Over-supply first lowers the scarcity premium, then mass adoption pushes core adopters to abandon the silhouette. Both steps follow from Nike's supply decisions in FY22 to 24.
- Amaldoss & Jain implies a "tiered supply" fix (scarce hero product plus a broad mass line) instead of flooding one silhouette.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study that measures Dunk or AF1 resale-premium collapse against Nike's own production volumes. StockX price series would be needed.
- I did not open the full texts of Balachander & Stock (2009) or the Amaldoss & Jain limited-editions paper. Their numeric findings are not captured.

## 3. Brand loyalty and switching in athletic footwear; rise of Hoka, On and New Balance; the comfort trend

### Takeaway
I found **no peer-reviewed study** of brand switching to Hoka or On, or of the "dad shoe" comfort trend. The academic record here is thin, which is itself a finding. Evidence on the STRUCT side is mostly industry and press data, plus older biomechanics work on maximalist shoes.

### Cited Findings
- Hoka's maximalist cushioned shoe launched in 2010 as a reaction against minimalist running. It took off only after runners "felt duped or had injured themselves" in minimalist shoes. This is a press account, not peer-reviewed., [Bend Bulletin, 2015](https://bendbulletin.com/2015/02/20/forget-barefoot-many-runners-seek-cushioning)
- Biomechanics: in a Harvard-linked study of 5 runners presented at ACSM, maximal cushioning did *not* reduce vertical impact peak, and loading rate was *higher* in the cushioned shoe. A 15-runner Oregon State study tested Hoka against traditional shoes. Both are tiny samples. Their relevance: Hoka's adoption appears driven by *perceived* comfort, not a proven injury benefit, which fits the Nigg "comfort filter"., [Runblogger summary](https://runblogger.com/2015/06/do-maximalist-running-shoes-reduce-impact-forces.html); [Oregon State Synergies](https://synergies.oregonstate.edu/2018/running-shoe/)
- Korean press reports that Nike preference among US teens fell by double-digit points in a few years, and that the gap with Hoka, On and ASICS narrowed. Korean consumers cite design, fit and value for money as reasons to switch. This is press, not academic., [Daum/Korean press](https://v.daum.net/v/ZkMC3f5TrT?f=p)
- Japan: President Online reports a fall in Nike's share of observed sneaker wearers, with On, HOKA and ASICS gaining. This is media., [President Online](https://president.jp/articles/-/113157)

### Inferences
- The lack of a peer-reviewed switching study is a gap the team could fill with its own survey. It also means STRUCT claims about permanent comfort-driven switching rest on industry data, not academic evidence.
- Nigg's comfort filter plus the maximalist biomechanics results suggest the comfort shift is a real consumer *preference* (STRUCT). Nike can still contest it with product (Vomero 18, Pegasus Premium), which is EXEC.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study of Hoka, On or New Balance brand switching, or of the dad-shoe trend.
- No peer-reviewed estimates of athletic-footwear brand loyalty (for example, repeat-purchase rates from Circana or NPD panels) were found.

## 4. CEO succession: insider vs outsider, returning ("boomerang") CEOs, turnaround leadership

### Takeaway
The evidence cautions against assuming a boomerang leader will succeed. Firms run by returning CEOs underperform peers by about 10% a year in stock return, mostly in dynamic industries and when founders return (Hendricks, Bingham, Kolev & Howell; MIT SMR 2020). But Elliott Hill is a returning *executive* who was not previously CEO or founder, so the boomerang finding applies only partly. Fit-based research (Chen & Hambrick 2012) says replacing the CEO has no general effect. What helps is replacing a misfit incumbent (Donahoe, an outsider with an eBay and ServiceNow tech background) with a well-fitted successor. Insiders are better at long-term competitive repositioning, and outsiders at quick cuts (Zhang & Rajagopalan 2010). Taken together, the literature mildly supports LEAD and EXEC: a fitted insider is well suited to a product- and wholesale-relationship rebuild, though not to a fast reset.

### Cited Findings
- **Hendricks, B., Bingham, C., Kolev, K., & Howell, T. (2020). "Boomerang CEOs: What Happens When the CEO Comes Back?" *MIT Sloan Management Review*.** Data: 6,429 CEO tenures in S&P Composite 1500 firms, 1992 to 2017, including 167 boomerang CEOs. Boomerang-led firms' annual stock performance was **10.1% lower** than first-time CEOs'. Founders were 4% of all CEOs but 44% of boomerangs, and underperformance was strongest for founder returns and in dynamic industries. Kolev's proposed mechanism: founders lack the administrative skills to run a large firm. Exception noted: Ron Shaich at Panera. Side: **against LEAD** if applied naively. Hill was President of Consumer & Marketplace, not a former CEO, so he is not a boomerang CEO in the study's sense. Verified through [MIT SMR](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/?p=79033), the [UNC Kenan Institute abstract](https://kenaninstitute.unc.edu/?p=8803) and [KUOW/NPR coverage](https://www.kuow.org/stories/for-companies-rehiring-a-founder-can-be-enticing-but-the-results-are-usually-worse). The co-author list comes from secondary coverage, and I did not find an academic-journal version.
- **Amore, M. D., Bennedsen, M., Le Breton-Miller, I., & Miller, D. (2021). "Back to the future: The effect of returning family successions on firm performance." *Strategic Management Journal*.** Sample: 489 Italian family firms whose non-family CEO left. Returns to a family CEO ("Type-R") were 42% of cases. Difference-in-differences shows **18% higher profitability** after Type-R successions. Side: **LEAD (pro).** Returning to people who carry the firm's identity can work. This is an analogy, since Nike is not a family firm, though Phil Knight remains chairman emeritus and controlling holder. Verified via abstract: [SUFE academic newsletter](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/376696)
- **Chen, G., & Hambrick, D. C. (2012). "CEO Replacement in Turnaround Situations: Executive (Mis)Fit and Its Performance Implications." *Organization Science*, 23(1), 225 to 243. DOI 10.1287/orsc.1100.0629.** Troubled firms perform substantially better *to the extent* they replace incumbents poorly suited to conditions with well-suited successors. "CEO replacement per se has no general effect." Side: **LEAD (conditional).** The case must argue Hill fits the situation (product and wholesale relationships), not simply that he is new. Abstract only: [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ororsc/v23y2012i1p225-243.html); [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/ceo-replacement-in-turnaround-situations-executive-misfit-and-its/); [INSEAD WP version](https://flora.insead.edu/fichiersti_wp/inseadwp2010/2010-101.pdf)
- **Zhang, Y., & Rajagopalan, N. (2010). "Once an Outsider, Always an Outsider? CEO Origin, Strategic Change, and Firm Performance." *Strategic Management Journal*, 31(3), 334 to 346.** Sample: 193 CEOs who left office between 1993 and 1998. Strategic change has an inverted-U relationship with performance, and the curve is steeper for outsider CEOs, especially in later tenure years. Zhang's interpretation: outsiders excel at fast cost-cutting and divestment, while insiders are better placed for long-term competitive change. Side: **LEAD (pro-insider for a product and brand rebuild)**, and it frames Donahoe (outsider) as having pushed "great" change (CDO), which falls on the costly side of the curve. Abstract via [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v31y2010i3p334-346.html); interpretation from [TWST interview](https://www.twst.com/?p=567966)
- **Zhu, Q., Hu, S., & Shen, W. (2020). "Why do some insider CEOs make more strategic changes than others? The impact of prior board experience on new CEO insiderness." *Strategic Management Journal*, 41(10), 1933 to 1951. DOI 10.1002/smj.3183.** Prior board experience at the focal firm *reduces* an insider CEO's strategic change, and outside board experience *increases* it. Side: nuances **LEAD**. Hill's four years away (2020 to 2024) may lower his "insiderness" and raise his willingness to change course. That is my inference, not the paper's. Abstract read: [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v41y2020i10p1933-1951.html)
- **Related:** "When Do Outsider CEOs Generate Strategic Change? The Enabling Role of Corporate Stability," *Journal of Management Studies*, 50(7), 1267 to 1294 (2013). Index record only; authors not confirmed from the page I saw (I believe Karaevli & Zajac, **unverified**)., [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jomstd/v50y2013i7p1267-1294.html)
- **Related:** Kowalzick et al. (2023), "Overconfident CEOs in Dire Straits: How Incumbent and Successor CEOs..." *Journal of Management Studies*. Open PDF on the Mannheim repository. Not read in detail., [Mannheim MADOC](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/64624/1/J%20Management%20Studies%20-%202023%20-%20Kowalzick%20-%20Overconfident%20CEOs%20in%20Dire%20Straits%20How%20Incumbent%20and%20Successor%20CEOs%20.pdf)

### Inferences
- The opposing team will likely cite the boomerang study's 10% underperformance. The rebuttal: (a) the effect is concentrated in *founder* returns, and Hill is not a founder or former CEO; (b) Chen & Hambrick show fit matters more than replacement itself; (c) Zhang & Rajagopalan favor insiders for exactly the long-cycle product and brand repair Nike needs.
- Starbucks/Schultz (2008 and 2022) is a useful anecdote, but I found no peer-reviewed single-firm study of it in this search.

### Gaps
- I did not find a peer-reviewed journal version of the boomerang-CEO study; the MIT SMR article is practitioner-facing. The Kenan abstract suggests a working paper exists.
- I found no study separating "returning non-CEO executives promoted to CEO" (Hill's category) from boomerang CEOs.
- I did not find the classic insider vs outsider meta-analyses (for example Karaevli 2007 SMJ, or Shen & Cannella 2002 AMJ). They were not searched, so they are not cited.

## 5. Corporate turnarounds (retrenchment vs recovery)

### Takeaway
Modern turnaround research rejects a strict "cut first, then grow" sequence. Firms do best when they combine retrenchment and recovery, weighting retrenchment early and shifting toward recovery (Schmitt & Raisch 2013, 107 turnarounds). Nike's mix of cost cuts plus a product and wholesale rebuild under Hill fits this model. The duration and outcome evidence is mixed.

### Cited Findings
- **Schmitt, A., & Raisch, S. (2013). "Corporate Turnarounds: The Duality of Retrenchment and Recovery." *Journal of Management Studies*, 50(7), 1216 to 1244. DOI 10.1111/joms.12045.** Uses 107 Central European turnaround initiatives. Retrenchment and recovery are contradictory but complementary, and integrating them improves turnaround performance. Successful firms keep a "biased balance" in each stage that shifts from retrenchment toward recovery over time. Side: **EXEC/LEAD**, because it gives a playbook against which Hill's sequencing can be judged. Abstract via [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jomstd/v50y2013i7p1216-1244.html) and the [UNIGE archive](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:48301); stage-shift detail from [HES-SO record](https://arodes.hes-so.ch/record/951)
- **Trahms, C. A., Ndofor, H. A., & Sirmon, D. G. (2013). "Organizational Decline and Turnaround: A Review and Agenda for Future Research." *Journal of Management*.** Updates the Pearce & Robbins two-stage (retrenchment then recovery) model. It adds managerial cognition, strategic leadership and stakeholder management as turnaround factors, and calls the field fragmented. Side: neutral framework; leadership and stakeholder (wholesale-partner) management are explicit levers., [Social Science Space summary](https://www.socialsciencespace.com/2013/02/organizational-decline-and-turnaround-a-review-and-agenda/)
- A 2021 follow-up review by Curto Rodríguez & García Chas builds on Trahms et al., [Revista Galega de Economía](https://revistas.usc.gal/index.php/rge/article/view/6617)

### Gaps
- I found no consumer-brand-specific turnaround study (for example adidas 2014 to 2016, Under Armour, Gap) with quantified recovery durations.

## 6. Non-English academic work (China, Japan, Korea, Germany)

### Takeaway
Chinese journals have a body of *guochao* (国潮) purchase-intention research showing that identity and cultural factors drive *intention*, while price and function drive *actual purchase*. That suggests domestic-brand preference in China is partly structural (STRUCT) but contestable on product and value. I found no peer-reviewed Korean (KCI) or Japanese (J-STAGE) study specifically on Nike switching; only theses and media turned up.

### Cited Findings
- **《国货消费意向与行为》("Domestic-brand consumption intention and behavior"), 《上海经济》 *Shanghai Economy*, 2024.** Using the theory of planned behavior and uses-and-gratifications theory, it finds that identity, value and belonging (spiritual factors) shape purchase *intention*, while perceived usefulness and functional value for price (practical factors) drive *actual purchase*. Side: **mixed.** Guochao affinity is real (STRUCT), but conversion depends on product and price, which Nike can contest (EXEC). Abstract only: [NAPSTIC record](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/010shjj202404006)
- **《传统文化广告与国潮品牌购买意愿》("Traditional-culture advertising and purchase intention for guochao brands"), 《毛纺科技》 *Wool Textile Journal*, 2024, 52(7): 69 to 76, DOI 10.19333/j.mfkj.20230506108.** With n = 241, perceived emotional and cognitive value partly mediate the effect of traditional-culture ads on purchase intention, and experiential value fully mediates for some ad types. Side: **STRUCT** (cultural resonance favors domestic brands). Abstract only: [NAPSTIC](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/010mfkj202407010)
- **国潮服装品牌属性研究 ("Guochao apparel brand attributes and purchase intention"), 《丝绸》 *Journal of Silk*, 2026.** Uses an SOR framework, consumer-identification theory, fsQCA and SEM. Sociocultural and distinctiveness attributes drive purchase intention through dual identification. Abstract only: [NAPSTIC](https://opaj.napstic.cn/periodicalArticle/0120260700364984)
- **Hua Chiew (HCU, Thailand) repository paper** argues that foreign brands like Nike face challenges from weak cultural sensitivity and public-opinion crises, while Li-Ning gains from cultural symbols. Index only; quality unknown., [HCU repository](https://has.hcu.ac.th/xmlui/handle/123456789/5856)
- **"Exploratory Study on the Perceived Fit and Consumer Purchase Intention in Chinese Sports Brand Co-Branding,"** *Journal of Arts Management* (TCI Thai journal)., [ThaiJO](https://so02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jam/article/view/272484)
- **Radboud University thesis (2022), "The Impact of the Xinjiang Cotton Issue on Sneaker Collecting."** Qualitative interviews find collectors cut Western-sneaker purchases and showed more interest in local brands, but the boycott was **temporary**. This is a student thesis with a small sample, so it is weak evidence. Side: argues **against** a permanent STRUCT loss in China., [Radboud theses](https://theses.ubn.ru.nl/items/c43e3528-80e9-4e70-a292-68fa1aac9e08)
- Press context (not academic): Anta and Li-Ning shares surged during the March 2021 Xinjiang cotton row., [SCMP](https://m.scmp.com/business/companies/article/3127107/anta-li-ning-shares-surge-chinese-consumers-back-domestic-brands). Discounted Nike items still sold out on Chinese e-commerce during the boycott., [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/en/news/4163043)
- **Japan:** Waseda (Tokorozawa) undergraduate thesis (2012) comparing student perceptions of Nike, ASICS and adidas basketball shoes ([PDF](https://tokorozawa.w.waseda.jp/kg/doc/20//sotsuron2012/1K09B060.pdf)). Biwako Seikei Sport College theses on women's sneaker purchase motives ([PDF](https://biwako-seikei.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/5036/files/267%20%E4%B8%AD%E5%B1%B1.pdf)) and on track-spike purchase intention ([PDF](https://biwako-seikei.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/5027/files/258%20%E9%96%A2%E5%8F%A3.pdf)). These are student work and only marginally useful.
- **Taiwan:** NCCU thesis (Tainan, n = 645) on sneaker purchase frequency and ad perceptions. Nike, Adidas, Reebok and Fila were the most purchased brands. It is dated., [NCCU](https://ah.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/73018)
- **Germany:** TUM dissertation on the brand effects of limited editions (see Section 2).

### Inferences
- The Chinese "intention vs behavior" split is the most useful non-English finding. Guochao sentiment raises consideration of Anta and Li-Ning, but actual purchase still turns on function and value. Nike can therefore win back China share with product, though probably not to pre-2021 levels.

### Gaps
- I could not reach CNKI directly. The NAPSTIC mirror gave abstracts only, and first authors' names were not visible in the snippets.
- I found no KCI (Korea) or J-STAGE (Japan) peer-reviewed article on Nike or sportswear brand switching. Direct database searches would be needed.

## 7. Business-school cases on Nike, 2020 to 2026

### Takeaway
The only verified recent case is the SMU CDO case (Osinga, Chandukala & Mittal, 2025). I found no HBS, Wharton, INSEAD, Ivey or CEIBS case on the Hill-era turnaround in this search.

### Cited Findings
- SMU case: see Section 1., [SMU InK](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cases_coll_all/520)
