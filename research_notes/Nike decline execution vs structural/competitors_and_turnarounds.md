## 1. Adidas turnaround under Bjørn Gulden (CEO from Jan 2023)

### Takeaway
Adidas went from its first loss in about 30 years (2023) to record revenue in 2024. Revenue rose again to about €24.8bn in 2025, and the company was still growing about 14% currency-neutral in Q2 2026. Revenue recovered in about 12 to 18 months. Margin recovery is slower: the operating margin was still about 8 to 9.6% in 2025 and H1 2026, against a 10% target. This is the strongest evidence that a big-brand slump can be fixed through execution by a returning insider. One caveat: part of the recovery rode a fashion cycle (terrace and retro shoes) that Adidas did not create, and Adidas footwear growth had slowed to +1% by Q2 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Starting point and trajectory**
  - 2023: sales fell by more than €1bn, and Adidas posted its first loss in three decades (−€14m net), with a 1.3% operating margin. 2024: sales rose by more than €2.2bn to €23.68bn, just above the 2019 record of €23.64bn, and the operating margin recovered to 5.6% against a 10% target. **EXEC** — [Börsen-Zeitung, "Adidas sprints ahead", Joachim Herr, 11 Feb 2025 [OPENED]](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/english/adidas-ahead)
  - 2023 operating profit was €268m (2022: €669m). That beat the company's own forecast of a €100m loss. 2024 operating profit was €1.337bn, more than €1bn higher, on +12% currency-neutral growth. **EXEC** — [RetailDetail [SNIPPET]](https://retaildetail.eu/news/fashion/adidas-bounces-back-after-painful-yeezy-break-up); [Paperjam [SNIPPET]](https://en.paperjam.lu/article/adidas-revenue-increased-11)
- **Yeezy wind-down**
  - The Ye (Kanye West) split left Adidas with about €1.2bn of unsold Yeezy stock. Adidas sold it off gradually rather than writing it all off. One report says Yeezy sales contributed about €200m to 2024 operating profit. **EXEC** (one-off tailwind) — [RetailDetail [SNIPPET]](https://retaildetail.eu/news/fashion/adidas-bounces-back-after-painful-yeezy-break-up)

- **Guidance pattern**
  - In Feb 2024, Adidas guided to about €500m operating profit, against a €1.29bn analyst consensus. It then beat that guidance repeatedly. This "under-promise" pattern is a playbook Nike's Elliott Hill could copy. — [BusinessDay, 1 Feb 2024 [SNIPPET]](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/2024-02-01-adidas-shares-fall-after-disappointing-2024-forecast/)
- **Wholesale partner reset**
  - Intersport, Europe's largest sporting-goods retail group, says Gulden restored trust with retailers, with weekly exchanges. It ranks Adidas as its #1 supplier, ahead of Nike, followed by Puma, On and Asics.

  - The same Bloomberg column argued that "a revival won't be instantaneous" for Nike. [SNIPPET]

### Inferences
- Timeline benchmark: under Gulden, revenue went from trough (2023) to a new record in about 2 years, but margin is still short of the 10% target after more than 3.5 years. If Hill (CEO since Oct 2024) follows the Adidas path, Nike revenue stabilisation could arrive around FY27, with full margin recovery around FY28 to FY29.
- Adidas's starting problems were largely self-inflicted and one-off: the Yeezy loss, a weak product pipeline and alienated retailers. This parallels Nike's DTC overreach and its franchise over-reliance on Dunk, AF1 and Jordan 1. That supports **EXEC**.
- The +1% footwear growth in Q2 2026, while Adidas grows through apparel and performance, warns that terrace-trend gains do not last. Share swings in this industry may be cyclical rather than permanent. That cuts against **STRUCT** ("permanent share loss").
- Gulden, like Hill, is a returning insider: ex-Adidas in the 1990s and ex-Puma CEO. The precedent fits Hill's profile well.

## 2. On Holding and Deckers/Hoka: decelerating or still structural?

### Takeaway
Both are still growing at double digits, well above the industry, but both are visibly slowing. On cut its FY2026 guidance in Aug 2026 after a Q2 miss; its Americas wholesale is soft while its own stores and Asia boom. Hoka slowed from about 24% to 16% in FY2026 and is guided to low-double-digit growth for FY2027. This supports **BOTH**: the challengers are not collapsing (**STRUCT**), but the hyper-growth phase is easing, especially in US wholesale (**EXEC**: there is room for Nike to win back running shelf space).

### Cited Findings
- **On Holding, Q2 2026 (released 11 Aug 2026)**
  - Net sales CHF 850.3m: +13.5% reported, +21.6% constant currency (CHF 749.2m a year earlier).
  - DTC CHF 388.4m (+34.3% constant currency, a 45.7% share of sales). Wholesale CHF 461.9m (+4.8% reported, +12.7% constant currency).
  - By region (constant currency): Americas +13.0% (only +4.5% reported), EMEA +20.5%, Asia-Pacific +54.7%.
  - Gross margin 65.4% (61.5% a year earlier), after absorbing higher US tariffs and excluding any refunds. Adjusted EBITDA margin 19.8%.
  - FY2026 guidance: low-20s% constant-currency growth (CHF 3.47 to 3.56bn at spot rates). Gross margin of at least 65%. Adjusted EBITDA margin 19.5 to 20%.
  - CFO Frank Sluis: "We do not compromise our full-price integrity for volume". The release also cites a "promotional environment in some markets" and says On is deliberately managing wholesale sell-in. **BOTH** — [On Holding Q2 2026 press release, SEC 6-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001858985/000185898526000018/a26q2-ex993xpressrelease.htm)
  - The guide was lowered from "at least 23%" growth. Revenue missed consensus of CHF 881.4m. Shares fell about 20 to 22% on the day. **EXEC** (signs of deceleration) — [Investing.com [SNIPPET]](https://www.investing.com/news/earnings/on-holding-shares-fall-sharply-after-q2-sales-miss-4850974). The prior "at least 23%" figure could not be confirmed in the opened release.
- **Deckers, FY2026 (year to 31 Mar 2026)**
  - Net sales $5.47bn (+9.8%), diluted EPS $7.02.
  - Hoka about $2.6bn (+16%), down from +23.6% in FY2025. UGG $2.7bn (+8%).
  - Q4: Hoka +14.5%.
  - FY2027 guide: $5.86 to 5.91bn, with Hoka growing low double digits. **BOTH** — [FashionUnited, 22 May 2026 [SNIPPET]](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/hoka-and-ugg-drive-deckers-brands-to-5-47-billion-dollars-in-fy26-net-sales/2026052288200); [Just Style [SNIPPET]](https://www.just-style.com/news/deckers-brands-fy26-result/)
  - Shares fell about 15% in late 2025 after Deckers trimmed Hoka and UGG guidance on tariff-related demand worries. Hoka had started slowing in Q3. — [Barchart, "Is Deckers' pain Nike's gain?" [SNIPPET]](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/32658850/is-deckers-pain-nike-s-gain-1-wall-street-analyst-thinks-so); [iTiger [SNIPPET]](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/2537927926)

### Inferences
- Both challengers are still taking share: growth of about 16 to 22% against an industry growing about 6% (Section 6). Nike is shrinking, so the gap remains. That is **STRUCT** in the short run.
- Their deceleration is concentrated in US wholesale, Nike's core channel, and comes as Nike re-enters running specialty. That is **EXEC**-consistent: the window for Nike to regain share is open.
- On's 65% gross margin (versus Nike's roughly 41%, see Section 4) shows that premium pricing in performance running is achievable. That is a bar Nike's product engine must clear.

## 3. Other competitors: New Balance, Asics/Onitsuka Tiger, Puma, Under Armour, Skechers, Lululemon

### Takeaway
The field splits in two:
- **Winners from the "heritage, comfort and running" shift**: New Balance (+19%, $9.2bn), Asics (+19.5%, record 17.6% margin) and Onitsuka Tiger (more than ¥100bn).
- **Strugglers fixing self-inflicted problems**: Puma (losses, distribution clean-up) and Under Armour (revenue still falling two years after Plank's return).

Skechers was taken private amid tariff exposure. Brand health in this industry varies firm by firm, which suggests execution matters. But the winners share the very consumer trends Nike missed.

### Cited Findings
- **New Balance (private)**
  - 2025 sales about $9.2bn (+19%), its fifth straight year of double-digit growth. North America grew more than 20% and Europe more than 30%.
  - Apparel and owned retail each passed $1bn. 80 stores were added.
  - Targeting $10bn in 2026. Company-reported and unaudited.
  - **STRUCT** (sustained share gains) — [FashionUnited, 19 Feb 2026 [SNIPPET]](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/new-balance-achieves-9-2-billion-dollars-in-annual-sales/2026021986388); [Jing Daily [SNIPPET]](https://jingdaily.com/intels/2026-02/24/new-balance-hits-usd9-2b-revenue-in-2025-eyes-usd10b-in-2026)

  - Net sales ¥810.9bn (+19.5%). Operating profit ¥142.5bn (+42.4%), a 17.6% margin. Net profit ¥98.7bn (+54.7%).
  - Onitsuka Tiger and SportStyle each passed ¥100bn in sales for the first time, each up more than 40%.
  - Guidance was raised repeatedly during 2025: ¥120bn to ¥136bn (Aug), then ¥140bn (Nov).
  - **STRUCT** (challenger momentum) and **EXEC** (Asics itself is a turnaround: margin went from mid-single digits a few years earlier to 17.6% through mix and premiumisation). — [Alchempro (page blocked; snippet only) [SNIPPET]](https://www.alchempro.com/news/apparel-news/japan-s-asics-posts-record-profit-as-fy25-operating-margin-hits-17-6--308433-newsdetails.htm); [FashionUnited, 12 Nov 2025 [SNIPPET]](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/asics-records-strong-net-sales-and-profit-growth-raises-outlook/2025111269130); [Bloomberg Gov, Aug 2025 [SNIPPET]](https://news.bgov.com/international-trade/asics-shares-surge-as-raised-guidance-predicts-record-profit)

### Inferences
- Under Armour is the cautionary case for "the returning insider fixes it": two years in, revenue is still falling. Adidas/Gulden is the success case. The difference is that Adidas had a product cycle and strong global wholesale relationships to lean on. Hill's success depends on whether Nike's product pipeline delivers.
- Asics, New Balance and Onitsuka all won on heritage and running. These are categories Nike had deprioritised in favour of lifestyle franchises and DTC. That points to the cause of the share loss being Nike's own strategy choices (**EXEC**), although the consumer shift itself is real (**STRUCT**).

## 4. Tariffs: Nike-specific or industry-wide?

### Takeaway
Tariffs hit the whole industry, because sourcing is concentrated in Vietnam, Indonesia, Cambodia and China. Every peer cites them:
- Adidas: about €400m of FY2026 headwind from currency and tariffs.
- Under Armour: −240bp gross margin.
- On: absorbed the hit.
- Skechers: went private.

So tariffs are a **STRUCT**-type headwind, but not one specific to Nike. Peers with premium pricing (On, at a 65% gross margin) absorbed them better than Nike (about 41%). The Supreme Court's Feb 2026 ruling against the IEEPA tariffs creates refund upside, which is a non-permanent element.

### Cited Findings
- **Nike's cost estimate**: in Sep 2025, Nike raised its gross annualised tariff-cost estimate from $1bn to $1.5bn. The net FY2026 gross-margin headwind rose from about 75bp to about 120bp. — [Modern Retail, 30 Sep 2025 [SNIPPET]](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/nike-grappling-with-further-fallout-from-tariffs-now-expects-1-5b-in-annualized-costs/); [CFO Dive [SNIPPET]](https://www.cfodive.com/news/nike-expects-15b-tariff-costs-this-year-cfo-says/801733/)
- **Nike's 10-Q for the 9 months to 28 Feb 2026**: gross margin 41.0% (−250bp year on year). Higher product costs cut Q3 gross margin by about 270bp, "primarily due to higher tariffs in North America." — [Nike 10-Q, FY26 Q3 (SEC) [SNIPPET; not opened here]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320187/000032018726000037/nke-20260228.htm)
- **Nike's sourcing concentration**: in 2024, about 95% of Nike footwear came from Vietnam, China and Cambodia. — [search summary [SNIPPET]](https://manufacturingdigital.com/news/nike-counts-cost-of-trump-trade-tariffs)
- **Supreme Court ruling**: *Learning Resources v. Trump* (20 Feb 2026) held that IEEPA did not authorise the tariffs. CBP opened a refund process in April 2026.
  - Nike's FY2026 filing reportedly cites a refund of about $986m (about $965m North America, $21m Converse), with about $300m received in cash.
  - Nike said recovery was "not probable" as of 15 May 2026.
  - Consumer class actions (filed May 2026, District of Oregon) allege a "double recovery."
  - **BOTH** — [Sportico (redirect to paywall; not opened) [SNIPPET]](https://www.sportico.com/law/analysis/2026/nike-trump-tariffs-lawsuit-refund-1234899233/); [SGB [SNIPPET]](https://sgbonline.com/nike-sued-by-customers-seeking-refunds-of-tariff-costs/)
  - The refund figures are reported and not verified against the Nike 10-K.
- **Peers**
  - Adidas Q2 2026: higher US tariffs were a gross-margin headwind, offset by a small refund. $250 to 300m of further refunds are possible but not in guidance. — [adidas EQS release [OPENED]](https://live.deutsche-boerse.com/news/EQS-Adhoc-adidas-grows-top-line-14-and-achieves-record-sales-in-Q2-increases-full-year-top-line-guidance-32ebe0ea-82c7-4bfa-8076-f52718607d4b)
  - Under Armour: FY2026 gross margin −240bp, "mainly due to higher tariffs." [UA 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001336917/000133691726000059/exhibit991-fiscal2026q4.htm)
  - On: gross margin still *rose* to 65.4% despite higher tariffs. [On 6-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001858985/000185898526000018/a26q2-ex993xpressrelease.htm)
  - Deckers: guidance was cut on tariff-related demand worries. [SNIPPET](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/32658850/is-deckers-pain-nike-s-gain-1-wall-street-analyst-thinks-so)

### Inferences
- Tariffs explain part of Nike's margin decline but **not its revenue decline**. Peers facing the same tariffs (Adidas, New Balance, Asics, On) grew 13 to 22%. Nike's revenue problem is therefore Nike-specific (**EXEC**). Its margin problem is partly industry-wide (**STRUCT**, but shared and partly reversible through refunds and pricing).
- Where tariffs hurt most, the cause was a weak brand: firms without pricing power (Skechers, Under Armour) could not pass on costs.

## 5. Historical precedents: Nike's own slumps, founder returns, and Under Armour

### Takeaway
Nike has recovered from two prior slumps through product innovation and leadership reset:
- **1980s**: Reebok overtook Nike on aerobics. Nike's answer was Air Jordan (1985) and visible Air (1987).
- **1998**: the Asian financial crisis and the sweatshop backlash. Profit fell about 40%.

Both precedents support **EXEC**. The Under Armour/Plank return is the counter-example.

### Cited Findings
- **1980s Reebok slump**: Reebok overtook Nike on the aerobics boom in the mid-1980s. Nike laid off hundreds of staff (one source cites about 10% of the workforce in Dec 1986). It recovered through renewed technical innovation, Air Jordan (1985) and the visible Air sole (1987). **EXEC** — [Nike timeline, Wikipedia [SNIPPET]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_timeline); [Univ. of Utah Nike case, Oct 1998 (PDF) [SNIPPET]](https://home.business.utah.edu/u0030108/nike_case_oct98.pdf)
- **FY1998 slump**:

## 6. Industry structure and market growth

### Takeaway
McKinsey/WFSGI expects sporting goods to grow about 6% a year from 2024 to 2029 (down from about 7% in 2021 to 2024). Challengers took about 3 percentage points of share from Nike and Adidas combined between 2019 and 2024, helped by lower barriers to entry. Fragmentation is a real **STRUCT** trend. Adidas's subsequent rebound shows that the incumbents' losses are not one-way, which supports **BOTH**.

### Cited Findings
- **McKinsey and WFSGI, *Sporting Goods 2025: The new balancing act***
  - Growth of about 6% a year from 2024 to 2029, versus about 7% from 2021 to 2024.
  - Challengers gained about 3pp of share from Nike and Adidas between 2019 and 2024, helped by lower barriers to market entry.
  - 84% of executives cite tariff and supply-chain disruption risk. 44% of executives were optimistic about 2025. — [McKinsey report page [SNIPPET]](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/retail/our-insights/sporting-goods-industry-trends); [Youth Sports Business Report summary [SNIPPET]](https://youthsportsbusinessreport.com/mckinsey-company-sporting-goods-industry-trends-2025-the-new-balancing-act-transforming-the-athletic-market/); [SGI News [SNIPPET]](https://sginews.com/Content/sporting-goods-industry-growth-to-slow-new-report-finds.aspx)

### Inferences
- A 3pp share shift over five years is meaningful, but it is shared between Nike and Adidas. Adidas has since grown faster than the market (about 13 to 14%). The share loss is therefore at least partly reversible by an incumbent (**EXEC**).
- Growth slowing to about 6% means Nike cannot rely on the market to lift it. Any recovery requires taking share back.

## Overall balance for the report writer

- **Strongest EXEC evidence**:
  - The Adidas/Gulden recovery: a loss in 2023, a revenue record in 2024, €2.06bn operating profit in 2025, and +17% in North America in Q2 2026.
  - Peers grew strongly despite the same tariffs, so Nike's revenue decline is Nike-specific.
  - Nike's own 1980s and 1998 recoveries.
  - On and Hoka are decelerating, especially in US wholesale.
  - Intersport's account of Nike leaving retailers short of goods.
- **Strongest STRUCT evidence**:
  - Challengers are still growing 16 to 22%. New Balance is at $9.2bn and +19%; Asics posts record margins.
  - The McKinsey estimate of a 3pp challenger share gain.
  - Under Armour's failed founder return.
  - Tariffs raising costs across the industry, hurting low-margin incumbents most.
  - Puma's reset is taking more than two years.
- **Timing caveat**: even the successful Adidas case needed about 2 years for revenue and more than 3 years for margin. For Hill, who took over in Oct 2024, that puts a credible revenue recovery around FY27 and a margin recovery later.
