# Retailer Commentary and Sneaker Resale Signals on Nike (2023-2026)

## Q1. What did wholesale partners say about Nike's product, sell-through, discounting and the relationship reset (2024-2026)?

### Takeaway
Most partners take the same line: Nike is "doing the right things" and its running product (Vomero, Pegasus) is working, but recovery is slow. Retailers have also spread their bets to other brands, partly for good. The biggest negative signal of 2026 is Dick's Q2 FY26 call (2026-08-25). Executive Chairman Ed Stack said brands were heavily promoting excess legacy lifestyle inventory on their own websites, and the outlook cut sent DKS down about 30%. The biggest structural signal from China is Nike ending its online distributor arrangements with Topsports and Pou Sheng from 2027-01-01 (announced 2026-07-21). Nike is still choosing a channel there, so it is not finished re-cutting the wholesale model.

### Inferences
- The retailer split matches the thesis question. Retailers selling **performance** product (DICK'S core +4.9%, Academy, JD's running) say Nike is recovering. Retailers tied to **legacy lifestyle/retro** product (Foot Locker, Hibbett/Community) are still deteriorating in mid-2026. So the "execution fix" is showing up in running and performance, but Nike's large lifestyle franchises (AF1, Dunk, Jordan retro) are still being cleared. That looks more structural.
- Stack's "promoted on their sites" comment (2026-08) means Nike Direct markdowns are spilling into wholesale pricing. The repaired relationship has not yet turned into price discipline.
- Even supportive partners (JD, Fleet Feet) now carry more brands for good. Shelf space Nike gave up in 2021-24 is being won back slowly, not handed back.

## Q2. Shelf space lost to On, Hoka, New Balance, Asics, Adidas (2021-2024), and is Nike winning it back?

### Takeaway
Retailers confirm that competitors took share while Nike focused on DTC. JD now reports higher share with some new brands than with Nike in key regions, and Hoka was Fleet Feet's top seller in 2024. Nike is regaining doors (Academy Jordan shops, Fleet Feet's national deal) and Vomero/Pegasus are selling. But no hard shelf-share figures were found.

### Cited Findings
- 2026-09-23, Schultz (JD): JD's share with "some of the new brands" in key regions is above its Nike share. JD's three-year On exclusive ended in 2025. **[STRUCT]** — [SGB Media (opened)](https://sgbonline.com/exec-jd-sports-not-waiting-for-nike-recovery/)
- 2025-11-25, Stack: Foot Locker "did not react quickly enough as its largest brand pivoted toward a direct-to-consumer model." **[EXEC]**: the DTC pivot caused the share loss. — [SGB Media (opened)](https://sgbonline.com/exec-dicks-sg-eyes-inventory-reset-store-closings-in-foot-locker-transformation/)
- Sept 2023 (Reuters): Nike was still a top-five StockX brand, but faster growth was coming from Asics and Salomon. Analyst Jessica Ramirez said Nike's innovation has not kept pace with newer brands: "They are still chasing that." **[STRUCT]** — [Cyprus Mail/Reuters (opened)](https://cyprus-mail.com/2023/09/28/nike-air-jordans-are-losing-resale-value-is-sneaker-culture-moving-on)

### Inferences
- Shelf share is being won back fastest where Nike reopened accounts it had cut (Academy, specialty run), which is an execution lever. Where retailers had already built On, Hoka and Salomon businesses (JD, Fleet Feet), Nike is competing for space rather than getting it back by default.

## Q3. Running specialty channel and Nike's return (Vomero, Pegasus)

### Takeaway
Nike is clearly back in specialty run and Vomero is its strongest franchise. Still, as of Dec 2025 Fleet Feet sold more sandals than total Nike shoes, so the return is starting from a very small base. Fleet Feet's CEO calls the product "just good," not great.

### Cited Findings
- 2026-01-05 (interview late Dec 2025), Fleet Feet CEO Joey Pointer: "It's good to see them back in stores, but the product is just good." "They've always excelled at the pinnacle product… Nike has corrected that." Fleet Feet still sells more sandals than total Nike shoes. Pointer expects Nike in about half of Fleet Feet's doors by end-2026, up from "a fraction" two years earlier. Hoka was Fleet Feet's No. 1 seller in 2024, and Hoka vs Brooks was too close to call for No. 1 in 2025. **[BOTH]** — [Footwear News via Yahoo (opened)](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/fleet-feet-ceo-sounds-off-150000978.html)

## Q4. Resale data: is Nike's hype premium collapsing, and is the cause oversupply or taste change?

### Takeaway
Yes, it has collapsed. StockX's average Air Jordan 1 High premium fell from 61% (2020) to 4% (2023). A 2026 study found 70.7% of Jordan releases trading below retail, with a median 20% under. Evidence points to both causes: Nike flooded supply (more than 700 Jordan releases each in 2022 and 2023), which is fixable, and buyer demand spread across Asics, Salomon, New Balance and Onitsuka, which is structural. StockX's Jan 2026 report shows small Nike/Jordan price recoveries (+5%/+6%), while running and Japanese brands lead growth.

### Cited Findings
- Sept 2023 (Reuters, StockX data via Altan Insights): the average premium on new Air Jordan 1 Retro High releases fell from **61% in 2020 to 4% in 2023**. Some 2023 colorways ("True Blue," "Skyline," "White Cement") sold at a discount. Altan's Dylan Dittrich tied the 2020-21 premiums to pandemic supply constraints. **[EXEC]** (supply-driven) — [Cyprus Mail/Reuters (opened)](https://cyprus-mail.com/2023/09/28/nike-air-jordans-are-losing-resale-value-is-sneaker-culture-moving-on)
- 2026-09-13: a SneakerPing study of 3,538 releases (Aug 2024-Aug 2026) found 59.5% last sold below retail, with the median 10.6% under. For **Jordan (605 releases), 70.7% sold below retail and the median was 20% under**. Causes cited were Nike releasing more than 700 Jordan styles each in 2022 and 2023 (reseller Anthony Treviso: Nike is "digging themselves a hole") plus buyers spreading money across Asics, Salomon and New Balance. The article also cites StockX 2024 data: Nike Dunk trades down 41% and Asics sales up 589%. The article says Jordan Brand revenue fell 16% in FY25. Note: BallerAlert is a secondary outlet, and SneakerPing's methodology was not verified. **[BOTH]** — [BallerAlert (opened)](https://balleralert.com/sneaker-resale-culture-market-decline/)
- 2025-11-12 StockX "Big Facts: 2025 Trends": Asics sales +45% YoY, and the Asics Gel-1130 was 2025's No. 1 best-selling sneaker on StockX. Mizuno +148%, Saucony +38%, Brooks +1,508%. Nike's top new models were the Ja 3, A'One and Total 90 3 (performance and sport, not lifestyle). Kobe 6 Protro sales were up more than 100%. Adidas' top new model was the Samba Jane, and Onitsuka's top new model was the TGRS. **[BOTH]**: Nike's resale momentum is in performance and basketball, while lifestyle is driven by running and Japanese brands. — [StockX press release (opened)](https://stockx.com/about/stockx-rolls-out-latest-big-facts-report-revealing-top-resale-trends-in-2025/)
- 2026-01-12 StockX "Big Facts: Current Culture Index" (7th annual): Nike, Jordan, Adidas, New Balance and Asics were the top five sneaker brands for the third straight year. Nike's average sneaker prices rose 5% and Jordan's 6%, described as "early signs of recovery." The fastest-growing sneaker brands were Mizuno (+124%), Maison Mihara Yasuhiro (+91%), Saucony (+59%) and Salomon (+58%). Nike was the fastest-growing non-sneaker footwear brand (+5,811%, ReactX Rejuven8 slide). **[EXEC]** (stabilizing prices) — [SGB Media (opened)](https://sgbonline.com/stockx-2025-big-facts/)
- 2020 baseline: StockX said Travis Scott AF1s climbed more than $240 on average in the year after release — [StockX (snippet only)](https://stockx.com/news/en-us/8-charts-that-explain-sneakers-in-2020/)

### Inferences
- The timing favors oversupply as the main cause of the premium collapse: premiums fell in 2022-23 while Nike was flooding Dunk and Jordan supply. That part is fixable by restricting supply, which is Hill's stated approach. The concurrent rise of Asics, Salomon, Mizuno, Onitsuka and New Balance is a taste and fragmentation shift that supply discipline will not reverse. The small +5% and +6% price recovery in 2025 is consistent with a supply fix starting to work. But 70.7% of Jordans still selling below retail through Aug 2026 shows the clean-out is not finished.

- No Dewu/Poizon, GOAT, eBay or Kicks Crew data were found on opened pages. No 2024-2026 series for Dunk, AF1 or Travis Scott premiums. StockX's 2023 and 2024 Big Facts reports and its full 2025 brand ranking order were not opened.

## Q5. Nike inventory and discounting signals (Nike Direct markdowns, off-price)

### Takeaway
Retailers say Nike's (and other brands') own-site promotions were a direct cause of the 2026 footwear promotional cycle (Stack, 2026-08). No reliable 2024-2026 off-price (TJX, Ross, Burlington) commentary naming Nike was found.

### Cited Findings
- 2026-08-26, Stack: "several suppliers… have got some inventory that have been promoted on their sites," calling it a "hangover" from excess legacy lifestyle sneakers. He expects promotions "through the balance of the year." **[EXEC]** if this is a one-time clearance, **[STRUCT]** if lifestyle demand is permanently lower — [MediaPost (opened)](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/417489/dicks-gets-clobbered-with-nike-fallout.html?edition=143678)

### Inferences
- Stack's 2025-11 view ("we don't see that promotional activity impacting our business a whole lot") had reversed by 2026-08 ("increasingly promotional").
