# N_RETAILERS: Nike sell-through vs sell-in, tested against U.S. athletic retailers' own filings

Prepared 2026-10-13. Question: Nike North America wholesale grew +11%, +24%, +11%, +10%, +9% year over year in Nike Q1 FY26 to Q1 FY27 (June 2025 to August 2026; growth figures as supplied by the caller, not re-verified here). Does retailer evidence show consumers buying the product (sell-through) or product piling up at retailers (sell-in)?

Sources: SEC EDGAR 10-K, 10-Q and earnings 8-K Exhibit 99.1 filings for DICK'S Sporting Goods (CIK 1089063), Foot Locker (CIK 850209, last 10-Q for the quarter ended Aug 2, 2025), Academy Sports + Outdoors (CIK 1817358), Genesco (CIK 18498), Hibbett (CIK 1017480, last 10-Q for the quarter ended May 4, 2024) and Shoe Carnival (CIK 895447, found via EDGAR full-text search for "purchased from Nike"). JD Sports Fashion plc documents were taken from jdplc.com (not an SEC filer). Raw files: scratchpad/retailers/ ; text with page markers: scratchpad/rtxt/.

Citation conventions:
- 10-K and 10-Q pages are printed page numbers (they match the sequential page in these filings).
- Earnings releases (Ex. 99.1) have no printed page numbers. "ER p. N" counts pages from the first page of the exhibit.
- Foot Locker HTML filings have no CSS page breaks, so they are cited by section heading.
- JD PDF pages are PDF page numbers.
- "derived" means computed by me from the figures cited. It is not a reported number.
- In the CSV, table rows are quoted with | between cells. Quotation marks inside source sentences were removed to satisfy the no-double-quotes rule. Otherwise quotes are verbatim, sometimes truncated or joined with ...

Fiscal calendar alignment: retailer fiscal quarters end about one month before Nike's. Retailer Q2 2025 (May to Jul 2025) roughly lines up with Nike Q1 FY26 (Jun to Aug 2025). Retailer Q3 2025 (Aug to Oct 2025) lines up with Nike Q2 FY26 (Sep to Nov 2025). Retailer Q4 2025 (Nov 2025 to Jan 2026) lines up with Nike Q3 FY26. Retailer Q1 2026 (Feb to Apr 2026) lines up with Nike Q4 FY26. Retailer Q2 2026 (May to Jul 2026) lines up with Nike Q1 FY27.

## (B) Narrative by retailer

### DICK'S Sporting Goods (DKS), including Foot Locker from September 8, 2025

- **Nike share.** Nike was 23% of DICK'S merchandise purchases in fiscal 2022, 24% in fiscal 2023 and 25% in fiscal 2024 (10-K Item 1, p. 7 in each year). In fiscal 2025 it was 31% of "consolidated merchandise purchases within the DICK’S and Foot Locker Businesses" (10-K FY2025 p. 9). The jump from 25% to 31% comes from consolidating Foot Locker, whose purchases were about 59% to 65% Nike. A derived check: with roughly $9B of DICK'S purchases at about 25% Nike, plus about $2B of post-close Foot Locker purchases at about 59% Nike, the blend is about 31%. This assumes DICK'S standalone Nike share stayed near 25% (my assumption, not disclosed).
- **Sell-through at DICK'S looks real.** Comps were +4.5% (Q1 2025), +5.0% (Q2), +5.7% (Q3), +3.1% (Q4), +6.0% (Q1 2026) and +4.9% (Q2 2026). Footwear is named as a growth driver each quarter (10-Q Q2 2025 p. 22; 10-Q Q3 2025 p. 32; 10-Q Q2 2026 p. 28). DICK'S Business inventory grew only +2%, +1%, +3% and +6% from Q3 2025 to Q2 2026, against sales growth of about +4% to +6% (derived). Merchandise margins rose. So during the period of Nike's strongest wholesale growth, DICK'S was not building inventory ahead of sales.
- **An earlier build that then cleared.** In fiscal 2024, DICK'S inventory ran ahead of sales: +11% vs +7.8% in Q2, +13% vs +0.5% in Q3, +18% vs +0.5% in Q4 (the prior-year Q4 had 14 weeks) and +12% vs +5.2% in Q1 2025. Management described this as "investments in key categories to support our sales growth" (10-Q Q3 2024 p. 23). The build worked through with full-price selling, not markdowns. Merchandise margin rose 104 bps in Q3 2024 (10-Q p. 19), and in 2025 the change in inventory and payables added to operating cash flow (10-Q Q2 2025 p. 25).
- **The Foot Locker side is where sell-in shows.** Foot Locker pro forma comps were -4.7% (Aug to Oct 2025), -3.4% (Nov 2025 to Jan 2026), +0.6% (Feb to Apr 2026) and -3.6% (May to Jul 2026). These quarters overlap Nike's +24%, +11%, +10% and +9% North America wholesale growth. DICK'S had to "clean out the garage". It took $217.9M of charges to write down and liquidate Foot Locker inventory in fiscal 2025 (10-K p. 39), plus $42.7M in Q1 2026 (10-Q Q1 2026 p. 25), and guided Foot Locker Q4 2025 gross margin down 1,000 to 1,500 bps (ER Q3 2025 p. 4).
  - By August 1, 2026, Foot Locker Business inventory was $2.0B, against $1,709M for standalone Foot Locker a year earlier. That is roughly +17% (derived; +14% to +20% given that DICK'S rounds to $0.1B), while pro forma comps were -3.6%.
  - Caveats: the August 2025 base already included a "strategic pull-forward of fall product" (FL ER Q2 2025), and DICK'S uses the retail inventory method for Foot Locker, which can differ from Foot Locker's prior basis.
- **Q2 2026 commentary (May to Jul 2026, overlapping Nike Q1 FY27).** DICK'S said industry inventory "grew within the industry, particularly in certain legacy footwear silhouettes and apparel franchises that are not resonating with our athletes", "resulting in an increasingly aggressive promotional environment for key athletic brands" (10-Q Q2 2026 p. 21). It also said Foot Locker has "greater exposure to legacy footwear silhouettes and greater dependence on footwear launch and retro product", and that launches "performed below both industry and our expectations" (ER Q2 2026 p. 1). DICK'S does not name Nike here. Nike is the dominant supplier of legacy franchises and retro launches at Foot Locker, so this is the strongest retailer-side signal of sell-in in the whole set (my inference). DICK'S cut its 2026 Foot Locker outlook to pro forma comps of -2% to 0% and a segment loss of $40M to $80M (10-Q p. 22).
- **Top-3 customer concentration.** The deal was signed May 15, 2025 and closed September 8, 2025, for $2.5B consideration. At close Foot Locker had 2,593 stores including licensed stores; North America was 1,637 (10-K FY2025 p. 37). By August 1, 2026, Foot Locker North America was down to 1,531, after 84 closures in the first half of 2026 (10-Q Q2 2026 p. 24).
  - Merging two of Nike's largest U.S. accounts into one customer mechanically raises "top 3 customer" concentration. The former number-three customer joins the top three, while the combined DICK'S plus Foot Locker is counted as one.
  - Nike's FY2026 (June 2025 to May 2026) includes about 8.7 months of the combined company, so most of the 21% to 29% rise can be explained by consolidation alone, with no change in underlying demand (inference; Nike's per-customer figures are not available in these filings).
  - Store closures and the Foot Locker inventory clean-out argue against reading the higher concentration as stronger sell-through.

### Foot Locker (FL), standalone until Q2 2025

- **Nike share.** Nike was 65% of purchases in 2022, 65% in 2023 and 59% in 2024 (10-K Item 1A and Item 7 Liquidity). The 2024 drop matches Foot Locker's repeated "changing vendor mix" comments in 2023 (ER Q2 2023, Q3 2023). Nike was cutting wholesale allocation to Foot Locker in 2023 and 2024. The 10-K also notes that hot product "is allocated by our suppliers based upon their own criteria".
- **2023 was clear sell-in.** In Q1 2023, inventory was +25% while sales were -11.4% and comps -9.1%, followed by heavy markdowns: gross margin fell 400 to 470 bps in each quarter of 2023 because of "higher markdowns" and "promotional activity" (ER Q1 to Q3 2023).
- **2024 to mid-2025 was lean.** Inventory was -5.6%, -10.0% and -6.3% in Q1 to Q3 2024, then +1.1%, +0.4% and +3.7% from Q4 2024 to Q2 2025. Comps were +2.4% to +2.6% in the second half of 2024, then -2.6% and -2.0% in the first half of 2025. North America was firmer: -0.5% in Q1 2025 and +1.4% in Q2 2025 (+2.6% excluding WSS).
- **Q2 2025 (overlapping Nike Q1 FY26, +11%).** Inventory grew +3.7% while sales fell -2.4%, "due largely to a strategic pull-forward of fall product" (ER Q2 2025). This is mild timing sell-in ahead of back-to-school and the DICK'S close.
- **Promotions.** Promotions were "more elevated than anticipated" in Q3 2024 (ER Q3 2024 CEO quote). In fiscal 2024, Foot Locker said it was "less promotional in 2024 despite the highly promotional marketplace" (10-K FY2024 Item 7 Gross Margin).

### Academy Sports + Outdoors (ASO)

- **Largest vendor.** ASO's largest vendor (not named) was 11% of inventory purchases in 2022 to 2024 and 12% in 2025 (10-K Item 1A). ASO names Nike and its Jordan Brand first among its national brands (10-K FY2025 p. 7), so the largest vendor is likely Nike (inference).
- **Jordan launch.** ASO launched Jordan in 145 stores and online in late April 2025, "the biggest brand launch in the Company's history" (ER Q1 2025 p. 1). This is new distribution: it fills a new pipeline of doors, which is sell-in by construction in Nike Q4 FY25 and Q1 FY26.
- **Inventory vs sales.** In 2025, inventory grew much faster than sales: +15.0% vs -0.9% (Q1), +16.2% vs +3.3% (Q2), +11.6% vs +3.0% (Q3) and +14.9% vs +2.5% (Q4). ASO attributes this mainly to "the pull-forward of inventory purchases at pre-tariff rates" (10-K FY2025 p. 54) and to new stores and new brands. In 2026 it normalized: +6.1% vs +6.7% and +4.4% vs +3.0%, with per-store units down 6.8% and 5.6% (ER notes).
- **Footwear sell-through.** Even with Jordan added, the footwear division grew only +3.6% (Q2 2025), +2.4% (Q3 2025), +2.8% (Q1 2026) and -1.0% (Q2 2026) (derived from 10-Q revenue notes). In Q2 2026, comps fell 0.4% "driven by lower comparable sales in the footwear and apparel merchandise divisions" (10-Q p. 24).
- **Margins.** Gross margin rose to 40.4% in Q2 2026 from 36.0%, helped by tariff-related benefits. That says little about Nike pricing.

### Genesco (GCO, Journeys)

- **Nike share and brand mix.** Genesco does not disclose Nike's share. Journeys is "predominately branded", and its gains in fiscal 2026 came from "brands across both casual and athletic" (10-K FY2026 p. 34).
- **Comps and inventory.** Journeys comps were +9%, +6%, +12%, +5% and +2% over Q2 FY26 to Q2 FY27. Total inventory grew +11%, +7%, +2%, +6% and +8%, mostly at Journeys. Inventory roughly tracks sales here.
- **Promotions.** Promotional pressure in these releases is mostly at Schuh (UK). In Q1 and Q2 FY27, Genesco reported "less promotional activity" (ER Q1 FY27 p. 2; ER Q2 FY27 p. 2). Journeys' momentum is a mild sign of healthy teen footwear demand, but it is not clearly tied to Nike.

### Hibbett (HIBB), until its acquisition by JD Sports on July 25, 2024

- **Nike share.** Nike was 70% of inventory purchases and 69% of net sales in fiscal 2023, and 74% and 73% in fiscal 2024 (10-K Item 1A).
- **2023 stuffing.** In Q1 FY24 (ended April 2023), inventory was +39.1% vs sales +7.4% (derived). Management said "the athletic inspired segment of our industry is carrying surplus inventory and therefore experienced an elevated level of promotional activity" (ER Q1 FY24 p. 1). Inventory was then cut 18% by the end of fiscal 2024. This confirms the 2023 Nike channel-stuffing episode. Hibbett has no filings for the 2025 to 2026 window; it now reports inside JD North America.

### Shoe Carnival (SCVL), additional retailer found via full-text search

- **Nike share.** Nike was 14% of net sales in fiscal 2022, 20% in fiscal 2023, 24% in fiscal 2024 and 24% in fiscal 2025 (10-K Item 1). Nike gained shelf share here through fiscal 2024 and then held flat.
- **Q2 2026 (overlapping Nike Q1 FY27).** Comps were -7.1% and gross margin fell 690 bps, "primarily reflecting increased promotional activity, liquidation of aged and excess inventory". Sales were hit by "an increasingly promotional footwear marketplace" (10-Q Q2 2026 pp. 20 to 21). Inventory was down 5.0% year over year (derived) after the fiscal 2025 pre-tariff build.

### JD Sports Fashion (UK; owns Finish Line, Hibbett, DTLR, Shoe Palace in the U.S.)

- **Nike share.** JD does not give a Nike percentage. Over 84% of its sales come from third-party brands, with Nike listed first (AR FY26 PDF p. 55).
- **FY26 North America.** In the year to January 2026, North America like-for-like sales were -1.8% (+1.2% excluding Finish Line). Footwear was "mixed overall due to softness in several end-of-cycle product lines", partly offset by "several successful retro basketball product launches" (AR FY26 PDF pp. 13 to 14).
- **HY27 North America.** In the 26 weeks to August 1, 2026, North America like-for-like sales were -4.0% and organic footwear about -5%. Conditions were "most acute in North America in Q227, where a slower quarter for high-heat footwear product" met a highly promotional market (HY27 PDF pp. 4 to 5). JD points to the "end of cycle product lines of some of our brand partners" (PDF p. 5).
- **Inventory.** Group inventory was +1.8% vs sales -0.7% (derived, PDF p. 22). JD is flat to slightly heavy, not clearly stuffed.

## (C) Verdict

**Mostly sell-in, with a narrow sell-through core.** From mid-2025 to mid-2026, Nike's North America wholesale grew about 9% to 24% a year. Across the same calendar windows, the Nike-heavy specialty channel shows comps of about -4% to +1%:
- Foot Locker pro forma: -4.7%, -3.4%, +0.6%, -3.6%.
- JD North America like-for-like: -1.8% for FY26 and -4.0% for HY27.
- Shoe Carnival: -7.1% in Q2 2026.
- Academy footwear: about -1% to +4%.

Inventory and liquidation evidence then appeared at exactly these accounts. The one clean sell-through story is DICK'S own stores: comps of +3% to +6%, footwear growth, and inventory in line with sales. Even there, the growth ran about 4 to 6 points, well below Nike's wholesale growth.

Most of the rise in Nike's top-3 customer share, from 21% to 29%, is explained by the DICK'S and Foot Locker merger, which combined two top accounts from September 8, 2025, and by new doors such as Academy's 145-store Jordan launch. It is not evidence of consumer demand.

Strongest three data points for **sell-in**:
1. Foot Locker Business inventory of $2.0B at August 1, 2026, against $1,709M a year earlier (about +17%, derived), while pro forma comps were -3.6% and segment gross margin was 25.66% (ER Q2 2026 p. 3; 10-Q Q2 2026 p. 28). In the same quarter, DICK'S said inventory "grew within the industry, particularly in certain legacy footwear silhouettes", producing "an increasingly aggressive promotional environment for key athletic brands" (10-Q Q2 2026 p. 21).
2. After the September 2025 close, DICK'S had to write down and liquidate $217.9M of Foot Locker inventory in fiscal 2025, plus $42.7M in Q1 2026, and guided Foot Locker Q4 2025 gross margin down 1,000 to 1,500 bps, while Foot Locker comps were negative. This happened in the same months Nike reported +24% and +11% North America wholesale growth (10-K FY2025 p. 39; ER Q3 2025 p. 4).
3. Other Nike-heavy U.S. sneaker retailers were weak in Nike Q1 FY27. JD North America: like-for-like -4.0% and organic footwear about -5% in HY27, blamed on "end of cycle product lines of some of our brand partners" and a slow high-heat Q2 (HY27 PDF pp. 4 to 5). Shoe Carnival, where Nike is 24% of sales: comps -7.1% and gross margin -690 bps from "liquidation of aged and excess inventory" (10-Q Q2 2026 pp. 20 to 21).

Strongest three data points for **sell-through**:
1. The DICK'S Business comped +4.5% to +6.0% in five of six quarters from Q1 2025 to Q2 2026 (+3.1% in Q4 2025), with footwear named as a growth driver. Inventory grew only +1% to +6% while sales grew +4% to +6%, and merchandise margins rose (ERs; 10-Q Q2 2026 p. 28). DICK'S is Nike's largest U.S. account by purchases, at 31% of the combined company's purchases.
2. DICK'S own sell-in from fiscal 2024 (inventory +11% to +18% against sales of +0.5% to +8%) sold through without margin damage. Merchandise margin rose and the inventory and payables change later added to operating cash flow (10-Q Q3 2024 p. 19; 10-Q Q2 2025 p. 25). Earlier Nike-led builds at DICK'S cleared at full price.
3. Specialty comps outside the Foot Locker pipeline were positive: Journeys comped +5% to +12% through fiscal 2026, Foot Locker North America comped +1.4% (+2.6% excluding WSS) in Q2 2025, and Foot Locker pro forma returned to +0.6% in Q1 2026. JD credited "several successful retro basketball product launches" in FY26.
   - Inventories at these retailers stayed near sales growth: Genesco +2% to +11%, ASO per-store units down in 2026, JD +1.8%.

Net assessment: the gap between Nike's North America wholesale growth (+9% to +24%) and retailer comps (DICK'S +3% to +6%; Foot Locker, JD North America, Academy footwear and Shoe Carnival -7% to +1%) is too large to be consumer sell-through.

Three items explain the gap, in rough order of weight:
- Restocking and merger-timing fill into Foot Locker and the combined DICK'S plus Foot Locker, followed by liquidation.
- New-door pipeline fill, such as Academy's Jordan launch.
- Pre-tariff pull-forward buying by retailers in 2025.

By mid-2026, retailers report rising industry inventory and promotions in legacy footwear. That is the classic late stage of a sell-in cycle.
