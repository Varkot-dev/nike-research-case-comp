Verdict in one paragraph. Europe: Nike's EMEA decline is not a currency story (cc is worse than reported), not a wholesale story in FY26 (wholesale was flat cc), and not a weak-category story for the athletic peers (three of four brand peers are growing, with the same weather and the same macro). It is a NIKE Direct story (digital -24% in Q4 FY26, -26% in Q1 FY27) layered on a sportswear sell-through problem and an inventory/discount problem, which together took EMEA EBIT margin from 24.9% to 19.2%. Two of the mechanisms are partly deliberate (cutting off-price and promotions on Nike Digital), and Q1 FY27 shows the margin payoff (GM +140 bps, EBIT margin +80 bps) with revenue still down 5%. What the filings cannot separate is wholesale sell-in from retailer sell-through, and the Middle East effect (section 5). Converse: units explain about 84% of the cumulative revenue decline, price about 16%; wholesale and Direct fell by the same percentage; the business is now at roughly breakeven EBIT, so further revenue loss costs only 14 to 45 cents of EBIT per dollar. Sizing is in section 8.

## 6. EMEA conclusion

Nike is both losing share and operating in a soft but not collapsing market. The financially measurable pieces, in order of size, for FY24 to FY26 (DERIVED, from the two-year EBIT bridge): lower revenue $458M, gross margin rate $301M, selling and administrative growth $210M. The revenue piece splits into NIKE Direct (about -3.1 pts FY26, -5.9 pts FY25) and wholesale (-3.8 pts in FY25, zero in FY26), with price roughly flat over three years and footwear units -13%. Currency is a reported-dollar tailwind only. The two items that are real unknowns, and that matter for FY28, are the sell-in/sell-through split and the Middle East effect.

Evidence for FY28: if EMEA EBIT margin returned to 24.9% on FY26 revenue, EBIT would be $713M higher (about $0.37 per share after tax at the Q1 FY27 effective rate of 22.7% and 1,484M diluted shares: 713 x 0.773 / 1,484, DERIVED). Q1 FY27 margin of 22.9% versus 24.9% leaves about $250M pre-tax on FY26 revenue, or $0.13 per share, between now and the FY24 margin (DERIVED).

### 7c. Splits

Units vs price, cumulative FY23 to FY26 (DERIVED from printed annual unit and ASP points): units index 0.88 x 0.88 x 0.69 = 0.534 (-46.6%). cc revenue index 0.85 x 0.82 x 0.68 = 0.474 (-52.6%). ## 8. Is Converse a drag to size into FY28?

Facts: Converse is 2.5% of FY26 revenue ($1,174M of $46,398M) and 0.5% of FY26 EBIT ($18M of $3,850M). Nike says Converse headwinds continue through FY27. At Q1 FY27 the run rate is about -28% revenue and +$25M EBIT (vs $39M). Diluted shares 1,484M; tax rate 22.7% (Q1 FY27 10-Q).

Scenarios for Converse revenue after FY26 (illustrative DERIVED arithmetic, not a forecast):

| Scenario | FY27 revenue | FY28 revenue | Revenue vs FY26 | EBIT effect at 14% decremental | at 45% decremental | EPS (after tax, 1,484M shares) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: Q1 rate persists (-28% FY27, flat FY28) | 845 | 845 | -329 | -$46M | -$148M | -$0.02 to -$0.08 |
| B: -20% FY27, -10% FY28 | 939 | 845 | -329 | same as A | same as A | same as A |
| C: stabilizes at the Q1 FY27 annualized level of about 4 x 263 = 1,052, which is -10% vs FY26 | 1,052 | 1,052 | -122 | -$17M | -$55M | -$0.01 to -$0.03 |

(Decremental rates: Q1 FY27 14%, FY26 43%, FY25 60% from the bridge above.) Upside: returning Converse EBIT to the FY23 level of $676M would be +$658M pre-tax, or +$0.34 per share (658 x 0.773 / 1,484, DERIVED), which is why it is an option on a turnaround rather than a risk to FY28 if you assume revenue stabilizes. Compared with the consensus FY28 EPS of about $1.65 quoted in the scoreboard (not re-verified here), the downside of scenarios A to C is $0.01 to $0.08 (0.6% to 4.8% of consensus), small relative to Greater China and EMEA margin recovery.

Recommendation: size Converse as a small drag in FY27 and a roughly neutral to modestly negative item in FY28 (worth $0.01 to $0.08 of downside EPS under unchanged assumptions), not as a pillar of the pitch. It matters more as evidence: a brand whose DTC and wholesale fell 33% together, with inventory cleared and EBIT at breakeven, shows what an unmanaged lifestyle franchise cycle does to a Nike segment. Open items: no Converse guidance in the filings; no Converse regional split in the 10-K beyond "all territories".
