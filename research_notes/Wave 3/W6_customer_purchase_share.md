# W6: Nike's share of purchases at its wholesale customers, 2017 to 2025, and what it says about FY26 sell-in

Prepared 2026-10-16. Data: `W6_customer_purchase_share.csv` (174 rows; sections A share disclosures, B implied dollars, C Nike side, D scenarios, E partner health). Extends `research_notes/thesis_tests/retailer_sell_through.md` (which has DKS 2022 to 2025, FL 2022 to 2024, HIBB 2023 to 2024, SCVL 2022 to 2025, ASO 2022 to 2025) and does not repeat its comps, inventory or promotion narrative. New here: 2017 to 2021 history, Pou Sheng and Topsports (HKEX), Designer Brands, the dollar reconciliation to Nike's own figures, and the reversion test.

Tags: OPENED = text read in the filing in this session (SEC filings read through the fetch tool, which returns the quoted sentence and the printed page it reports; PDFs from HKEX and JD read locally with pdftotext). DERIVED = my arithmetic, inputs shown. SNIPPET = press or search result only. REPO = taken from this repo's data files. Calendar-year label CY = the year in which a retailer's fiscal year starts (a fiscal year ending Jan 31 2026 is CY2025). Retailer CY(Y) overlaps Nike FY(Y+1) by about 7 to 8 months, so CY2025 lines up with Nike FY26.

## 1. Headline

**Smoking gun: NO. Nike's disclosed share at its biggest wholesale partners did not rise in FY26 on a like-for-like basis. The rise in Nike's three-largest-US-customer figure (21% to 29%, FY24 to FY26) is not a share-of-wallet gain at DICK'S, and cannot be decomposed further from public filings.**

1. **DICK'S Business Nike share did not rise in CY2025 (DERIVED).** The 10-K says Nike was 31% of consolidated DICK'S plus Foot Locker purchases (10-K FY2025 p. 9), against 25% at DICK'S alone in CY2024. DICK'S Business purchases proxy $9,033M (cost of sales $8,982.6M from the segment table, p. 40, plus about $50M inventory build) and Foot Locker Business $2,347M (cost of sales since Sep 8 2025, p. 40). Solving the blend: if Foot Locker was still 59% Nike (its CY2024 level), DICK'S Business was **23.7%** (22.2% to 26.1% across Foot Locker at 65% to 50%, 23.1% to 24.4% across rounding of the 31%). DICK'S needs Foot Locker at 54.1% to be unchanged at 25%. Had each business held its FY23 (CY2022) share, DICK'S 23% and Foot Locker 65%, the blend would be **31.7%**, above the reported 31%. So the 31% is reproduced by the old shares plus consolidation; there is no gain to reverse.
2. **Where share did rise, it rose earlier, in Nike's FY22 to FY24 wholesale push (OPENED).** DICK'S 17% (CY2021) to 25% (CY2024), Shoe Carnival 14% (CY2022) to 24% (CY2024), Hibbett 61% (CY2021) to 74% (CY2023). Foot Locker went the other way: 75% (CY2020) to 59% (CY2024). Nike moved volume from Foot Locker to DICK'S, Shoe Carnival and Hibbett before FY26.
3. **Top-3 US customer dollars grew +$1,378M from FY24 to FY26 (DERIVED).** 21% x $21,551M = $4,526M (FY24); 25% x $19,725M = $4,931M (FY25); 29% x $20,358M = $5,904M (FY26). Each percentage is rounded, so each dollar figure carries about +/- $100M. In the same two years Nike US revenue fell $1,193M, North America wholesale rose $954M ($11,004M to $11,958M) and NA Direct fell $1,839M. Decomposition of the 8-point rise: if top-3 dollars had stayed at $4,526M, the FY26 share would be 22.2% (+1.2 points from the shrinking denominator, mostly Direct); the other +6.8 points is dollar growth.
4. **What explains the +$1,378M.** The rise started before the Foot Locker deal: FY25 was already 25% (+4 points, +$405M) while Foot Locker was still independent. Hibbett joined JD Sports on Jul 25 2024 (Nike FY25 Q1), and Hibbett's Nike purchases were up to about $0.8B (CY2023 proxy, section 3 below), so JD plus Hibbett consolidation can by itself cover the FY25 step. The FY26 step (+$973M) coincides with DICK'S plus Foot Locker consolidating from Sep 8 2025. A consolidation can only add the former number-four customer, which is at most the size of the former number three, at most $1,509M (a third of $4,526M; DERIVED). The observed +$1,378M is below that ceiling, so consolidation can explain all of it, none of it is proven, and the filings cannot separate consolidation from organic gain at the top accounts. **What would decide it:** Nike's named top-3 customers by year (not disclosed), or DICK'S, Foot Locker and JD US Nike purchases by quarter (not disclosed).
5. **Reversion costs are small at the partner level and large only if the concentration figure itself reverts (DERIVED).** Partner-level reversion to CY2022 shares removes about $0.2B to $0.25B (DICK'S -$90M, Shoe Carnival -$72M, Academy -$41M, Hibbett moot). Reverting the top-3 concentration from 29% to the FY23 level of 22% removes **$1,425M of revenue (3.1% of FY26 $46,398M)**, **$650M of gross profit at the 45.6% NA gross margin, 16.9% of FY26 EBIT of $3,850M and -$0.35 of EPS (-16.7% of $2.10)**, before any recapture. At 25% and 50% recapture through other wholesale or Direct at the same margin the EBIT hit is 12.7% and 8.4%. The two views disagree because the concentration increase is mostly perimeter (who counts as one customer), not share of wallet; the first-order dollar risk to Nike is the top-3 dollars, the second is the shares.

For the pitch, writable with these words: "Nike's three largest US customers rose from 21% to 29% of US sales between FY24 and FY26, +$1.4B of revenue, while Nike's US revenue fell 5.5%. Retailer filings show no rise in Nike's share of purchases at DICK'S, Shoe Carnival or Academy in 2025; the increase is consistent with customer consolidation (JD plus Hibbett, DICK'S plus Foot Locker) and with fill at the top accounts, and the filings cannot separate the two. Reverting to the FY23 concentration of 22% would remove about $1.4B of revenue and about 17% of FY26 EBIT before recapture." Do not write "Nike stuffed its top three customers": the filings do not show that.

## 3. Implied dollars, and reconciliation to Nike's own numbers

Method (DERIVED): Nike dollars = disclosed share x (retailer cost of sales + change in inventory), XBRL companyfacts (data.sec.gov) for cost of sales and inventory. Shoe Carnival uses share of net sales x cost of sales. Cost of sales includes occupancy and distribution at these retailers, so these are **ceilings** for Nike's revenue from the retailer. Examples ($M): Foot Locker CY2019 3,835, CY2022 4,116, CY2024 3,352. DICK'S CY2017 1,111, CY2022 1,982, CY2024 2,280. Hibbett CY2019 566, CY2022 914, CY2023 791. Shoe Carnival CY2018 231, CY2021 225, CY2022 111, CY2025 173. Academy CY2018 444, CY2022 472, CY2025 497. Combined DICK'S plus Foot Locker CY2025 about 3,528 (31% x 11,380, with only 4.8 months of Foot Locker).

Reconciliation test: the five US retailers sum to $7,595M in CY2022, $7,155M in CY2023 and $6,262M in CY2024 (ex Hibbett). Against Nike North America wholesale (FY23 $11,273M, FY24 $11,004M, FY25 $10,484M) that is 67%, 65% and 60%. But Foot Locker plus DICK'S alone in CY2022 is $6,098M, which is **26% above** Nike's own three-largest-US-customers figure of $4,842M (22% x $22,007M, FY23), before counting a third customer and before removing Foot Locker's non-US purchases. So the cost-of-sales base overstates what these retailers pay Nike by at least a quarter (occupancy and buying costs inside cost of sales, plus Foot Locker's international purchases from Nike EMEA and APLA). **Use the dollars as ceilings and rely on the share changes.** Alternatives for the gap that I could not separate: occupancy in cost of sales, non-US purchases at Foot Locker, Nike counting customers by legal entity, and top-3 being measured on US revenue that includes Converse and Direct.

Greater China (DERIVED, RMB at an assumed 7.18 per dollar for 2025): Pou Sheng largest supplier 55% x (cost of sales 11,396 + inventory change 74) = RMB 6,309M, about $0.88B. Topsports Nike at the floor of 60% of RMB 15,955M cost of sales = RMB 9,573M, about $1.33B (Nike alone is not filed, so this is a range anchored on a press floor). Together about $2.2B, or 68% of Nike FY26 Greater China wholesale of $3,255M (REPO). Nike GC wholesale fell 12% in FY26 ($3,699M to $3,255M); Topsports cost of sales fell 4.1% and its inventory fell 8.6% to RMB 5,486M (annual report FYE Feb 2026, PDF pp. 16 and 17), Pou Sheng cost of sales fell 6.2% and inventory rose 1.5% to RMB 5,020M. Nike shipped less than these two partners sold, so partner stock was drawn down or held, not built. The Jan 1 2027 online termination touches, at cost, about 22% x 15,955 = RMB 3.5B ($0.49B) at Topsports and 15% x 11,396 = RMB 1.7B ($0.24B) at Pou Sheng, together $0.73B or 22% of FY26 GC wholesale (DERIVED, assuming online sells at the group cost ratio); this is a channel conversion into Nike Direct, not a lost sale, and W5 covers the consequence.

(d) The loss of margin is concentrated at Foot Locker, where a write-down and liquidation, not a change in Nike's share, is the measured cost.

## 5. Test 3: what happens to Nike if shares revert to the FY23 level

Inputs (REPO, FY26 10-K): US revenue $20,358M, NA gross margin 45.6% (p. 37), EBIT $3,850M, net income $3,108M, diluted EPS $2.10 (so about 1,480M shares), tax rate 20.3% ($3,900M pre-tax, $3,108M net).

| Scenario | Revenue lost | Gross profit lost | EBIT | EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Top-3 29% to 22% (FY23), no recapture | $1,425M (3.1% of revenue) | $650M | -16.9% | -$0.35 (-16.7%) |
| same, 25% recaptured | $1,069M | $487M | -12.7% | -$0.26 |
| same, 50% recaptured | $713M | $325M | -8.4% | -$0.17 |
| Top-3 29% to 21% (FY24), no recapture | $1,629M | $743M | -19.3% | -$0.40 |
| Partner shares to CY2022: DICK'S 24% to 23%, Shoe Carnival 24% to 14%, Academy 12% to 11% | $203M | about $93M | -2.4% | about -$0.05 |
| DICK'S plus Foot Locker blend at FY23 shares | 31.7% vs 31% reported | no loss | none | none |

All DERIVED. Assumptions: no operating-expense offset (wholesale selling costs are small, so this is a high estimate of EBIT loss) and recaptured sales earn the same gross margin (Direct earns more gross margin but carries cost; wholesale-to-Direct conversion is covered in the channel notes). The partner-level row is what the retailer filings support; the top-3 row is the larger exposure and is the one Nike discloses.

## 6. Alternatives ruled out, and routes tried

Why did the top-3 US customers rise from 21% to 29%?
1. Nike gaining share at DICK'S: ruled out (implied DICK'S Business 22% to 26% vs 25%; section 1).
2. Foot Locker taking more Nike product: not supported (59% in CY2024, down from 75% in CY2020; Foot Locker 10-K says it targets no supplier above 55%); CY2025 Foot Locker share not filed.
3. Shrinking denominator (Direct decline): contributes +1.2 points, not the rest (decomposition above).
4. Consolidation (JD plus Hibbett, DICK'S plus Foot Locker): cannot be ruled out, ceiling $1,509M, observed +$1,378M.
5. Organic fill at the top accounts: cannot be ruled out. 4 and 5 survive. What decides it: Nike's named top-3 customers by year, or each customer's quarterly Nike purchases.

Routes tried, with results (rule: at least five different routes before calling a series inconclusive).
- Quarterly Nike share: (1) 10-Q DICK'S Q2 2026 read to p. 22, worked, no Nike percent; (2) 10-Q Foot Locker Q2 2025 read to Note 8, MD&A truncated, no percent; (3) Shoe Carnival 10-Q Q2 2026 read to p. 8, MD&A truncated, partial; (4) earnings releases covered by the existing note, no Nike percent; (5) XBRL companyfacts, the concentration tag exists only for Designer Brands (19.5% in FY2018) and is annual; (6) EDGAR full-text search (efts.sec.gov) returned 403, blocked. Conclusion: not disclosed quarterly. Settling it needs Nike or retailer sell-in by quarter (Nike orders data, Circana).
- Genesco (Nike share): (1) 10-K FY2026 read in full, no Nike mention; (2) 10-K FY2021 read in full, none; (3) 10-K FY2019 first 100k characters, none; (4) web search for call transcripts, none; (5) XBRL companyfacts, no concentration tag; (6) the only concentration it files is wholesale customer receivables. Not disclosed.
- JD Sports: (1) Annual Report FY26 full PDF grepped, no Nike percent (p. 55 says over 84% of sales are third-party brands); (2) HY27 results full PDF, none; (3) AR 2020, 2023, 2024 on q4cdn returned 404 (moved); (4) jdplc.com guessed paths 404; (5) web.archive.org connection reset; (6) press: about 45% (MarketScreener 2025-05-21, Reuters) and "over 40%" 2026, SNIPPET. Company-filed share unavailable.
- Topsports: annual reports FYE Feb 2020 to Feb 2026 read; Nike and Adidas disclosed only combined; Nike alone from press (60% floor) and the 22% online disclosure.
- Pou Sheng: all annual reports 2018 to 2025 and the 2026 interim searched for Nike or Adidas: zero mentions by name; supplier concentration only unnamed.
- Zalando AR 2025 (30 MB, three Nike mentions, none quantified), Frasers AR FY25 (strategic partner wording only), Accent AR 2023 and 2024 and Super Retail FY25 (one and zero mentions), ABC-Mart securities report FY2/2026 (category table only), Intersport and Deichmann (private, web search found nothing): not disclosed.
