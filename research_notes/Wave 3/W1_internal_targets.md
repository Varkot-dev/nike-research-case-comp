## 1. Bottom line

1. **No FY27 to FY29 internal goal is public.** Every PSP and PSU goal is disclosed only after the period (PSP goals about 14 months after they were set; PSU growth-rate goals only after the Aug 2028 certification). What is public are the FY24, FY25 and FY26 PSP goals, the structure of the FY26 to FY28 PSU and the FY27 margin retention award, and the FY27 guide. So the comparison with consensus has to run through the FY27 guide, not through a leaked internal target.
2. **Management's own guide already sits below the premise of its own retention awards.** The Dec 2025 retention awards pay 0% unless FY27 EBIT margin rises (target in bps undisclosed). The Oct 1 2026 guide (adjusted EPS $1.15 to $1.35, revenue down high single digits, "EBIT to decline by a greater percentage than revenue") implies an FY27 adjusted EBIT margin of about 5.2% to 6.1% (central 5.7%), which is 99 to 227 bps below the FY26 PSP-adjusted 7.28% and 72 to 199 bps below FY26 ex one-offs (DERIVED, section 4). On any baseline the guide is a margin decline, so the margin half of the awards is at risk of a zero for Hill and the four non-CEO NEOs (Hill's performance half is $7.5M at target). The one exception is Denton's $4.0M cash award, which has a floor of 100% (section 3).
3. **Consensus FY28 EPS of $1.646 equals only a 7.2% to 7.8% EBIT margin** (about $3.2B EBIT), not the 11% to 14% the price needs. At that EBIT, an 11% margin needs revenue of $29.3B and a 14% margin $23.0B, against an FY27 central revenue of $42.7B (DERIVED). The long-run margin the price needs is outside consensus's horizon; the internal-target evidence says nothing about FY31.
4. **Management's budget record:** FY25 PSP target EBIT was $6,034M (12.1% margin) and actual adjusted EBIT was $3,523M, 41.6% below target and 35.1% below threshold. FY26 target $3,211M (6.9% margin) versus actual $3,334M (+3.8%). FY24 target $7,127M versus $7,062M (-0.9%). The one large internal miss was the FY25 budget set in June 2024; the FY26 budget set in June 2025 was conservative (target margin 69 bps below FY25's 7.60% adjusted).
5. **Credit lines: no financial covenants at all, the 364-day line is a yearly renewal, not an addition, and the March 2026 renewal is cheaper and rating-proof.** The "A-rated issuer added a 364-day line" premise is false: Nike has renewed a $1.0B 364-day line every March since at least 2023 (Mar 10 2023, Mar 8 2024, Mar 7 2025, Mar 6 2026).

**Smoking gun: no.** Nothing here decides long versus short. The strongest new fact is the FY27 guide implying a margin decline against awards that need a margin rise, which is a mild SHORT signal for FY27 and neutral for FY31.

My interpolation checks the printed earnouts: FY26 revenue 25 + 75 x (45,809 - 44,214) / (46,541 - 44,214) = 76.4%; FY26 EBIT 100 + 100 x (3,334 - 3,211) / (3,693 - 3,211) = 125.5%; FY24 revenue 37.7%, EBIT 93.2% (DERIVED).

**What the FY26 PSP payout of 74% implies about the FY26 goal.** Almost nothing about the PSP goal itself. The PSP formula gave 101% (average of 76% and 125%). The Committee cut it to 74% only because the broad-based employee bonus, which uses the same two metrics over two six-month periods with "more aggressive" goals, paid 74% (2026 DEF 14A p. 30). So the 27-point gap between 101% and 74% measures how much harder the half-year goals were than the annual goal, and the half-year goals are not disclosed. Alternatives for why the broad plan paid less, tested: (a) a stronger H2 than H1 would raise an annual payout relative to two half-year payouts (not testable, half-year goals undisclosed); (b) tougher half-year targets (stated by the proxy as the design intent, "enable more aggressive target setting"); (c) the EBIT beat being concentrated in Q4 because the IEEPA recovery and severance fell in H2 (see below), which an annual measure fully credits to FY26 while a half-year plan credits only to H2. Filings cannot separate (a), (b) and (c). Data that would settle it: the H1 and H2 goals of the broad-based plan, which Nike has not published (HR or Item 402 disclosure does not require them).

**Why FY26 adjusted EBIT ($3,334M) differs from reported ($3,850M).** The adjustment definition neutralizes restructuring, FX, changes in law and unusual items (2026 DEF 14A p. 24 footnote). Reported EBIT includes a one-time $986M IEEPA tariff recovery in cost of sales (FY26 10-K p. 30) and $385M of severance (10-K Note 18). 3,850 - 986 + 385 = 3,249, which is $85M below the adjusted $3,334M; the residual cannot be decomposed from the filings (FX and other neutral adjustments). Revenue: reported $46,398M versus adjusted $45,809M, a $589M neutralization (DERIVED).

**Retention awards, sized (DERIVED).** Margin-half target values: Hill $7.5M; each of four non-CEO NEOs $2.0M, so $15.5M in total if paid at target. Hill's full 234,119 units at $39.06 are worth $9.1M versus the $15.4M grant fair value. The proxy's own wording gives the board's intent: the awards "collectively align the newly established leadership team to drive operating margin growth in the near term" (p. 34). Whether the board set the target at a positive or a negative bps number is the open question; if positive, the Oct 1 guide implies 0%.

Why I do not call the target positive as fact: the proxy says "below target results in a 0% payout" and describes the metric as "growth", but gives no number. A target below zero bps would be unusual given that wording, yet the FY26 base includes a $986M one-time benefit (reported 8.30% versus 6.17% without it), so a goal defined on reported figures could be much harder than one defined on adjusted figures. The award text says both years are "as set forth in the audited consolidated financial statements" while the defined term says "Adjusted" (Key Defined Terms table); filings do not say which basis is final. Data that would settle it: the FY27 10-K (about Jul 2027) and the 2027 proxy (about Jul 2027), which must disclose the earnout.

## 5. Pace savings vs what the PSU and retention goals could assume

Stated (OPENED): "approximately $2.5 billion in cumulative savings through fiscal 2031", before expected charges and before "any future reinvestment"; pre-tax charges about $1.0B through FY31 on top of $0.3B severance recognized in the FY26 plan (FY26 10-K Note 18 shows $385M recognized in total); about $0.3B of charges in FY27, the rest through FY31 (8-K Oct 1 2026 Item 2.05; Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 20 and Note 14; Ex 99.1 p. 2). Phasing (CFO Denton, Q1 FY27 call): "the majority of the program savings to be realized in fiscal years '29 and '30, with the full realization ... into fiscal year '31"; "maximum savings to be appreciated and accrued in '29 and '30"; some supply chain benefit already in Q1 gross margin. Reinvestment: "we intend to reinvest a portion", amount unstated. Three geographies from FY28 (10-Q p. 20).

Comparison with PSU goals:
- The FY26 to FY28 PSU runs to May 2028. If the majority of $2.5B arrives in FY29 and FY30, then the FY27 to FY28 PSU years get at most a minority of the program (DERIVED bound: under 50% of $2.5B, so under $1.25B cumulative, but "majority in FY29 and FY30" does not say FY27 plus FY28 combined is small; the exact split is undisclosed). The FY27 retention measure is a one-year measure and sees only FY27 savings, which management itself guided as pressured: "EBIT to decline by a greater percentage than revenue", SG&A "to remain down".
- Even evenly spread, $2.5B over five years is $500M a year (1.2% of FY27 central revenue); a full-run-rate of perhaps $0.8B to $1.0B a year by FY31 would add about 1.8 to 2.3 points of margin before reinvestment (DERIVED illustration; the phasing is mine and not Nike's, labelled). Compare the prior program: Dec 2023 "up to $2 billion in cumulative cost savings over the next three years" (8-K Dec 21 2023, per `19_management_track_record.md`). FY24 to FY26 total selling and administrative expense fell from $16,576M to $16,114M (-$462M, FY26 10-K p. 31 table) while revenue fell $4,964M; operating overhead fell $931M but demand creation rose $469M. Nike never published a realized figure for that $2B plan (repo note 19). So the realized-to-targeted ratio for the last program on a gross S&A basis was about 23% (462 / 2,000), with the caveat that reinvestment in demand creation is the offset (DERIVED).

Rating levels (both 2025 exhibits): Level III = A+ / A1; Level IV = less than A+ or less than A1 (or unrated); if the agencies differ by one level the better level applies (Ex 10 sec 1). Nike at A+/A2 per the FY26 10-K: Level III (so 57.5 + 10 + fee 5 = 72.5 bps on the five-year, matching the 10-K's stated 0.725%; the 0.625% on the new 364-day equals 59.5 + 3).

**Answers to the specific questions.**
- *Interest coverage and leverage covenants:* none. The 8-K says "does not include any financial covenants"; I searched the full Ex 10.1 for EBITDA, interest coverage, leverage ratio and fixed charge (zero hits). Operating covenants only: negative pledge with baskets (the general lien basket is the greater of $2,112.75M and 15% of consolidated net worth, sec 6.12.13), merger, sale of substantially all assets, sanctions, anti-corruption. Events of default include any Change in Control (defined in the exhibit as 50% or more of Class B acquired by anyone other than the Knight Family, plus a Class A test on the Knight Family), cross-default at $200M, unpaid judgments over $200M. A borrowing condition includes no material adverse change since the last audited statements (Ex 10.1 sec 4, line "material adverse change").
- *Downgrade:* S&P cut Nike to A on Oct 5 2026 (secondary source, repo `ratings_history.csv`; the Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 34, filed Oct 2, still shows A+/A2). Both agencies then sit at Level IV. On the five-year line that adds 10.5 bps to a drawn margin and 2 bps to the undrawn fee (5 to 7 bps on $2.0B = $0.4M a year); the 364-day line is flat and unaffected. The grid stops at Level IV, so further cuts add nothing to pricing. The 10-K states a ratings change "would not trigger acceleration". Nothing is drawn (10-K p. 69; Q1 10-Q p. 34).
- *Why an A-rated issuer has a 364-day line (alternatives tested):* (1) newly added line: ruled out, it is the fourth annual replacement (Mar 10 2023, Mar 8 2024, Mar 7 2025, Mar 6 2026 terminated and replaced concurrently, each $1.0B); (2) liquidity stress: ruled out, undrawn every year and cash plus short-term investments $8.4B at Aug 31 2026 (Q1 10-Q p. 34); (3) commercial paper backstop: supported, the 8-K says it is for "supporting the issuance of commercial paper", and $2.0B five-year plus $1.0B 364-day equals the $3.0B CP program exactly (DERIVED match; Nike does not state the sizing logic); (4) near-term maturities: consistent but not stated, $2.0B of notes fall in FY27 (Nov 1 2026 $1.0B and Mar 27 2027 $1.0B, repo `debt_schedule.csv`, FY26 10-K p. 70) and the line expires Mar 5 2027. The surviving explanation is (3), with (4) as a possible additional reason; the filings do not say which.
- *Repricing detail the 8-K does not highlight:* the 2026 renewal removed the rating grid and the 10 bps adjustment. At A+/A1 the 2025 line cost 10 + 58.5 = 68.5 bps drawn plus a 4 bps fee; the 2026 line costs 59.5 plus 3 (9 and 1 bps cheaper) and cannot reprice after the S&P cut. If the 2025 form had stayed, the S&P cut would have moved it to Level IV (79 bps plus a 6 bps fee, DERIVED). Cost avoided on $1.0B undrawn: $0.3M a year. Immaterial to EPS (under 0.1 cent).

## 8. What this changes in the pitch

- Add to the pitch: the FY27 guide implies adjusted EBIT margin of about 5.7% (range 5.2% to 6.1%), and FY28 consensus implies about 7.5%; both are 350 bps or more below the 11% minimum the price needs. The Pace program is $2.5B cumulative to FY31 before reinvestment, majority in FY29 and FY30; at $500M a year it is 1.2% of revenue.
- Do not use: "Nike added a 364-day line because of stress"; it is a yearly rollover with no financial covenants.
- Do not use: any claim that management has committed to a FY27 to FY29 margin; no such number is public.
