Research date: 2026-10-13. Primary data comes from the adidas quarterly results releases (PDFs on the adidas IR site, hosted on res.cloudinary.com) and adidas press releases on adidas-group.com, all opened and read in full text. ## Q1. Starting position in late 2022, and who Gulden is

### Takeaway
Gulden inherited a company that was in crisis mainly because of things inside it: Greater China sales down 36% for FY2022 (down 50% in Q4 2022), inventory up 49% to EUR 6.0bn, a Q4 2022 operating loss of EUR 724m, and the Yeezy termination taking out more than EUR 1.2bn of high-margin revenue. He is best described as a "returnee-outsider": he had spent seven years at adidas in the 1990s, but came straight from running its arch-rival Puma (2013-2022), where he had a proven turnaround record. That profile is very close to Hill's (a long-time Nike insider recalled from retirement), with one big difference: Gulden had already been a CEO at Puma, Pandora and Deichmann.

### Cited Findings
**Rorsted exit timeline**
- 22 Aug 2022: the Supervisory Board said it had "initiated a CEO transition" and that Kasper Rorsted would hand over during 2023. Chairman Thomas Rabe credited Rorsted with growing online sales "by a factor of more than five" and doubling North America sales. He called the move "the right time to initiate a CEO transition and pave the way for a restart" after "three challenging years" ([adidas press release, 22 Aug 2022](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-ag-initiates-ceo-transition)).
- 20 Oct 2022 profit warning: Q3 2022 c.n. growth was +4%, and Greater China fell at "a strong double-digit rate" partly because of "significant inventory takebacks". Inventory was up 63% c.n. FY2022 op margin guidance was cut to about 4.0% (from about 7.0%), and net income from continuing ops to about EUR 500m (from about EUR 1.3bn). A "business improvement program" was set up to offset up to EUR 500m of cost headwinds in 2023 ([adidas press release, 20 Oct 2022](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-reports-preliminary-q3-results-and-adjusts-full-year-guidance)).
- 25 Oct 2022: adidas ended the Ye partnership "immediately", stopped production of Yeezy products and all payments to Ye, and expected "a short-term negative impact of up to EUR 250 million" on 2022 net income. It said it "is the sole owner of all design rights to existing products" ([adidas press release, 25 Oct 2022](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-terminates-partnership-with-ye-immediately)).
- 4 Nov 2022: adidas confirmed it was "in talks with Bjorn Gulden, CEO of PUMA SE" ([adidas press release, 4 Nov 2022](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-ag-in-talks-with-a-potential-successor-to-ceo-kasper-rorsted)). 8 Nov 2022: Gulden was formally appointed effective 1 Jan 2023. Rorsted left on 11 Nov 2022 and CFO Harm Ohlmeyer led the company in the interim ([adidas press release, 8 Nov 2022](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/bjorn-gulden-to-become-ceo-of-adidas-ag)).

**Financial starting point (FY2022, reported 8 Mar 2023)**
- FY2022 revenue was EUR 22,511m (+1% c.n.), gross margin 47.3% (down 3.4pp from 50.7%), op profit EUR 669m (op margin 3.0%, down from 9.4% in 2021), net income from continuing ops EUR 254m. Greater China revenue fell 36% c.n. "due to the challenging market environment, company-specific challenges as well as significant inventory takebacks". North America was +12% c.n. Inventories rose 49% to EUR 5,973m ([FY2022 release, 8 Mar 2023](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/raw/upload/v1706003622/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2022/20230308_adidas_ag_fy2022_results_en_xdfsuy.pdf)).
- Q4 2022: c.n. revenue -1%, gross margin 39.1%, operating loss EUR 724m, net loss from continuing ops EUR 482m. Greater China fell 49.7% c.n. in the quarter (table); North America grew 6% c.n. (same source).
- Initial 2023 guidance (9 Feb 2023, Gulden's first public number): c.n. sales down at a high-single-digit rate, and a reported operating LOSS of EUR 700m. That figure assumed losing about EUR 1.2bn of Yeezy revenue and about EUR 500m of operating profit if no Yeezy stock was sold, a possible further EUR 500m Yeezy write-off, and up to EUR 200m of "strategic review" one-off costs. Gulden: "The numbers speak for themselves. We are currently not performing the way we should ... We need to put the pieces back together again ... But we need some time." ([adidas press release, 9 Feb 2023](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-provides-top-and-bottom-line-outlook-for-2023)).
- Yeezy scale: "more than EUR 1,200 million of Yeezy revenues in 2022" ([FY2023 release](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/raw/upload/v1710499226/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2023/adidas_ag_fy2023_results_en_final_bmt7gz.pdf)). Reuters put the unsold Yeezy shoes at "1.2 billion euros" ([Reuters via Gulf News, 8 Nov 2023](https://gulfnews.com/business/retail/adidas-says-demand-for-samba-shoes-far-outstrips-supply-1.1699443359705)). Note the ~EUR 1.2bn is a revenue figure (lost sales / sales value of stock), not inventory at cost.

**Wholesale and DTC under Rorsted**
- Rorsted's plan was to push DTC/e-commerce (online sales grew more than 5x under him). Textilwirtschaft wrote at the time of the appointment that retail partners "welcomed" Gulden and hoped for a more partnership-based approach to wholesale, and that he was likely to scale back the DTC push somewhat ([Textilwirtschaft, "Guldens Agenda: Was der neue CEO fuer Adidas bedeutet" (Gulden's agenda: what the new CEO means for adidas), 9 Nov 2022](https://www.textilwirtschaft.de/fashion-management/guldens-agenda-118677)).
- Boersen-Zeitung (9 Apr 2026) drew the parallel directly: both Hill and Gulden are shifting distribution back to wholesale after their predecessors (Donahoe at Nike, Rorsted at adidas) favored DTC. That approach "raised margins quickly but cost shelf space and market share" ([Boersen-Zeitung, "Eine Branche in Tristesse" (A sector in gloom), 9 Apr 2026](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/meinung-analyse/die-aktienkurse-von-nike-adidas-und-puma-sind-weit-von-ihren-vor-fuenf-jahren)).

**Gulden background**
- Norwegian, 57 at appointment, Puma CEO since 2013. Earlier: adidas Senior Vice President Apparel and Accessories 1992-1999; CEO of Pandora; Managing Director of Deichmann (Europe's largest footwear retailer); President of Rack Room Shoes; roles at Helly Hansen; Chairman of Salling Group. Former pro footballer (1. FC Nuernberg, 2. Bundesliga). Rabe: "We are very pleased to welcome Bjorn Gulden back at adidas ... As CEO of Puma, he re-invigorated the brand and led the company to record results." ([adidas press release, 8 Nov 2022](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/bjorn-gulden-to-become-ceo-of-adidas-ag)).
- Gulden on his move: "Ich habe noch sehr viel Energie fuer eine operative Rolle" ("I still have a lot of energy for an operational role") and "Die Sportbranche ist keine Monobrand-Branche" ("The sports industry is not a monobrand industry"). Textilwirtschaft also wrote that "Weltmarktfuehrer Nike immer weiter enteilt" ("world market leader Nike keeps pulling away"), and that Deutsche Bank expected a brand revival over about three years ([Textilwirtschaft, 9 Nov 2022](https://www.textilwirtschaft.de/fashion-management/guldens-agenda-118677)).
- Puma record (snippet only, not opened): one analysis says Puma sales grew at a CAGR of about 12% and its EBIT margin improved by 610bp between 2013 and 2021; Puma 2021 sales were EUR 6.8bn (search summary citing BoF/Reuters, [BoF](https://www.businessoffashion.com/news/news-analysis/pumas-new-chief-faces-tougher-turnaround-task-than-at-pandora/)). I did not verify these against Puma annual reports.
- Market reaction: the adidas share closed at EUR 93.95 on 3 Nov 2022 and EUR 114.04 on 4 Nov 2022 (+21.4%), the day adidas confirmed the talks ([Yahoo Finance daily data, ADS.DE](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/ADS.DE?period1=1661990400&period2=1792000000&interval=1d)). Boersen-Zeitung noted the stock "had nearly halved in 2022" ([Boersen-Zeitung, 13 Jul 2023](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/kapitalmaerkte/die-aufholjagd-von-adidas)).

### Inferences
- Classification: "returnee-outsider". Gulden had adidas DNA from the 1990s and personal ties (he worked with CFO Ohlmeyer at adidas in the 90s, per the 8 Mar 2023 board release below). But he arrived as a sitting rival CEO with an outside operating playbook (Puma: retailer-friendly, product-first, cost-disciplined). Hill is a pure "returning insider" (32 years at Nike, retired 2020) without prior CEO experience. Gulden had already done a turnaround as CEO; Hill had not.
- The share price had already been cut roughly in half and jumped 21% on the appointment news, so the market priced in much of the "new CEO" premium before day 1. Gulden's Jan 2023 starting price (about EUR 127.7) was well off the low.
- Gulden's problem set was more "acute and cleanable" than Nike's: one-off Yeezy loss, Russia exit, excess inventory and a cyclical China collapse. Nike's is a broader loss of product newness and share across several franchises.

### Gaps
- I could not open a primary source for Gulden's Puma track record (sales, EBIT, share price 2013-2022). Puma annual reports would be needed.
- The adidas 2022 headcount and the Rorsted-era DTC share of sales were not extracted.

## Q2. What Gulden did, and when (sequence of actions)

### Takeaway
The sequence was: (1) first 90 days: lowball guidance, inventory clean-up through a conservative wholesale sell-in, and he took over Global Brands himself to speed up decisions; (2) mid-2023: sell the Yeezy stock (not write it off) and donate part of the proceeds, while scaling Samba/Gazelle/Spezial/Campus supply in steps and keeping it below demand; (3) 2023-2024: reward retailers with hot product and rebuild wholesale, put Adizero running and football innovation at the center of Performance, and reinvest in marketing once margins recovered; (4) 2025 onward: formalize "global brand with a local mindset" (local decision-making, local design and sourcing), cut up to 500 HQ jobs, and widen beyond Terrace into low-profile, Superstar, Evo SL, apparel and the World Cup. Executive Board turnover was heavy, but every replacement was an internal or returning adidas person.

### Cited Findings
**Q1 2023 (first quarter)**
- Gulden on his first quarter: "I have spent Q1 working on our product ranges, brainstormed about future innovations, talked to a great number of retailers about improving our cooperations, met suppliers ... and of course started to work on simplifying and speeding up our processes." He also said: "2023 will be a bumpy year with disappointing numbers, where maximizing our short-term financial results is not our goal. It is a transition year to build a strong base for a better 2024 and a good 2025 and beyond." ([Q1 2023 release, 5 May 2023](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/raw/upload/v1705999372/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2023/adidasag_q1_2023results_en_final_ciz6cw.pdf)).
- North America -20% "in line with our conservative sell-in strategy due to the high levels of inventory and discounts in the market". Inventories were "still too high, but already EUR 300 million lower than at the beginning of the year". On Terrace: "we have started to scale up volumes for our Samba, Gazelle and Campus franchises", and "While adidas continued to limit the supply at the beginning of the year, the company is slowly starting to scale its offering as the year progresses." (same source).

### Inferences
- The order matters for Nike: Gulden (a) reset expectations to a very low bar within six weeks, (b) cleaned channel inventory before chasing growth (North America was deliberately shrunk for seven straight quarters), (c) monetized the problem asset (Yeezy) instead of writing it off, which funded the transition year, and (d) only lifted marketing spend once gross margin had recovered (2024, not 2023).
- He centralized first and decentralized later: he took Global Brands himself in March 2023, then formalized "local mindset" and cut HQ jobs in 2025. The HQ cuts are the closest thing to a cost program, and they came only after growth was running at double digits.
- Every board replacement was internal or a returning adidas person (Hoeld, Robertson, Sidokpohou, Kretschmer). That suggests Gulden relied on adidas veterans plus his own outside playbook rather than bringing in a Puma team.
- I could not find the phrase "we will be boring" in any opened source; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- "We will be boring": no opened source contains this quote. It may be paraphrased from Gulden's habit of conservative guidance. Do not use it without a primary source.
- No primary Foot Locker, JD Sports or Intersport statement about the Gulden-era relationship reset was opened. Retailer comments are limited to Snipes at FNAA.
- No primary data on Samba unit volumes or allocation rules. Only company narrative ("limit the supply", "far from covering demand") and the unverified 36Kr "millions of pairs" claim.
- The exact size of HQ job cuts was never confirmed by adidas, and total group headcount change 2022-2025 was not extracted.

## Q3. Quarter-by-quarter metrics, Q4 2022 (baseline) to Q2 2026

### Takeaway
Currency-neutral revenue was flat to slightly negative for the first four Gulden quarters (2023), then double-digit from Q2 2024 in every quarter. Gross margin crossed back above 50% by Q1 2024 and stabilized at 51-52.5%. Operating margin reached 9-11% in non-Q4 quarters by 2025. Q4 is structurally weak at adidas (1.0% in Q4 2024, 2.7% in Q4 2025). Greater China turned positive in Q2 2023 (his second quarter). North America did not grow on a reported basis until Q4 2024 (his eighth quarter). By 2026 the growth engine shifted from footwear (now +1-4%) to apparel (+31-35%).

## Q4. Share price path (Jan 2023 to Oct 2026) and current analyst views

### Takeaway
The adidas share roughly doubled from Gulden's start (EUR 127.70 on 2 Jan 2023) to a peak of EUR 263.10 on 13 Feb 2025. It then gave back most of the gain, even while fundamentals kept improving, falling to EUR 153.25 on 13 Oct 2026 (+20% vs his start, -42% from peak). The de-rating was driven by tariffs, a sector-wide de-rating, worries about lifestyle footwear and the Samba cycle, and an 11.5% one-day fall on 30 Jul 2026 after a Q2 profit miss caused by heavy World Cup marketing. ## Q5. Time to inflection (quarters from Gulden's 1 Jan 2023 start)

### Takeaway
About 3 quarters to the first positive reported c.n. growth, 5 quarters to sustained growth, 6 quarters to double-digit growth, 2 quarters to the first gross margin gain (Yeezy-aided) and 4-5 quarters to a clean gross margin recovery. ## Q6. Critiques and limits: luck vs execution; German press

### Takeaway
A real share of the 2023-2024 recovery came from things Gulden did not create: the Terrace/Samba trend was already building in late 2022 (it predates him, though he scaled it), and the Yeezy stock he chose to sell brought in about EUR 1.4bn revenue and about EUR 500m operating profit over 2023-2024. A rival's (Nike's) wholesale retreat also left shelf space open. The execution was in how he used these: deciding to sell Yeezy rather than write it off, scaling Terrace in controlled steps, cleaning channel inventory, and building the next waves (Evo SL, apparel, low-profile). German press has shifted from praise in 2023 ("Aufholjagd", catch-up race) to a 2026 framing of "strong business, weak share" and criticism of intuitive management and conservative guidance.

## Q8. What is and is not transferable to Nike

### Takeaway
Transferable: expectation-setting (lowball guidance, a declared "transition year"), clean the channel before chasing growth, retailer-first allocation of hot product, controlled scaling of a hit, putting performance innovation (running) at the center as the credibility engine, and local decision-making with a small, fast top team. Not transferable or only partly: the Yeezy windfall, a pre-existing global trend (Terrace) arriving in quarter 1, the 21% "new CEO" stock jump and very low starting margins (easy comparisons), and the scale gap (Nike is about twice adidas's size, so a share shift from Nike to others is not mirrored the other way). Adidas also benefited from Nike's weakness, which Nike cannot benefit from in turn.

### Cited Findings
- Expectation reset: 2023 initial op guide of EUR -700m vs EUR +268m actual, with raises every quarter (see Q2 table and sources).
- Channel clean-up: North America was deliberately shrunk for 7 quarters, and inventory fell almost EUR 1.5bn in 2023 ([FY2023](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/raw/upload/v1710499226/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2023/adidas_ag_fy2023_results_en_final_bmt7gz.pdf)).
- Starting margin base: adidas started from a 3.0% op margin (FY2022) and a 47.3% gross margin ([FY2022](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/raw/upload/v1706003622/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2022/20230308_adidas_ag_fy2022_results_en_xdfsuy.pdf)). The mid-term target is still only "more than 10%" in 2028 ([FY2025](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/image/upload/v1772605768/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2025/Q4/adidasAG_FY_2025_Results_EN_Final_tb9z6m.pdf)).
- Scale: Nike annual revenue above $45bn vs adidas EUR 24.8bn ([Boersen-Zeitung, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/kapitalmaerkte/stark-im-geschaeft-schwach-an-der-boerse)). The North America gap is about $13bn of the about $19bn revenue difference ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/nike-turnaround-adidas-us-opportunity-north-america/803047/)).
- The Gulden playbook has limits even at adidas: lifestyle footwear went from +12% (FY2025) to +2% (Q2 2026), and North America is still called structurally weak ([Q2 2026](https://res.cloudinary.com/confirmed-web/image/upload/v1785388904/adidas-group/investors/financial-publications/2026/Q2/EN/adidasAG_Q2_2026_Results_EN_Final_z7f9t2.pdf); [Boersen-Zeitung, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/kapitalmaerkte/stark-im-geschaeft-schwach-an-der-boerse)).

### Inferences
- Nike under Hill already copies several Gulden moves (wholesale return, clearing classics such as Dunk/AF1/Jordan retro, sport-led running focus). The difference is the absence of an on-trend franchise to sell into the freed shelf space and the absence of a monetizable problem asset. Expect Nike's path to margin recovery to be slower than adidas's 4-5 quarters to gross margin recovery.
- Nike's starting margins were much higher (low-to-mid teens EBIT historically), so its "recovery" is partly back toward a high base. That makes adidas-style percentage-point gains easier to show if gross margin normalizes, but makes the quality-of-growth question (full price vs discount) more important.
- Investor patience at Nike appears lower than at adidas in 2023. adidas got about a 45% share rally in its first six months on hope alone. Nike's share has fallen about 56% in Hill's first two years (Yahoo data), so the "story stock" phase that funded Gulden's patience has not happened for Hill.
- The "playbook risk" visible at adidas in 2026 (a hit cycle fading, heavy marketing for events, conservative guidance read as negative) suggests that even a successful Nike turnaround may hit a de-rating if growth relies on one product cycle.
