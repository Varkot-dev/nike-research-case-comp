## 1. Background: Plank as founder, the Frisk and Linnartz years, and the return

### Takeaway
Plank never really left: he founded UA in 1996, was CEO until 2019, then Executive Chair and Brand Chief from January 2020 to March 2024 while holding majority voting control, and retook the CEO job on April 1, 2024 after Stephanie Linnartz was pushed out after about one year. Unlike Elliott Hill (a retired Nike executive who was never CEO or founder and holds no control stake), Plank is a controlling founder returning to a business whose problems began on his own first watch.

### Cited Findings
- 10-K biography: Plank "has served as President and Chief Executive Officer since April 2024. He served as Executive Chair and Brand Chief from January 2020 through March 2024. Prior to that, he served as Chief Executive Officer and Chair of the Board of Directors from 1996, when he founded our Company, to 2019." - [UA FY2026 10-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000073/ua-20260331.htm)
- The proxy pay-versus-performance table lists the sequence of principal executive officers: Patrik Frisk (fiscal years from April 2020 to March 2023 periods), Colin Browne (2022-23, interim), Stephanie Linnartz (2022-23 through 2024-25 periods), Kevin Plank (2024-25 and 2025-26). - [UA DEF 14A, July 2026 [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000102/ua-20260715.htm)
- On March 13, 2024 UA announced Linnartz would step down and Plank would return as CEO effective April 1, 2024; Linnartz had joined in February 2023 after nearly 30 years at Marriott (most recently president). Bloomberg's headline framed it as Plank "ousting Linnartz after she led just over a year of fitful efforts." Mohamed El-Erian was named board chair. UA stock fell about 10% between March 13 and midday March 19, 2024. Analyst Neil Saunders said the return was "emblematic of a brand that can't quite decide which direction it wants to go in." - [ASI Central [SNIPPET]](https://members.asicentral.com/news/newsletters/promogram/march-2024/plank-returns-as-under-armour-ceo-linnartz-out-after-one-year/); [Bloomberg [SNIPPET]](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-03-13/under-armour-returns-founder-plank-to-ceo-ousting-linnartz); [CNBC [SNIPPET, 403 when opened]](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2024/03/13/kevin-plank-returns-as-under-armour-ceo-mohamed-el-erian-named-board-chair.html)
- Linnartz's own statement said "approximately two-thirds of my direct reports across product, marketing, communications, operations, and the regions are new to their roles," i.e. she was mid-rebuild of the team when removed. - [ASI Central [SNIPPET]](https://members.asicentral.com/news/newsletters/promogram/march-2024/plank-returns-as-under-armour-ceo-linnartz-out-after-one-year/)
- Retail Dive (Aug 19, 2024): Matt Powell said Plank has "always had a problem handing off the reins of the company" and that the board "pretty much signs off" on his changes; Tom Nikic said "the idea of ceding control to somebody else just ended up not being appealing to him," that "Patrik's departure was very poorly received by the market," and that Linnartz was not in the role long enough to be blamed for results. Retail Dive notes that as executive chair since 2020 few major decisions under Frisk, Browne or Linnartz would have been made without Plank's knowledge. - [Retail Dive, "Kevin Plank can't let go of Under Armour" [OPENED]](https://www.retaildive.com/news/kevin-plank-under-armour-turnaround/723957/)
- Origins of the decline under Plank's first tenure: in 2016 executives "pulled forward" future sales, sometimes a third or more of a quarter's sales, to hit growth targets (Plank reportedly wanted 20% annual revenue growth); ex-CFO Chip Molloy said in a deposition "Many people in the company were obsessed with pleasing Kevin" and complained of a "culture of fear." The SEC charged UA; UA paid $9 million in 2021 and a $434 million shareholder settlement in 2024, both without admitting wrongdoing; Plank was barred from being both CEO and chair for at least three years. - [Baltimore Banner, Dec 22, 2025 [OPENED]](https://www.thebanner.com/economy/under-armour-kevin-plank-baltimore-peninsula-BQMMFEBFNJHX3LM7KJ6AF4BULY/)
- Revenue growth fell to 3% in 2017 and stayed under 5% or negative every year since except the 2021 rebound; sales grew 3% in 2022 and fell 3% in 2023. - [Retail Dive [OPENED]](https://www.retaildive.com/news/kevin-plank-under-armour-turnaround/723957/)
- In the quarter just before Plank's return (Q4 FY2024, Jan to Mar 2024) revenue fell 5% to $1.3bn and North America fell 10%; full FY2024 (Apr 2023 to Mar 2024) revenue was $5.7bn (-3%), North America $3.5bn (-8%), adjusted operating income $310m. - [UA Q4 FY2024 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691724000058/exhibit991-fiscal2024q4.htm)

### Inferences
- The founder/insider distinction matters for the Nike analogy: Plank returned to undo problems he created (or at least oversaw as executive chair), and with no one able to remove him. Hill returned as an operator brought in by a board (with Phil Knight's backing) after a non-founder outsider (John Donahoe) failed; he is closer to the "returning insider who was not the architect of the recent strategy" profile that Bingham described as a reason for optimism about Iger (see Section 6).
- The UA revenue problem pre-dates Plank's return by about seven years (2017 onward), so the 2024 reset was attacking a long structural slide, not a one-strategy error. Nike's decline (FY2024 onward) is more recent and more clearly tied to a specific strategy (DTC shift, franchise over-reliance), which is a better fit for Gulden-style "fix the self-inflicted errors" playbooks.

### Gaps
- Official board rationale for removing Linnartz was not found; the company framed it as "stepping down." CNBC and Bloomberg articles were not openable (403/paywall).
- Exact Frisk tenure dates (CEO Jan 2020 to Feb 2023 per my [KNOWLEDGE]) not confirmed from an opened page beyond the proxy's fiscal-year labels.

## 2. What Plank did: the "premium reset," restructuring, people, Curry, marketing, tariffs

### Takeaway
Plank deliberately shrank the business to rebuild a premium position: sharply less discounting (eCommerce down 20 to 27% a quarter in FY2025), about 25% fewer SKUs, tighter distribution, a restructuring plan that grew from $70 to $90m (May 2024) to about $305m (May 2026), a new HQ, a distribution-centre exit, the Curry Brand separation (November 2025) and heavy management churn. Margins improved in FY2025 but tariffs erased the gain in FY2026, and by mid-2026 he was still re-shuffling his own hires.

### Cited Findings
**Strategy statements (dated)**
- May 16, 2024 (first results as returning CEO): "Due to a confluence of factors, including lower wholesale channel demand and inconsistent execution across our business, we are seizing this critical moment to make proactive decisions to build a premium positioning for our brand, which will pressure our top and bottom line in the near term... Over the next 18 months, there is a significant opportunity to reconstitute Under Armour's..." - [UA Q4 FY2024 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691724000058/exhibit991-fiscal2024q4.htm)
- Plank's plan included "cutting SKUs by 25%" and shortening go-to-market timeframes; goal to be "a premium brand of choice." - [Retail Dive [OPENED]](https://www.retaildive.com/news/kevin-plank-under-armour-turnaround/723957/). The Banner instead describes the plan as "cutting inventory by 25% and reducing bargain sales"; the two outlets describe the 25% cut differently (SKUs vs inventory). - [Banner [OPENED]](https://www.thebanner.com/economy/under-armour-kevin-plank-baltimore-peninsula-BQMMFEBFNJHX3LM7KJ6AF4BULY/)
- Plank said Fall-Winter 2025 is when the turnaround should begin to show. - [Banner [OPENED]](https://www.thebanner.com/economy/under-armour-kevin-plank-baltimore-peninsula-BQMMFEBFNJHX3LM7KJ6AF4BULY/)
- Feb 6, 2025: a "shift to a category-led operating model" and a "pivotal new chapter in our marketing strategy... multi-year initiative of storytelling." - [UA Q3 FY2025 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691725000013/exhibit991-fiscal2025q3.htm)
- Aug 8, 2025: "increasing our average selling prices through innovative offerings, optimizing our top-volume programs, and creating a more compelling full price-to-value proposition." - [UA Q1 FY2026 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691725000134/exhibit991-fiscal2026q1.htm)
- Feb 6, 2026: "In North America, we believe the December quarter marked the most challenging phase of our business reset, and we expect greater stability ahead." - [UA Q3 FY2026 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000025/exhibit991-fiscal2026q3.htm)
- May 12, 2026: "Building world-class, modern marketing excellence is now our highest priority"; "As our topline stabilizes in fiscal 2027..." - [UA Q4 FY2026 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000059/exhibit991-fiscal2026q4.htm)
- Aug 7, 2026: "By simplifying the business, we are operating with greater discipline and better positioned to protect profitability... with the goal of driving a more premium Under Armour that will consistently earn demand at full price." - [UA Q1 FY2027 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000108/exhibit991-fiscal2027q1.htm)

**Pulling back from promotions (the visible mechanism)**
- eCommerce revenue fell 25% (Q1 FY25), 21% (Q2), 20% (Q3), 27% (Q4) "due to planned decreases in promotional activities"; full FY2025 eCommerce -23%. - UA FY2025 quarterly 8-Ks [OPENED] ([Q1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691724000114/exhibit991-fiscal2025q1.htm), [Q2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691724000164/exhibit991-fiscal2025q2.htm), [Q3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691725000013/exhibit991-fiscal2025q3.htm), [Q4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691725000058/exhibit991-fiscal2025q4.htm))

**Restructuring plan escalation (the "Fiscal 2025 Restructuring Plan")**
- May 2024: $70m to $90m pre-tax charges approved. - [Q4 FY2024 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691724000058/exhibit991-fiscal2024q4.htm)
- September 2024: raised to $140m to $160m, mainly for exiting the Rialto, California distribution centre. - [Q2 FY2025 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691724000164/exhibit991-fiscal2025q2.htm)
- Q3 FY2025 included a $28m impairment "related to exiting our previous global headquarters." - [Q3 FY2025 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691725000013/exhibit991-fiscal2025q3.htm)
- By Feb 2026: plan "now expected to cost up to $255 million." - [Q3 FY2026 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000025/exhibit991-fiscal2026q3.htm)
- By Aug 2026: $266m incurred to date; total "approximately $305 million"; "substantially complete by December 31, 2026." - [Q1 FY2027 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000108/exhibit991-fiscal2027q1.htm). The 10-K table totals $305.0m ($171.5m FY2026, $89.2m FY2025, $44.3m FY2027 expected). - [FY2026 10-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000073/ua-20260331.htm)

**Headcount and HQ**
- About 600 fewer employees in 2025, a 4% cut. The scaled-back new HQ at Baltimore Peninsula opened in 2024 with about 1,500 employees, versus Plank's earlier vision of 10,000 across several towers. - [Banner [OPENED]](https://www.thebanner.com/economy/under-armour-kevin-plank-baltimore-peninsula-BQMMFEBFNJHX3LM7KJ6AF4BULY/)
- As of March 31, 2026 UA had about 14,100 "teammates," of whom about 10,100 were in stores and about 6,200 full-time. - [FY2026 10-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000073/ua-20260331.htm)

**Management hires and churn (all from FY2026 10-K and July 2026 proxy [OPENED])**
- Eric Liedtke (ex-Adidas, CEO of a prior company per 10-K) joined August 2024 as EVP Brand Strategy; Brand President January 2025 to January 2026; Chief Marketing Officer and EVP Strategy from February 2, 2026; then on June 1, 2026 "transitioned to the role of Strategic Advisor to the Chief Executive Officer" (i.e. moved out of the CMO line role four months after the May 2026 "marketing is highest priority" statement). - [Proxy [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000102/ua-20260715.htm); [10-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000073/ua-20260331.htm)
- Yassine Saidi, Chief Product Officer, "left on February 2, 2026 to pursue other opportunities," retained as consultant ("Special Advisor, Design and Expression") through February 2027. - [Proxy [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000102/ua-20260715.htm)
- Kara Trent: President of the Americas Feb 2024 to Jan 2026; Chief Merchandising Officer since February 2, 2026. - [10-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000073/ua-20260331.htm)
- CFO: David Bergman (CFO since November 2017) stepped down February 11, 2026; Reza Taleghani (CFO of Samsonite, Nov 2018 to Feb 2026) joined February 11, 2026 at $825,000 base plus $250,000 signing bonus. - [Proxy [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000102/ua-20260715.htm); [10-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000073/ua-20260331.htm)
- Executive officers listed at 10-K filing (May 2026): Plank, Taleghani, Shawn Curran (supply chain), Liedtke, Adam Peake (President of the Americas), Mehri Shadman (CLO), Kara Trent. - [10-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000073/ua-20260331.htm)

**Curry Brand separation**
- UA and Stephen Curry announced a split on November 13, 2025 after about 12 years; Curry keeps Curry Brand; the Curry 13 (February 2026) is the last UA Curry shoe, with product continuing through October 2026; Plank framed it as letting "two strong teams do what they do best." - [Search summaries of CNBC/theGrio/Inside Retail [SNIPPET]](https://thegrio.com/2025/11/14/under-armour-and-stephen-curry-split); [Inside Retail [SNIPPET]](https://insideretail.us/under-armour-curry-to-part-ways-after-12-years/)
- Cost: FY2026 restructuring included non-cash inventory reserves for the Curry separation and "$69.7 million of non-cash contract termination costs, primarily relating to the separation of the Curry Brand." - [FY2026 10-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000073/ua-20260331.htm)

**Marketing and tariffs**
- FY2025: about $25m extra marketing added after better-than-expected profits (Nov 2024). - [Q2 FY2025 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691724000164/exhibit991-fiscal2025q2.htm)
- FY2027 guide includes about $30m incremental marketing. - [Q4 FY2026 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000059/exhibit991-fiscal2026q4.htm)
- Tariffs: FY2026 gross margin fell 240bp to 45.5% "primarily due to higher tariffs." UA then received IEEPA tariff refunds in Q1 FY2027, lifting gross margin 590bp to 54.1%; about 150bp of the full-year FY2027 margin improvement and about $70m of adjusted operating income is that one-off refund. - [Q4 FY2026 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000059/exhibit991-fiscal2026q4.htm); [Q1 FY2027 8-K [OPENED]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000108/exhibit991-fiscal2027q1.htm)
- Balance sheet: refinanced $600m 2026 notes with $400m new notes due 2030, revolver borrowings ($200m outstanding at June 30, 2026) and cash; cash fell from $859m (Mar 2024) to $396m (Jun 2026). Buybacks of $115m of Class C stock through Sept 2025 under a $500m programme. - UA 8-Ks [OPENED] ([Q4 FY24](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691724000058/exhibit991-fiscal2024q4.htm), [Q2 FY26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691725000193/exhibit991-fiscal2026q2.htm), [Q1 FY27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336917/000133691726000108/exhibit991-fiscal2027q1.htm))

### Inferences
- The playbook (cut promotions, clean distribution, fewer SKUs, raise ASPs, more marketing) is essentially the same playbook Hill is running at Nike and Gulden ran at Adidas. The difference in outcome so far is not the playbook but whether there is a product and brand heat to sell at full price once the discounting stops.
- Repeated plan expansions ($70-90m to ~$305m), annual re-orgs of the people Plank himself hired (Liedtke three roles in 22 months, Saidi out after ~18 months as CPO), and a mid-turnaround CFO change are classic signs of a turnaround still searching for its model. Investors should watch Nike for the same tell: rising restructuring estimates and churn among Hill's own appointees.

### Gaps
- Exact date and title of Liedtke's and Saidi's original hire announcements (Saidi joined ~2024 per my [KNOWLEDGE]) not opened from press releases.
- A 2024-2025 layoff count from WARN notices or a company announcement was not found; only the Banner's "about 600 fewer employees, 4%" figure.
- No opened source quantifies the Curry Brand's revenue.

## 3. Quarter-by-quarter metrics since the return (all from UA 8-K Exhibit 99.1 press releases [OPENED])

### Takeaway
Ten quarters after his return, revenue has fallen in every quarter, North America has declined every quarter (-5% to -14%), FY2026 revenue was $5.0bn (-4%) versus $5.7bn in the last pre-return year, adjusted operating income has roughly halved ($310m FY2024 to $107m FY2026), and in August 2026 UA cut FY2027 to a mid-single-digit revenue decline. The earlier figures to verify are confirmed: FY2026 revenue $5.0bn (-4%), North America -8%, net loss $496m, gross margin -240bp, initial FY2027 guide "decline slightly."

## 6. Other returning or insider CEO cases, and Puma under an outsider

### Takeaway

## 7. Academic frame: what predicts a successful return?

### Takeaway
The best-known study (Bingham, Hendricks, Howell and Kolev, MIT Sloan Management Review, 2020) finds boomerang CEOs underperform by about 10% a year, with founders (44% of returnees vs 4% of all CEOs) doing even worse; weaker outcomes are linked to founders, fast-changing industries and poor succession planning, while aggressive innovation helps. Plank matches all three negative predictors; Hill is not a founder and was never CEO, so he is technically not a "boomerang CEO" in the study's definition at all.

### Cited Findings
- Sample: 6,429 CEOs of S&P 1500 companies, 1992 to 2017; 167 boomerang CEOs (former CEO returns to the top job). Boomerang-led firms had annualized stock returns (including dividends) about 10% lower than non-boomerang firms and lagged their industries. - [Fortune, Mar 16, 2020 [OPENED]](https://fortune.com/2020/03/16/return-ceos-effect-on-stock)
- Founders were 44% of boomerang CEOs vs 4% of all CEOs, and returning founders performed even worse on average; Travis Howell: a founder's entrepreneurial vision may not fit a mature company, and skills for growing a new business do not necessarily transfer. - [Fortune [OPENED]](https://fortune.com/2020/03/16/return-ceos-effect-on-stock)
- Worse outcomes: re-hiring often signals "a failure of CEO succession planning"; returns in fast-changing industries were much worse than in stable sectors; some returns came amid new crises. Better outcomes: aggressive innovation. Only 2 of 167 boomerangs were women. - [Fortune [OPENED]](https://fortune.com/2020/03/16/return-ceos-effect-on-stock)
- Reuters reports the figure as 10.1% lower performance, with founder status, dynamic industries and neglected succession planning linked to weaker results; authors Christopher Bingham, Bradley Hendricks, Travis Howell and Kalin Kolev, MIT Sloan Management Review, 2020. - [Reuters via IBTimes [OPENED]](https://www.ibtimes.com/boomerang-ceos-dont-always-work-out-disney-hopes-this-one-bucks-trend-3638902); [UNC Kenan Institute [OPENED]](https://kenaninstitute.unc.edu/news-media/schultzs-return-may-leave-bitter-taste-for-starbucks-investors/)
- Chen and Hambrick (2012, Strategic Management Journal), "CEO replacement in turnaround situations: executive (mis)fit and its performance implications": argues that new CEO characteristics should fit the turnaround context (e.g. outsiders fit better when the firm's strategy needs to change). - [KNOWLEDGE, not opened; verify before citing]

### Inferences
- Scoring Plank on the study's predictors: founder (yes, negative), dynamic industry (athletic apparel with fast-shifting fashion/athleisure trends: arguably yes, negative), succession failure (three CEOs in four years then the founder: yes, negative), aggressive innovation (no breakthrough product evident; footwear shrinking: negative). Plank fits the failure profile on every predictor.
- Scoring Hill: not a founder; not a former CEO (so not a boomerang in the strict sense, but a "returning insider" after a four-year retirement); Nike's succession failure (Donahoe) is a negative; dynamic industry is shared; innovation pipeline is the open question. On the evidence, Hill sits closer to Gulden (returning industry insider, not the architect of the failed strategy, with outside-CEO experience) than to Plank, but the outcome will hinge on product innovation, which the literature identifies as the main positive predictor.
- Practical tells that would move Hill toward the Plank pattern: repeated "trough is behind us" calls followed by guidance cuts; restructuring cost estimates that keep rising; churn among his own hires; margin improvement without revenue stabilization; continued footwear declines.
