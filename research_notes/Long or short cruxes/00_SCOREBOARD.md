# Nike LONG or SHORT: the scoreboard

Written 2026-10-14. Stock $35.35 (Oct 12, 2026). Submission Nov 1, finals Nov 19, Investor Day Nov 16 to 17.

This page sums up eight questions. Each one was investigated, then checked by a skeptic. The full notes are in this folder, one file per question. Tags: OPENED means we read the source itself, SNIPPET means we only saw a search summary, REPO means it came from earlier work in this repo, and DERIVED means our own arithmetic.

## 1. Bottom line

**Lean SHORT, with low to moderate confidence. Honestly, it is close, and one question decides it.**

- **The business has not turned.** All six questions about the business point the same way: North America consumer demand, factory orders, China, discounting, lifestyle classics and running quality. None of them shows a bottom yet. Every month of data from June to September 2026 is still flat or getting worse. Nike's own management says the pressure lasts "into fiscal 28".
- **But most of that bad news is already in Nike's guidance and in Wall Street's numbers.** Factory orders (about -8.5% in USD) match Q2 consensus revenue (-10.1%). Nike has already told investors that China gets worse than -26%. The skeptics marked down four of the six business findings from "strong" to "moderate" for this reason: they confirm the guide, they do not show a miss.
- **The one question that decides it is FY28.** Consensus FY28 EPS is $1.65. That needs about +28% EPS growth on about +1% revenue, while Nike says most cost savings land in FY29 to FY30. The stock trades at about 21.5x FY28 EPS against a peer median of about 13x. If the Investor Day (Nov 16 to 17) frames FY28 below about $1.50 EPS, or revenue below flat, the SHORT works. If it holds FY28 and sets a margin target above about 9% for FY29, the SHORT is in trouble.
- **Why we lean SHORT and not neutral:** our own business evidence points to FY28 coming in at or below consensus, not above it. Three findings say the problems run into FY28: China ("bleed into fiscal 28", and the reset extends "beyond fiscal 2027" in the 10-Q), the Jordan retro cut, and aged sportswear cutting future order books. Factory partners expect Nike's 2027 orders to be only flat to slightly up. Nothing we found supports a FY28 that beats consensus.
- **What could make the SHORT wrong:** short interest is at a record 99.2M shares, 6.7% of shares outstanding (FINRA, Sep 30, 2026, OPENED), which means a crowded short. Tariffs now in force are 10% to 12.5%, below Nike's 15% plan (USTR Section 301 notice, Jul 2026, OPENED), so gross margin could surprise upward. And history says a company whose gross margin keeps rising while revenue falls is a coin flip from here, not a clear short.

**In one sentence:** the business is still getting worse and FY28 numbers look too high, but the market already expects a bad FY27, so this is a SHORT on FY28 expectations, not on FY27 news, and Investor Day will settle it.

## 3. Which questions carry the most weight, and why

1. **Expectations (Q7) carries the most weight.** A stock moves on surprises, not on bad news everyone already knows. Questions 1 to 6 tell us the direction of the business. Q7 tells us whether that direction is already in the price. For FY27 it is. For FY28 it probably is not fully, and that is the only gap we found.
2. **China (Q3) and lifestyle (Q5) come next,** because they are the two findings that reach into FY28, which is where the gap is. China is the only "strong" rating left after the skeptic review.
3. **Factory orders (Q2) are the best early warning.** They are monthly, and October data lands before the finals. They tell us about Nike's buying, though not about whether Nike beats or misses its guide.
4. **NA sell-through (Q1), discounting (Q4) and running (Q6) matter less for the call.** They are solid evidence that the business has not turned, but Nike has already disclosed most of it, and none of them can show a miss against guidance on its own.
5. **History (Q8) is a tiebreaker only.** It says the outcome depends on gross margin, and Nike's own guide implies gross margin flat to up in Q2 and Q3. That pulls slightly away from SHORT.

## 4. What is still unknown, and when new data could flip it

**Biggest unknowns:**
- Nike's FY28 plan and five-year targets (only Investor Day answers this).
- Whether partner inventory unwinds faster than Nike's guided roughly 400 bps Q2 headwind.
- Whether Feng Tay's decline means fewer Nike pairs, or orders moving to other factories, or lower prices.
- Whether China consumer demand (as opposed to Nike's reported sales) is bottoming. Pou Sheng's September -2.5% is the only hint, and it is one month.
- Whether Nike's guide still assumes 15% tariffs or the 10% to 12.5% now in force (about $0.05 to $0.10 of FY27 EPS, DERIVED).
- No public Nike-specific US point-of-sale data exists for 2026.

**How we weighed it overall:** we did not average the eight. Six business questions all say "not yet turned", but four of them only confirm what Nike has already guided. The call rests on whether FY28 consensus is too high. The business evidence says yes, but tariff relief and a crowded short say the margin for error is thin. That is why the answer is lean SHORT with low to moderate confidence, and why Investor Day on Nov 16 to 17 matters more than anything else on the calendar.
