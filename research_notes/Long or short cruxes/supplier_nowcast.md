# Supplier nowcast: what Nike's factories say about Q2 and Q3 FY27

**Written:** Oct 14, 2026. **Question:** are Nike's footwear orders at its factories turning up (long) or still falling (short) going into Q2 FY27 (Sep to Nov 2026) and Q3 FY27 (Dec 2026 to Feb 2027)?

Labels: OPENED = I opened the primary page or file this session. SNIPPET = seen only in a search result summary. MIRROR = FinMind reproduction of Taiwan MOPS data (matches MOPS wherever checked). DERIVED = my arithmetic.

## 1. Pre-registered decision rule (written before pulling new data)

- **Points LONG:** Feng Tay (Nike about 85% to 90% of sales) revenue **in USD** turns from Q1 FY27's -8.8% toward flat or positive across Jul, Aug and Sep 2026 (a clear improving trend of at least 3 to 4 points), and the gap between Feng Tay and non-Nike footwear suppliers narrows. Qualitative order commentary (Feng Tay, Yue Yuen) says orders are recovering.
- **Points SHORT:** Feng Tay USD revenue stays at or below about -8% YoY through September, with no sequential improvement beyond normal seasonality, while non-Nike suppliers grow (showing the weakness is Nike-specific, not industry-wide), and order commentary stays cautious.
- **Neutral:** mixed months, or Feng Tay weakness explained by FX, base effects or non-Nike customers.

## 2. Result in one paragraph

Orders are **still falling, and the weakness is Nike-specific**. Feng Tay's USD revenue fell 8.0% in July, 8.5% in August and 8.8% in September 2026 (DERIVED from MOPS/Feng Tay IR NT$ values and average TWD/USD), with no improvement through the quarter. Over the same months suppliers whose main customers are adidas, HOKA and On grew (Lai Yih +9.1% in September in USD; Fulgent Sun +65%), and Vietnam footwear exports were up about 1% year to date. After stripping out Feng Tay's growing non-Nike customer (Salomon, double-digit growth), Feng Tay's Nike business is running about -9% to -12% in USD. That is roughly the pace Nike's own full-year guidance already implies for Q2 to Q4 (-8% to -11%, DERIVED below), so the factories say "no turn yet" (SHORT on the decisive question), but they do **not** yet show a miss against guidance.

So Feng Tay's Nike run-rate (-9% to -12%) is inside to slightly worse than the guided path. It confirms "still falling" but is not, by itself, evidence of a guidance miss.

### 3g. How good is Feng Tay as a lead? (DERIVED from `nike_fiscal_quarter_alignment.csv`)

Regressing Nike Brand footwear YoY on the prior quarter's Feng Tay USD YoY, FY2016 onward excluding FY2020 to FY2022 (n = 25): slope 0.38, intercept +0.75, residual standard deviation 7.1 points; signs agree in 18 of 25 quarters. Fitted Q2 FY27 footwear from Q1 FY27's -8.8%: -2.6%, plus or minus about 7 points. The repo's 0.68 correlation is real but the magnitude mapping is loose, and in FY2025 footwear fell 11% to 13% while Feng Tay was near flat. Conclusion: use Feng Tay for **direction** of orders, not for a point estimate of Nike revenue.

## 5. Alternative explanations tested

1. **FX translation makes Feng Tay look weak.** Ruled out, the opposite: TWD weakness flatters NT$ figures; in USD the decline is larger (-8% to -9%), Section 3c.
2. **Base effect (tough 2025 comparisons).** Partly. September 2025 was +4.1% USD, July and August 2025 were negative in NT$; on a two-year basis Jul to Sep run -6.7%, -5.8%, -5.1%, a slight easing but still negative, Section 3d. Does not change "no turn".
3. **Seasonality makes September look bad.** Ruled out: September / summer ratio 0.901 equals 2025, Section 3d.
4. **Industry-wide footwear slump, not Nike.** Ruled out: non-Nike-led footwear suppliers grew in the same months (Lai Yih +1.3% Jul-Sep USD and +9.1% September; Fulgent Sun +32.7%); Vietnam footwear exports +1% YTD. Weakness concentrates at the Nike-dedicated factory.
5. **Feng Tay's non-Nike customers drag it down.** Ruled out: the non-Nike customer (Salomon) is growing double digits, so Nike's part is worse than the total, Section 3e.
6. **Nike moving orders from Feng Tay to other factories (allocation shift, so Nike total is fine).** Not fully ruled out. Against it: Nike's own Q1 FY27 footwear was -6.2% and guidance implies -8% to -11% for the rest of the year, consistent with Feng Tay; Yue Yuen (Nike's other big shoe maker, multi-brand) only turned positive in September, one month, and its customer split is not disclosed. Settles it: Feng Tay Q3 2026 results (pairs and ASP, about mid-November) and Nike's Q2 FY27 footwear number.
7. **Price, not volume (Feng Tay giving price concessions or tariff cost-sharing).** Not ruled out from monthly data. Feng Tay does not publish monthly pairs. Either way it is lower Nike spend at the factory. Settles it: Feng Tay Q3 2026 financial report pairs and ASP.
8. **Shipment timing (typhoon, holidays, port delays).** Ruled out as the main cause: all three months are at -8% to -9%, not one bad month.
9. **The September pickup at Pou Chen / Yue Yuen means Nike is turning.** Not supported: Yue Yuen is multi-brand (largest customer 18% to 26%), one month only, and its own August 12 outlook says visibility "yet to improve". Lai Yih and Fulgent Sun show non-Nike brands driving the industry uptick.

## 6. Verdict

- **Q2 FY27 (Sep to Nov 2026):** factory evidence says Nike footwear orders are still falling at about -9% to -12% in USD (Feng Tay Nike part, Jul to Sep), with no improvement month by month. Points SHORT on the decisive question. In line with, not worse than, the guided path.
- **Q3 FY27 (Dec 2026 to Feb 2027):** goods for this quarter are produced roughly Oct to Dec. The only forward data: analysts relaying Feng Tay expect 2H26 YoY "roughly the same" as Jan to Jul, and 2027 orders "still in negotiation" (MoneyDJ, Sep 9). Yue Yuen says visibility has not improved. No evidence of a turn; October revenue (published about Nov 10) is the first hard read.
- **Strength:** strong for "orders not turning up yet" and "the weakness is Nike-specific"; weak as evidence of a guidance miss, because the run rate matches the guide and the Feng Tay to Nike revenue mapping is loose (residual SD 7 points).

## 7. What would change the call

- Feng Tay October 2026 (about Nov 10) and November (about Dec 10) USD YoY better than -5% would be the first sign of a turn (LONG). Worse than -10% would suggest Q3 FY27 below the guided path (stronger SHORT).
- Feng Tay Q3 2026 results (mid-November): pairs vs ASP split.
- Nike Investor Day (Nov 16 to 17) order-book commentary for Spring 2027.

### B. One input is off, and it makes the SHORT reading slightly stronger

- The "Nike 88% to 90% of Feng Tay" figure is press (Commercial Times). Feng Tay's audited FY2025 report shows its single largest footwear customer at NT$69.6bn of NT$83.5bn external revenue, **83.3%** (repo `customer_concentration.csv`, VERIFIED from fengtay.com 2025Q4.pdf). Receivables from that customer fell from 66% (Dec 2025) to **60%** (Jun 2026) of the total (2026Q2 report, VERIFIED in repo).
- Redoing Section 3e with 83% Nike (DERIVED): non-Nike flat gives Nike part -10.1%; non-Nike +10% gives -12.1%; +20% gives -14.1%. So Nike's run rate at Feng Tay is about -10% to -14% in USD, not -9% to -12%.

### C. Evidence the investigator under-weighted that points the OTHER way (or toward neutral)

1. **Yue Yuen's turn is real, not just a base effect.** Two-year stacks of Yue Yuen manufacturing (DERIVED from HKEX monthly announcements in repo): Jul -9.6%, Aug -11.1%, Sep +2.5%. Nike is very likely Yue Yuen's largest customer (24% of 2025 sales, unnamed in the annual report; Nike is named first among its brands). This does not prove Nike is turning: if Nike were -10% at Yue Yuen, the other 76% would need about +12%, which is plausible given Lai Yih and Fulgent Sun. But it keeps "Nike moving orders between factories" open.
2. **Supplier consensus for 2027 is flat, not down.** MoneyDJ "《DJ在線》Nike力拚走出低潮，台廠訂單止穩、真正復甦看後年", 林昕潔, Aug 25, 2026, OPENED (moneydj.com NewsViewer a=db9de9ff-6762-4d82-b271-cf167550a810): "供應商普遍預期，Nike明(2027)年下單量可能較2026年持平至小幅成長，真正恢復較明顯成長動能，預估要到2028年." Paiho: Nike share of 1H26 revenue 16.1%, "與2025年同期相當，顯示Nike相關材料訂單已逐步止穩". Eclat: Nike apparel orders "維持個位數成長". Sheng Chiang (6768): "北美市場及跑鞋需求已逐步回升". These are analyst and supplier views relayed by press, not orders. Note the headline itself: orders "stabilising", real recovery in 2028.
3. **The repo's own regression implies Q2 footwear better than guidance.** Recomputed: lag-1 fit (n = 25, r = 0.67, slope 0.38) gives Q2 FY27 Nike footwear about -2.6%; concurrent fit is weak (r = 0.43, signs agree 15 of 25). Against consensus Q2 revenue of -10.1% (`expectations_gap.md`), that would be a beat. I do not trust it: the residual SD is 7 points, and the repo's Test 1 shows Feng Tay fell 10% to 23% in 2023 while Nike footwear grew, and was near flat in FY2025 while Nike footwear fell 11% to 13%. Feng Tay tracks Nike's buying (inventory cycle), not end demand. That cuts both ways and is the main reason this signal cannot be decisive for the stock.
4. **Nike inventory is not bloated.** Aug 31, 2026 inventory $7,846m vs $8,114m a year earlier, -3.3% (REPORT.md 5.6, from 10-Qs). Last year's May to Aug build was +8% ($7,489m to $8,114m, per repo); this year +4.6%. Buying below sales with lean inventory reads as discipline, which in 2023 preceded a gross margin recovery, not a revenue collapse.

### D. What the factory data mean for the pitch (the point the original verdict understates)

- Feng Tay's Nike run rate (-10% to -14% USD, Jul to Sep) sits at or slightly below Q2 FY27 consensus revenue of -10.1% and Piper's -11% (`expectations_gap.md`, OPENED there). Footwear is roughly two thirds of Nike Brand revenue, so a 2 to 3 point gap is within noise.
- For Q3 FY27, the only forward read is analyst commentary: 2H26 Feng Tay YoY about the same as Jan to Jul (NT$ -3.0% YTD Jul, MIRROR). With TWD now about 2% to 4% weaker than Oct to Dec 2025, that is roughly -5% to -7% in USD for Oct to Dec production. Consensus implies H2 FY27 revenue about -6.7%. In line again.
- So the factories answer the literal question (no turn, still falling, Nike-specific) but they do not create a variant view against consensus in either direction.

### E. Skeptic verdict

- **Direction:** I agree. Orders are still falling and the weakness is Nike-specific (Feng Tay flat at about -8.5% USD for three months while Yue Yuen, Lai Yih and Fulgent Sun all turned up in September). Points SHORT on "are orders turning up?".
- **Strength:** downgrade from strong to **moderate**. Reasons: (1) the run rate matches consensus and guidance, so it is priced; (2) Feng Tay's link to Nike revenue is loose and inventory-driven; (3) Yue Yuen's September inflection and the supplier view of flat 2027 orders leave a credible path to stabilisation by calendar 1H27.
- **What would flip it:** Feng Tay October 2026 (MOPS, about Nov 10) in USD. Using 83% Nike share: total better than -4% while Yue Yuen stays positive means Nike's part has improved to about -6% or better, a turn (LONG lean). Total worse than -10% with Yue Yuen and Lai Yih positive would mean Nike's cut is deepening against a recovering industry, implying Q3 FY27 below consensus (SHORT strengthens). Yue Yuen's Q3 2026 pairs (results about mid-November) and Feng Tay's Q3 2026 report would test the order-shift and price-versus-volume explanations.

### What the new data say about the two legs of this crux

**Leg 1: "Orders are still falling, not turning up."** Confirmed by a different source. Feng Tay USD on a two-year stack (DERIVED from the hunt panel): Apr to Jun 2026 -6.4%, Jul -6.7%, Aug -5.8%, Sep -5.1%, Jul to Sep -5.9%. That is a drift of about 1 point, not a turn. Independent check: Nike's own reported footwear revenue, Q3+Q4 FY26 vs two years earlier, -11.8% against Feng Tay -7.7% over the matching quarters (checker, trade_imports README). Feng Tay is falling less than Nike footwear, so the "Nike moved orders away from Feng Tay" explanation (Section 5, item 6) now runs the wrong way: if anything Feng Tay is holding share inside Nike, and the decline is Nike's buying, not supplier allocation. That alternative is largely ruled out.

**Leg 2: "The weakness is Nike-specific (industry is growing)."** Weakened for the current quarter. The original note and the skeptic leaned on one-year comparisons (Lai Yih +1.3%, Fulgent Sun +32.7% in Jul to Sep). On a two-year stack, which removes the 2025 tariff front-loading base, Jul to Sep 2026 reads: Feng Tay -5.9%, Lai Yih (adidas, HOKA) -5.7%, Pou Chen -7.1%, US footwear imports (Jul only, the last Comtrade month) -12.2% world and -7.8% on Nike's VN/ID/CN sourcing mix. Only Fulgent Sun (On) is up (+52%). In Apr to Jun the two-year gap was large (Feng Tay -6.4% vs Nike-mix US imports +11.7%, Lai Yih +13.4%), so Nike did trail the pipeline through H1 2026 (structural share loss: H1 2026 vs H1 2024 Feng Tay -6.9% vs +9% to +26% on correlated import benchmarks). But in the quarter that feeds Q2 FY27, the industry slowed down to Nike's pace rather than Nike falling behind further. The checker also puts the one-year holiday gap (-7.1 points) at only the 25th percentile of its own history.

**Side reads.** Nike inventory growth minus sales growth is +1.2pp vs a peer median of -1.35pp (peers_reported, re-labelled weak SHORT by the checker): not bloated, not clean, so no sign of a forced order cut beyond what is already guided. Hiring shows a product-creation rebuild (US product share of postings 32% to 44%) with a 12 to 18 month lag to shelves: nothing that would show up in Q2 or Q3 FY27 factory orders. Macro (checker-corrected): Nike NA footwear units ran at or above real PCE in the last four quarters, so the US unit pull is not collapsing either.

### Does the verdict move?
