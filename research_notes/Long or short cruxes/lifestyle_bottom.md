# Crux: Are Nike's lifestyle classics (Dunk, AF1, AJ1, Jordan retro) near a bottom?

Prepared 2026-10-14. Labels: OPENED = fetched and read in this session. OPENED (repo) = fetched by an earlier session and stored in this repo, not re-fetched. SNIPPET = search-result summary only. [my arithmetic] = computed by me. No em-dashes.

## 0. Pre-registered decision rules (written 2026-10-14 before any new data was gathered)

Decision: "the classics drag shrinks within 2 to 3 quarters (LONG)" versus "the drag has further to fall (SHORT)".

Points LONG:
- L1. Timing: the classics cuts began in Q4 FY24 / Q1 FY25 and Nike's own dollar headwind per quarter is visibly falling (for example about $1B in Q4 FY25, then smaller each quarter), with no new large franchise cut announced that starts after Q1 FY27.
- L2. Remaining base: the classics base left after the cuts is small relative to revenue (say under 10% of FY26 revenue), so even a further 30% decline costs under 3 points of growth.
- L3. Resale (StockX, GOAT): trade volume and prices for the mass classics (Panda Dunk, white AF1, AJ1) have stopped falling in 2026 versus 2025, i.e. prices flat or up and sales counts flat or up on comparable models.
- L4. Google Trends: US and worldwide search for Dunk, Air Force 1 and Jordan 1 is flat year on year in mid to late 2026 (YoY decline narrowing toward zero).
- L5. Replacement: new lifestyle styles (P-6000, V5 Runner, Vomero 5, Air Max, etc.) show rising search and resale/retail traction large enough that Nike or retailers say they offset a meaningful part of the classics loss.
- L6. Retailers (Foot Locker/Dick's, JD, Genesco, others) say classics inventory is clean and the comparisons ease.

Points SHORT:
- S1. Nike announces or is executing a NEW cut (for example the Jordan retro reset) that starts at or after Q1 FY27, so the drag annualizes no earlier than Q1 FY28 or later.
- S2. Remaining exposed base (classics plus Jordan retro plus aged sportswear) is still large (well above 10% of revenue) and falling at double digits.
- S3. Resale prices and sales counts for the classics are still falling YoY in 2026, and below-retail trading is spreading.
- S4. Search interest for the classics is still falling at a steep YoY rate (worse than -15%) with no narrowing.
- S5. New styles are small, flat or fading in search and resale, or are themselves already discounted, so they cannot refill the hole.
- S6. Retailers describe legacy silhouettes as still over-inventoried or say Nike lifestyle remains weak into holiday 2026.

Neutral / unresolved: signals split (for example classics drag shrinking in dollars but a new Jordan cut starting), or data too thin.

## 1. Verdict (after the data)

**SHORT, strong.** The Dunk piece of the drag is close to a bottom in dollars (it can only cost about $200M a quarter more and laps in Q1 FY28), and Air Force 1 has bottomed. But the classics drag is not shrinking in total within 2 to 3 quarters, because (a) the Jordan retro cut is new and mostly in the future, (b) Nike's own October guide implies the non-China decline gets worse, not better, in Q2 to Q4 FY27, (c) search demand for Jordan 1, Jordan 4 and Dunk is still falling 23% to 31% a year in the US with no narrowing, and (d) the replacement styles are small in search, partly flat or fading, and as heavily discounted on nike.com as the classics. Pre-registered rules hit: S1, S2, S4, S5, S6 fully; S3 mostly (Jordan brand resale prices up but AJ1 and AJ4 releases still trade below retail). LONG rules hit: L1 partly (Dunk dollars shrinking), L3 partly (StockX Jordan brand trades +6% H1 2026).

Revenue (10-Qs and 10-K via `data/nike_filing_facts.csv`, OPENED repo): FY26 $46,398M; Q1 FY26 $11,720M; Q2 to Q4 FY26 = 12,427 + 11,279 + 10,972 = $34,678M. Q1 FY27 $11,213M.

Strip out the parts that are not lifestyle (all assumptions mine): Q2 one-off lap "approximately 400 basis points" of Q2 (about $500M, Nike); Greater China at its Q1 rate of -26% on $4,335M (about $1,130M); Converse at about -28% on $808M (about $230M). Remaining decline needed in NIKE Brand ex China: about $0.8B to $1.8B on a base of about $29.5B, i.e. **-2.8% to -6.2%**. The same measure in Q1 FY27 was **-0.8%** (NIKE Brand ex Greater China $9,772M vs $9,850M). So the guide implies the non-China NIKE Brand decline gets 2 to 5 points worse over the next three quarters. That is where Sportswear and Jordan sit (Q1: "a high single-digit drag on consolidated NIKE").

Alternatives for that deterioration, and status:
- World Cup boost in Q1 (Jun to Jul 2026) rolls off. Real, but Football is a minority of Performance; it cannot explain 2 to 5 points of NIKE Brand. Partly explains, not fully. Remains open in size.
- New CFO setting a low bar ("reasonable and attainable", Denton). Cannot be ruled out. Even at the top of the range (-7%) the implied rest-of-year decline (-7.9%) is worse than Q1. A conservatism cushion of 1 to 2 points still leaves no improvement. Partly ruled out.
- China worse than -26%. Possible; if China fell 40% the non-China decline needed would ease by about $0.6B, still about -0.8% to -4.2%, no better than Q1. Does not reverse the conclusion.

## 4. How big the remaining classics base is [my arithmetic on Nike statements]

- Q4 FY25: >30% decline = "almost $1 billion", so the Q4 FY24 classics quarter was about $3.0B to $3.3B (about $12B annualized at peak).
- Q2 FY26: about $550M, down "over 20%", so the Q2 FY26 classics quarter was about $1.65B to $2.2B (about $6.6B to $8.8B annualized).
- Peak minus FY25 (>20%, about $2.4B) minus FY26 (>$2B) gives an FY26 exit level of roughly **$6B to $8B, 13% to 17% of FY26 revenue**. Rule L2 (under 10%) fails.
- Dunk alone: Q1 FY27 $200M = about 50%, so Dunk was about $400M in Q1 FY26 and about $200M in Q1 FY27, roughly **$0.8B to $1.0B a year** now. Further Dunk drag is mechanically capped at about $200M a quarter and laps in Q1 FY28. This is the one piece that is near a bottom.
- Jordan Brand: 13% of Q1 FY27 revenue = about $1.46B in the quarter, down mid-teens. FY26 $7,034M (10-K p. 32, OPENED repo). The retro portion is not disclosed. If retro is half of Jordan footwear, the newly exposed base is about $2B to $3B, and a 20% cut is $0.4B to $0.6B a year of new drag starting just as the Dunk drag ends. (Retro share is an assumption; Nike has not disclosed it.)

### B. Where the original note is wrong or overstated

1. **The Jordan retro cut is not purely "still ahead". It is already in the Q1 numbers.** Nike framed Jordan's mid-teens Q1 decline as "the scale of these actions in Q1". Jordan was -3% reported (-5% currency-neutral) for all of FY26 (FY26 10-K, repo fact base), so Jordan went from about -3% to about -15% in one quarter [my arithmetic: Q1 FY27 Jordan about 13% x $11.2B = about $1.46B, implying about $1.7B a year earlier]. The repo's own Sole Retriever counts (`data/jordan_retro_release_counts_annual.csv`, branch wonderful-franklin, OPENED repo) also show the cut on specific silhouettes already in calendar 2026: AJ4 adult releases 33 (2025) to 14 (2026 dated, to Oct 13), AJ1 Mid 16 to 5, AJ1 High OG 15 to 10, all Jordan incl. kids 186 to 132. The note's line "the cut has not shown up in counts yet" is wrong for AJ4 and AJ1; only the core-retro total (113 to 91 so far) is ambiguous because the 2026 calendar is not complete. Consequence: the retro cut started around Q4 FY26 to Q1 FY27, so it laps around Q1 FY28 at the earliest, not Q2 to Q3 FY28. This makes the SHORT case on the 2 to 3 quarter horizon slightly weaker in degree (some of the pain is already in the base) but does not flip it, because Nike still says North America "will feel the biggest impact" and North America grew +2% in Q1 (so the NA leg is still ahead).
2. **The narrow "three classics" drag has in fact shrunk a lot, which the note understates.** Nike's own disclosed classics headwind: about $1B in Q4 FY25, about $550M (4.4 points) in Q2 FY26, "roughly a 5-point headwind" in Q3 FY26, and only the Dunk's $200M (about 1.7 points) called out in Q1 FY27, with AF1 "stable" (Q2 FY26, Q3 FY26 and Q1 FY27 transcripts, OPENED). For Dunk, AF1 and AJ1 alone, the drag has roughly fallen from about 5 points to about 2 points, i.e. that piece is near its bottom. The SHORT verdict survives only because the question also includes Jordan retro and "aged, higher volume, sportswear footwear", and those new legs replace the old one: Sportswear plus Jordan was still "a high single-digit drag" in Q1 FY27.
3. **Google Trends for Jordan 4 partly measures the supply cut, not only demand.** Search for a silhouette spikes on release weeks. With AJ4 releases down from 33 to 14, a 30% fall in "Jordan 4" search is partly mechanical. So S4 is weaker evidence of falling demand than the note implies. It is still consistent with falling revenue, which is what the crux asks. Dunk Low and Jordan 1 searches fell just as much in Jan to Mar 2026 (US, -19% and -21% YoY, my recompute from the same CSVs) as in Jul to Oct (-26%, -23%), so there is no narrowing; AF1 was +12% in Jan to Mar, so its bottom is confirmed twice.
4. **World Cup roll-off is under-weighted.** The note dismisses it as a minority of Performance. Nike said Performance was up high single digits "benefiting from the World Cup", and Football grew double digits. If the World Cup added even $250M to $350M to Q1, that alone is 2.5 to 3.5 points of NIKE Brand ex China. That could explain most of the gap between Q1's -0.8% and the -2.8% (top of guide) in the rest of the year. It cannot explain the -6.2% (bottom of guide). So the guide-implied deterioration is real but may be mostly non-lifestyle at the top of the range. The size of the World Cup boost is not disclosed; this remains open.

### D. Data that would point the other way (LONG) and was checked

- Nike Q1 FY27 total classics dollar drag: down to about 2 points (above). LONG for the original three classics only.

### E. Skeptic verdict

**SHORT holds on the question as asked (2 to 3 quarters), but strength is "moderate", not "strong."** Reasons to keep SHORT: Nike's own guide and words ("remainder of fiscal '27 and into '28"; the H2 improvement line dropped); Jordan decline accelerated from -3% to mid-teens and the NA retro leg is still ahead; aged sportswear "impacted our future order books"; category data (Circana) shows sport lifestyle still falling; nike.com sale assortment +52% YoY. Reasons to cut strength: (1) the Dunk/AF1/AJ1 drag has already fallen from about 5 points to about 2 points, so the original classics really are near a bottom; (2) the retro cut started in Q1 FY27, so it laps in Q1 FY28, not later; (3) part of the guided deterioration could be World Cup roll-off rather than lifestyle; (4) the new CFO set the guide to be "reasonable and attainable". For the pitch, the honest framing is: the lifestyle drag does not shrink before Q1 FY28, but by Q1 FY28 both the Dunk and the first year of the retro cut lap, so the case for a bottom is a FY28 story, not a FY27 one.

What would flip this: Q2 FY27 (late December) showing Sportswear plus Jordan drag at low single digits, or Investor Day (Nov 16 to 17) sizing the retro cut as small and front-loaded in Q1 FY27. Fall 2026 Piper Sandler teen survey (due this fall) showing Nike footwear mindshare up would be the first independent demand signal.

### B. Does the new data independently confirm the verdict?

Independent confirmations (different source, same answer):
- **Jordan retro has not bottomed.** SNKRDUNK AJ1 High resale fell faster in 2026 than 2025 in USD (-22% vs -16%) and AJ4 is still about -15%, with trade days down 27% to 28% on the old cohort. This confirms the SneakerPing US finding (AJ1 and AJ4 general releases 22% to 27% below retail) from a different market and platform. That is the leg Nike says hits North America next, so it supports S1/S3 for the 2 to 3 quarter window.
- **Dunk attention has not bottomed.** Wikipedia excess (-15 to -21 pp) agrees with Google Trends (-27%) and with organic nike.com lifestyle reviews (-32%, second year running). Three different measurement systems, no narrowing.
- **No clean-up visible at Nike's own full-price channel.** Lifestyle-classics discount share on nike.com in Aug to Sep rose for the second year (41% to 47%).

Independent confirmations of the LONG pieces the skeptic already granted:
- **Dunk and AF1 prices are at or near a floor.** In JPY both are up YoY; in USD Dunk is -5.5% (from -11.7%) and AF1 about -2%. This matches Nike's own words ("stable" AF1, Dunk drag capped at about $200M a quarter). It is the narrow "three classics" bottom, which was already in the skeptic review and does not change the 2 to 3 quarter answer because the question also covers Jordan retro and aged sportswear.
- **Relative resilience.** Nike classics lose resale activity about half as fast as rival lifestyle lines (-23% vs -40%). This says the whole lifestyle fad is fading, consistent with the taste-shift reading, which supply cuts do not fix. It is not a demand recovery.

Contradictions: none of the new sources shows the classics or Jordan retro drag narrowing on a 2 to 3 quarter horizon. The hiring data is the only LONG signal and it is explicitly a FY28 lever.

### C. Re-graded verdict

**SHORT, moderate (unchanged in grade; firmer within moderate).** Not upgraded to strong because (1) the strongest new sources (SNKRDUNK, Wikipedia, Reddit, Common Crawl discounts, nike.com organic reviews) are each weak to moderate on their own, with checker caveats (yen, sample breaks, wide crawl CIs, thin review counts); (2) the new resale data independently confirms the skeptic's LONG point that Dunk and AF1 are at a price floor; and (3) the skeptic's timing points (retro cut started in Q1 FY27 so laps in Q1 FY28; part of the guided deterioration may be World Cup roll-off) are untouched by the new data. Not downgraded because every new source that measures Jordan retro or Dunk demand/attention points the same way as Nike's Oct 1 guide, and the AJ1 High resale decline is accelerating in USD.

Pitch framing: "The old classics (Dunk, AF1) are at a floor, and resale in Japan and the US shows it. The next leg is Jordan retro, and resale shows AJ1 High still falling faster than a year ago. The product rebuild is real in hiring but lands in FY28." Catalysts that would change this: Q2 FY27 Sportswear plus Jordan drag at low single digits; Investor Day sizing the retro cut as small; the Fall 2026 Piper Sandler teen survey.
