# Deep Dive: David Denton (CFO) and Jane Ewing (Chief Commercial Officer)

**Prepared:** October 12, 2026 | **Part of:** Nike case competition research (see `REPORT.md`)

**Why these two.** They are the only outside hires among Nike CEO Elliott Hill's senior team. Hill filled almost every other senior role with Nike insiders or returnees. So these two are the clearest statement of what Hill believes Nike cannot supply from within: **financial discipline** (Denton) and **retail and channel execution, especially in China** (Ewing).

**Evidence standard.** Direct fetches of SEC and company pages failed in this environment, so everything below comes from search-result summaries. Labels used: **checked** (stated consistently in the summaries), **conflicting** (sources disagree), **my inference** (reasoning, not a sourced fact), **not found** (searched and absent). Treat all of it as needing confirmation against primary documents.

### 1.1 Snapshot

### 1.2 Career timeline
| Period | Employer and role | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|---|
| About 1998 to 2018 | **CVS Health**, rising to EVP and CFO | Reuters (2018) reported he **led the integration of Caremark** after CVS bought the pharmacy-benefits manager, and was a key figure in negotiating the terms and financing of CVS's **$69 billion Aetna acquisition**. |
| Earlier | Deloitte & Touche, senior manager (one LinkedIn-based source) | Medium confidence |
| Aug 2018 to Apr 2022 | **Lowe's**, EVP and CFO | Announced Aug 22, 2018. Marvin Ellison (Lowe's CEO) praised his finance and transformation experience. In April 2022, Lowe's said he would leave "to pursue another opportunity at a publicly traded company outside the industry." |
| May 2, 2022 to mid-2026 | **Pfizer**, EVP and CFO | See 1.3. Pfizer said he was leaving for "another opportunity"; Cecile Guegan (about two decades at Pfizer) became interim CFO. |
| Aug 2026 | **Nike**, EVP and CFO | Joined as Nike's turnaround entered its third restructuring phase. |

**Pattern (my inference):** Denton's career is a series of **CFO roles at large companies during large transformations or deals**: CVS (acquisitions: Caremark, Aetna), Lowe's (operational turnaround), Pfizer (post-COVID reset and acquisitions). He moves roughly every four years. Each time, the company was reshaping itself, which is exactly Nike's position.

### 1.3 What he did at each stop, and what the evidence does and does not show

**CVS (about two decades).**
- Checked: led the Caremark integration; helped negotiate and finance the Aetna deal ($69 billion).
- Not found: his specific titles and dates inside CVS, and how those integrations ultimately performed.

**Lowe's (2018 to 2022).**
- Checked: Lowe's 2022 annual report quotes CEO Ellison saying adjusted operating margin reached **13% in 2022, a more than 440 basis point improvement from 2018** (so about 8.6% in 2018, my arithmetic). GAAP operating margin in 2022 was reported as 10.5%, so the 13% figure is the adjusted measure.
- **Important limit:** the sources credit the margin gain to Lowe's strategy under Ellison. **None attributes it to Denton personally.** He was the CFO during the period, so he sat at the center of the numbers, but this is not evidence that he drove the improvement.
- Conflict: a third-party table lists different year-by-year margins (e.g., 11.64% for 2022) that do not match Lowe's own 10.5%. Rely on the company's figures.
- Context: Lowe's shares fell roughly one-third from a late-2021 peak to about $171 by June 2022, which one analysis attributes to fading pandemic project demand. The full 2018 to 2022 share-price return was not found.
- Not found: any mention of him in the Total Home strategy sources.

**Pfizer (2022 to 2026).**
- Checked: **cost program grew over time.** Per Pfizer remarks: a $4 billion net savings target, raised to about $4.5 billion, then $5.7 billion by the end of 2026 (a year ahead of plan), and **$6.7 billion through 2029** per the August 2026 earnings remarks. These are cumulative targets that changed across several announcements; use the latest.
- Checked: Pfizer completed the roughly **$43 billion Seagen** acquisition in December 2023. CEO Albert Bourla credited Denton with leadership through the Seagen, Biohaven, and Metsera acquisitions.
- Checked: his 2025 reported pay was **$9.6 million**, up from $8.1 million in 2024.
- **Conflicting:** Pfizer's share price during his tenure. One article says the stock lost over 55% from its 2021 pandemic peak (a different starting point from his May 2022 start). Another source reports a +23.9% total return over the 12 months to Sept 4, 2026. Recent prices were about $24 to $28 in July to August 2026. **Neither source measures his tenure.** Do not use Pfizer's stock to judge Denton.
- Context: Pfizer's COVID product revenue (Paxlovid and the vaccine) fell sharply from its peak, and 2026 guidance embeds a drag of about $1.1 billion from loss of exclusivity (secondary sources).
- Not found: why he left Pfizer beyond "another opportunity."

### 1.4 Why Nike wanted him (what is sourced versus my inference)
- **Sourced:** Hill called him "a proven public-company CFO" who knows how to help "great consumer brands operate with discipline and invest to win." Per Bloomberg's reading of the employee memo, Hill said Nike is on the right path but needs to "move faster and be more consistent." Commentary notes his career is built **entirely outside sporting goods** (healthcare, pharmacy retail, home improvement).
- **Sourced:** Fortune published a piece (July 2026) on why companies pay a premium for "battle-tested" CFOs and what Nike's struggling turnaround shows about that cost. I could not read it, only its headline.
- **My inference on fit:**
  1. **Cost program credibility.** Pace targets about $2.5 billion in savings through FY2031. Denton just ran a cost-savings program at Pfizer that grew from $4 billion to $6.7 billion in targets.
  2. **Capital allocation.** Nike pays about $2.4 billion in dividends while FY27 guided EPS ($1.15 to $1.35) is below the $1.64 annual dividend. A CFO who "oversees capital allocation" will face the dividend question first.
  3. **Retail and operations.** Lowe's and CVS are large retail operations with supply chains, a better fit for wholesale rebuilding than a pure pharma background.
  4. **Investor credibility.** A big-name, public-company CFO after a CFO exit and a chief accounting officer resignation gives investors a steady name.

### 1.5 Risks and open questions (my inference unless noted)
1. **No sports or apparel experience.** Nike's biggest risk is demand and brand, not cost. Cost discipline alone cannot fix falling revenue.
2. **Short tenure pattern.** About four years at each of the last two employers. A turnaround that pays off in FY2029 to FY2030 may outlast that pattern.
3. **Six weeks from start to Pace announcement.** Pace was announced Oct 1, 2026 and he started Aug 17. It is unclear how much he shaped it. (Not found.)
4. **Double role.** He is both CFO and interim principal accounting officer, a concentration of finance and accounting leadership while the control environment is changing. I found **no evidence of any accounting problem**; this is a governance observation, not an allegation.
5. **Reporting lines unclear.** Dec 2025: Global Sales and Nike Direct moved under the CFO. Sept 2026: Marketplace (Sales and Nike Direct) is now under Ewing. Whether Denton holds any commercial units is not confirmed.
6. One source says Hill's memo **expanded Michael Gonda's role to include strategy**. I could not identify Gonda's title or confirm this. (Not verified.)

### 2.2 Career timeline
| Period | Employer and role | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Before 2012 | **Diageo**: leadership roles including the Middle East, South Africa, and the **Global Travel Business** | **Conflicting tenure:** one source says about nine years leading a major global division; another says 20 years. |

**Correction to my earlier report.** `REPORT.md` originally described her Walmart work as "merchandising, digital, international." The sources found here show something different: **international operations, people (HR), sustainability, China.** I did not find merchandising or digital roles, so I have corrected the report.

### 2.3 What she did, and what the evidence does and does not show

**Sam's Club China (her most recent post).**
- Checked: coverage of the Nike hire describes her as overseeing a **multibillion-dollar membership business across about 30 cities**.
- Checked (context, from separate articles): Sam's Club China's **2025 sales exceeded 140 billion yuan, up about 40% year over year**, with paid members past **10.7 million** and over 67 stores by May 2026, and about 80% of Walmart China's revenue. Walmart's July-quarter China sales rose nearly 21% to about $7 billion.
- **Critical limit:** none of these sources credits Ewing. The Jan 2025 announcement placed her in the role; she may have led it through part of the growth period, but the sources do not say how long she held it, who built the strategy, or when she left. **The strong numbers belong to the business, not provably to her.**
- Also found: after a **June food-safety summons from Beijing authorities**, a former Alibaba vice-president, **Liu Peng**, was appointed president of Sam's Club China (the year is not stated; likely 2026). Whether this relates to Ewing's exit from the role is **not found** and should not be assumed.
- Current Walmart China CEO: Christina Zhu (Fortune, Oct 2026), the first native Chinese CEO.

**Why China matters to Nike.** Coverage says Greater China revenue has declined for **eight consecutive quarters** (secondary), and Nike plans to end partner-run online China storefronts from January 2027. Q1 FY27 Greater China fell about 22% (26% currency-neutral).

**Walmart International.** Running people operations for 700,000 associates across 27 countries and operations across international markets signals **large-scale organizational and operating experience**, not brand or product experience. (Not found: P&L results for any Walmart division she led.)

**Diageo.** Roles in emerging markets (Middle East, South Africa) and **travel retail** point to experience with **premium consumer brands sold through third-party channels**, which is structurally similar to Nike's wholesale model. (My inference.)

**Nike link.** Per Nike's bio, her **first job was selling Nike product in a sporting-goods store.** A personal link, not evidence of ability.

### 2.4 Why Nike wanted her (sourced versus my inference)
- **Sourced (single secondary source, BigGo Finance):** Hill's memo described her as a "**builder**" with deep global experience, and the piece says the appointment signals leadership recognizes the urgency of its most troubled market. Treat the characterization with caution; I could not read the memo.
- **Sourced:** Nike shares fell to a **12-year low** the day the hire was announced (BigGo). The move is not attributed to the hire.
- **My inference on fit:**
  1. **China experience at scale.** A leader who ran Walmart's China business, and its fastest-growing format, understands local consumers, digital retail, and fast delivery, all things Nike's China business is losing on.
  2. **Channel reset.** She owns Sales and Nike Direct, which Nike is rebalancing between its own stores and wholesale partners. A retailer's eye may help set terms.
  3. **Simpler structure.** Replacing a CCO role eliminated in December with a new CCO in September looks like the "remove layers" move was reversed or reshaped (see `REPORT.md` Section 10, point 2).

### 2.5 Risks and open questions (my inference unless noted)
1. **No sports or apparel brand experience at the operating level.** Her background is general retail, international operations, HR, and a spirits company.
2. **Role scope.** If regions report to Hill and Marketplace to Ewing, accountability for regional revenue is split. How that works day to day is not found.
3. **Timing of her China exit.** Her role at Sam's Club China ended around the time a food-safety summons and a leadership change were reported there. No source links the two. It is a question to ask, not a conclusion.
4. **Analyst reception unknown.** I found no analyst commentary on the Ewing hire specifically, or on Hill's other outside hires.

## Part 3: Reading the two hires together

| | **Denton** | **Ewing** |
|---|---|---|
| Source of expertise | Finance, deals, cost programs | International retail operations, China, people |
| Industry | Pharmacy/healthcare, home improvement, pharma | Mass retail, spirits, membership retail |
| Sports experience | None | None (first job sold Nike product) |
| Problem Hill is solving | Costs, capital allocation, investor credibility | Channel mix, China, retailer relations |
| Insider or outsider | Outsider | Outsider |
| Fit with the thesis | Supports "Nike needs discipline it lacks" | Supports "Nike needs retail muscle it lacks" |

**What the pair suggests (my inference):** Hill believes the **product and brand** can be rebuilt by insiders (McCartney, Montagne, Graham, Cain) but that the **operating machinery**, meaning finance and the commercial engine, needs outside professionals. If correct, it is a coherent division of labor. If wrong, Nike has put non-sport outsiders over its two most important numbers (profit and revenue by channel) while the product leaders are insiders who were present during the decline.

**Competing readings.**
- *Bullish reading:* experienced operators arriving as the plan moves from fixing the brand to fixing the business model; a clean break from the old commercial structure.
- *Bearish reading:* both arrive after exits of the previous holders, both lack sports-business experience, and both are brought in during a stretch of high turnover (see the exits chart); another reshuffle may follow.

**Neither reading can be settled by their résumés.** The evidence that will decide it is operational: Q2 FY27 results (about Dec 17), Investor Day (Nov 16 to 17), China trends, wholesale order books, and whether the dividend holds.

## Part 4: Questions to ask (for Investor Day or a case presentation)

**For Denton**
1. How did Pace's $2.5 billion target change after you joined, and what is your confidence in it?
2. What is your view on the dividend when guided EPS ($1.15 to $1.35) is below it ($1.64 a year)?
3. What happened with the chief accounting officer's resignation, and when will a permanent replacement be named?
4. What are the specific metrics you will hold the business to (inventory days, gross margin, SG&A)?

**For Ewing**
1. What did you learn running Sam's Club China that applies to Nike's China channel?
2. Who owns the P&L in each region now that Marketplace sits under you and regions report to Hill?
3. How will you set wholesale terms and avoid repeating the earlier mistake in either direction (too little or too much wholesale)?
4. What is the plan for China after partner-run online sales end in January 2027?
