# Deep Dive: David Denton (CFO) and Jane Ewing (Chief Commercial Officer)

**Prepared:** October 12, 2026 | **Part of:** Nike case competition research (see `REPORT.md`)

**Why these two.** They are two of the three outside hires on Nike CEO Elliott Hill's senior team. The third is Michael Gonda, Chief Communications and Strategy Officer since July 7, 2025, who joined from McDonald's (Nike IR release; Nike leadership roster). Hill filled almost every other senior role with Nike insiders or returnees. Denton and Ewing are the clearest statement of what Hill believes Nike cannot supply from within: **financial discipline** (Denton) and **retail and channel execution, with China experience** (Ewing).

**Evidence standard.** This version was rebuilt from primary sources: Nike's SEC filings (8-Ks and the 2026 proxy, read in full), Nike's own pages (newsroom releases and leadership profiles at about.nike.com, opened and read), and the SEC filings of Lowe's and Pfizer for their figures. Citations follow the report's format, for example (2026 proxy p. 27) or (8-K filed June 23, 2026). Labels: **checked** (primary source opened), **secondary** (news page opened), **conflicting** (sources disagree), **my inference** (my reasoning, not a sourced fact), **not found** (searched and absent).

**Pattern (my inference):** Denton's career is a series of finance roles at large companies during large transformations or deals: CVS (Aetna), Lowe's (operational turnaround) and Pfizer (post-COVID reset and acquisitions). He has moved roughly every four years. Each time, the company was reshaping itself, which is Nike's position too.

### 1.3 What he did at each stop, and what the evidence does and does not show

**CVS (about two decades).** Checked: CFO from 2010; balance-sheet restructuring; a role in the Aetna deal (Lowe's release, 2018). Not found: how those integrations ultimately performed.

**Lowe's (2018 to 2022).** Lowe's announced his appointment on Aug 22, 2018, and his start was moved up to Nov 19, 2018 (Lowe's 8-Ks filed Oct 4 and Nov 20, 2018, Item 5.02). He left effective Apr 30, 2022 "to accept another role at a publicly traded company outside the Company's industry and is not due to a dispute or disagreement" (Lowe's 8-K filed Apr 8, 2022, Item 5.02). *The previous draft quoted this as "to pursue another opportunity," which is not the filed wording.*
- **Margins:** GAAP operating margin rose from **5.64%** in fiscal 2018 ($4,018M of operating income on $71,309M of sales; Lowe's FY2018 10-K p. 43) to **10.47%** in fiscal 2022 ($10,159M on $97,059M; Lowe's FY2022 10-K p. 38), a gain of about 483 bps (my arithmetic). On an adjusted basis, Ellison's shareholder letter says "we grew adjusted operating margin to 13% in 2022 [...] which is more than a 440-basis point improvement from 2018" (Lowe's 2022 annual report, ARS filed Apr 13, 2023, letter p. 3). The fiscal 2018 adjusted margin was 8.56% (Lowe's 8-K Ex. 99.1 filed Feb 27, 2019), and the 2023 proxy gives 13.0% for 2022.
- **Important limit:** the filings credit the margin gain to Lowe's strategy under Ellison. **None attributes it to Denton personally.** He was the CFO during the period, so he sat at the center of the numbers, but that is not evidence he drove the improvement.
- Not found: any filing language on his specific role in the Total Home strategy.

**Pfizer (2022 to 2026).** Elected CFO on Apr 11, 2022, effective May 2, 2022, with a $1.25M base salary and a $5M one-time cash payment to replace forfeited Lowe's pay (Pfizer 8-K filed Apr 12, 2022, Item 5.02). He stepped down effective Aug 15, 2026 "for a professional opportunity outside of the pharmaceutical industry in consumer goods" (Pfizer 8-K filed June 18, 2026, Item 5.02).
- **Cost program, in Pfizer's own filings:** launched in October 2023 at "at least $3.5 billion" (8-K Ex. 99 filed Oct 13, 2023), raised to at least $4.0B in December 2023 (8-K Ex. 99 filed Dec 13, 2023), then "approximately $4.5 billion" by the end of 2025 (8-K Ex. 99 filed Feb 4, 2025). In April 2025 it became "approximately $5.7 billion from this program through 2027" (8-K filed Apr 29, 2025, Item 2.05); by August 2026 Pfizer said $5.7B by the end of 2026. Finally, "total net cost savings of approximately $6.7 billion [...] through 2029" (8-K Ex. 99 filed Aug 4, 2026). *Correction: the previous draft said the program started at $4 billion; it started at $3.5 billion.*
- **Seagen:** completed Dec 14, 2023 at $229 a share; "The total fair value of the consideration transferred was $44.2 billion ($43.4 billion, net of cash acquired)" (Pfizer FY2023 10-K, Note 2A, p. 64). Pfizer's release on his departure says his tenure "included several major business transactions, including Seagen, Biohaven, and Metsera" (secondary copy). The previous draft's claim that CEO Albert Bourla personally credited him was not found on an opened page.
- **Pay:** summary-compensation total of **$9,670,182 for 2025** (about $9.7M, not the $9.6M in the previous draft), $8,124,542 for 2024 and $5,279,536 for 2023 (Pfizer 2026 DEF 14A, p. 78).
- **Boards:** "Director of Tapestry, Inc. from 2014 to 2023" (Pfizer FY2023 10-K p. 28) and "Director of Haleon plc from March 2023 to December 2024" (Pfizer FY2024 10-K p. 26).
- **Share price:** Pfizer's stock performance over his tenure was not measured in any source, and the previous draft's secondary figures measured different periods. Do not use Pfizer's stock to judge Denton.
- **Context, not attributable to him:** Pfizer's COVID product revenue fell sharply from its peak during his tenure (Pfizer 10-Ks; not quantified here).

### 1.4 Why Nike wanted him (sourced versus inference)
- **Sourced:** Hill called him "a proven public-company CFO who knows how to help great consumer brands operate with discipline and invest to win" (Nike release, June 23, 2026).
- **Sourced:** Fortune (July 10, 2026, opened) described the one-time cash award as "a make-whole for the compensation he's walking away from at Pfizer."
- **Sourced:** analysts called the change a surprise. One said "it is a little bit surprising that [Nike] has decided to look outside the company" (secondary: Retail Dive).
- **My inference on fit:**
  1. **Cost program credibility.** Pace targets "approximately $2.5 billion in cumulative savings through fiscal 2031" with "approximately $1.0 billion of pre-tax charges" (8-K filed Oct 1, 2026, p. 2). Denton oversaw Pfizer's cost program as it grew from at least $3.5 billion to about $6.7 billion in targets (see 1.3).
  2. **Capital allocation.** Nike paid $2,407 million of dividends in FY2026 while cash from operations minus capex was $2,184 million (FY26 10-K p. 58; my arithmetic). The quarterly dividend is $0.41, or $1.64 a year (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 5), above the FY2027 adjusted EPS outlook of $1.15 to $1.35 (Q1 FY27 release p. 1). A CFO who oversees capital allocation will face the dividend question first.
  3. **Retail and operations.** Lowe's and CVS are large retail operations with supply chains, a better fit for a wholesale rebuild than a pure pharma background.
  4. **Investor credibility.** A big-name public-company CFO arriving after a CFO exit and a chief accounting officer resignation gives investors a steady name.

### 1.5 Risks and open questions (my inference unless noted)
1. **No sports or apparel experience.** Nike's biggest risk is demand and brand, not cost. Cost discipline alone cannot fix falling revenue.
2. **Short tenure pattern.** About four years at each of his last two employers. A turnaround whose savings run to FY2031 may outlast that pattern.
3. **Six weeks from start to Pace.** He started Aug 17; Pace was announced Oct 1. How much he shaped it is not found. Pace "includes and builds upon the previous cost realignment plan announced in March 2026" (Q1 FY27 release p. 1), which predates him.
4. **Double role.** He is both CFO and interim principal accounting officer (8-K filed Aug 10, 2026), which concentrates finance and accounting leadership while the control environment is changing. I found **no evidence of any accounting problem**; this is a governance observation, not an allegation.
5. **Reporting lines.** In December 2025, Global Sales and NIKE Direct moved under the then-CFO (Nike release, Dec 3, 2025). From September 2026 the Marketplace organization (Sales and NIKE Direct) sits under Ewing (Nike release, Aug 27, 2026). So the commercial units have left the CFO's remit.

### 2.3 What she did, and what the evidence does and does not show

**Sam's Club China (interim CEO, Feb 2025 to Jan 2026).**
- Nike credits her with "accelerating top- and bottom-line growth during a leadership transition while overseeing a multibillion-dollar membership business across 30 cities" (Nike release, Aug 27, 2026). This is the hiring company's characterization, not independent evidence.
- The period-of-tenure facts are now clearer: she was interim head until Liu Peng's appointment on Oct 27, 2025, then moved to ethics and compliance for Walmart China. The previous draft linked the leadership change to a food-safety summons "likely in 2026." The appointment date is October 2025, and no source opened links her move to any regulatory matter. **Do not assume a link.**
- Context, not attributable to her: Sam's Club China's strong growth in 2025 and 2026 was reported in separate articles; current Walmart China CEO Christina Zhu is credited in Fortune (Oct 2026) with the format's growth.

**Why China matters to Nike.** Greater China revenue has declined for **nine consecutive quarters** through Q1 FY2027 and fell 22% in Q1 FY2027 (26% currency-neutral) to $1,180 million (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 25). Nike says "The actions related to Converse are expected to continue throughout fiscal 2027 and for Greater China to extend beyond fiscal 2027" (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 20). From January 2027 Nike plans to sell online in China only through its own channels and official Tmall, JD.com and Douyin stores (secondary: Just Style).

**Nike link.** Per Nike's profile, her "first job was selling Nike products in a sporting goods store," and she was a competitive middle-distance and cross-country runner. A personal connection, not evidence of ability.

### 2.4 Why Nike wanted her (sourced versus inference)
- **Sourced:** Hill's memo: "She's a builder. She's competitive." (Nike release, Aug 27, 2026). The previous draft attributed this to a single secondary source; it is now confirmed in Nike's own release.
- **Market context (market data):** NKE closed at $38.44 on Aug 27, 2026, the announcement day. That was the lowest close since August 2014, so the previous draft's "12-year low" is confirmed. The move is not attributable to the hire.
- **My inference on fit:**
  1. **China experience at scale.** Someone who ran Walmart's fastest-growing China format through a transition understands local consumers, digital retail and fast delivery, all areas where Nike's China business is losing.
  2. **Channel reset.** She owns Sales and NIKE Direct, which Nike is rebalancing. NIKE Direct fell 8% and wholesale 1% in Q1 FY2027 (Q1 FY27 10-Q p. 22). A retailer's eye may help set wholesale terms.
  3. **Structure.** The CCO role was eliminated in December 2025 and re-created in September 2026 in narrower form, without the geographies. The "remove layers" move was partly reversed (see `REPORT.md` Section 10, point 2).

### 2.5 Risks and open questions (my inference unless noted)
1. **No sports or apparel brand experience at the operating level.** Her background is mass retail, international operations, compliance and spirits.
2. **Split accountability.** Regions report to Hill and Marketplace to Ewing, so revenue accountability is shared. How that works day to day is not found.
3. **Timing of her China role change.** She left the Sam's Club China post when a permanent president was named in October 2025. No source links this to performance. Ask, don't conclude.
4. **Analyst reception unknown.** No analyst commentary on the Ewing hire specifically was found.

## Part 3: Reading the two hires together

| | **Denton** | **Ewing** |
|---|---|---|
| Source of expertise | Finance, deals, cost programs | International retail operations, merchandising, digital, China |
| Industry | Pharmacy and healthcare, home improvement, pharma | Mass retail, spirits, membership retail |
| Sports experience | None | None professionally (first job sold Nike product; competitive runner) |
| Problem Hill is solving | Costs, capital allocation, investor credibility | Channel mix, China, retailer relations |
| Fit with the thesis | Supports "Nike needs discipline it lacks" | Supports "Nike needs retail muscle it lacks" |

**What the pair suggests (my inference):** Hill believes the **product and brand** can be rebuilt by insiders (McCartney, Montagne, Graham, Cain) but that the **operating machinery** (finance, the commercial engine and, with Gonda, communications) needs outside professionals. If correct, it is a coherent division of labor. If wrong, Nike has put non-sport outsiders over its two most important numbers (profit and revenue by channel) while the product leaders are insiders who were present during the decline.

**Competing readings.**
- *Bullish:* experienced operators arriving as the plan moves from fixing the brand to fixing the business model; a clean break from the old commercial structure.
- *Bearish:* both arrive after the previous holders left, both lack sports-business experience, and both join during heavy turnover (14 named senior exits since Hill started; `REPORT.md` Section 9). Another reshuffle may follow.

**Neither reading can be settled by their résumés.** The evidence that will decide it is operational: Investor Day (Nov 16 to 17, 2026; Nike release, Oct 1, 2026), Q2 FY2027 results in December, China trends, wholesale order books, and whether the dividend holds.

## Part 4: Questions to ask (for Investor Day or a case presentation)

**For Denton**
1. How did Pace's $2.5 billion target change after you joined, and what is your confidence in it?
2. What is your view on the dividend when guided adjusted EPS ($1.15 to $1.35) is below the $1.64 annual rate?
3. When will a permanent chief accounting officer be named?
4. Which metrics will you hold the business to (inventory days, gross margin, overhead)?

**For Ewing**
1. What did you learn running Sam's Club China that applies to Nike's China channel?
2. Who owns the P&L in each region now that Marketplace sits under you and regions report to Hill?
3. How will you set wholesale terms and avoid repeating the earlier mistake in either direction (too little or too much wholesale)?
4. What is the plan for China after partner-run online stores end in January 2027?
