Pricing slipped again

The price line in Nike's margin bridge went from +0.8 points (Q3 FY26) to −0.3 points (Q1 FY27), "primarily due to higher discounts and channel mix."

Marketing costs more per dollar of sales

Advertising and sponsorship rose from 8.3% of revenue (FY24) to 10.3% (FY26), about $880M a year more.

To be precise: Nike added new actions that run past fiscal 2027 (which ends May 31, 2027) rather than formally moving the old date, and the December date no longer appears in the 10-Q or on the call. The full 10-Q sentence reads: "additional actions related to NIKE Sportswear and Jordan Brand are expected to extend beyond fiscal 2027. The need for these additional actions reflects, in part, higher levels of discounting and broader marketplace pressure." Caveat on pricing: channel mix alone could explain much of the -30 basis points, so this is supporting evidence; the stronger evidence is Nike's own sentence linking the new actions to discounting. We use the FY26 marketing figure (10.25%) rather than Q1 FY27's 11.2%, which was inflated by World Cup timing.
