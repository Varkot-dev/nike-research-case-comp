The other side

The three best arguments against us

"Nike is best in class. At its normal margin, the stock is fine."

That is our point: the price already assumes the normal margin or better. Nike is at 8.3% today and 7.2% in analysts' FY28 forecast.

"The worst is over. Beaten-down stocks bounce."

North America and running are improving, and fallen large caps often rebound. But Nike itself guides the next three quarters lower.

"The short is crowded, you pay a 4.6% dividend, and Berenberg said it first."

We accept the cost. But guided earnings of $1.15 to $1.35 do not cover the $1.64 dividend, and our case rests on filings, not taste.

Sources: Nike filings and Q1 FY27 guide; repo note 20 (base rates); consensus estimates

Things we concede openly: the decline was self-inflicted (Nike fell 10.8% currency-neutral over FY24 to FY26 while peers grew 15.4%); North America profit margins are close to their past level on a like-for-like basis (about 26.4% vs 27.2%); analysts already expect FY28 earnings up about 28%; deeply fallen large caps have had a median one-year rebound of +44% to +52%; and the downside we see is moderate ($20 to $26), not dramatic. On valuation: even on FY29 consensus earnings ($2.31), Nike trades at 15.3x, still above peers at 13.2x on 2027 earnings. A dividend freeze or token raise in November is the likely outcome and would be a mild positive surprise against us.
